1.  How to read this

   Each entry carries a CLASS:

   5a     a value chosen because it produced the right pixels, which
          is what CLAUDE.md rule 5a forbids. These are the ones that
          silently fight the real code when it lands.

   gap    the original does something we cannot currently express.
          Not a hack - an unimplemented mechanism. Honest, but it
          keeps the port from matching.

   ours   code with no original at all, written to fill a hole. Fine
          when it is a debug aid and named as one; not fine when it
          stands in for game logic nobody has read yet.

   The order is by DEPENDENCY, not by severity: 1 must be read
   before 2 and 3 can be, because all three come out of the same
   block of code.

2.  The list

2.1.  The race bike placement is two lifts, not one matrix

   Class 5a.  src/race.c.

   maybe_Racer_Draw builds ONE placement and both the body and the
   part pivots come off it. The port has two different numbers:

       s_race_bikeLift  = 2 * 96 * rayonAr                 = 61.44
       bikeWorld[10]    = 96 * hauteurCadre + 48 * rayonAr = 71.04

   The first is derived, but from maybe_Racer_PlaceWheels 0x0045c7c0
   - a different function than the one that places the bike for
   drawing. The second is a formula fitted to a single stationary
   measurement of a single bike, and its own comment says so.

   WHAT WAS MEASURED. The position handed to FUN_00403740 at
   0x0045ff99 sits at racerPos.y + 43.13, constant while the bike
   yaws and leans:

       pos - racerPos   (-33.0098, 43.1268, -44.9199)
                        (-34.9307, 43.1295, -43.3926)

   In matBikeRoot's frame the first component reads 55.74 against
   96 * hauteurCadre = 55.68.

   DO NOT FIT A FORMULA TO THE TOTAL. Four have been fitted and
   refuted already, because rayonAv equals rayonAr on both pocket
   sets AND on the full dirt bike - only the street bike separates
   them, so a wrong formula agrees with three of the four bike
   sets.

   AND IT IS NOT A LIFT AT ALL. Read out of 0x0045f8c0..0x0045f9ee,
   the placement is assembled from the WHEEL CONTACTS plus two
   suspension-dependent vectors, each rotated by its own matrix:

       dir  = normalize(frontContact - rearContact)
       mid  = rearContact + dir * 48 * axeDistance

       A    = ( 0, 96 * hauteurCadre + 40 * forkDive,       0 )
       B    = ( 0, rayonAr * (12 * springBounce + 48),      0 )

       pos  = mid + M_A * A + M_B * B

   The four scalars are verified: 0x004d102c = 96.0, 0x004d0f8c =
   40.0, 0x004d0e98 = 48.0, 0x004d1050 = 0.125, and 12.0 is
   0.125 * 96.0 folded. hauteurCadre is carac 22 at 0x005092d0 and
   rayonAr carac 16 at 0x005092b8, both at stride 292. forkDive is
   racer+0x2e39 and springBounce racer+0x2e91.

   SO A CONSTANT CAN NEVER BE RIGHT. forkDive and springBounce move
   every frame, which is exactly why the bike sinks under braking
   and over crests and why four static formulas each matched one
   captured frame and nothing else.

   CAPTURED, THREE FRAMES, hooks at 0x0045f95d, 0x0045f98c and
   0x0045f9d9 - one at rest on the grid and two under power in a
   left turn. Every sign is POSITIVE; nothing is negated.

   AT REST, forkDive = springBounce = 0:

       rearContact  (-128.0000,   0.0000, 1974.0000)
       B'           (  0.0000,   15.3600,    0.0000)
       A'           (  0.0000,   55.6800,    0.0000)
       pos          (-128.0000,  71.0400, 2058.0000)

   so 15.36 = 48 * rayonAr with rayonAr 0.32, 55.68 = 96 *
   hauteurCadre with hauteurCadre 0.58, and pos.z - rearContact.z
   = 84.0 = 48 * axeDistance with axeDistance 1.75.

   LEANING, two frames, forkDive 0.18635 and 0.17634:

       A'  (-6.3360, 62.7651, 2.5091)   |A'| = 63.134
       A'  (-5.2932, 62.4681, 2.2962)   |A'| = 62.734

   against 96 * hauteurCadre + 40 * forkDive = 63.134 and 62.734.
   Exact. So A KEEPS ITS LENGTH AND IS ROTATED by a matrix that
   carries the bike's orientation.

   B IS NOT ROTATED. On the same two frames it read (0.0063,
   15.3600, 0.0165) and (0.0193, 15.3599, 0.0494) - the length is
   48 * rayonAr to five figures and the direction is world up to
   within a twentieth of a degree. springBounce was 0 throughout,
   so the 12 * springBounce term is untested.

   THE y ARITHMETIC CLOSES EXACTLY. pos.y - A'.y - B'.y is 0.0000
   on all three frames, and both contacts sit at y = -0.0, so the
   anchor lies in the contact plane.

   AND THE ANCHOR IS THE REAR WHEEL CONTACT, NOT THE RACER
   POSITION. That single fact is the bug: race.c hangs the bike off
   r->pos and adds a constant, where the original hangs it off
   racer+0x338 and adds two suspension vectors.

   THE ANCHOR IS THE MIDPOINT OF THE TWO CONTACTS, captured
   directly at 0x0045f9a8 where EDX holds it and ECX holds the
   sum. Three frames in a left turn:

       |front - rear|   168.0000   = 96 * axeDistance, exactly
       |mid   - rear|    84.0000   = 48 * axeDistance, exactly
       (mid-rear)/(front-rear)     = 0.500000, exactly
       pos - (mid + sum)           < 1.3e-4 on every component

   AN EARLIER RUN OF THIS MEASUREMENT WAS POLLUTED BY WALL
   COLLISIONS. It reconstructed the anchor by subtraction and got
   82.65 and 84.93 against the required 84.0, which looked like a
   flaw in the model and was not: the bike was being pushed off
   the rails between the contacts being written and the draw
   reading them. With tools/mr_cheats.py --on nowalls the same
   measurement is exact on every frame. Any capture that involves
   cornering needs the rails off.

   THE FRONT CONTACT IS WRITTEN HERE TOO. The first Vec3_Add, at
   0x0045f87c, stores rear + dir * 96 * axeDistance into
   racer+0x1B4 - so the front contact is recomputed from the rear
   one and the wheelbase every draw, not carried independently.

   A IS ROTATED, B IS NOT. Over five frames |A'| tracked
   96 * hauteurCadre + 40 * forkDive exactly:

       forkDive   0.18635  0.17634  0.16635  0.15635  0.14635
       |A'|        63.134   62.734   62.334   61.934   61.534

   while B' held 48 * rayonAr to five figures pointing at world up
   to within a twentieth of a degree. springBounce read 0 on every
   frame, so the 12 * springBounce term is still untested.

   WHAT IS STILL OPEN: WHICH MATRIX ROTATES A. Its up axis is
   A'/|A'|, which is measured - (-0.06806, 0.99702, 0.03637) on
   one frame - but the matrix itself is not. racer+0x2e55 is NOT
   it: read live it is a pure yaw, up axis exactly (0, 1, 0), so
   the roll is composed on somewhere between there and the draw.

   The port already builds that composition for the body - the
   rolled matBikeRoot in race.c - so the implementable step is to
   use its up axis for A and CHECK it against the five captured
   A' values. That is a prediction to test, not a value to fit.

   IMPLEMENTED in race_bike_place, and the arithmetic checks out
   against the three clean frames:

       forkDive   |A| measured   |A| formula      error
       0.16635        62.3345       62.3340    +5.1e-04
       0.15635        61.9355       61.9340    +1.5e-03
       0.14635        61.5344       61.5340    +4.1e-04

   with pos - (mid + A + B) closing to 1.2e-4 on every component.

   THE UNIT-LENGTH CHECK IS THE INDEPENDENT ONE. Recovering the up
   axis as (sum - B) / |A formula| gives exactly 1.000000 on all
   three frames. Any error in B - a wrong scalar, a rotation it
   does not actually get - would leave that off unity, so it
   confirms B as 48 * rayonAr along world up without relying on
   the same reasoning that produced it.

   A RIDES THE LEAN AXIS - SETTLED. Captured at a real 52 degree
   lean, with A' and racer+0x2e1d read on the same frame:

       lean deg   forkDive   |A| meas   |A| form   tilt deg
       -52.0835   -0.01246    55.1815    55.1815    52.0753
       -52.1345   -0.01400    55.1201    55.1201    52.1191
       -52.1787   -0.01476    55.0896    55.0896    52.1631
       -52.1716   -0.01514    55.0745    55.0745    52.1631
       -52.2137   -0.01532    55.0672    55.0672    52.2070
       -52.1510   -0.01541    55.0636    55.0636    52.1411

   |A| matches the formula to four decimals on every frame, and the
   tilt of A' away from world up matches |lean| to within 0.016 of a
   degree. So the up axis A rides IS the lean-rolled one, which is
   what race_bike_place uses. The earlier frames could not show this
   because they leaned only 2 to 4 degrees.

   springBounce read 0 on every frame captured, at full lean and
   under power, so the 12 * springBounce term is inert in practice
   and still untested. [open, but it changes nothing observed]

   CORRECTION: B RIDES THE SAME AXIS AS A. The first version of
   this section said B was world up, on five frames that were
   captured with the bike jammed against a guard rail and with the
   lean INFERRED from A rather than read. Re-measured with A', B'
   and racer+0x2e1d on the same frame, across leans from 8.5 to 52
   degrees:

       lean deg    |B|      B tilt    |A|      A tilt   |Bhat-Ahat|
       -52.1449   15.3600   52.1411   55.0621  52.1411   0.000471
       -11.6040   15.3600   11.6016   55.0678  11.6016   0.000000
       -10.4453   15.3600   10.4370   55.0683  10.4370   0.000000
        -9.4020   15.3600    9.3823   55.0688   9.3823   0.000000
        -8.4627   15.3600    8.4595   55.0693   8.4595   0.000000

   so the placement collapses to one scalar along one axis:

       pos = mid + (96 * hauteurCadre + 40 * forkDive
                    + rayonAr * (12 * springBounce + 48)) * up

   Adding B straight up instead is 13 units out at 52 degrees.

   SYMPTOM: the bike sits about a tyre's rubber depth into the
   road, rim flush with the surface.

2.2.  The ride height is a static stand-in - CLOSED

   It disappeared rather than being replaced, which is what 2.1
   predicted. The placement is built from the wheel contacts, and
   those already sit on the tarmac, so the ride height is whatever
   the suspension made it that frame. Both the bounds scan in
   race_load and s_race_bikeLift are gone.

2.3.  The rider is not placed separately from the bike

   Class gap.  src/model.c, src/race.c.  SCOPE: CRASHES ONLY.

   maybe_Racer_Draw calls Skl_BuildPartMatrices TWICE and composes
   parts 3..14 against the RIDER position and every other part
   against the bike position. MR_BikeDraw has one placement for
   the whole skeleton.

   IN NORMAL RIDING THE SPLIT IS A NO-OP, which is the finding
   that resizes this item. Both halves were read live:

       matRiderRoot   racer+0x3085   up axis tilt 52.1411 deg
       bike root      racer+0x2e55   up axis tilt  0.0000 deg
       lean                          52.1510 deg

   matRiderRoot is the bike's yaw matrix WITH THE LEAN ROLLED IN -
   exactly the matrix the port already passes as bd.orient - while
   racer+0x2e55 carries yaw alone. And riderPosition, at
   racer+0x30b5, is written from ESP+0x1b0 at 0x00460e5a, which is
   the bike placement itself. So while crashLatch is clear the
   rider and the bike share both matrix and position.

   THE SPLIT ONLY BITES IN A CRASH. There maybe_Racer_Draw skips
   the writeback, so matRiderRoot keeps the tumble that
   UpdateCrash composes into it and riderPosition keeps its last
   value plus a vertical offset - 0x004d1060, or 0x004d1024 when
   PersoAnim is set.

   IMPLEMENTED. MR_BikeDraw gained riderOrient and riderPos, and
   bike_draw_body_parts composes parts 3..14 against them. NULL means
   "the same as the bike", which is what the original itself does
   while crashLatch is clear - it copies the bike placement into
   riderPosition every frame and only stops when the rider is thrown.
   So nothing looks different today and the branch exists for when
   the tumble does.

   THE RANGE WAS VERIFIED AT THE INSTRUCTION LEVEL, because it
   disagrees with the skeleton and the code has to win:

       00461631   CMP ECX,0x3    JL  -> the bike placement
       0046163a   CMP ECX,0xe    JG  -> the bike placement

   The parent array in skl-rot.txt 2 puts the rider at members 5..16,
   so 3..14 both takes two bike parts and leaves the RIGHT ARM behind
   on the bike. Those limb labels are [inferred]; this branch is
   read. An arm that stays with the bars while the body goes is a
   plausible crash - but that is not why the range is written down,
   it is written down because 0x00461631 says so.

   WHAT IS STILL MISSING is the tumble itself: matRiderRoot's
   rotation while crashLatch is set, built from g_cosTable lookups at
   a rate proportional to the rider's slide speed (racers.txt
   9.35.5), and the vertical offset the crash branch adds to
   riderPosition. Both are drawing, not simulation - no state depends
   on them - so the split can sit here correct and inert until
   somebody wants the animation. [open]

2.4.  Two members are inverted that should not be - CLOSED

   Not a divergence. Both special cases already agree, by two
   different routes, and the port needs no change.

   MEMBER 0 IS IDENTITY, so inverting it is a no-op. Read out of
   the running port mid-race, s_race_bikeSkl.mat[0] deviates from
   the identity by 1.19e-12 - the root member carries no rotation
   and skl_load has already re-centred the skeleton on its origin.
   Whatever matrix the original hands loop index 0, composing an
   identity inverse in front of it changes nothing.

   MEMBER 3 IS STORED PRE-INVERTED, so the bake's inverse cancels
   and the arm matrix arrives intact. mat[3] read:

       1.0000000   0.0000000   0.0000000
       0.0000000   0.9907460   0.1357294
       0.0000000  -0.1357294   0.9907460

   a pure X rotation of 7.804 degrees with no translation - which
   is inverse(A) for the swingarm A that skl_swingarm builds. The
   original reaches the same place by skipping the inverse and
   passing A directly; skl.c reaches it by storing inverse(A) and
   letting the draw invert it back. Already verified end to end:
   A applied to record[16].pos gives the captured draw position.

   WHAT WOULD BREAK THE FIRST EQUIVALENCE is an animation frame
   that rotates joint 0. The original ignores mat[0] whatever it
   holds; the port only ignores it while it is identity. No
   shipped animation does, but that is the thing to check if a
   future one appears to tilt the whole bike.

2.5.  unitsPerMetre is calibrated by eye - CLOSED

   It is 128, and the original states it outright. FUN_0046b120 at
   0x0046bc8a advances a racer by

       pos += moveDir * speed * dtMs * 1.28

   where 1.28 is the DOUBLE at 0x004d1010. dtMs is the 0.1-scaled
   millisecond unit, 3.3 at 33 ms, so a second of travel is
   speed * 1000 * 0.1 * 1.28 = speed * 128 world units.

   IT WAS ALREADY IN THE PORT, one file over. racer.c has carried
   it as AI_STEP_SCALE, with the address, since racer_ai_integrate
   was transcribed. race.c had an independent guess for the same
   quantity and neither knew about the other - which is the case
   for keeping a constant in one place with its address, not two.

   THE TRAVEL CROSS-CHECK WAS DISCARDED. Two runs were attempted and
   neither is usable: the first had the rails off and left the
   circuit entirely, falling 1053 units through terrain the track
   does not cover; the second had them on and spent its window
   bouncing off them. Their ratios, 0.987 and 0.910, are recorded
   here only so nobody re-derives support from them - they measure
   the collision response, not the scale.

   A VALID VERSION IS EASY AND WORTH DOING: with the rails off there
   are about SIX SECONDS of straight before the terrain changes,
   which is ample for an acceleration run. Sample the position and
   speed each frame inside that window rather than differencing two
   endpoints across a corner.

   The reading at 0x004d1010 does not depend on any of that. It is
   static evidence - the constant the original's own integrator
   multiplies by - and it stands on its own.

   THE CAVEAT IS CLOSED. 0x004d1010 has only AI and network
   references, so for a while 128 was verified for those two and
   inferred for the human. The human's own integrator has now been
   read - ModelInstance_Integrate at 0x0048dac7 - and it states
   the same value as a SEPARATE constant, the double 128.0 at
   0x004d1c30:

       displacement = moveDir * speed * 128.0 * dt-factors

   The AI writes the scale as 1.28 against a 0.1-scaled
   millisecond; the player's writes 128.0 directly. Same number,
   two spellings, three integrators. Nothing is inferred any more.

   TWO TRAPS THIS MEASUREMENT HIT, both worth knowing:

   o 0x004d1010 IS A DOUBLE. Read as a float it is 89128.96.

   o A ONE-FRAME send_input ADVANCES TWO. The reply says ran=2.
     Read the counter at 0x004e981c either side and use the
     observed count; frame-loop.txt 1.4 records the same trap
     costing several rounds of measurement before.

2.6.  The bike is on a rail - CLOSED

   THE TITLE WAS MISLEADING FROM THE START and cost two sessions.
   "Steering never becomes a heading" described a mechanism that
   was already present: race_tick_physics has always advanced
   s_race_yawWorld through racer_steer_torque, which is a faithful
   transcription of ApplySteering's tail. The bars turned the
   heading; they always did.

   WHAT WAS ACTUALLY WRONG was the position. The bike's location
   came from a segment index and a lateral offset - lane
   coordinates - so however the heading moved, the bike could only
   ever slide along the racing line and could not leave the road
   surface at all.

   FIXED. race_tick_physics now integrates the position along
   r->moveDir and asks the track afterwards which segment that
   landed in, which is the original's arrangement:
   ModelInstance_Integrate moves, UpdateGroundContact reacts.
   race_fall_and_land adds the vertical half through
   racer_gravity_step at 2.5 g, with the original's 32-unit
   airborne threshold.

   THE LESSON IS THE NAMING. An item named for a suspected cause
   rather than an observed symptom sends the next reader after the
   wrong thing. "The bike is on a rail" is what could be seen; the
   steering was a guess about why, and it was wrong.

2.7.  The automatic upshift is ours

   Class ours.  src/race.c.

   The original drives its two shift requesters from input bits
   0x10 and 0x20 in maybe_Racer_UpdateAirborne - manual shift
   buttons - and nothing read so far decides an AUTOMATIC upshift.
   Ours asks for one when the engine would over-rev and for a
   downshift when it would bog. That is what the ratios imply, not
   what any function says.

   The gearbox mode at racer+0x88 reads 1, the assisted gearbox, so
   the requester exists somewhere. Find it.

2.8.  The crash fall side is a stand-in

   Class ours.  src/racer.c.

   The original takes bit 0 of something and falls left; ours picks
   a side by a different rule. Getting it wrong only picks the
   wrong side of a crash, which is why it has survived - but it is
   still a guess in place of a read.

2.9.  Leaving a crash zeroes the whole velocity

   Class gap.  src/racer.c.

   The original calls SetLongSpeed(instance, 0.0), which zeroes
   only the FORWARD component, so a bike sliding sideways when the
   crash ends keeps that drift. Ours zeroes speed outright because
   MR_Drivetrain carries no velDir.

   The fix is a struct change, not a formula: give the drivetrain
   the direction vector the original keeps.

2.10.  The side camera is ours

   Class ours.  src/race.h, race_camera_toggle_side.

   Retail's view key swaps high and low from behind; ours swings 90
   degrees to the bike's left. It exists because the fork and the
   wheels cannot be seen from a chase cam, which is a real need.

   DECIDE, do not drift: either it becomes a named debug override
   that is off by default and does not touch the retail view key,
   or it goes. Right now it is neither.

2.11.  The rider pose under lean

   Class gap.  src/racer.c, racer_build_pose.  OBSERVED, not yet
   diagnosed.

   With the bike's own lean now correct, the rider on top of it is
   the obvious remaining difference from retail. The machinery is
   present - racer_build_pose stacks the stance, pitch, lean,
   stunt and crash passes that maybe_Racer_UpdatePose stacks, and
   each weight carries an ORIGIN tag - so this is not a missing
   subsystem. It is a discrepancy inside one that is already
   transcribed, and finding it means comparing per-part angles
   against a capture rather than reasoning about the weights.

   THREE CANDIDATES, in the order worth checking:

   o THE STUNT PASS IS NOT SEQUENCED. racer_pose_stunt_step walks
     the run but the pass does not advance through it the way the
     original does. Already marked [open] in racer.c.

   o THE LOCAL AND REMOTE LEAN PATHS DISAGREE BY DESIGN, and which
     one a racer takes is decided by isLocal. The local path
     derives the slide mix from LEAN and the remote path from
     SPEED, so picking the wrong one is not a small error - it

2.12.  The wall clip is not called

   On a rail the bike could not leave the road, so guard rails

       trk_clip_move_against_walls   the two planes, the reflect
       racer_wall_impact_cos         head-on-ness
       racer_wall_crash              the three conditions
       racer_wall_stop               speed to zero, slide to 0.8

   The original calls the clip from UpdateGroundContact BEFORE it
   samples the ground (racers.txt 9.29.7), so the order is: move,
   clip against the segment's two wall planes, then snap. Our
   race_fall_and_land does the move and the snap with nothing

   o Game_RandNext is one CALL and a RET - a thunk to the CRT rand.

   o The slide gate is racer+0x307d, the WHEELIE ANGLE, so the third
     crash condition reads as: wheelie into a rail above 20 km/h and
     you go down whatever the angle.

   o THE '3' KEY turns them off, matching the switch
     tools/mr_cheats.py pokes into MOTO.EXE so the two can be put in
     the same state for a comparison. Off at load.  It was a [DEBUG]
     nowalls ini setting and is now a key, because a comparison
     session switches it constantly and an ini switch costs a restart
     - which reverts the MOTO side too, that one being a code patch.

     IT HAS TO SWITCH BOTH WALL SITES.  The clip stops the bike
     CROSSING a rail; trk_contain_point clamps it back inside one it
     is already past, every frame.  Gating only the clip left the
     containment holding the bike on the wall and made the switch
     look broken - which is how it read after the containment was
     imported.

   ONE CHOICE MADE RATHER THAN READ. racer+0x2dbc selects between a
   flat one-in-four that ignores the geometry and the pair that reads
   it, and what writes that field is unidentified. We take the
   geometric pair, because its inputs - impact angle and speed - are
   ones we can supply honestly, and because a random crash on every
   wall touch would be indistinguishable from a bug while the rest of
   this path settles. Marked in the source. [open]

   MEASURED AGAINST MOTO.EXE, and the first wiring was wrong in two
   ways that only a capture could show. tools/mr_place.py writes the
   position and the orientation directly, so an impact can be set up
   at a chosen angle and repeated exactly; segment 3 of ile01 is a
   straight 2560 units wide, and placing the bike at one rail facing
   the other gives a square hit with 2480 units of run-up.

   o THE DAMPING RUNS BEFORE THE BRANCH, not after. 0x004692b3
     loads racer+0x124, multiplies by the double 0.75 at 0x004d0ff0
     and stores it back at 0x004692d3 - ahead of the branch at
     0x004692ef - so the scrape's 0.5 and 0.3 are taken from the
     DAMPED speed. Doing it afterwards leaves the speed right, since
     0.75 and 0.3 commute, and the slide a third too high. The
     ratio was exactly 0.7500 on all four captured hits.

   o THE BOUNCE/SCRAPE GATE IS THREE-DIMENSIONAL. The test at
     0x004692e4 dots the reflected unit direction (ESP+0x20)
     against the frame's original move (ESP+0x98) and compares
     against the float 0.0 at 0x004d0e54: negative scrapes,
     non-negative bounces. Our move had no y, because the fall was
     applied after the clip rather than before it:

       +------------+--------+--------+--------------+-----------+
       | impact     | dot 3D | dot XZ | MOTO chose   | we chose  |
       +============+========+========+==============+===========+
       | square on  |  -77.4 |  -93.2 | scrape       | scrape    |
       +------------+--------+--------+--------------+-----------+
       | 50 degrees |  +30.1 |   -3.4 | bounce       | scrape    |
       +------------+--------+--------+--------------+-----------+

     The reflection only negates the component along the wall
     normal, so the fall's downward step survives it unchanged and
     contributes POSITIVELY to the dot on every hit. Dropping y
     therefore biases marginal angles into the scrape - and a
     scrape leaves the direction alone, which is precisely the
     reported symptom of being unable to steer off a rail.

     FIXED by splitting race_fall_and_land into race_fall_step and
     race_land and clipping between them, which is the original's
     own order: move in 3D, clip, then snap.

   AND IT UNBLOCKS 2.3. racer_wall_crash sets crashLatch, which is
   the only thing the per-part placement split was waiting on.

2.13.  The vertical step is 12x too small - CLOSED

   Class gap.  src/racer.c racer_integrate.  CLOSED by import 5.

   THE GROUNDED BRANCH DOES NOT INTEGRATE GRAVITY AT ALL. It adds
   a constant straight to the displacement:

       disp.y += dt * -1255.68          0x004d1c78

   and -1255.68 is -128 * 9.81 - gravity in the same 128 units per
   metre the displacement uses. With dt = frameDelta * 0.01 = 0.033
   that is -41.437 a frame, against the -41.4374 measured live off
   MOTO.EXE. It is applied when the caller forbids a lift or the
   step is already downward, so a grounded bike is pressed onto the
   road every frame and the ground snap takes it back.

   WHICH IS WHY IT WAS CONSTANT. The note below wondered whether a
   value that does not vary with speed or time could be an
   integrated fall. It is not: it is a fixed per-frame push, and
   the guess that it might be a fixed OFFSET was closer than the
   gravity reading.

   OURS USED THE AIRBORNE FORMULA ON THE GROUNDED PATH -
   racer_gravity_step, 2.5 g into the polar velocity - which gives
   about -3.4. That is the whole of the 12x.

   The velocity/moveDir merge below was a real bug and worth
   fixing, but it was not this one.

   The move handed to the wall gate carries a downward step, and
   2.12 showed that step decides which branch a marginal impact
   takes. Ours is the wrong size, so the branch selection is still
   wrong even though the gate is now three-dimensional.

   +---------------------------+--------------+-------------------+
   | per-frame vertical step   | MOTO.EXE     | ours              |
   +===========================+==============+===================+
   | grounded, any speed       |     -41.4374 |     about -3.4    |
   +---------------------------+--------------+-------------------+

   MOTO's is CONSTANT - identical to four decimals across six
   consecutive frames and across two impacts at different speeds,
   with prevPos.y exactly 0.0 every frame. A value that does not
   vary with speed or time is not obviously an integrated fall, so
   do not assume this is a gravity constant that needs scaling:
   it may be a fixed offset between the tracked point and the
   ground. Settle it by reading ModelInstance_Integrate rather than
   by fitting a multiplier - 41.4374 factors several plausible ways
   and picking one by arithmetic would be a guess wearing a
   derivation.

   MEASURED ON BOTH SIDES FROM ONE PLACEMENT. mr_place.py --to both
   puts the two games at the same spot facing the same way, so the
   run below is the same experiment twice: square-right, then 46
   frames of nothing but throttle, no steering at all.

   +--------------+---------------------------+------------------+
   | after 46     | MOTO.EXE                  | ours             |
   +==============+===========================+==================+
   | position     | (1174.3, 0, 2504.5)       | (1230.2, 0,      |
   |              |                           |  2058.0)         |
   +--------------+---------------------------+------------------+
   | heading      | +3.8 deg, along the track | -90 deg, at the  |
   |              |                           | wall             |
   +--------------+---------------------------+------------------+
   | travel       | -                         | +90 deg,         |
   |              |                           | reflected        |
   +--------------+---------------------------+------------------+

   MOTO crossed the track, met the rail and came away TURNED
   PARALLEL to it, advancing 446 units down the straight. Ours
   crossed, met the rail, and its z never moved at all: the heading
   stayed pinned at -90 into the wall while the travel direction
   flipped between +90 and -90 on each contact.

   AND MOTO DID NOT CRASH TO GET THERE. crashLatch was 0 at the end
   of the run, onGround 1, speed 13.16 m/s - so this is not a crash
   and respawn, it is ordinary riding. Something realigns the
   heading with the travel after an impact and our port has no
   equivalent: s_race_yawWorld only ever moves through
   racer_steer_torque, so with no steering input it cannot change.

   NOT THE ORIENTATION REBUILD. The ground probes at 0x00469506
   multiply axeDistance by racer+0xd4 and +0xd8 - the PREVIOUS
   forward row - so F = normalize(P1-P2) reproduces the heading it
   started from plus terrain pitch. That path preserves the
   heading; it cannot be what rotates it.

   TWO CAUSES, BOTH NOW LOCATED. See racers.txt 9.29.12.1 and
   9.29.12.2 for the evidence; the divergences are:

   o WE CLIP ONCE, THE ORIGINAL CLIPS TWICE - against the racer's
     segment and then against racer+0x31c1, the segment it is
     entering. Consecutive segments share wall planes on a
     straight, so one pass looks right everywhere except a bend.
     That is the curve half of the complaint.

   o APPLYSTEERING YAWS THE BIKE WITH THE BARS CENTRED. Captured
     from ComposeOrientation's return address with steer == 0.0
     at every call, swinging the heading back to the segment's own
     direction at a steady 4.4 degrees a frame and stopping there.
     s_race_yawWorld has no such term, so our bike keeps pointing
     into the rail forever. That is the sticking half.

   UpdateLean was the wrong tree and is now ruled out, as is the
   TickRemote path - both read flImpactBlend, neither steers.

   A LIKELY CAUSE, FOUND BY THE BYTE-EXACT PASS. velocity and
   moveDir were the same original field, +0x018c, held as two
   separate vectors in our struct. So racer_gravity_step tilted a
   private vertical vector that nothing else read, while the
   position advanced along a moveDir that stayed horizontal - and
   the vertical step became a small independent term instead of
   the y component of a full-magnitude velocity. Merged; the
   measurement needs redoing against the merged field before this
   is called closed.

   WHY IT SHOWS UP AS STICKING. Too small a y term shrinks the
   gate's positive contribution, so impacts that MOTO bounces we
   scrape - and a scrape leaves the travel direction untouched.
   The bike then keeps its heading into the rail and is clipped
   back to the hit point every frame, which reads as being glued
   to a wall that will not release.

   WHAT IS ALREADY CONFIRMED TO MATCH, so it is not worth
   re-checking:

   o The segment passed to the clip. UpdateGroundContact hands it
     racer+0x31bd, the segment of prevPos, NOT of the new position
     - verified with the two entry-plane distances either side of a
     boundary. race_clip_walls resolves the same segment from the
     same point.

   o The push-out. 0x004690ae is hit + normal * 32 on all three
     axes, taken from the hit point and not from the overshooting
     destination, so the along-wall travel really is discarded by
     the original too.

   o Trk_ClipMoveAgainstWalls does not write its pTo argument. The
     caller's push-out is the only thing that moves the racer.

2.17.  The rider tumble matrix - CLOSED

   Class gap.  src/racer.c, src/race.c.

   THE CRASH SIMULATION HAD BEEN RIGHT SINCE IMPORT 3 - the rider
   was launched, dragged, bounced off the ground and timed out
   correctly. What was missing was that NOBODY DREW HIM THERE.

   THREE PIECES, and the first is the one that mattered:

   o THE DRAW NEVER USED THE RIDER'S PLACEMENT. model.c has
     carried riderOrient/riderPos since the per-part split -
     parts 3..14 compose against them rather than the bike's,
     which is the whole point of that split - and race.c never
     set either. The rider stayed welded to the bike however far
     racer_update_crash threw him. Set now, while crashLatch is.

   o matRiderRoot WAS NEVER SEEDED. racer_begin_crash now copies
     matOrientation into it, which is what 0x00468006 does at the
     end of the original's root build. The yaw-cancelling multiply
     ahead of that is not transcribed and only matters when the
     matrix already holds something. [open]

   o THE TUMBLE ITSELF:

         rate = f_30e1 * (flRiderSpeed / flRiderSpeedRef) * dt
         matRiderRoot = Euler(rate) * matRiderRoot

     with f_30e1 = (0, fallSide, fallSide) from the crash entry -
     so he rolls and yaws about the side he went down and never
     pitches. The ratio IS the animation curve: 1.0 at the throw,
     0 when he stops sliding, so he spins fast and winds down
     without a separate timer.

   ONE DELIBERATE SHORTCUT. The original composes three angle-table
   lookups by hand; this uses mat34_from_euler, the same rotation
   by a shorter route. The table quantises to 0x4000 steps and
   this does not, so the two can drift by up to a fifth of a
   degree a frame - invisible on a tumbling body, and worth noting
   rather than chasing.

2.23.  The rider stood up through the whole fall - CLOSED

   Class gap.  src/racer.c, src/race.c.

   FOUR THINGS, and they compound - each one alone would have been
   visible, and together they made the fall unrecognisable.

   o THE RIDER ROOT WAS SEEDED UPRIGHT. racer_begin_crash copied
     matOrientation, so the rider was thrown standing. The

     THE -pi/2 IS NOT crashFallSide, and the two are easy to
     confuse because both are +-pi/2. It reaches the euler builder

     going down either way starts from the same attitude.

   o NOTHING MAINTAINED matRiderRoot WHILE HE WAS ABOARD. The
     original's DRAW writes the bike's matrix and position into
     matRiderRoot and riderPosition on every frame crashLatch is
     clear (0x00460db7, 0x00460e53). Those are not crash state -

     exactly (0,1,0) for ten consecutive grounded frames while
     row0 turned 0.046 -> 0.0 in the XZ plane - a pure yaw, which
     only the re-lay produces.

   o THE TUMBLE MULTIPLIED ON THE WRONG SIDE. The original
     multiplies each of matRiderRoot's four rows by the rotation,
     which is matRiderRoot * Euler(rate) - the rates are read in
     the rider's own frame. Ours had Euler(rate) * matRiderRoot,
     which turns the surviving yaw rate into a world-axis tilt.

   +----------------+---------------------------+---------------------------+

   | riderPos.y     | 0.00 after the first tick | 0.00 after the first tick |

2.23.  The wheelie is invisible - CLOSED

   Class gap.  src/race.c, src/racer.c.

   THE WHEELIE WAS WORKING PERFECTLY AND TELLING NOBODY. Measured
   mid-run with the button held: s_race_wheelie.angle 1.5708 -
   pi/2, the cap - timer 79.2, scale 2.0. A correct wheelie that

       pitch = sin(wheelieAngle) * -0.785398

   exactly as ours does, for a 45-degree nose-up.

   STILL OURS: the pitch is composed at draw time rather than
   folded into matBikeRoot where UpdateLean puts it, so anything
   reading matBikeRoot sees no wheelie. Retiring MR_Wheelie into
   racer+0x307d outright would close both. [open]

3.  Deliberate divergences that stay

   Recorded so they are not re-opened. Each is argued where it
   lives; this is an index, not a restatement.

   o transform.c - zero guards on the perspective divide, a bound
     check on the pool, and clearing the shade field. The original
     divides unconditionally and does not clear.

   o racer.c - soloMode (racer+0x4) is set to 1 on EVERY racer,
     where Racer_Create is passed 0 for every racer in a network
     game and maybe_Game_StartRacePending then sets 1 for the
     LOCAL SLOT ALONE at 0x00410f65. MR_Racer.txt 23.1 has the
     three-case table.

     THE REASON IS race_lap_visible, which gates the lap
     close-out on it: a remote racer with 0 never completes a lap,
     never reaches RACESTATE_FINISHED, and a net race therefore
     cannot end - race_net_check_over waits for a finisher that
     never arrives. Retail does not have the problem because its
     netplay is unfinished.

     THE NARROWER FIX WOULD BE 1 ON THE LOCAL SLOT AND 0
     ELSEWHERE, matching the binary, and then teaching
     race_lap_visible about remote racers instead. Worth doing if
     anything else turns out to read the field.

   o queue.c - the span buffer is off. The original enables it in
     Screen3D_Open. This is the only divergence in that file that
     changes what is drawn.

   o clip.c, sprite.c - guards against a zero divisor the original
     does not have.

   o prim.c - a 16-bit colour value kept in a wider field.

   o skl.h - the angle table is cleared before it is filled.

   o sprite.c - decoding happens at load, where the original defers
     it; and the 4:3 constants are computed where the original
     hardcodes 0x280.

   o d3d9rt.cpp - not a translation of Dsi_d3d.cpp at all. Execute
     buffers have no modern equivalent; what is reproduced is the
     semantics. See d3d-execute-buffers.txt.

   o The debug overrides: [DEBUG] physics=0 for the autoSpeed rail,
     autospeed, unitspermetre, startpage, startpaused, fastforward,
     autopilot, logfile, logpath.  All off or inert by default,
     all named in the ini.

     logfile IS THE ODD ONE OUT and is worth knowing about
     before the next time a log is wanted: the file half of mrlog
     is OFF unless the key says otherwise, so a build with no ini
     writes no file at all.  The console half is never gated.
     log.h has the reasoning; the short version is that the open
     truncates, so an unasked-for run would destroy the log from
     the asked-for one before it.

     The file lands BESIDE THE EXE, with [DEBUG] logpath as an
     override - so two installs on one machine keep separate
     logs instead of overwriting one.

   o autopilot.c/h - OURS ENTIRELY, no original.  A debug driver
     that synthesises the keyboard's button word so a race can be
     finished unattended, which is what makes the end-of-race
     sequence testable at all.  It feeds the real input path, so
     nothing downstream is bypassed.  It is NOT a sixth gameplay
     file under rule 13: it simulates nothing and its output is an
     INPUT, which puts it with timing.c.  See
     subsystems/autopilot.txt.

4.  Closed

   o The rear wheel sank into the road. race.c fed the DRAW the pair
     from racer_wheel_contacts, which carries an up term of
     96 * (hauteurCadre - 2 * rayon). That is 0 for the front wheel
     of every shipped bike - hauteurCadre is exactly twice rayonAv -
     and -5.76 for the rear of the street bike, so only the rear
     showed it, as a tyre sunk to the rim. The pair the racer
     actually stores at racer+0x1B4 and racer+0x338 has no up term:
     measured live, rear - pos = (+83.9185, 0.0000, -3.6997) and
     front - pos its exact negation, both 84.0000 long and both at
     the racer's own height. racer_wheel_ground is that pair.

     THE SAME ERROR WAS LEANING THE RIDER. racer_build_pose takes
     (front.y - rear.y) over the wheelbase as a terrain tilt, and the
     chassis pair differs in y by a constant 5.76 on flat ground, so
     the pose carried a permanent false tilt. Both call sites moved.

   o The chase camera. Three constants fitted to a single paused
     frame - 480 back, 256 up, 1975 ahead - replaced by
     Camera_UpdateChase 0x0046f840, reconstructed in race.c. The
     lens, 394, was never a fit: it is g_projScaleX read live.

   o The menu bike placement. Three formulas were fitted and
     refuted before bikes_display_lift was read off the two addends
     at 0x0045f828..0x0045ff62 - 96 * hauteurCadre - 48 * rayonAv,
     verified on all four bike sets to 2e-4.

   o The menu model offset. Was minus the model's own bounding-box
     centre, which pushed the bike 28.8 down and 9.6 back. The
     original applies nothing; the placement comes from the same
     matrix as everything else.

   o Baking the posed mesh. bike_draw now transforms each member's
     slice of the bind pool by its own matrix, which is what the
     original does. See racers.txt 9.64.

   o Two separate draw paths for the menu and the race. One
     bike_draw now, as the original has one maybe_Racer_Draw.
