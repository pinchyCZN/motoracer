MotoRacer PC Reverse Engineering                           Analysis Note


                       THE FRAME LOOP AND PACING


Abstract

   What the original does to pace itself, why the reconstruction does
   something different, and what that means for input and audio.

   The short version: THE ORIGINAL TARGETS 30 FPS AND ENFORCES IT BY
   BUSY-WAITING. It is single threaded, its only clock is timeGetTime,
   and it never raises the timer resolution. None of that survives into
   the port, and this note records the decisions so they are choices
   rather than drift.

1.  What the original does

1.1.  Frame_LimitAndMeasure

   0x00459fd0, renamed from FUN_00459fd0. It is the entire pacing
   mechanism, and it does two jobs [verified].

   THE LIMIT. It reads g_frameRateMax (0x004dc200), whose value in the
   image is 30, divides 1000 by it to get a millisecond budget, and
   then spins:

       if (g_frameRateMax != 0) {
           budget = 1000 / g_frameRateMax;
           do {
               now = timeGetTime();
               g_lastFrameTimeMs = now;
               if (now - prevFrameStart >= budget) break;
           } while (now - thisFrameStart < budget);
       }

   THERE IS NO Sleep ANYWHERE IN MOTO.EXE. A search of the import
   table and every reference finds timeGetTime with 75 references,
   SetThreadPriority with 2, and nothing else in this area - no Sleep,
   no QueryPerformanceCounter, no GetTickCount, no timeBeginPeriod, no
   timeSetEvent, no WaitForVerticalBlank. So the limiter holds a core
   at 100 per cent for whatever is left of every frame.

   The second condition is a runaway guard: it also gives up once a
   full budget has passed since the function was ENTERED, so a frame
   that already overran cannot spin for a second budget on top.

   THE METER. A 24-entry ring at g_frameDurationRing (0x005809f8) is
   shifted down one slot per frame with the newest duration written at
   [0], and the count at g_frameDurationCount saturates at 24. The
   function returns

       1000 * count / sum(durations)

   the average frame rate over the last 24 frames. Any single frame
   longer than 1000 ms truncates the history. The constant 1000 is
   built from three LEAs and a shift rather than an IMUL.

1.2.  The help text is wrong about the range

   The binary documents the flag itself at 0x004dc664:

       -FrameRateMax<XX>   : Limit frame rate of the game to XX frame/sec
                             (20 to 99, default=30, 0=no limit)

   The parser in Main_InitAndParseCommandLine disagrees [verified]:

       00414c6c  CMP  EAX,0x14
       00414c6f  JLE  skip
       00414c71  CMP  EAX,0x63
       00414c74  JGE  skip
       00414c76  MOV  [0x004dc200],EAX

   It stores only when 20 < n < 99, so THE ACCEPTED RANGE IS 21 TO 98.
   A literal -FrameRateMax20 or -FrameRateMax99 is silently ignored and
   the default stands. Zero is handled separately, further down, and
   does disable the limit.

   Small, but it is the kind of thing that turns into an afternoon if
   somebody tests the documented boundary and concludes the flag is
   broken.

1.3.  The clock is timeGetTime and only timeGetTime

   75 references, spread across the menus, the sprites, the racers and
   D3D_DrawScene. There is no second clock.

   AND THE GAME NEVER CALLS timeBeginPeriod. Without it, timeGetTime
   advances at the default system timer resolution - about 15.6 ms.
   So the original's own notion of time moves in 15.6 ms jumps, and at
   a 33 ms budget that is half a tick of quantisation per frame.

   At 30 fps that is survivable, and it is probably why 30 was chosen
   rather than 60: the clock is not good enough to pace a 16 ms frame.
   At 144 fps it would be absurd - the budget would be smaller than
   the clock's resolution.


1.3.9.  race_update SUBSTEPS, AND WITHOUT IT FAST-FORWARD TUNNELS

   The fixed-timestep pump in timing.h exists and the race never
   used it: race_update is called once per RENDERED frame and takes
   the whole frame delta as ONE physics step.

   FOR GEOMETRY, AT NORMAL SPEED, THAT IS HARMLESS.  A 16 ms step
   moves the bike 53 world units at 90 km/h, and every piece of
   geometry it tests against is far larger than that.

   BUT IT IS NOT HARMLESS FOR EVERYTHING, and this was found the
   expensive way.  The AI speed needle integrates as

        speedKmhNeedle += (int)(needleRate * dt * 0.01)

   with needleRate clamped to 40.  At the original's one 33 ms
   step that is int(1.32) = 1 and the needle climbs; split into
   two 16 ms steps it is int(0.66) = 0 TWICE, so the needle never
   moves at all.  HALVING THE STEP DOES NOT HALVE THE RESULT, IT
   ZEROES IT - the fraction is discarded every sub-step.

   The consequence reached the mixer: a stuck needle feeds
   Gearbox_AutoSelect a ground speed of 0, so every AI racer sat
   in gear 1 with tachoRpm on its 800 idle floor, and their
   engine notes droned at the bottom of the gear-1 band instead
   of revving.  Measured against MOTO at the same moment: its AI
   read needle 53, tachoRpm 5811, rpm 0.484; ours read needle 0,
   tachoRpm 800, rpm 0.0667.  [verified - live, both builds]

   READ THAT COMPARISON WITH 1.4's WARNING IN HAND: MOTO was
   harness-pinned to a 33 ms step while the port was taking two
   16 ms ones, so the two were never stepping the same amount.
   The ARITHMETIC above stands on its own and is the point -
   int(40 * 1.7 * 0.01) is 0 whatever the other side is doing -
   but the numbers beside it are not a like-for-like.

   o ANY INTEGRATOR THAT TRUNCATES TO AN INT IS SUSPECT HERE.
     dt-scaling is not enough on its own; a sub-stepped build
     needs the remainder carried, or the quantity computed once
     per FRAME like the camera and the HUD already are.

   AT 10x FAST-FORWARD THE STEP IS 166 ms AND THE BIKE TUNNELS.  It
   covers 533 units in one step - more than a whole 512-unit
   segment, and 1.4 times the road half width through ile01's
   bridge.  Wall clipping, ground contact and the segment walk are
   each handed a displacement larger than the geometry they test
   against, so it passes through walls a smaller step would have
   caught.  Fast-forwarded gameplay was not a valid measurement of
   anything that touches a wall.

   dt-SCALING IS NECESSARY AND NOT SUFFICIENT, which is the part
   that misleads: every routine scales by its delta correctly and it
   still breaks, because one large Euler step is not several small
   ones once collision is involved.

   So the block is cut into slices of at most RACE_MAX_SUBSTEP:

        1x  @60fps    16.7 ms ->  1 step   (bit-for-bit as before)
        1x  @30fps    33.3 ms ->  2 steps of 16.7 ms
       10x  @60fps   166.7 ms ->  9 steps of 18.5 ms
       stall clamp   250.0 ms -> 13 steps of 19.2 ms

   WHAT IS IN THE LOOP IS WHAT INTEGRATES OR TESTS GEOMETRY - the
   racer tick, progress, standings, the clock, the decor animation
   and the bike-to-bike push.  The particles, the camera, the HUD
   and the draw prep stay per-FRAME: the camera is a first-order
   filter, so running it N times would pull it in N times as fast.

   THE COUNT IS BOUNDED at RACE_MAX_SUBSTEPS for the reason
   FRAME_MAX_STEPS is - a slow machine must fall behind in real time
   rather than demanding more steps that take longer still.

1.4.  The frame delta, and a ceiling applied one line too late

   Game_UpdateFrameTimer @ 0x004120e0 derives every consumer of
   frame time from that one clock.  [verified]

   +----------------------+------------+----------------------------+
   | Global               | Address    | Meaning                    |
   +======================+============+============================+
   | g_clockMs            | 0x004e9cfc | Clock_Sample @ 0x00459fa0  |
   |                      |            | stores timeGetTime here;   |
   |                      |            | Clock_Now @ 0x00459fc0     |
   |                      |            | returns it. Both are two   |
   |                      |            | lines long.                |
   +----------------------+------------+----------------------------+
   | g_framePrevMs        | 0x0050bbc8 | the previous sample        |
   +----------------------+------------+----------------------------+
   | g_frameDeltaMs       | 0x004e980c | raw delta, integer ms      |
   +----------------------+------------+----------------------------+
   | g_frameDeltaSmoothed | 0x004dc150 | exponentially smoothed ms  |
   +----------------------+------------+----------------------------+

   The order of operations is the whole point:

       g_frameDeltaMs = Clock_Now() - g_framePrevMs;
       if (g_dwReplayMode != 1)
           g_frameDeltaSmoothed += (raw - g_frameDeltaSmoothed) * a;
       g_frameDeltaScaled = (float)g_frameDeltaMs;
       if (raw > ceiling) { g_frameDeltaMs = 200; scaled = 200.0; }
       else if (scaled < 1) { g_frameDeltaMs = 30; scaled = 30.0; }

   THE LONG-FRAME CEILING IS APPLIED AFTER THE SMOOTHING, so it
   protects g_frameDeltaMs and not g_frameDeltaSmoothed. A stall of
   any length is clamped to 200 ms for the physics and folded in at
   full value for everything reading the smoothed figure. Measured
   against a frozen game: four seconds took g_frameDeltaSmoothed
   from 35.159 to 542.293.  [verified]

   That is not academic. maybe_Racer_UpdateLean DIVIDES by it:

       0046ac1b  MOV  EAX,[g_frameDeltaSmoothed]
       0046ac24  MOV  ECX,[ESI + 0x2e29]     ; nonzero skips the clamp
       0046ac2e  MOV  EAX,0x41a00000         ; 20.0, a FLOOR
       0046ac3d  MOV  [ESP+0x10],EAX         ; ...and no ceiling
       0046ac45  FDIV [ESP+0x10]
       0046ac4f  FSTP [ESI + 0x2e1d]         ; lean

   so one long frame flattens the bike for exactly one frame. In
   normal play a stall that big does not happen and the smoothing
   hides the rest. Under a stepped harness it happens at every
   resume, which is what made the reconstruction look wrong when
   the harness was at fault - see racers-convergence.txt 9.61.5.

   THE FIX IS A VIRTUAL CLOCK, NOT A CORRECTION AT THE BOUNDARY.
   mrhook replaces Clock_Sample with

       g_clockMs += 33 for each frame the counter advances

   so game time is a function of the frame counter and nothing
   else. It is seeded at engagement so the clock does not jump.

   THE STEP FOLLOWS -FrameRateMax, as an integer 1000/n - the
   same arithmetic the game's own limiter spends, so 30 buys 33 ms
   and 60 buys 16.  It is read once at engagement from
   g_frameRateMax (0x004dc200); the rate is parsed at startup and
   nothing moves it afterwards.

   o IT WAS A HARDCODED 33 UNTIL THIS WAS FOUND, and at
     -FrameRateMax60 that ran the game at DOUBLE SPEED: the
     renderer ticked twice as often and the clock still advanced
     33 a frame.  It also pinned the physics at 33 ms where an
     unhooked 60 fps game steps 16, so every driven A/B at 60 was
     33 against the port's 17 and came back about 1.97x apart -
     which reads exactly like a port defect.  A long session went
     through the torque curve, the carac table and the gain block
     before the clock turned out to be the cause; Engine_TorqueAt
     hooked on both sides returned 7630.1411 against 7630.1415
     from identical inputs.  [verified - live, both builds]

   o BOTH SIDES NOW SPEND THE SAME INTEGER 1000/n.  The port
     rounds an explicit maxfps to that step rather than taking it
     as a rate, so 60 buys 16 ms on both and an A/B is exact at
     any setting.  Its DEFAULT always did this - kOriginalStepMs
     is a step, not a rate - which is why 30 fps matched all
     along and only an explicit maxfps drifted.

     UNTIL THAT LANDED THE PORT SPENT 1/60 = 16.67 ms, and
     race_update quantises dt to whole ms, so its step alternated
     16/17/17 against MOTO's flat 16.  Every drivetrain quantity
     came back at 0.965 of MOTO's - which is 16.0/16.67 exactly,
     and no part of it was physics.  [verified - live, both
     builds]

     THE ROUNDING IS COARSE AT HIGH RATES and that is the game's
     own arithmetic, not an approximation of it: 144 asks for
     6.94 ms, gets 6, and runs at 166.7.

   o THE PORT MUST ALSO TAKE ONE STEP PER FRAME for the
     comparison to hold - see 1.3.9.  At maxfps=30 with
     RACE_MAX_SUBSTEP at its shipped 0.02 the frame is cut into
     two 16 ms slices and the mismatch returns by another route.

   IT ACCUMULATES RATHER THAN MULTIPLYING, because THE FRAME
   COUNTER IS NOT MONOTONIC - a race restart puts it back to zero.
   Deriving the clock as base + frameCount * 33 therefore ran it
   backwards across a reset and handed the game a negative delta:
   g_frameDeltaSmoothed read -159.534 on the first frame after
   mr_reset and needed some fifty frames to climb back to 33, so a
   run started from a fresh reset began inside a corrupted window.
   A backward or absurd jump now counts as one frame.  [verified]
   The frame limiter is unaffected: it calls timeGetTime directly
   rather than through Clock_Sample (1.3), and only three sites
   call Clock_Sample at all.

   CORRECTING g_framePrevMs AT THE BOUNDARY IS NOT ENOUGH. That
   global has THREE writers - maybe_Game_ReadInput @ 0x00411821,
   Game_UpdateFrameTimer @ 0x00412125, and the tail of
   FUN_00410ee0, which sets it to now at the end of every solo
   frame. A correction applied at one instant is a race against
   the other two and the last writer before the resumed frame
   wins. Measured: the boundary fix took the lean lost on the
   first resumed frame from 63 per cent down to 0.3, and twelve
   stepped bursts still drifted 22 per cent from one continuous
   run.  [verified]

   HOLDING g_frameDeltaSmoothed FROM ANOTHER THREAD IS WORSE THAN
   USELESS. The game writes it at the top of the frame and reads
   it a few instructions later, so an external write only ever
   wins before the sample: the value reads as pinned to whoever
   polls it and the frame that used it is untouched. That is a
   measurement that lies, not a fix.

   With the virtual clock engaged the raw delta is 33 on every
   frame including resumed ones, g_frameDeltaSmoothed sits at
   exactly 33.000, and twelve frames produce the same lean whether
   taken as one burst, three or six - -0.82303, -0.82292 and
   -0.82293, agreeing to 0.01 per cent.  [verified]

   THE HARNESS CANNOT STEP A SINGLE FRAME. A resume costs two
   frames minimum - the game finishes the frame it was frozen in
   and starts the next before the pause flag lands. Requests of
   two or more are exact; a request of one advances two and says
   so in its reply.  [verified]

1.5.  What may advance on a paused frame

   A PAUSED FRAME STILL RENDERS. tm_now stops, so anything reading
   it takes a zero step - but the frame loop, the draw and the
   menu tick all still run. Anything that integrates state WITHOUT
   going through that zero step keeps moving while the game is
   frozen.

   THAT IS NOT ONLY A COSMETIC BUG. It silently biases every
   stepped measurement, because a scripted comparison pauses
   between bursts and the original is genuinely frozen across
   those gaps. The chase camera did exactly this and it cost a
   long hunt through constants that were never wrong - see
   camera.txt.

   SWEPT, and the state as of this pass:

   +---------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | Site                      | Verdict                            |
   +===========================+====================================+
   | race_camera(1)            | WAS BROKEN, now gated on dt > 0.   |
   |                           | A filter chasing a point fixed to  |
   |                           | the bike converges for free on a   |
   |                           | frame the bike did not move.       |
   +---------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | race_tick_physics,        | dt-scaled throughout; dt is 0 on a |
   | s_race_segPos,            | paused frame, so they no-op.       |
   | s_race_ticks              |                                    |
   +---------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | glass.c, menu page 14     | read tm_now deliberately, and say  |
   |                           | so. The original reads timeGetTime |
   |                           | for both; the divergence is that a |
   |                           | paused game is not a state it has. |
   +---------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | p10_pan_step              | Fixed per-frame increment with no  |
   |                           | dt - and FAITHFUL, the original    |
   |                           | accumulates the same way at        |
   |                           | 0x0044eeab. Input-driven on a menu |
   |                           | page, nothing measures it.         |
   +---------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | audio.c delayed cues      | FIXED - now tm_now. They used to   |
   |                           | schedule on tm_real_now and fire   |
   |                           | while the game was frozen, which   |
   |                           | announces something that has not   |
   |                           | happened yet.                      |
   +---------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | menu key repeat,          | Real time, and correctly so - both |
   | agent.c timeouts          | are I/O, not simulation.           |
   +---------------------------+------------------------------------+

   NOTHING IN THE FRAME LOOP INTEGRATES OUTSIDE THE GATE NOW,
   except p10_pan_step, which is faithful.

   THE RULE FOR NEW CODE: if it accumulates and it runs in the
   frame loop, it takes dt or it reads tm_now. Reading the wall
   clock is a deliberate choice that has to be argued in a comment,
   as glass.c does.


1.6.  A scripted run must STEP the clock, not free-run it

   MOTO's harness integrates a FIXED 33 ms per scripted frame; ours
   integrated REAL elapsed time, and that was the largest remaining
   error in every accumulating comparison the project has made.

   agent_run_start called tm_set_paused(0), which puts clock_advance
   on its unpaused branch - advance = realDt * speed.  So a scripted
   frame was worth however long that frame happened to cost, and no
   two runs of the same script integrated the same amount.

   MEASURED.  Sixteen frames of throttle from an identical placement,
   the same script twice:

       +---------+-----------+-----------+
       | run     | MOTO      | port      |
       +=========+===========+===========+
       | first   |   11.4806 |   11.4516 |
       | second  |   11.4806 |   11.4555 |
       +---------+-----------+-----------+

   MOTO to six figures both times; ours differing from itself by more
   than a third of its difference from MOTO.  That is the signature -
   a side that cannot reproduce ITSELF is not being compared, it is
   being sampled.

   THE VIRTUAL CLOCK WAS NOT AT FAULT AND WAS NOT CHANGED.  timing.c
   is untouched: clock_advance already had both branches and the
   fixed-step one was correct.  The defect was one line in agent.c -
   the scripted-run driver reached for tm_set_paused(0) rather than
   the step path the pause work had already built.  It survived
   because that work was exercised at the KEYBOARD, where the step
   key runs the fixed branch; SEND_INPUT was the one caller taking
   the other road.

   THE FIX IS tm_step_frames, NOT A NEW CLOCK.  The fixed-step branch
   of clock_advance already existed for the step key, and its step is
   1/targetFps, which defaults to 1/(1000/33) - the original's 33 ms
   exactly.  So a scripted run queues one step per frame and the two
   sides now integrate the same time as well as the same count.

   WHAT THIS INVALIDATES: any figure this project quoted from a
   multi-frame scripted run, in proportion to how many frames it
   accumulated over.  Single-frame deltas and per-frame hook traces
   are unaffected - they never integrated more than one step.

   IT DID NOT EXPLAIN THE ACCELERATION DIFFERENCE, only hide it.
   With the clock stepped the port reproduces itself exactly -
   11.4508 three runs running - and the gap to MOTO settles at a
   stable -0.260 per cent instead of wandering.  A real defect was
   underneath it (drag applied twice, racers-convergence.txt 9.18.22); the
   jitter had been large enough to argue about which.

   THE TRAILING TICK CHANGES MEANING.  agent_run_tick holds the keys
   for one tick after the count runs out, because under free-run that
   tick still integrated and releasing early ran a frame of centring
   backwards through the measurement (1.4).  With a stepped clock
   that trailing tick has no step left, so it integrates nothing and
   the keys it holds no longer matter.  N queued steps are now N
   integrating frames rather than N plus a fraction.

2.  Why our loop was choppy

   The reconstruction's loop already presented with
   D3DPRESENT_INTERVAL_ONE, so every frame was arriving on the vblank
   and the frame rate was correct. The motion still stuttered, and the
   reason was not the pacing at all:

       demo3d_draw((float)timeGetTime() * 0.001f);

   THE ANIMATION WAS DRIVEN BY A 15.6 MS CLOCK WHILE THE DISPLAY RAN
   AT 16.67 MS. The logo's angle advanced in 15.6 ms quanta, the
   screen refreshed every 16.67 ms, and the two beat against each
   other: most frames the angle moved one quantum, occasionally two or
   none. Every frame was on time and the motion was visibly uneven.

   o THAT IS WHY VSYNC ALONE DID NOT FIX IT, and it is worth
     remembering as a class of bug. Smooth presentation and smooth
     motion are different properties. A correct frame rate with a
     coarse animation clock looks exactly like dropped frames.

   The trailing Sleep(1) was a second, smaller problem - it makes the
   loop miss vblanks under load - but it was not the main one.


3.  Pacing on modern Windows

3.1.  The options, and why not each one

   +---------------------------+--------------------------------------+
   | Approach                  | Verdict                              |
   +===========================+======================================+
   | Sleep(1) in a loop        | Granularity is the system timer      |
   |                           | quantum, ~15.6 ms by default and     |
   |                           | ~1 ms at best. Cannot hit 16.67 ms   |
   |                           | reliably, let alone 6.9 ms.          |
   +---------------------------+--------------------------------------+
   | Busy-wait on QPC          | Exact, and what the original does    |
   |                           | with a coarser clock. Burns a whole  |
   |                           | core, heats the machine, drains a    |
   |                           | laptop. Correct result, unacceptable |
   |                           | cost.                                |
   +---------------------------+--------------------------------------+
   | A DirectX facility        | THERE ISN'T ONE. D3D9 has no wait    |
   |                           | primitive beyond Present blocking on |
   |                           | vsync. D3DKMTWaitForVerticalBlank    |
   |                           | exists but is a kernel-thunk API not |
   |                           | intended for applications.           |
   +---------------------------+--------------------------------------+
   | Vsync alone               | Genuinely good, and free - Present   |
   |                           | blocks in the driver, off-CPU. But   |
   |                           | it locks you to the refresh rate and |
   |                           | its divisors, so it cannot express   |
   |                           | "cap at 30 on a 144 Hz panel".       |
   +---------------------------+--------------------------------------+
   | High-resolution waitable  | THE ANSWER. Sub-millisecond, and a   |
   | timer                     | real sleep - the thread is off the   |
   |                           | CPU. Windows 10 1803 and later.      |
   +---------------------------+--------------------------------------+

   o SO THE "CLEANER WAY THAN SHORT SLEEPS" IS REAL AND IT IS NOT A
     DIRECTX THING. It is CreateWaitableTimerEx with
     CREATE_WAITABLE_TIMER_HIGH_RESOLUTION, which Windows 10 1803
     added for exactly this problem. Before it existed, burning cycles
     really was the only way to hit a sub-quantum deadline, which is
     why so much older game code does.

3.2.  What was chosen

   src/timing.c. A hybrid, because no OS wait is exact:

     1  QueryPerformanceCounter is the clock. Sub-microsecond, and it
        does not wrap in any plausible session.

     2  Wait on a high-resolution waitable timer until a small margin
        before the deadline - 1.2 ms with the good timer, 3 ms
        without. This is the part that is genuinely asleep.

     3  Spin out the remaining fraction with YieldProcessor (the PAUSE
        instruction), which hints the core that this is a spin-wait so
        a hyperthreaded sibling gets the pipeline and the power draw
        drops.

   THE SPIN IS BOUNDED BY THE MARGIN, not by the frame. That is the
   whole difference from the original: under a millisecond of burn per
   frame instead of the entire remainder of a 33 ms budget.

   timeBeginPeriod(1) is raised as well, and paired with timeEndPeriod
   at shutdown. It bounds the worst-case overshoot and it is what
   makes the no-high-resolution-timer fallback tolerable. Leaving it
   raised on exit is antisocial - it costs power on the whole machine -
   so the pairing is not optional.

   FALLBACK IS AUTOMATIC. If the high-resolution flag is rejected the
   code takes a plain waitable timer, and if that fails, Sleep. The
   startup line says which one it got.


4.  Fixed simulation, variable render

   The loop runs the simulation at a FIXED rate and the renderer as
   fast as the cap allows:

       dt = frame_begin(&pacer);
       while (frame_next_step(&pacer))
           game_tick(pacer.fixedStep);      // constant step
       render();                            // as often as we can
       frame_end(&pacer);

   WHY, RATHER THAN JUST RUNNING EVERYTHING FASTER. The original ties
   its physics step to its frame, so its step IS 33 ms. Raising that
   loop to 144 Hz would quietly change the simulation - a bike's
   handling would depend on the display rate, and every tuned constant
   in the physics would mean something different. That is the standard
   way a port of a game this age breaks, and it breaks subtly enough
   to survive testing.

   The step defaults to 30 Hz, the rate the original's code was
   written against. Nothing forces that once the physics is understood
   well enough to know it is stable at other rates.

   TWO GUARDS, both of which matter:

     o dt IS CLAMPED at 0.25 s. A frame stalled by a window drag or a
       blocking load would otherwise inject a huge step and teleport
       everything. Clamping makes the game run slow for one frame,
       which is the right failure.

     o THE STEP COUNT IS BOUNDED at 8 per frame, and the backlog is
       dropped rather than carried. Carrying it means the next frame
       needs the same overrun plus more, which is a spiral that ends
       at zero frames per second.

   frame_alpha() is provided for interpolating drawn positions between
   simulation states. Nothing uses it yet. It is there because a
   renderer faster than the simulation needs it to look smooth, and
   retrofitting it later means touching every drawable.


5.  Input does not need a thread

   THE ORIGINAL HAS NO CreateThread AT ALL [verified] - the whole game
   is one thread. Input is winmm joyGetPosEx for the joystick and
   GetKeyState for the keyboard, polled inline.

   Nothing about a modern input API changes that answer:

     o RAW INPUT arrives as window messages, which the loop already
       pumps. Polling GetRawInputBuffer once a frame is a memory read.

     o XINPUT is a user-mode call into a cached state block. Fast for
       a CONNECTED pad.

     o DIRECTINPUT GetDeviceState is a buffered read and is fine on
       the main thread. DirectInput is deprecated for gamepads anyway;
       it remains the only option for wheels and pedals with more axes
       than XInput exposes, which for a racing game is not a footnote.

   o ONE REAL CAUTION. XInputGetState against an EMPTY slot is slow -
     it goes looking for a device. Polling four slots every frame when
     none are plugged in costs milliseconds. The fix is to rescan
     disconnected slots on a timer, a few times a second, not to move
     input to a thread. A thread would hide the cost rather than
     remove it.

   A thread is only warranted for an input source that BLOCKS. None of
   the above does. If a wheel's force-feedback effects turn out to
   block on upload, that upload is what moves off the main thread -
   not the polling.


6.  Audio

   src/audio.h and src/audio.c, currently a silent stub with the full
   interface in place.

   WHAT THE ORIGINAL DOES [verified from the import table]: music is
   RED BOOK CD AUDIO through mciSendCommandA - tracks 2..n of the game
   disc, played by the drive itself, never decoded by the game. Sound
   effects are DirectSound, with the winmm mixer* API for volume.

   NEITHER SURVIVES. Nobody runs with the disc in the drive, and
   DirectSound on Windows 10 is emulated with no hardware mixing.

   o AND THIS IS A REAL LIMIT OF CLAUDE.md RULE 10, which deserves
     recording rather than discovering later. A stock install has NO
     MUSIC FILES, because the music was never installed - it was on
     the disc. A drop-in EXE can find every sound effect in the
     archives and cannot conjure the soundtrack out of a DATA folder.
     Music will need files the user supplies, most likely ripped from
     their own disc, in a directory we define.

   The interface therefore addresses music BY TRACK NUMBER, exactly as
   the original does. A back end resolves track 4 to whatever it finds;
   game code never learns the difference.

   THE LIKELY BACK END IS XAUDIO2 - it ships in Windows 10 as
   XAudio2_9.dll with no redistributable, which is the same constraint
   that chose D3D9. Decoding is the open part: Media Foundation covers
   MP3 and AAC with nothing extra, but not FLAC or Opus, which would
   need a small static library. That decision is deferred and does not
   leak past audio.h.

   audio_update() is called once per frame from the loop so that audio
   needs no thread in GAME code. XAudio2 runs its own mixing thread,
   and a streaming worker - so a disk hitch cannot stall a frame -
   belongs inside the back end, behind that call.


7.  Configuration

   [VIDEO] in mracer.ini, read at startup:

   +----------+---------+---------------------------------------------+
   | Key      | Default | Meaning                                     |
   +==========+=========+=============================================+
   | maxfps   | 60      | Render cap.  DEFAULTS TO 60 - see below.    |
   |          |         | -1 is the ORIGINAL'S 33.0 ms step, 30.303   |
   |          |         | fps.  0 = uncapped, let vsync pace it.  N   |
   |          |         | is taken literally, with no clamp.          |
   +----------+---------+---------------------------------------------+
   | simhz    | 30      | Fixed simulation rate.  Reaches the race    |
   |          |         | ONLY when maxfps is 0 - see below.          |
   +----------+---------+---------------------------------------------+

   THE DEFAULT IS 60, AND IT IS A DELIBERATE DIVERGENCE.  main.cpp
   initialises cfgMaxFps to 60, which the explicit arm turns into an
   integer 16 ms step - 62.5 fps - rather than the original's 33.0.
   A 30 fps default is a 1997 display assumption, and everything
   below is why it is not free: A/B RUNS AGAINST MOTO.EXE MUST SET
   maxfps=30 ON BOTH SIDES or a frame stops meaning the same thing.

   AND THE CAP IS LOAD-BEARING.  race_update takes its dt from the
   REAL frame time and is called once per RENDERED frame, so the
   scaled delta the physics reads is 3.3 only because a frame is held
   at 33.0 ms.  Raising maxfps is a change to the simulation, not to
   presentation.

   WHAT ACTUALLY BREAKS, in three kinds:

   +---------------------+------------------------------------------+
   | Kind                | Behaviour off 30 fps                     |
   +=====================+==========================================+
   | dt-SCALED           | approximately right - only the           |
   | most things         | integration error differs                |
   +---------------------+------------------------------------------+
   | dt-DIVIDED          | changes.  racer_lean_response is the     |
   | lean                | case, but BOTH sides floor the divisor   |
   |                     | at 20.0 ms when leanMode is 0, so above  |
   |                     | ~50 fps it stops getting worse and pins  |
   |                     | at 33/20 = 1.65x.  With leanMode set the |
   |                     | floor is skipped and it scales all the   |
   |                     | way - that is the 2x case.               |
   +---------------------+------------------------------------------+
   | NO dt at all        | runs literally N times faster.  The air  |
   | frame-COUNT         | timer is the known one - racers.txt 7547 |
   +---------------------+------------------------------------------+

   THE FLOOR IS THE ORIGINAL'S, at 0x0046ac2e: MOV EAX,0x41a00000 is
   20.0f, reached only when the racer's leanMode field is 0.  The
   port carries it as LEAN_DT_FLOOR.

   o 33.0 IS THE ORIGINAL'S INTEGER 33, NOT 1000/30.  Its own limiter
     spends an integer 1000/n ms, so 30 buys 33 ms and 30.303 fps.  At
     1000/30 the port ran every dt-driven quantity 1.01x hot.

   o RETAIL HAS THE SAME COUPLING.  Frame_LimitAndMeasure caps a loop
     that also drives the physics, and its tick is variable-timestep
     too - so 30 fps is load-bearing there as well, and this is not a
     defect the port introduced.  It is one the port has not yet
     fixed.

   maxfps 0 with vsync on would be the sane default IF the simulation
   were decoupled: Present blocks in the driver, off-CPU, at exactly
   the refresh rate.  The cap is also for when vsync is off, or to
   hold something BELOW the refresh rate - capping at 30 on a 144 Hz
   panel is the case vsync cannot express.

   HAVING BOTH ON IS SAFE. Whichever is tighter wins, and frame_end
   becomes a no-op when Present has already blocked past the deadline.


8.  The virtual clock: pause and frame-step

   A debug facility, not game behaviour. None of it exists in the
   original and none of it changes what the game does when it is not
   being driven by hand.

8.1.  Why a pause flag does not work

   The obvious implementation - a g_paused flag, and skip the update
   when it is set - is wrong here, and it is worth writing down why
   because it fails silently rather than obviously.

   GAME CODE READS THE WALL CLOCK ITSELF. MenuPage14_Title_Tick calls
   timeGetTime and diffs it against a stamp its own enter handler
   took; the reconstruction does the same through tm_now. So not
   calling the tick for two seconds does not pause anything. The
   moment it is called again it observes that two seconds passed and
   advances the animation by two seconds in one frame. Skipping the
   update is not pausing, it is DEFERRING - and the deferred time is
   all paid back in a single step.

   A flag would therefore have to be checked by every reader of the
   clock, forever, and the one reader that forgot would resume with a
   jump. That is a rule nobody can keep.

8.2.  Two clocks

   So the clock pauses rather than the caller.

   +---------------+------------------------------------------------+
   | tm_real_now() | REAL. QueryPerformanceCounter since tm_init.   |
   |               | Never pauses. For the pacer's deadlines, the   |
   |               | fps counter, and measuring the machine.        |
   +---------------+------------------------------------------------+
   | tm_now()      | VIRTUAL. What the game reads. Advances only    |
   |               | when frame_begin advances it, and not at all   |
   |               | while paused.                                  |
   +---------------+------------------------------------------------+

   frame_begin is the only place the virtual clock moves. It takes the
   real elapsed time, decides what the frame is entitled to, adds that
   to the virtual clock and returns it. Every subsystem reading tm_now
   then freezes for free, and none of them needs to know a pause
   exists.

   WHAT KEEPS WORKING, and this is the part that makes the whole
   approach cheap: the message pump, the game draw, and the present
   all run every frame exactly as before. So the window keeps
   repainting, input keeps being delivered, and the last frame stays
   on screen - not because it is being preserved, but because the game
   keeps redrawing it identically from a clock that is not moving.
   There is no "hold the last framebuffer" path to get wrong.

   frame_end still waits against a REAL deadline, so a paused game
   paces at the cap instead of spinning as fast as it can redraw.

   A USEFUL SIDE EFFECT: anything that keeps animating while paused is
   reading the wrong clock. That is a bug in the caller, and this is a
   cheap way to find it.

8.3.  Stepping

   tm_step_frames(n) queues n frames and pauses if not already paused,
   so one key does both jobs and the caller never has to know which
   state it was in. Requests accumulate and are consumed ONE PER
   FRAME, which is what makes holding the key down produce slow motion
   at the keyboard repeat rate rather than one large jump.

   A step is a FIXED amount - one period of the frame cap, or one
   simulation step when uncapped. Not the real elapsed time, which
   would make every step a different size; a single-step that is not
   reproducible is not much of a debugging tool.

8.4.  The primitive limit

   g_queuePrimLimit in queue.h, negative for none, which is the
   default.

   The queue is the only point in the pipeline where every primitive
   of a frame exists at once, in draw order, before any of them has
   been rasterized. Capping the dispatch there and walking the cap
   upward draws the frame one primitive at a time, which turns "there
   is a stray line on the screen" into "primitive 47 is the stray
   line" - and 47 can then be traced back to its object and its face.

   Two details that are not optional:

   o IT LIMITS DISPATCH, NOT THE WALK. queue_walk clears bucket heads
     as it passes them, and that IS how the queue is emptied for the
     next frame. Breaking out early would leave nodes linked and the
     next frame would draw both frames. This is the same reason the
     original keeps walking past its own 2000-primitive limit instead
     of returning.

   o IT ALSO LIMITS BUCKET 7999, which the game's own limit exempts.
     The exemption is right for an always-on-top HUD and wrong for a
     bisection tool, since a limit that cannot reach some primitives
     cannot isolate them. Limit 0 draws nothing, which is the contract
     worth having. The dispatched STATISTIC is still taken before the
     top bucket, so it keeps meaning what it meant.

8.5.  Keys

   Handled in WndProc ahead of menu_on_key, so a page cannot swallow
   them.

   +-------+----------------------------------------------------------+
   | SPACE | Pause / resume. Auto-repeat ignored - bit 30 of lparam - |
   |       | because holding a toggle down would flap it.             |
   +-------+----------------------------------------------------------+
   | A     | Step one frame, pausing first if running. Auto-repeat    |
   |       | DELIBERATELY not ignored: each repeat queues another     |
   |       | frame, so holding A is slow motion.                      |
   +-------+----------------------------------------------------------+
   | [ ]   | Lower / raise the primitive limit. The first '[' with no |
   |       | limit set starts from what was dispatched last frame, so |
   |       | it removes the last primitive rather than blanking the   |
   |       | screen.                                                  |
   +-------+----------------------------------------------------------+
   | \     | Clear the primitive limit.                               |
   +-------+----------------------------------------------------------+

   The overlay shows PAUSED and PRIM n/total when either is active.
   The fps stays real while dt goes to zero, and the two disagreeing
   is exactly the signal that the clock is being driven by hand.


9.  Open questions

   o THE FIXED-STEP LOOP IS A STUB, AND THIS IS THE BIG ONE.  main.cpp
     runs frame_begin / frame_next_step / frame_end, but its update
     step only advances s_simTime - game_tick is commented out and was
     never wired.  The race ticks from page01_tick instead, once per
     RENDERED frame, taking its dt from the real frame time.

     SO THE DECOUPLING timing.h DESCRIBES IS SCAFFOLDING.  simhz moves
     only the debug single-step amount; the render cap is what
     actually holds the physics step steady.  frame_alpha has no
     callers either, so even a correct fixed-step sim would judder at
     a high refresh until drawables interpolate.

     Moving race_update into that loop is the fix and it is not small:
     it would put every physics number measured against MOTO.EXE at
     risk, and it would not help the frame-COUNT dependencies below.

   o SOME CODE IS FRAME-COUNT DEPENDENT, not delta dependent, so no
     amount of fixing the timestep would make it rate-independent -
     the air timer (racers.txt 7547, 3414, 7251) and the page-10 bike
     pan (menu-pages.txt 3285).  These have to be found and converted
     one at a time.

   o WHETHER THE GAME LOGIC IS RATE-INDEPENDENT. The pacer runs the
     simulation at 30 Hz because that is what the original ran at.
     Whether the reconstructed gameplay would be stable at 60 or 120
     is untested; the two entries above are the reasons to assume not.
     Do not raise simhz on the assumption that it is.

   o WHAT SetThreadPriority IS FOR. Two references, both in
     Main_InitAndParseCommandLine, matching -ThreadHighest and
     -ThreadTimeCritical in the help text. Raising priority is how a
     busy-wait limiter stops being descheduled mid-spin; with a real
     sleep it should be unnecessary. Not reproduced, and not obviously
     needed.

   o WHETHER ANY GAME CODE DEPENDS ON THE 15.6 MS QUANTISATION. A
     timer compared for equality rather than for having elapsed would
     behave differently on a fine clock. Nothing seen so far does, but
     75 timeGetTime sites have not all been read.

   o VSYNC IS NOT YET RUNTIME-CONFIGURABLE. d3d9rt.cpp hardcodes
     D3DPRESENT_INTERVAL_ONE. Changing it needs a device reset, which
     is a bigger change than an ini read, so it was left alone.
