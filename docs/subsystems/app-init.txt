MotoRacer PC Reverse Engineering                           Analysis Note


                             GAME BRING-UP


Abstract

   App_Init at 0x00472c40 is everything between the command line being
   parsed and the Delphine logo appearing. At 1408 bytes it was the
   largest unnamed function in screen3d\dd.cpp, and naming it completes
   that translation unit.

   This note also records maybe_Hud_BindCornerSigns, the other function
   finished in the same pass, which is a HUD routine that happens to
   live in dd.cpp.

1.  The entry wrapper

   Main_InitAndParseCommandLine calls App_InitEntry once, at 0x00415408.
   It is three statements [verified]:

       maybe_g_appInitStarted = 1;
       return App_Init() != 0;

   The flag is also written by FUN_00427e80 and read by FUN_00461f70 and
   FUN_00461aa0, so it is shared state rather than a private startup
   marker. Not identified.

2.  The bring-up sequence

   In order, with the parts this project has already documented cross-
   referenced [verified]:

   +------+------------------------------------+--------------------+
   | Step | What                               | See                |
   +======+====================================+====================+
   | 1    | Input_SetDefaultBindings           | input-bindings.txt |
   +------+------------------------------------+--------------------+
   | 2    | Trig_BuildCosTable, g_cosTable     | trig-table.txt     |
   +------+------------------------------------+--------------------+
   | 3    | seed 25 racer slots, stride 0x3209 | racers.txt         |
   +------+------------------------------------+--------------------+
   | 4    | Data/Design/Texts/LowRes.RAW       | -                  |
   +------+------------------------------------+--------------------+
   | 5    | Data/Config.spt, Settings_LoadAll  | settings.txt       |
   +------+------------------------------------+--------------------+
   | 6    | detail level forced to 4 if HW 3D  | renderer-mode.txt  |
   +------+------------------------------------+--------------------+
   | 7    | Data/list.spt                      | -                  |
   +------+------------------------------------+--------------------+
   | 8    | Data/champ_<c>.spt by difficulty   | -                  |
   +------+------------------------------------+--------------------+
   | 9    | the seven texture banks            | dd-statics.txt     |
   +------+------------------------------------+--------------------+
   | 10   | fonts, then a font rebuild pass    | -                  |
   +------+------------------------------------+--------------------+
   | 11   | construct g_menu (0xd4c bytes)     | MR_Menu.txt        |
   +------+------------------------------------+--------------------+
   | 12   | Data/Demo/{EA,BMG}.dem by branding | -                  |
   +------+------------------------------------+--------------------+
   | 13   | Menu_GotoPage(25) - Delphine logo  | menu-pages.txt     |
   +------+------------------------------------+--------------------+

   STEP 2 IS THE SECOND INSTRUCTION-LEVEL CALL IN THE FUNCTION, at
   0x00472c66, right after the input bindings and before anything reads
   a file [verified]. It fills g_cosTable, the 0x4000-entry cosine
   table every rotation in the game indexes, and it is the table's only
   writer. Nothing checks whether it has run - an unbuilt table is all
   zeros and every rotation collapses. See trig-table.txt.

   Step 3 is a useful independent confirmation of the racer array. The
   loop writes an incrementing index at each slot base and advances by
   0x3209 until it passes 0x005d22c9, which is exactly 25 slots from
   0x00583fe8 - the same geometry racers.txt derived from the other end.

   Step 6 is the one gameplay-visible decision made here: when the
   hardware renderer is up, in mode 0, and the selected device reports
   texture support, the detail level is forced to 4 regardless of what
   the settings file said.

   Step 9 walks the bank array at 0x005d3a20 with a 0x149 stride,
   calling maybe_Cdd_LoadRawToSurface then maybe_DdCaps_FreeTexture for
   each, with maybe_g_deferBlits raised across the whole loop. So the
   banks are loaded and immediately released - a preload and measure
   pass rather than a load that sticks.

   Step 12 chooses between Data/Demo/EA.dem and Data/Demo/BMG.dem on
   g_useBmgBranding. Electronic Arts and BMG Interactive were the two
   publishers of the original release [inferred].

   At the end it chmods three files to 0x180 - Data/bestscor.dat,
   Data/Config.bpt and Data/list.bpt - making them writable. That is how
   the game copes with files marked read-only by having been installed
   from a CD [inferred].

2.1.  The whole boot chain, for a port skeleton

   Section 2 covers what App_Init does. What a new project needs is what
   surrounds it - where the display comes up, and how the frame loop is
   entered. The chain is short [verified]:

       MFC WinMain
        +- CWinApp::InitInstance      0x00414080
        |    +- parse the command line
        |    +- FUN_004995a0 -> maybe_Cdd_SetVideoMode -> Cdd_Init
        |    +- App_InitEntry -> App_Init      (the 12 steps above)
        +- MFC message pump
             +- the game vtable at 0x004d04c8
                  +- maybe_Game_Tick         0x0047d660
                  +- maybe_Game_RenderFrame  0x0047ff90

2.1.1.  The entry is an MFC override

   Main_InitAndParseCommandLine at 0x00414080 is __thiscall and has
   exactly one reference in the image - a vtable slot at 0x004d0478, no
   direct call anywhere [verified]. It is CWinApp::InitInstance, which
   is why the database showed it no callers, and it matches the late-
   boundary repair recorded in CLAUDE.md that moved this function's
   entry back 0x22 bytes.

   So the program is an MFC application and the entry point a port has
   to supply is not WinMain but an InitInstance override. The MFC
   surface at this level is small - InitInstance and the message pump -
   so replacing it with a plain WinMain and a PeekMessage loop is
   straightforward, and avoids linking MFC for a first build.

   o Worth deciding early, because it determines whether the MSVC
     project is an MFC project or a plain Win32 one.

2.1.1.1.  DECIDED: plain Win32, not MFC

   The reconstruction targets plain Win32 plus Direct3D 9. No MFC.
   Project decision, 2026-08-31.

   The evidence supports it more strongly than the paragraph above
   suggested. MFC is not doing the windowing at all [verified]:

   +-------------------------+----------------------------------------+
   | Job                     | Who actually does it                   |
   +=========================+========================================+
   | register the class      | RegisterClassA, called directly by     |
   |                         | FUN_004157d0                           |
   +-------------------------+----------------------------------------+
   | create the window       | CreateWindowExA, same function         |
   +-------------------------+----------------------------------------+
   | the window procedure    | maybe_Event_WndProc 0x00432980, a      |
   |                         | plain switch falling through to        |
   |                         | DefWindowProcA - not a message map     |
   +-------------------------+----------------------------------------+
   | keyboard                | WM_KEYDOWN / WM_KEYUP / WM_CHAR plus   |
   |                         | GetKeyState                            |
   +-------------------------+----------------------------------------+
   | mouse                   | WM_MOUSEMOVE, WM_?BUTTONDOWN/UP,       |
   |                         | SetCapture / ReleaseCapture            |
   +-------------------------+----------------------------------------+
   | joystick                | winmm joyGetPosEx / joyGetDevCapsA     |
   +-------------------------+----------------------------------------+
   | drawing                 | DirectDraw; there is no WM_PAINT and   |
   |                         | no WM_SIZE handler at all              |
   +-------------------------+----------------------------------------+

   So the window is already a hand-rolled Win32 window with a hand-
   rolled procedure, and input is already raw messages - there is no
   DirectInput either. See window-proc.txt for the full message map.

   That leaves five places MFC is genuinely reached, and each has an
   obvious replacement [verified]:

   +----------------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | MFC use              | Replacement                               |
   +======================+===========================================+
   | CWinApp::InitInstance| WinMain                                   |
   +----------------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | the MFC message pump | GetMessage / TranslateMessage /           |
   |                      | DispatchMessage, or PeekMessage for a     |
   |                      | render loop                               |
   +----------------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | CString              | std::string, or a small owned type        |
   +----------------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | AfxMessageBox        | MessageBoxA - which the binary already    |
   |                      | calls directly elsewhere                  |
   +----------------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | one modal CDialog    | WM_COMMAND id 0x9c43 only; a debug or     |
   |                      | about box, droppable outright             |
   +----------------------+-------------------------------------------+

   o One caveat, and it is the only one. CString is not confined to
     error paths: MR_DisplayMode's field at +0x00 is very likely a
     CString, and dd-statics.txt records CString::Format building mode
     description strings. So replacing it touches a reconstructed
     struct layout, not just message text. Decide the replacement
     string type before transcribing MR_DisplayMode, or the field
     width changes underneath the struct. See MR_DisplayMode.txt.

   o The message pump is where the frame loop lives, so a plain
     PeekMessage loop calling Game_Tick and Game_RenderFrame directly
     also lets a port skip the nine-entry game vtable at 0x004d04c8
     described below. Two pieces of MFC-shaped indirection removed by
     one decision.

   o Nothing in the renderer depends on this either way. Screen3D_Open
     takes an HWND and never asks where it came from.

2.1.2.  The display comes up from InitInstance, not App_Init

   FUN_004995a0 is called from two places, Main_InitAndParseCommandLine
   and App_Init, and it reaches maybe_Cdd_SetVideoMode and so Cdd_Init
   [verified]. So the surfaces exist before App_Init's step 9 loads the
   texture banks, which is what that step requires.

   That ordering is the one hard constraint in the boot sequence:
   display first, assets second, menu last. Everything else in section 2
   is asset loading that can be stubbed while a port is brought up.

   o A minimal first build therefore needs: an InitInstance equivalent,
     the display bring-up, enough of App_Init to have something to draw,
     and the frame loop of renderer.txt section 3.1. Steps 2, 6, 7, 11
     and 12 of section 2 - racers, champ tables, demo files, the logo
     page - are not on the path to a first frame.

   o Both Game_Tick and Game_RenderFrame are VIRTUAL, reached through
     the nine-entry vtable at 0x004d04c8 that Game_StaticCtor
     (0x00413f30) installs. A port can call them directly and skip the
     vtable entirely; nothing else dispatches through it that matters
     for drawing.


3.  Failure handling

   The function is a ladder of nested failure exits, each unwinding one
   more constructed object through an SEH state variable. Every rung
   returns 0, and the caller treats 0 as fatal.

   This is consistent with the conclusion in dd-errors.txt that ASSET
   LOADING failures are fatal while runtime display failures are not.
   Everything App_Init can fail at is an asset or an allocation.

4.  The corner-sign table

   maybe_Hud_BindCornerSigns clears a 16-entry table of 0x18-byte
   records at 0x005d2eb8 and then walks g_meshArrayA binding meshes to
   slots BY NAME [verified]:

   +-------------------+------------+---------------------------+
   | Mesh name         | Placed at  | Meaning                   |
   +===================+============+===========================+
   | counter           | (93, 0)    | a lap or position counter |
   +-------------------+------------+---------------------------+
   | g_45 / d_45       | (320, 400) | 45 degree left / right    |
   +-------------------+------------+---------------------------+
   | g_90 / d_90       | (320, 400) | 90 degree left / right    |
   +-------------------+------------+---------------------------+
   | g_chica / d_chica | (320, 400) | chicane left / right      |
   +-------------------+------------+---------------------------+

   g_ and d_ are gauche and droite. These are the turn-warning signs
   shown ahead of a corner.

   THE COORDINATES ARE INSETS FROM THE BOTTOM RIGHT, not positions
   [verified, 0x0048650c]. Hud_DrawSignMeshes builds

       offsetX = g_viewMaxX - recordX * scaleX
       offsetY = g_viewMaxY - recordY * scaleY

   so (93, 0) puts the counter plate in the BOTTOM-RIGHT corner - 93
   units in from the right edge and flush with the bottom - and
   (320, 400) puts a corner sign at x 319, y 79, near the top. An
   earlier reading of these as screen positions had the counter along
   the top edge, which is the opposite of where it is.

   AND THE CALLER IS NOT App_Init. maybe_Game_InitRace (0x0047bf70)
   is its only caller, which it has to be: the meshes it binds are
   TRACK DECOR TEMPLATES and do not exist until a track is loaded.
   The string "counter" appears in all eleven .trk files.

   The record is 0x18 bytes: +0x00 the mesh index, initialised to -1 for
   unbound; +0x04 and +0x08 the screen position; +0x0c unknown, cleared;
   +0x10 a flag defaulting to 1 which only "counter" overrides, to 0.

   Sixteen slots for seven known names, so nine are unused or bound
   elsewhere. Note the placements are absolute 640x480 pixel coordinates
   with no scaling, which matters for a port that wants resolutions the
   original did not offer.

5.  dd.cpp is complete

   With App_Init, App_InitEntry and maybe_Hud_BindCornerSigns named,
   screen3d\dd.cpp has no unnamed functions left over 16 bytes
   [verified]:

   +-----------------------+-------+---------+-----------------------+
   | Range                 | Named | Unnamed | Thunks under 17 bytes |
   +=======================+=======+=========+=======================+
   | 0x00472000-0x00478fff | 70    | 0       | 56                    |
   +-----------------------+-------+---------+-----------------------+

   The 56 excluded entries are the SEH unwind thunks and fragments
   described in code-map.txt, not real functions.

   Note that the cluster at 0x00479080 and above is menuTools.cpp, not
   dd.cpp - code-map.txt puts the boundary at 0x00479000. It contains
   several substantial unnamed functions, but under the spine-first rule
   they are translate-list and off-spine.

   dd.cpp is now covered by twelve notes: dd-init, dd-chain, dd-primary-
   surface, dd-main-surface, dd-present, dd-blit, dd-caps, dd-vidmem,
   dd-statics, dd-errors, mode-change and this one. The next spine
   target is screen3d\screen3d.cpp or Dsi_d3d.cpp.
