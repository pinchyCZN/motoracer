                   THE RACER PORT CONVERGENCE LOG


Abstract

   How the reconstruction was brought onto MOTO.EXE for the racer
   subsystem: the A/B runs, the transcription records, the defects found
   in src/ and what each turned out to be. It is a record of the WORK,
   not of the binary - what a function computes stays in
   subsystems/racers.txt, and this file points at it rather than
   restating it.

   SECTION NUMBERS ARE RACERS.TXT'S AND ARE DELIBERATELY NOT
   CONTIGUOUS. Around 110 section numbers are cited from docs/, src/ and
   tools/, so the split kept every number exactly where it was; the gaps
   are the sections that stayed behind. A citation of the form
   "racers.txt 9.46.10" means this file.

9.17.  LIVE VERIFICATION - the model measured against the game

   Sections 9.1 to 9.16 are static; this is runtime evidence.
   Method: mrhook trace hooks and hardware watchpoints, with the user
   driving (CLAUDE.md rule 8).

9.17.1.  The torque curve predicts the game to 4 per cent

   Engine_TorqueAt (9.11) was decoded from twenty lines and five
   .rdata doubles and had never been observed. It is now.

   The bike's characteristics, read out of live memory rather than
   the file - and these are STOCK, not the hacked CARAC2 values, so
   the sample is clean [verified]:

   +----------------------+--------------+----------------------------------------+
   | MoteurVitesseMax     | 7.0          | the redline in engineSpeed units       |
   +----------------------+--------------+----------------------------------------+
   | MotoMasse            | 280.0        | divides the force                      |
   +----------------------+--------------+----------------------------------------+
   | RealSpeedMax         | 80.0         | matches racer+0xb4 read live           |
   +----------------------+--------------+----------------------------------------+

   +----------------------+--------------+----------------------------------------+
   | RegimeCoupleMax      | 10500.0      | rpm at peak torque                     |

   THE ONE CONSTANT 9.10.3 MISSED. maybe_Physics_TickDrivetrain
   rescales its dt at entry:

       dt *= 10.0;                     _DAT_004d1da8
       if (dt > 500.0) dt = 500.0;     _DAT_004d1dac

   so the drivetrain works in RAW MILLISECONDS while everything else
   in the tick uses the 0.1-scaled unit from 9.2. Without that
   factor the predicted acceleration is out by ten.

   With it, over five consecutive 33 ms frames [verified]:

   | engineSpeed   | roadSpeed   | observed    | predicted   | ratio   |
   +===============+=============+=============+=============+=========+
   | 3.0601        | 16.0001     | 0.3702      | 0.3979      | 0.931   |
   +---------------+-------------+-------------+-------------+---------+
   | 3.1309        | 16.3704     | 0.3871      | 0.4024      | 0.962   |
   +---------------+-------------+-------------+-------------+---------+
   | 3.2050        | 16.7574     | 0.3912      | 0.4072      | 0.961   |
   +---------------+-------------+-------------+-------------+---------+
   | 3.2798        | 17.1487     | 0.3954      | 0.4120      | 0.960   |
   +---------------+-------------+-------------+-------------+---------+
   | 3.3554        | 17.5441     | 0.3997      | 0.4169      | 0.959   |

   o A FUNCTION NEVER RUN, PREDICTING A LIVE ENGINE TO 4 PER CENT.
     That is the strongest confirmation any part of this
     reconstruction has had, and it validates the whole chain at
     once: the piecewise torque curve, the kgf.m to N.m conversion,
     the PI/30, the P = F.v division and MotoMasse as a real mass.

   o THE RESIDUAL WAS A SAMPLING ERROR, NOT A MISSING TERM, and
     9.17.7 runs it down. engineSpeed was read at function entry

     small speed-dependent remainder survives and is still [open].

   o THE ACCELERATION CAP WAS NOT EXERCISED. dt * 0.03 at 33 ms is
     0.99 and the observed increments were about 0.4, so
     _DAT_004d1dc8 remains untested.

9.17.3.  The wheelie cap is exactly 200

   Held on a straight until the bike came down of its own accord,
   with the button never released [verified]:

       wheelieTimer  200.1001      the cap constant is 0x4347ffff
                                   = 199.99998
       wheelieAngle  0.0           decayed to nothing WITH THE
                                   BUTTON STILL HELD
       landing thump fired ONCE, -32.0f written from
                     maybe_Racer_UpdateWheelie at 0x004661c2, with
                     ESI pointing at wheelieAngle

   o SO 9.11.2's READING IS CONFIRMED END TO END: the ramp, the
     cap, the decay and the one-shot thump.

   o THE DURATION MODEL ALSO HOLDS. With K_UP = 0.03*PI/2,
     K_DOWN = -0.016*PI/2 and dt = 3.3: 10.1 frames to reach PI/2,
     60.6 frames to reach the cap (2.00 s), 18.9 frames to decay
     (0.62 s) - 2.62 s total, against an observed wheelie of about
     two seconds.

9.17.4.  racer+0x0f0 has two writers - SUPERSEDED by 9.18

   The field 9.10.1 recorded as gating the entire tyre-slip path,
   with no known writer. A write watchpoint with NO changed filter
   caught eight hits, alternating between two functions [verified]:

       maybe_Racer_UpdateGroundContact   at 0x004694e9
       FUN_00466660                      at 0x00466697

   Every hit old=00000000 new=00000000. Twice a frame, always zero.

   o A changed FILTER WOULD HAVE SHOWN NOTHING here. The
     writes-of-zero caveat in structs/MR_Racer.txt 21 applies to
     watchpoints as well as to captures.

   o AND A value_ne WATCH OVER A WHOLE STRAIGHT CAUGHT NOTHING, so
     the non-zero branch had never been seen taken. RE-ARMING IT ON
     A DIRT TRACK SETTLED IT - see 9.18. The field is an AIR TIMER,
     the two writers are the two arms of one if, and it was always
     zero here only because the bike never left the ground on
     tarmac. Everything above is correct as an observation; the
     name "gate" and the implication of a single writer pair
     writing zero were both wrong.

9.17.6.  Two practical lessons about the tooling

   THE WHEELIE CANNOT BE DRIVEN BY POKING stuntHeld.  Writing
   racer+0x31f9 lands - the readback confirms it - and is then cleared
   inside the same frame by the key handler, so the ramp never starts
   and wheelieAngle stays 0.  It has to go in as a real input:

       MOTO   BIT 6 of the hold mask, which is NOT in the input word
              and goes to action6Down
       port   key c

   A crash test that needs the front wheel up is silently a plain
   glancing hit without this, and the bike rides away.

   THE HOLD MASK IS NOT ORDERED THE WAY THE KEYS ARE, and reading it
   as "bit 0 is right" produces a run where MOTO advances zero frames:

       +-------+-------+------------+---------------------------+
       |  Bit  | Value | Action     | Port key                  |
       +=======+=======+============+===========================+
       |   0   |   1   | accelerate | z                         |
       +-------+-------+------------+---------------------------+
       |   1   |   2   | brake      | x                         |
       +-------+-------+------------+---------------------------+
       |   2   |   4   | left       | left                      |
       +-------+-------+------------+---------------------------+
       |   3   |   8   | right      | right                     |
       +-------+-------+------------+---------------------------+
       |   6   |  40   | stunt      | c - see above             |
       +-------+-------+------------+---------------------------+

   So gas and right is 9, brake and right is 0xa, and 3 is gas with
   BRAKE - which is refused, not steered.  The bridge names the
   offending pair in the error, so read the reply rather than the
   speed column.

   ACCELERATE WITH BRAKE IS REFUSED BY MOTO AND ACCEPTED BY THE PORT.
   moto_send_input rejects a mask holding both - the game cannot
   reach it from a keyboard - so MOTO RUNS NO FRAMES AT ALL and
   reports whatever it held before, while mracer takes keys=z,x
   happily and drives on.  The result reads as a total divergence
   (one side had 113 particles, the other none) and is a harness
   difference.  The same applies to left with right.

   A GATED ACCUMULATOR IS NOT A LIVE MEASUREMENT.  racer+0x184 is
   written only inside the wheel-FX gate, so on a run where the gate
   never opens it still holds whatever the LAST run left there.  Read
   side by side it looked like the lateral-slip term differed by four
   orders of magnitude between the two games; computed live from
   row0 . velDir * speed it is 1e-5 noise on both, agreeing in
   magnitude every frame of a 45-step turn.  Compute the quantity
   from its operands; do not read the field something else derived
   from it.

   CHECK THE PLACEMENT LINE AFTER SUCH A RUN.  mr_place --to both
   prints MATCHED or a per-field complaint, and one side having
   silently not moved shows up there immediately.


   o A HOOK INSIDE A PER-RACER FUNCTION SPENDS ITS WHOLE BUDGET IN
     ONE FRAME. Hooking maybe_Racer_UpdateWheelie gave eight hits
     with ECX stepping 0x584421, 0x58762a, 0x58a833 ... by 0x3209 -
     eight racers in a single tick, not eight frames. To get frames,
     hook something on the HUMAN-ONLY path:
     maybe_Racer_Tick -> maybe_Racer_ApplyDrive ->
     maybe_Physics_TickDrivetrain is called exactly once per frame.

   o AND ARMING BEFORE THE LIGHTS WASTES IT. maybe_Racer_ApplyDrive
     runs during the grid countdown with drive suppressed, so a hook
     armed at the line records eight frames of zeroes. Arm it while
     already moving.

9.17.7.  The 4 per cent, run down

   9.17.1 left a stable 0.96 ratio open and named three candidates
   inside maybe_Physics_TickDrivetrain. Two hooks bracketing the
   suspect region settled it, and the answer was none of the three.

9.17.7.1.  The gearbox round trip is lossless

   Hooks at 0x0048f492 (after the engine increment and the
   realSpeedMax clamp) and at 0x0048f50d (after FUN_0048dd90 stores
   its result) both captured gearboxRoadSpeed on the same frame.

   THE TWO VALUES WERE BYTE-IDENTICAL ON 8 OF 8 FRAMES [verified].
   So maybe_Gearbox_SpeedToEngine turning road speed into engine
   speed, the per-gear clamp against the table at 0x00509290, and
   FUN_0048dd90 turning it back, together change gearboxRoadSpeed by
   nothing at all.

   o A GEAR-QUANTISATION HYPOTHESIS WAS TESTED AND REFUTED. Worth
     recording as a refutation rather than deleting: the round trip
     LOOKS lossy in the decompiler - three transforms and a clamp -
     and is not.

   o engineSpeed DOES change across it, and by a lot: 0.7000 became
     0.4131 on the first frame and 1.7738 became 2.0147 on the
     eighth. So the resync is real work; it just leaves road speed
     alone.

9.17.7.2.  The model is exact once engineSpeed is sampled correctly

   The mistake was WHERE the input was read. engineSpeed must be
   taken at 0x0048f492, which is what the torque call uses. The
   earlier attempt read it at function entry.

   +-------------+------------+-------------+-------------+---------+
   | entryRoad   | engSpeed   | observed    | predicted   | ratio   |
   +=============+============+=============+=============+=========+
   | 1.0800      | 0.7000     | 0.83256     | 0.83265     | 1.000   |
   +-------------+------------+-------------+-------------+---------+
   | 1.9126      | 0.7313     | 0.49469     | 0.49524     | 0.999   |
   +-------------+------------+-------------+-------------+---------+
   | 2.4072      | 0.9204     | 0.51884     | 0.51961     | 0.999   |
   +-------------+------------+-------------+-------------+---------+
   | 2.9261      | 1.1188     | 0.54415     | 0.54517     | 0.998   |
   +-------------+------------+-------------+-------------+---------+
   | 3.4702      | 1.3268     | 0.57068     | 0.57197     | 0.998   |
   +-------------+------------+-------------+-------------+---------+
   | 4.0409      | 1.5450     | 0.59847     | 0.60007     | 0.997   |
   +-------------+------------+-------------+-------------+---------+
   | 4.6394      | 1.7738     | 0.62759     | 0.62955     | 0.997   |
   +-------------+------------+-------------+-------------+---------+

   o 0.1 TO 0.3 PER CENT. That is rounding, not a missing term. The
     acceleration model of 9.10.3 is solved.

   o AND IT IS A LESSON ABOUT SAMPLING, NOT ABOUT THE MODEL. The
     same formula and the same constants, fed a value from the right
     instruction, went from 4 per cent out to exact. When a
     prediction is systematically off by a few per cent, suspect the
     sample point before suspecting the arithmetic.

9.17.7.3.  What is left of the residual

   The ratio still drifts 1.000 -> 0.997 as road speed goes 1.08 ->
   4.64, and the earlier capture at road speed 16-17.5 in gear 4 sat
   at 0.96. So a SPEED-DEPENDENT term remains.

   o THE TYRE-SLIP LERP IS THE CANDIDATE. It pulls gearboxRoadSpeed
     toward the body-space longitudinal velocity (9.10.2) and would
     be negligible at walking pace and material at speed. NOT
     MEASURED - a hook after the slip block at high speed would
     settle it the same way this one settled the round trip. [open]

9.17.7.4.  An idle floor

9.17.8.4.  What a port needs from all this

   o THE ENGINE AND GEARBOX ARE DONE. Torque curve, power, force,
     mass, the dt x10 rescale, the idle floor and the gear ladder
     are all verified against the running game.

   o DRAG IS DONE ENOUGH TO IMPLEMENT: speed^2 * dt * 0.3 /
     MotoMasse * 0.001, on speed rather than on road speed.

   o THE ORDER MATTERS MORE THAN THE MISSING THIRD. road speed and
     speed converge every frame, so a reconstruction that applies
     the engine gain, then drag, then re-converges the two will be
     within a few per cent of the original even without the last
     term - and will be structurally right, which the last term
     will not change.

9.17.9.3.  The drag formula is exact

   The arithmetic, read instruction by instruction at 0x0049005d:

       FMUL [0x004d1c80]             * 0.001

9.17.9.4.  What the earlier 4 per cent actually was

   TWO SEPARATE SAMPLING MISTAKES, and neither was a physics term.

   o 9.17.1's 4 per cent: engineSpeed read at function entry instead
     of at 0x0048f492. Fixed in 9.17.7.2, ratio went to 1.000.

   o 9.17.8's remaining third: "observed drag" was computed as
     speed@C - speed@B, but a SetLongSpeed call sits BETWEEN those
     two probes and moves speed as well. That call accounts for the
     -0.0065 seen in the B to C road-speed column. Measuring drag by
     the C to D drop instead - where nothing else intervenes - gives
     the formula exactly.

   o SO THE WHOLE OF THE DRIVETRAIN IS NOW ACCOUNTED FOR, and every
     discrepancy in 9.17 turned out to be WHERE a value was sampled
     rather than WHAT the model computes. Three times in a row. For
     a reconstruction that is the reassuring outcome: the decompiled
     arithmetic was right every time.

9.17.9.5.  The frame, end to end

       1  engine     gearboxRoadSpeed += min(dt * power / roadSpeed
                     / MotoMasse * 0.001, dt * 0.03)      ratio 1.000
       2  clamp      to realSpeedMax
       3  resync     SpeedToEngine -> FUN_0048dd90, LOSSLESS for
                     road speed (9.17.7.1)
       4  slip block road speed untouched; SetLongSpeed sets
                     speed = roadSpeed
       5  a further SetLongSpeed call, -0.0065 on both [open - the
          call is located, the argument is not quantified]
       6  drag       speed -= speed^2 * dt * 0.3 / MotoMasse * 0.001
       7  final lerp road speed pulled down onto speed
       8  exit       roadSpeed == speed

   o WHAT A PORT NEEDS IS STEPS 1, 6 AND 7. Steps 3 and 4 are
     no-ops for road speed, and step 5 is worth 0.0065 out of 0.027
     at speed 24 - about a quarter of the drag, and the only piece
     still unquantified.

9.18.12.  maybe_Racer_ApplySteering's conversion, read in full

   THE BAR ANGLE IS NOT A SOURCE OF DIFFERENCE. Held hard over, both
   sides sit at exactly -600.000, the roueLimiteAngulaire lock stop,
   ratio 1.000. So racer_apply_steering's bar model is right and
   anything left is in the conversion to yaw.

   THE CONVERSION, out of maybe_Racer_ApplySteering:

       fade   = clamp((speed / vitesseMaxBoite) * 0.5, 0, 0.5)
                                          00466471..004664bc
       factor = rotationTransfert - fade * rotationTransfert * 0.5
                                          004664cc..004664e6
       if (wheelieAngle != 0) factor *= 0.5
                                          004664d6 / 004664f2
       mult   = factor * steering * 0.01  00466500 / 00466511
       grip   = fxGate ? clamp(1 + 0.1*|slip|, 1, 1.6) : 1.0
                brake && turning -> 1.6   0046658f..00466612
       yaw    = grip * mult * dt          0046661e / 00466625

   euler is (0, yaw, 0) at 0x00466639 - the MIDDLE slot - then
   Racer_ComposeOrientation at 0x0046664a. X is fade *
   rotationTransfert: 0x004664d0 is FMUL float [EBX + 0x5092c8], the
   same carac field the subtraction uses.

   carac 19 is vitesseMaxBoite (0x005092c4) and carac 20 is
   rotationTransfert (0x005092c8), both against the g_bikeCarac base at
   0x00509278. 0.5 is 0x004d1018 and 0.01 is 0x004d1004.

   THAT IS racer_steer_torque TERM FOR TERM AND CONSTANT FOR CONSTANT,
   the grip block structurally identical, so the steering conversion is
   not a defect. 9.18.14 and 9.18.15 have what the apparent 0.73 turn
   ratio actually was.

9.18.14.  The steering is CORRECT - measured, not argued

   An arc comparison put the port at 0.73 of MOTO's turn rate and this
   section used to conclude that some unknown writer was adding yaw to
   MOTO's matOrientation.  IT IS NOT SO, and both halves of that were
   wrong.

   WHERE THE YAW COMES FROM.  Trace hooks at 0x0045d775 (just after
   ModelInstance_Integrate) and 0x0045d7f7 (the tail of the tick),
   each capturing the forward row at racer+0xd4, through three
   turning frames:

       +-------+------------------+---------------+---------------+
       | frame | after Integrate  | end of tick   | across tick   |
       +=======+==================+===============+===============+
       |   1   |     -58.7329     |    -58.7330   |       -       |
       |   2   |     -61.5234     |    -61.5234   |      -2.7904  |
       |   3   |     -64.2920     |    -64.2920   |      -2.7686  |
       +-------+------------------+---------------+---------------+

   The WITHIN-tick change is 1e-4 degrees.  So nothing after Integrate
   rotates the heading - not ApplyCornering, not the ground contact,
   not UpdateAirborne, not UpdateLean.  Every degree of it is
   maybe_Racer_ApplySteering, across the tick boundary.

   AND IT MATCHES ITS OWN FORMULA.  At 28.745 m/s the conversion in
   9.18.12 predicts 2.8023 deg a frame; measured 2.79 and 2.77.

   BOTH SIDES AGREE AT EQUAL SPEED.  Placed at a matched speed and
   gear and stepped two frames at a time:

       +-------------+---------------------+---------------------+
       | placed      | MOTO                | port                |
       +=============+=====================+=====================+
       | 40 m/s      | 36.28 m/s  -2.677   | 36.36 m/s  -2.670   |

       | 60 m/s      | 56.23 m/s  -2.432   | 60.92 m/s  -2.366   |
       +-------------+---------------------+---------------------+

   Row one is the like-for-like: 0.3 per cent apart.  Every row is
   within 1 per cent of its own prediction, so racer_steer_torque is
   right at both ends of the speed range and the fade curve with it.

   WHAT PRODUCED THE 0.73.  A whole 15-frame chunk was predicted using
   the speed read at the END of it, while fade - and therefore the
   factor - moved throughout.  The arithmetic was too coarse to
   compare, and it pointed at the one function that was already
   correct.  PER-FRAME AT A MATCHED SPEED is the only form of this
   measurement worth trusting.

9.18.22.  The drivetrain tail was transcribed twice, so drag was

   maybe_Physics_TickDrivetrain @ 0x0048ef30 has ONE drag site.  The
   port had two, because two different functions transcribe that one
   original function's tail:

   SetLongSpeed rebuilds speed, so its already-dragged result fed the
   longitudinal scalar and the second application compounded it.

   the error growing as the square of speed - which is drag's own
   shape and not torque's:

   on real elapsed time, the port varied from itself by 0.004 m/s run

9.18.25.  COAST: THE ENGINE-DECAY TABLE, FOUND BEHIND A STRING

   Coasting cost the port 15.4 per cent over twenty frames from 20
   m/s.  Per frame it is not a steady loss at all:

   +--------+------------+------------+
   | frames | MOTO d/frm | port d/frm |
   +========+============+============+
   |   4-10 |   -0.0205  |   -0.0193  |
   |  12-16 |   -0.1450  |   -0.6000  |
   |  18-20 |   -0.1404  |   -0.0136  |

   Steady coast in the high gear MATCHES.  The damage is a burst of
   three frames after the DOWNSHIFT and then nothing - the port sheds
   four times too much for three frames, then stops braking on the
   engine entirely while MOTO keeps shedding 0.14 a frame.

   That shape is the signature of the coast branch in
   physics_drivetrain_frame: it decays the engine and converts the
   result back to road speed, so the loss tracks engineSpeed, and
   when the engine falls under the 1000 rpm floor the branch stops
   firing.  Measured, the engine decayed 0.966 a frame against MOTO's

   physics_engine_decay_ratio returned a placeholder 1.0 because the
   table it indexes had never been read, with a note saying a wrong
   coefficient would show up the moment the decay rate was compared
   against MOTO.  It did.

   0x004f17d4 + gear*4 + 8, and 0x004f17d4 is the twelve bytes of the
   string "AnimDecor" plus its NULs - so the floats start where the
   string ends, the +8 steps over its tail, and Ghidra labelled the
   whole region s_AnimDecor_.  That is why the note called the label
   "certainly wrong" without being able to say what was there.

   | gear | address | ratio      |

   WHAT IS LEFT IN COAST, about -0.52 per cent, is two things:

   o THE FIRST-FRAME TRANSIENT IS A COASTING FRAME TAKING THE DRIVE
     BRANCH.  Bisecting the placement one write at a time:

     +--------------------------------+---------------------------+
     | placement                      | frame 1 / 2 / 3           |
     +================================+===========================+
     | full                           | -0.126 / -0.020 / -0.020  |
     | --keep-move                    | -0.126 / -0.020 / -0.020  |
     | --speed and --gear only        | -0.126 / -0.020 / -0.020  |
     | --speed only                   | -0.021 / -0.021 / -0.021  |

     It is the GEAR write, and specifically the shift machinery that
     goes with it.  --speed alone leaves the gearbox where the last
     test left it - state 2, pending -1, mid-downshift - and --gear
     clears that to state 0.

     THAT FLIPS WHICH BRANCH A COASTING FRAME TAKES.  In
     physics_drivetrain_frame the throttle-off case (num = -1) falls
     through to:

         else if (skipSync || airborne)  { free rev }
         else                            { the DRIVE branch }

     and skipSync is (gearbox.state != 0).  So with a shift pending a
     coast frame free-revs, and with the gearbox idle the same frame
     runs the drive slice - which costs 0.105 m/s on the frame the
     engine is still zero.

     IT IS A PORT DIVERGENCE, NOT A HARNESS ONE.  MOTO gets the
     identical placement, gear and machinery both, and shows no such
     frame - its first two average -0.0206, the same as its steady
     state.

     AND IT IS NOT A BRANCH INVERSION.  That was claimed here and it
     was wrong.  The original dispatches:

         0048f322  JL  0048f8fa   num < 0  -> the coast/brake block
         0048f36a  JNZ 0048f6b2   target < engineSpeed
         0048f372  JLE 0048f6b2   num <= 0
         0048f37a  JNZ 0048f65c   driveInterlock
         0048f382  JNZ 0048f65c   airborne
         0048f388                 the DRIVE path

     and the port's if (num >= 0) chain maps onto it: COAST USES
     num = 0, not -1 - racer_apply_drive sets `out->num = 0; /* coast
     */` - so a throttle-off frame takes the num >= 0 arm, satisfies
     num < 1, and lands on the decay path, which is 0x0048f372's JLE
     to the same target.  The drive slice is reachable only with
     num >= 1 AND target >= engineSpeed.  The structures agree.

     ISOLATED TO THE GEARBOX state FLAG, one frame, everything else
     held identical - same placement, timer poked to 300 so no shift
     completes, only state and pending differing:

     +-----------------------+-----------------+-----------------+
     | state                 | engine 0 ->     | d(speed)        |
     +=======================+=================+=================+
     | 0, idle               |          2.5366 |        -0.12602 |
     | 2, downshift pending  |          0.0000 |        -0.02061 |

     THE MEASUREMENT DOES NOT FIT THE SOURCE, and that is the state
     of it.  skipSync is (preRace || disengaged) and race.c passes
     disengaged = (state != 0).  It gates four sites - two free-rev
     arms and two engine resyncs - and NONE of them writes
     gearboxRoadSpeed: physics_speed_to_engine takes roadSpeed by
     address but only ever reads it, whatever its comment says about
     the original recomputing the road speed down.

     And the engine contradicts the branch structure outright.  With
     state 2, skipSync is 1, the free-rev arm should run and leave
     the engine at moteurVitesseMax * dtMs * 0.0006 = 0.1386.  It
     reads exactly 0.0000, so that arm did not run either.

     THE PER-STAGE TRACE SETTLED IT.  g_race_driveTrace records
     (gearboxRoadSpeed, r->speed) at ten points in the tick; one frame
     with state 0 against one with state 2:

     +----------------------+-------------+-------------+----------+
     | stage                | state 0     | state 2     | d(gbox)  |
     +======================+=============+=============+==========+
     | top of tick          |    20.00000 |    20.00000 | +0.00000 |
     | after drive step     |    19.89440 |    20.00000 | -0.10560 |
     | after the tail       |    19.87398 |    19.97939 | -0.10542 |
     | after ground contact |    19.87398 |    19.97939 | -0.10542 |
     +----------------------+-------------+-------------+----------+

     It is lost inside physics_drivetrain_frame and merely carried
     from there on.  AND THE NUMBER IS THE ENGINE DECAY FACTOR:

         1 + 0.16 * 33 * (-0.001) = 0.99472       gear 3
         20.0 * 0.99472           = 19.89440      the trace, exactly

     with the engine following, 7/54.9 * 19.89440 = 2.5366, which is
     what the trace reads.  So gearboxRoadSpeed was multiplied by the
     gear's decay coefficient.

     THE CHAIN:

       1. race.c resyncs the engine from road speed BEFORE the
          drivetrain, gated on gearbox state == 0, so engine goes
          0 -> 2.5501.
       2. the drivetrain's coast arm now sees engine > the decay
          floor, so it decays it 0.53 per cent to 2.5366.
       3. THE SAME ARM WRITES IT BACK -
          gbox = physics_engine_to_speed(engine) - and road speed
          becomes 19.8944.

     Road speed is re-derived from an engine that was itself derived
     from road speed one step earlier, so the decay lands on the
     speed.  With state 2 step 1 is skipped, the engine stays zero,
     step 2's guard fails and gbox is untouched - which is the whole
     0.105.

     0x0048f6b2..0x0048f7a0 READ.  The write-back is REAL and the
     port's transcription of it is faithful:

         0048f6b2  TEST EDI,EDI            driveInterlock -> free rev
         0048f6ba  TEST EBX,EBX            airborne       -> free rev
         0048f6c2  FLD  [EDX] / FDIV regimeMax / FMUL 1000
         0048f6d0  FCOMP [ESI+0xc8]        the decay FLOOR vs engine
         0048f6db  JZ   0048f8fa           below it -> coast/brake
         0048f6e4  FLD  [EAX*4 + 0x4f17dc] the per-gear decay ratio
         0048f6eb  FMUL engineSpeed / dtMs / -0.001
         0048f6fe  FADD engineSpeed
         0048f704  FSTP [ESI+0xc8]         engine = decayed
         0048f733  CALL 0x0048dd90         EngineToSpeed
         0048f738  FSTP [ESI+0x108]        gearboxRoadSpeed = it

     So road speed IS re-derived from the decayed engine.  The table

         0048f73e  TEST [ESI+0xcc]         airborne -> skip to 0048f8fa
         0048f750  invert matOrientation   (0x0040fcd0)
         0048f775  FLD  [ESI+0x100]        speed

         0048f7b6  0x0040ff80 / 0x0040fe80 / 0x00410010
         0048f7ef  MOV [ESP+0x1c], 1.0

9.18.26.  TURN-IN: THE STEERING SEES LAST FRAME'S SPEED

   Accelerating into a turn from rest, the heading ends 2.944 degrees
   apart over twenty frames while the SPEED matches to six figures.
   The whole error arrives in the first four frames and the rates then
   agree to three decimals for the rest of the run:

   +-------+-------------+-------------+----------+
   | frame | MOTO deg/f  | port deg/f  | cum diff |
   +=======+=============+=============+==========+
   |     2 |     -1.1316 |     -0.2747 |   +1.714 |
   |     4 |     -3.1201 |     -2.4170 |   +3.120 |
   |     6 |     -3.0982 |     -3.1091 |   +3.098 |
   |    20 |     -2.9663 |     -2.9773 |   +2.944 |
   +-------+-------------+-------------+----------+

   THE BARS ARE ONE FRAME BEHIND.  After two frames the steering angle
   reads -326.70 on MOTO and -108.90 on the port, a ratio of exactly
   3.0 - and 108.90 is 33 * 3.3 while 326.70 is (33 + 66) * 3.3.  The
   port after TWO frames is where MOTO is after ONE.  Both ramp
   steerRate by the same 33 a frame and both clamp at the same 600
   lock stop, so nothing about the ramp itself is wrong.

   THE CAUSE IS TICK ORDER.  The digital steering branch is gated on
   speed >= 0.5556 m/s (0x004d10c0, 2 km/h).  Starting from rest the
   port's steering runs BEFORE its drivetrain, so on frame one it
   still sees speed 0, takes the centring branch and adds nothing;
   MOTO's has already accelerated to 1.9 by the time its steering
   runs.

   maybe_Racer_Tick calls ApplyDrive at 0x0045d713 and ApplySteering
   at 0x0045d743, and the port has that order too - the difference is
   what ApplyDrive DOES.  maybe_Racer_ApplyDrive's ONLY callee is
   maybe_Physics_TickDrivetrain, so the original updates speed inside
   the drive call.  The port's racer_apply_drive only computes the
   drive mode, and physics_drivetrain_frame runs about 150 lines
   later - after the steering.

   FIXED BY MOVING THE STEERING, NOT THE DRIVETRAIN.  Hoisting the
   drivetrain up to racer_apply_drive would have dragged
   gearbox_tick_shift and two engine resyncs to the wrong side of it.
   The steering is the smaller move and reproduces the same data
   dependency - and drive_load turned out not to be an obstacle at
   all, because it already runs at the TOP of the tick (race.c 1713);
   the one in the crash block is a second reload, not the first.

   racer_apply_steering and racer_orient_quantise now sit after
   physics_drivetrain_tail, so the whole drive step - frame, shift
   request and tail - completes before the bars are touched, which is
   what ApplyDrive does in the original.  The quantise travels with
   the steering because it is ComposeOrientation s tail and
   ApplySteering is what calls it.

   MOVING THE STEERING WAS ONLY HALF OF IT.  With the steering after
   the drive step the heading error did not shift at all - still
   2.944 degrees - because the drivetrain writes s_race_drive and the
   steering gate reads r->speed, and drive_store sat 130 lines
   further down.  The port carried this frame s speed in the mirror
   past the reader that needed it.

   THE MIRROR IS OURS, NOT THE ORIGINAL S.  MOTO has no s_race_drive

9.18.28.  THE DOWNSHIFT LANDS TWO FRAMES LATE - THE TIMER MISSES A TICK

   Coasting from 20 m/s in gear 3, MOTO drops to second at frame 10
   and the port at frame 12.  Reading the gearbox state machine on
   both sides shows where it goes:

   +-------+------------+------------+
   | frame | MOTO timer | port timer |
   +=======+============+============+
   |     2 |        236 |        267 |
   |     4 |        172 |        201 |
   |     8 |         44 |         69 |
   |    10 |        -20 |          3 |
   +-------+------------+------------+

   Both request the downshift on the first frame - state 2, pending
   -1 - and both count 300 ms down.  The port is simply ONE TICK
   BEHIND from the start and never catches up.

   THE ORDER WAS WRONG.  maybe_Racer_UpdateAirborne calls
   Gearbox_RequestAutoShift at 0x00466730 and Gearbox_TickShift at
   0x004667d0 - request first, tick second, one function, one frame.
   The port ticked the timer near the top of the tick and requested
   the shift a hundred lines later, so the frame a shift was ASKED
   FOR never counted against its own clutch delay.  Every shift in
   the game was one frame late.

   Moved: gearbox_tick_shift now runs immediately after the request
   block.  That also puts it after the drive step, which is where the
   original has it - UpdateAirborne is at 0x0045d7a6 and ApplyDrive
   at 0x0045d713.

   THE SHIFT TABLE ITSELF IS EXACT, and worth recording since it was
   the first suspect.  s_race_shiftTable against MOTO's racer+0x90:

   +-----+-----------+-----------+
   | idx | port      | MOTO      |
   +=====+===========+===========+
   |   1 |  17.80003 |  17.80003 |
   |   2 |  34.50005 |  34.50005 |
   |   3 |  50.89980 |  50.89980 |
   |   4 |  65.69957 |  65.69957 |
   |   5 |  72.89946 |  72.89946 |
   +-----+-----------+-----------+

   gearbox_build_shift_table reproduces the original's table to five
   decimals.  Index 0 differs - the port has 0.0 where MOTO has
   2.12785 - and that is harmless: the code reads T[gear] and
   T[gear-1] with gear >= 1, so entry 0 is never touched.  This also
   corrects 9.18.27, which read an access watchpoint on racer+0x90
   taking zero hits as "nothing reads the table".  Nothing reads THAT
   ENTRY; the rest are read every frame.  [verified]

   RESULT: THE GEAR NOW LANDS ON THE SAME FRAME.

   +-------+------------+------------+-----------+
   | frame | MOTO timer | port timer | port, was |
   +=======+============+============+===========+
   |     8 |         44 |         36 |        69 |
   |    10 |        -20 |        -30 |         3 |
   +-------+------------+------------+-----------+

   The timer goes negative on the frame the gear moves, so MOTO and
   the port both take second at frame 10 where the port used to be
   still counting - 3 ms left, still in third - and did not shift
   until frame 12.  [verified]

   THE SIX-STATE MATRIX DID NOT MOVE, and that is not a failed fix.
   At two-frame sampling the old lag sat INSIDE one bracket - the
   downshift transition from -0.020 to -0.150 a frame falls between
   the same two sample points either way - and its effect on a
   twenty-frame endpoint was cancelled by the frames on each side of
   it.  The mechanism is what was wrong and the mechanism is what was
   measured.  A finer sampling or a longer coast would show it; the
   matrix will not.

   THE 32-VERSUS-33 DECREMENT IS A float THAT SHOULD BE A double.

   MOTO subtracts 32 a frame from the clutch timer and the port
   subtracted 33 - the 44/36 gap above, widening by one a frame.  The
   arithmetic:

       dtScaled              = 3.2999999523162842
       dtScaled * 10.0f      = 33.0            -> (int) 33
       dtScaled * 10.0  (dbl)= 32.999999523    -> (int) 32

   The product lands a whisker under 33, and at that magnitude the
   whisker is below half a float ULP - so a float rounds it to
   exactly 33.0 and the cast reads 33.  The original computes on the
   x87 at 80-bit and truncates the same quantity to 32.  race.c's
   dtMs is now a double; the float consumers convert back to 33.0f at
   the call and are unaffected, and only the integer cast changes.

   THE GEARBOX IS NOW EXACT.  The clutch ladder reads identically on
   both sides, every sample:

   +-------+------------+------------+-------+
   | frame | MOTO timer | port timer | delta |
   +=======+============+============+=======+
   |     2 |        236 |        236 |    +0 |
   |     4 |        172 |        172 |    +0 |
   |     8 |         44 |         44 |    +0 |
   |    10 |        -20 |        -20 |    +0 |
   +-------+------------+------------+-------+

   Same table, same threshold, same call order, same decrement, same
   landing frame.  The six-state matrix is unchanged - dtMs feeds
   drag, roll, the brake step and lean as well, and all four states
   that were exact stayed exact.  [verified]

   THE GENERAL POINT, since this is the second time precision has
   decided a frame: an intermediate that the original keeps in an x87
   register is NOT a float, and storing it in one can round a value
   across an integer boundary.  Anything the port truncates or floors
   deserves the double.  [verified]

9.18.29.  THE PRECISION SWEEP

   Prompted by 9.18.28: an intermediate the original keeps in an x87
   register is not a float, and storing it in one can round a value
   across an integer boundary.  Every float-to-integer truncation in
   the gameplay path was checked against the binary.  ONE was wrong -
   the one already found - and the rest are recorded here so nobody
   sweeps them twice.

   +---------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | site                      | verdict                             |
   +===========================+=====================================+
   | race.c dtMs               | WAS WRONG. float rounded 32.9999995 |
   |                           | to 33.0; now a double. 9.18.28      |
   +---------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | physics.c brakeHoldMs     | CORRECT. MOTO adds 33 here while    |
   | += (int)dtMs              | subtracting 32 from the clutch      |
   |                           | timer - different quantities, and   |
   |                           | the measured ladder 0/33/66/99      |
   |                           | agrees on both sides.               |
   +---------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | model.c trig index        | CORRECT. TRIG_TWO_PI is an          |
   |                           | unsuffixed double literal, so the   |
   |                           | macro is int/double and the product |
   |                           | promotes - already double.          |
   +---------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | racer_orient_quantise     | CORRECT, and the (float) narrowing  |
   | (int)((double)(float)a    | is FAITHFUL: 0x0045cc03 is FLD float|
   |  * RADIANS_TO_INDEX)      | ptr [ESP+0x14] then FMUL double ptr |
   |                           | [0x004d0ed0] then __ftol. The       |
   |                           | constant is BIT-IDENTICAL to ours.  |
   +---------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | racer_compose_yaw         | CORRECT. yaw is already a float     |
   |                           | parameter, so (double)yaw is the    |
   |                           | narrowed value.                     |
   +---------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | racer.c pose k            | CORRECT. computed in double         |
   |                           | throughout.                         |
   +---------------------------+-------------------------------------+

   THE TEST THAT SETTLES ONE OF THESE is whether the original stores
   the value to memory between computing it and truncating it.  A
   4-byte FLD or FSTP means narrow; keeping it on the x87 stack means
   do not.  Reading that off the disassembly is cheaper than arguing
   about it, and it is what made the quantiser a non-finding and dtMs
   a real one.

   ONE INDEX IS 0.021973 DEGREES, and the turn-accel heading residual
   of -0.022 is exactly one trig entry.  MEASURED PER FRAME, in index
   units, it is a boundary crossing and not a lost index:

   +-------+-------------+-------------+----------+
   | frame | MOTO idx    | port idx    | d        |
   +=======+=============+=============+==========+
   |    18 |   -2330.001 |   -2330.001 |   +0.000 |
   |    20 |   -2600.000 |   -2601.001 |   -1.001 |
   |    22 |   -2867.002 |   -2867.998 |   -0.996 |
   |    24 |   -3133.001 |   -3133.001 |   +0.000 |
   +-------+-------------+-------------+----------+

   The heading agrees exactly at ten of twelve sample points.  At 20
   and 22 the two land either side of a table boundary - MOTO's index
   reads a flat -2600.000, which is the angle sitting ON the boundary
   - and they re-converge by 24.

   racer_orient_quantise truncates toward zero, so MOTO's pre-quantise
   yaw was just inside -2600 and the port's just past -2601.  The
   quantiser itself is verified faithful (9.18.29): the (float)
   narrowing matches FLD float ptr [ESP+0x14] and the constant is
   bit-identical.  So this is sub-index noise in the yaw ENTERING it,
   made visible by a truncation that happens to sit on a boundary for
   two frames.

   NOT WORTH CHASING FURTHER at this accuracy - it is transient,
   self-correcting, and 0.022 degrees.  Recorded so the next reader
   does not mistake it for a systematic one-index offset.  [closed]

9.18.30.  PLACING AWAY FROM THE START STRAIGHT NEEDS THE SEGMENT

9.18.31.  THE WALL SPEED PENALTY IS TOO HIGH  [open]

   With the segment fixed, both games behave the same GEOMETRICALLY at
   the bridge: same segment 662, same y to 0.03, and the containment
   clamps both to a wall distance of 32.00.  What differs is speed.

   Placed at 8.5 m/s and steered into the rail:

       first contact    MOTO 4.25    port 3.24
       second contact   MOTO 3.63    port 2.65

   The port sheds about a quarter more on every touch, which is what
   makes it feel snagged - the rider has to stop and turn away where
   the original carries speed along the rail and rides out of it.

   THE DEEP CLIP IS THE MISSING RAIL CONTAINMENT.  [inferred, strong]

   Reported from play: the first hit behaves; approach a rail slowly
   at about 45 degrees and then hold the throttle into it, and after
   three or four contacts the bike digs progressively deeper until it
   is inside the mesh, after which it cannot be steered away and
   skips along still snagging.

   THE ORIGINAL HAS TWO WALL ROUTINES AND THE PORT HAS ONE.

       0x0040ca80  the guard-rail REFLECT - the bounce and scrape,
                   transcribed in racer_update_ground_contact
       0x0040cd90  the rail CONTAINMENT - `A marked hole` in racer.c

   That is also why tools/mr_cheats.py patches TWO sites to turn walls
   off; they are separate routines.

   CONTAINMENT RUNS EVERY FRAME, NOT ON IMPACT:

       004693f9  LEA  EAX,[ESI+0x30]     &racer->position
       004693fc  MOV  ECX,[ESI+0x31c1]   segmentStart
       00469404  MOV  ECX,0x52c108       the track object
       00469409  CALL 0x0040cd90         contain
       0046941e  CALL Trk_GroundHeightAt

   and it clamps the POSITION back inside the segment's two wall
   planes before the ground height is asked for:

       if (Plane_Distance(p) < 0x004d02d0)
           p = Plane_IntersectSegment(seg planes, p);      wall A
       if (Plane_Distance(p) < 0x004d02d0)
           p = Plane_IntersectSegment(seg planes, p);      wall B

   0xfc in the indexing is 252, the segment stride, which confirms
   the record it reads.

   EVERY SYMPTOM FOLLOWS.  The first contact is fine because the
   reflect handles it.  Nothing resets the penetration afterwards, so
   repeated pushes accumulate instead of being clamped out each
   frame.  Once inside the wall volume the reflect keeps re-firing,
   which is the skipping and the inability to steer away.  And it
   takes a SLOW angled approach because a fast hit bounces clear
   before the depth can build.

   IMPLEMENTED as trk_contain_point, whole, from 0x0040cd90 with
   Plane_Distance (0x004b10c0) and Plane_IntersectSegment
   (0x004b0f60).  The threshold at 0x004d02d0 is 0.0, so the test is
   simply "beyond the rail", and the pull-back runs from the SEGMENT'S
   OWN pos to the point rather than along the wall normal.

   IT DID NOT FIX THE SNAG, AND THE SNAG IS NOT PENETRATION.

   THE STUCK STATE IS NOT A PLACE.  Captured live from a stuck bike at
   (-3709.02, -1452.89, 55347.75), segment 108: both wall distances
   were POSITIVE - 31.7 inside wall B - so nothing was through a
   plane.  Placing BOTH games at that exact point and stepping, they
   behave identically: y tracks to 0.01 a frame and both roll down
   the slope together.  The snag is an accumulated STATE, which is
   why it takes three or four contacts to reach.

   DRIVING INTO THE EDGE REPRODUCES IT.  Throttle and steer into the
   rail from the same placement:

   +-------+------------+------------+
   | frame | MOTO spd   | port spd   |
   +=======+============+============+
   |    16 |       7.61 |       7.60 |
   |    18 |       9.27 |       4.27 |

   |    22 |       7.99 |       0.82 |
   |    24 |       9.69 |       0.22 |
   +-------+------------+------------+

   Four contacts track together, then MOTO accelerates away and the
   port collapses to a standstill.

   THE RATIO NAMES IT: 1.34/4.27 is 0.31, and RACER_WALL_SCRAPE is
   0.3.  The port takes the SCRAPE arm on contacts where MOTO takes
   the BOUNCE arm, and 0.3 compounding over three contacts is the
   whole of it.  The arm is chosen by the dot test at 0x004692e4 -
   the reflected direction against the frame's original move, against
   0.0 - so that test is coming out negative for the port and
   positive for MOTO.

   Note too that MOTO goes DEEPER and rides out - distB 10.45 at the
   frame the port is held at exactly 32.00 - so the containment is
   standing the port off where the original lets the bike in and lets
   the reflect carry it along the rail.

   THE DOT TEST'S INPUTS, HOOKED AT 0x004692e4 ON A SLOPE:

       hit 1  dir  (-0.44352, -0.87808,  0.17964)
              move (   -12.12,   -47.66,    22.97)   dot 3D  +51.357
       hit 2  dir  (-0.53530, -0.80438,  0.25774)
              move (   -19.57,   -50.74,    31.96)   dot 3D  +59.530

   BOTH BOUNCE, AND THE Y TERM IS WHY.  dir.y * move.y is +41.8 of
   the +51.4 on hit 1.  Downhill, both the reflected direction and
   the frame's move carry a large negative Y, and their product is
   what forces the bounce.  The horizontal part alone is only +9.5.

   AND THE PORT HAS NO Y TERM TO GIVE IT.  move is built the same way
   - move[i] = position[i] - positionPrev[i] - but in the stuck state
   the port's y is FROZEN at exactly -1452.89 frame after frame while
   x and z creep.  So move.y is 0, the dominant term vanishes, the
   test falls back to the horizontal part and can go negative.

   THAT IS A SELF-REINFORCING LOOP, which is why it takes several
   contacts to enter and will not let go:

       y stops changing  ->  move.y = 0  ->  dot loses its Y term
       ->  scrape instead of bounce  ->  speed * 0.3  ->  bike stops
       ->  nothing moves y  ->  y stops changing

   It also explains the shape of the bug exactly: one clean hit is
   fine, repeated shoves are needed to get in, and once in, throttle
   does not help because the scrape takes 70 per cent of whatever the
   throttle just added.

   THE CHAIN TO THE SNAG: heading.y flattens -> moveDir[1] follows it,

   choice is right, the bounce arithmetic is exact, the containment
   is now imported, the ground height is right and the moveDir refit

   AND IT IS NOT THE GROUND FIT EITHER.  Placed at the same position
   and heading and stepped the same number of frames, the two sides

   grass bank the bike is wedged against genuinely rises ahead of

   SO heading.y IS AN OUTPUT, NOT AN INPUT.  The fit is a pure
   function of the position and the previous forward row, and the
   two sides agree on it; so by the frame the pitch parts company,
   the position or the yaw feeding it already has.  The table above
   is that divergence seen one level downstream.  [open - find the

9.18.33.  THE SNAG IS THE RECOVERY YAW, AND IT TURNS THE WRONG WAY

   9.18.32 blamed the pitch and was wrong; the ground fit is exact.
   Driven down the same descent with both riders upright and both on
   the same segment, the two sides hold together for twenty-two
   steps - segments 100 to 110 in step, speed within 0.01 m/s, a
   CONSTANT 26-unit position offset that never grows.  The descent
   is clean.  The divergence starts at the FIRST WALL CONTACT and
   nowhere else.

   AT THAT CONTACT the inputs are the same on both sides and the
   outputs are not [verified]:

   +--------------------+-------------+-------------+
   | at the contact     | MOTO        | port        |
   +====================+=============+=============+
   | wallNormal.x       |   -0.638950 |   -0.638950 |
   | wallNormal.z       |   -0.769248 |   -0.769248 |
   | bike yaw, rad      |   +0.450379 |   +0.450*   |
   | flLeanTarget, rad  |   -0.414401 |   +1.328    |
   +--------------------+-------------+-------------+

   The drain is identical - 0.15551 a step on both - so the rate is
   right and the VALUE is wrong.  The consequence is immediate: over
   the next three steps MOTO's forward row runs -0.55, -0.67, -0.77
   in x, converging on the along-wall direction, while ours runs
   -0.28, -0.13, +0.02 and comes back across the wall.  Hence "you
   cannot turn away from it".

   THE FAULT IS ONE OPERATOR IN racer_wall_recovery_yaw.  Step 3
   computed the along-wall heading as

       aTarget = pi/2 - aNormal        a MIRROR about pi/4

   where the original rotates:

       aTarget = aNormal - pi/2        a QUARTER TURN

   The two agree when and only when aNormal is exactly pi/2, which
   is a square hit on a rail running down +Z - and every check ever
   written for this function was a square hit.  On the 140-degree
   wall above the mirror put the target at -50.286 degrees where the
   along-wall direction is +50.286, so the bike was told to turn 76
   degrees the wrong way.

   Which of the two along-wall directions applies is already settled
   by step 2 negating the normal, so step 3 is a fixed rotation and
   never a choice - that is the check that the rotation is right and
   the mirror cannot be.

   AFTER THE FIX, driven down the same descent with both sides clean,
   they hit the same wall on the same step [verified]:

   +------+-------------+-------------+-----------+
   | step | MOTO lean   | port lean   | apart     |
   +======+=============+=============+===========+
   |    2 |    -0.25966 |    -0.25942 |   0.00024 |
   +------+-------------+-------------+-----------+
   |    3 |    -0.10415 |    -0.10391 |   0.00024 |
   +------+-------------+-------------+-----------+
   |    4 |     0.00000 |     0.00000 |         0 |
   +------+-------------+-------------+-----------+

   0.014 degrees, and the raw returns - MOTO's captured at the store,
   ours recovered from the field and the identical 0.15551 drain -
   are -0.415169 against -0.414911.  The forward row now converges
   the same way on both, and the bike drives out of the contact and
   carries on down the track in step.

   THE 0.74 DEGREES WAS MINE, NOT THE GAME'S.  An offline check of
   the formula modelled the bike yaw as atan2(-fwd.x, fwd.z), which
   throws away the forward row's y - -0.255 at that contact - and
   came out 0.74 degrees off.  racer_get_yaw is what the function is
   actually handed and it is not the xz projection.  A model of a
   function is not a measurement of it.

9.18.34.  AND THE SAME SLIP AGAIN, IN THE OTHER OPERAND

   With 9.18.33 fixed the bike clears the first contact and runs on
   in step - and diverges again at the SECOND, on a wall coming in
   at a different angle.  Same function, same shape of error, step 2
   this time instead of step 3.

   Step 2 picks WHICH of the two along-wall directions to turn to,
   by dotting the bike's left against the wall normal and negating
   the normal if that is negative.  The original builds that vector
   by putting (-1, 0, 0) through Mat34_FromEuler(0, flCurrentYaw, 0),
   and flCurrentYaw is MINUS the bike's yaw, so it is

       left = (-cos b, 0, -sin b)

   The port had +sin b - that vector mirrored about the x axis.

   MOTO'S RAW RETURN AT BOTH CONTACTS OF ONE DESCENT, captured at
   the store, against what each branch of the test would give
   [verified]:

   +---------------------------+-----------+----------+-----------+
   | wall normal               | MOTO raw  | keep     | negated   |
   +===========================+===========+==========+===========+
   | (-0.6390, 0, -0.7692)     |   -23.70  |  -24.46  |  +155.54  |
   +---------------------------+-----------+----------+-----------+
   | (-0.5017, 0, -0.8650)     |    -5.81  |   -6.69  |  +173.31  |
   +---------------------------+-----------+----------+-----------+

   MOTO keeps at both.  With +sin b the dot came out +0.240 at the
   first and -0.392 at the second, so the port kept the first - which
   is why 9.18.33 measured clean - and negated the second, asking for
   a 173-degree turn where MOTO asked for 6.

   A SQUARE HIT IS EXACTLY ZERO WHICHEVER WAY left IS BUILT, so this
   error survived every square-hit check as well.  That is now twice
   in one function, and it is worth stating as a rule rather than as
   two anecdotes: A SQUARE HIT CANNOT VALIDATE THIS FUNCTION.  Its
   two sign-carrying steps both degenerate there - aNormal is exactly
   pi/2 so mirror and rotation agree, and left.n is exactly 0 so
   either side of the test is taken on noise.  Check it on an oblique
   wall or not at all.

   THE EPSILON ON THE TEST IS STILL RIGHT and its old justification
   was not.  It guards float noise about a true zero; the note that
   used to say the spurious flip "negated the answer back to the
   right one by accident" described the behaviour under the step 3
   mirror and stopped being true when that was fixed.

9.18.35.  THE WALL PHYSICS MATCHES; THE ROLL DOES NOT

   Reported symptom: after 9.18.33 and 9.18.34 the bike no longer
   snags, but it looks like it sits DEEPER in the rail than retail -
   "as if the wall boundary is farther out on mracer".

   THE COLLISION IS NOT IT [verified].  The repro is a standstill
   beside a rail, then throttle and steer into it and let it bounce
   along.  Placed identically and driven 46 steps of that, the two
   sides agree on the thing the complaint is about - the signed
   distance from the bike's position to the wall PLANE:

   o both cycle approach, hit, push out, repeat, in step;
   o both land on EXACTLY 32.00 after every hit, which is
     WALL_PUSHBACK at 0x004d1024;
   o the closest approaches are MOTO 1.61, 2.65, 3.42 against ours
     4.12, 5.11, 9.45 - so ours is if anything FARTHER OUT;
   o the gap oscillates in [-5, +2.6] with no drift either way until
     the two fall out of phase.

   The rail mesh confirms the plane is the rail face rather than an
   offset: of segment 146's 56 vertices, two lie within 5 units of
   the wall plane.  And that mesh is the same shipped data on both
   sides, so it cannot differ between them.

   THE ROLL IS.  matOrientation's lateral row is racer+0xbc, and its
   Y is exactly zero on our side ALWAYS, because
   racer_frame_from_forward takes lateral = cross(worldUp, forward)
   and that has no Y by construction (9.29.9).  MOTO's does not:

   +------+---------------+---------------+
   | step | MOTO lat.y    | port lat.y    |
   +======+===============+===============+
   |  0-3 |      0.000000 |      0.000000 |
   +------+---------------+---------------+
   |    4 |     +0.007972 |      0.000000 |
   +------+---------------+---------------+
   |    8 |     +0.010338 |      0.000000 |
   +------+---------------+---------------+
   |   17 |     +0.013418 |      0.000000 |
   +------+---------------+---------------+
   |   43 |     +0.017211 |      0.000000 |
   +------+---------------+---------------+

   Zero until the FIRST WALL CONTACT, then growing with every bounce.
   So the bike banks against the rail in retail and stays bolt
   upright in ours.

   THE GROUND FIT DOES NOT ALWAYS HAVE THE LAST WORD, which is how
   the roll survives at all.  A hardware watchpoint on racer+0xc0
   gives three writers a frame, in this order:

       0045cb2b   the compose   - non-zero
       0045cc62   the rebuild   - non-zero
       00469706   the ground fit - ZERO

   and the third is not always present.  MOTO's lat.y accordingly
   alternates between about 0.016 and exactly 0.000000 from frame to
   frame - the flat frames are the ones the ground fit closed.
   race.c's note that "the ground fit has the last word" is true of
   matOrientation[6] and NOT true of the lateral row every frame.

   PROPORTION: 0.0172 is about one degree, far too small to read as
   "much deeper in the wall", and it is NOT the reported symptom -
   this was the wrong matrix.  The visible bank is racer+0x2e1d and
   the difference there is 35 degrees the other way.  See 9.18.36,
   which is the actual fault; this section stands only as the record
   that the physics frame's roll differs by a degree.  [open, minor]

9.18.36.  THE BIKE LEANS INTO THE RAIL; RETAIL PINS IT UPRIGHT

   9.18.35 measured matOrientation's lateral row and found MOTO
   carrying about a degree of roll where we carry none.  THAT WAS THE
   WRONG MATRIX.  matOrientation is the physics frame and has no roll
   by construction on either side; the bike's VISIBLE bank is
   racer+0x2e1d, and there the difference is twenty times larger and
   the other way round.

   Same repro - standstill beside a rail, throttle and steer into it
   [verified]:

   +------+----------------+----------------+
   | step | MOTO lean deg  | port lean deg  |
   +======+================+================+
   |  0-3 |  0.12 -> 5.48  |  0.12 -> 5.42  |
   +------+----------------+----------------+
   |    4 |           0.11 |           0.20 |
   +------+----------------+----------------+
   |    9 |           0.01 |           7.14 |
   +------+----------------+----------------+
   |   14 |           0.07 |          18.48 |
   +------+----------------+----------------+
   |   19 |           0.06 |          34.75 |
   +------+----------------+----------------+

   Identical until the FIRST WALL CONTACT at step 4.  From there MOTO
   pins the bike upright - under 0.7 degrees for the whole ride -
   while ours lays it over to 35, cornering hard into the rail.  With
   the contact patch in the same place (9.18.35), a bike banked 35
   degrees has its body inside geometry a upright one clears, which
   is the "clipped much deeper in the wall" report.  Steering is
   saturated at 600.0 and leanMode is 0 on both, so it is not the
   input.

   THE MECHANISM IS flImpactBlend, racer+0x31d1.  Every wall path
   sets it to 200.0, and at the top of UpdateLean, 0x00469c8e:

       if (impactBlend != 0) impactBlend -= g_frameDelta, floored at 0
       stack = impactBlend * 0.005            0x004d100c, 1/200

   then at 0x00469cd6, the heading-against-travel blend weight:

       a = 1 - speed/realSpeedMax * 8         0x004d1020
       if (a < 0) a = 0
       if (a < stack) a = stack               0x00469cfc..00469d13

   That max() is the whole thing.  a is alpha in
   alpha*heading + (1-alpha)*moveDir, so alpha 1 makes the sampled
   frame the heading outright; matBikeRoot then equals matOrientation,
   the two yaws racer_lean_delta differences are the same number, the
   slip angle is zero and so is the lean.  It eases back as the
   counter decays and each fresh bounce re-arms it to 200.

   THE PORT HAD BOTH HALVES AND CONNECTED NEITHER.
   racer_lean_impact_decay was written, correct, and CALLED FROM
   NOWHERE - so flImpactBlend was set by four sites and never
   decremented - and racer_lean_blend_weight carried a comment saying
   the original floors it here and that "we do not carry
   flImpactBlend yet", which had stopped being true.  Two dead ends
   pointing at each other.

   THE FLOOR GOES ON BOTH, BECAUSE IN THE ORIGINAL THERE IS ONLY ONE.
   0x00469ccb computes 1 - speed/realSpeedMax*8, clamps it at zero,
   floors it at flImpactBlend/200 and KEEPS THE RESULT; the blend
   weight and racer_lean_delta's speed gate are the same value read
   twice.  The port split it into two functions, so it was possible
   to floor one and not the other.

   WITH THE FLOOR ON THE BLEND WEIGHT ALONE the impact counter
   matched MOTO to the bit - 193.4000, 186.8000, 180.2000 on both,
   difference exactly zero every step - and the lean was still about
   three times MOTO's.  The RATIO is what identified it: gate without
   the floor over gate with it predicts the excess exactly.

   +------+-------+-----------+--------+-----------+
   | step | floor | MOTO deg  | ours   | predicted |
   +======+=======+===========+========+===========+
   |    5 | 0.934 |      0.36 |   1.10 |      3.03 |
   +------+-------+-----------+--------+-----------+
   |    7 | 0.868 |      0.69 |   2.10 |      3.11 |
   +------+-------+-----------+--------+-----------+
   |   11 | 0.917 |      0.33 |   2.14 |      6.70 |
   +------+-------+-----------+--------+-----------+

   against measured ratios of 3.06, 3.04 and 6.5.

   A MATCHING INPUT IS NOT A MATCHING OUTPUT.  flImpactBlend agreeing
   bit for bit proved the decay was right and said nothing about how
   many places consume it.  When one consumer is fixed and the
   symptom only shrinks, look for a second.

   VERIFIED with both floors in place [verified]:

   +------+-------+-----------+-----------+
   | step | floor | MOTO deg  | port deg  |
   +======+=======+===========+===========+
   |    4 | 0.967 |      0.11 |      0.11 |

9.18.38.  THE FORK DIVE RAN A TICK EARLY, AND OFF A STALE MIRROR

   Two faults, both structural rather than arithmetic, and the second
   would have hidden the fix for the first.

   ORDER.  s_tickOrder has UpdateGroundContact at 6, UpdateAirborne
   at 7 and FUN_004667e0 - the fork dive - at 8.  THE WALL IMPACT
   SCRUBS THE SPEED INSIDE THE GROUND CONTACT, so the original's fork
   sees that deceleration in the same tick.  race.c ran the fork some
   230 lines earlier, ahead of the integrate, the cornering, the
   ground contact AND the airborne step, so it sampled a speed the
   impact had not touched yet.

   MEASURED on a wall ride, both sides settled to a matched forkDive
   first by coasting with no input [verified]:

   +------+--------------+--------------+
   | step | MOTO fork    | port fork    |
   +======+==============+==============+

   THE MIRROR.  Moving the call is not enough on its own.  The

   NOT PART OF THIS, AND WORTH KNOWING BEFORE MEASURING: mr_place

9.18.39.  THE DOWNHILL WASHBOARD IS THE ORIGINAL'S, AND IT IS THE
          GROUND FIT MEETING A FACETED ROAD

   Reported symptom: going down a hill the bike pitches as though it
   were riding down steps, the tail visibly bouncing, on a road that
   looks smooth - and it does it on RETAIL too, worse the faster you
   go.

   THE PORT IS NOT AT FAULT.  Straight-line run down segments 100 to
   106, both sides placed alike, pitch tracks to about 0.001 the
   whole way [verified].  This is a faithful reproduction of a 1997
   artefact.

   THE ROAD IS PIECEWISE PLANAR AND THE FIT STRADDLES THE JOINTS.
   Each segment carries its own pair of ground planes; adjacent
   segments' planes are not coplanar, so the surface is C0 - heights
   agree at a joint to within a few units - and NOT C1: the gradient
   jumps.  racer_orient_grounded probes 84 units fore and aft and
   snaps each probe in ITS OWN segment (9.29.9), so for the 168 units
   either side of every joint the two probes sit on planes of
   different slope.

   COMPUTED FROM TRACK DATA ALONE, no game running, walking the
   centre line at 20-unit steps:

   +-----------+-----------+-----------------------------------+
   | along     | probes    | pitch                             |
   +===========+===========+===================================+
   |  1140     | 103/103   | -0.23403  both in, steepest       |
   +-----------+-----------+-----------------------------------+
   |  1400     | 103/103   | -0.19146  eased back              |
   +-----------+-----------+-----------------------------------+
   |  1500     | 104/103   | -0.19673  straddling, nose drops  |
   +-----------+-----------+-----------------------------------+
   |  1660     | 104/104   | -0.24967  rear catches up         |
   +-----------+-----------+-----------------------------------+

   One cycle per segment, range about 0.06 rad - 3.4 DEGREES OF
   PITCH.  It is a function of POSITION, not of speed, which is
   exactly why speed makes it worse: a segment is around 520 units,
   so at 27 m/s the bike crosses five a second and the same 3.4
   degrees becomes a 5 Hz oscillation.  At walking pace it is a slow
   nod nobody notices.

   THERE ARE TWO FACETS PER SEGMENT, not one.  The pitch moves while
   both probes are inside a single segment as well, because the
   selector plane picks between ground[0] and ground[1] and the
   diagonal between those triangles is another joint.

   NOT SPECIFIC TO THIS HILL, AND NOT THE WORST OF IT.  Gradient
   change across all 788 joints:

       median 0.0039   90th 0.0175   99th 0.0345   max 0.0423
       56 joints over 0.02 rad, none over 0.05

   The descent under test runs 0.0164 to 0.0171 at EVERY joint from
   99 to 113 - only the 90th percentile, but sustained over fourteen
   consecutive joints, which is what turns single bumps into a
   washboard.  Segments 469-515 are worse still, up to 0.0423 (2.4
   degrees) at 514-515 and 164-165.

   SO IT IS NOT AN INTENTIONAL EFFECT FOR THIS STRETCH.  It falls out
   of a planar-per-segment road sampled by a two-point fit, and it
   will appear anywhere consecutive segments change gradient.  Any
   smoothing would be a deliberate DEPARTURE from the original rather
   than a fix, and belongs in the modernization phase if it belongs
   anywhere.  [open, by choice]

   through one - the bike wheelies TILTED.  Retail brings it vertical
   whatever the bike was doing.  Retail also lets you steer in a
   wheelie but heavily damped, wide turns only, recentring when the
   input is released; the port does not damp it.

   maybe_Racer_UpdateLean READS wheelieAngle AT FOUR SITES and forks
   on it twice:

       00469e1f   MOV EAX,[ESI+0x307d] / CMP EAX,EBP / JLE 00469f13
       00469e6c   FLD [ESI+0x307d] / FSIN / FMUL 0x004d1218
       0046a193   MOV ECX,[ESI+0x307d] / TEST ECX,ECX / JLE 0046a4fe
       0046a343   FLD [ESI+0x307d]

   EBP is zero here, so both forks read "wheelieAngle > 0".  Each
   guards a block that runs ONLY during a wheelie: the first builds
   a frame from the wheel contacts and rotates it by
   sin(wheelieAngle) * 0x004d1218 - the same constant racer.c calls
   K_WHEELIE_PITCH - and the second, 0046a1a1..0046a4fe, is some 860
   bytes taking front minus rear (racer+0x1b4 - racer+0x338)
   directly.

   WHAT THOSE FORKS ARE IS THE WHEELIE PIVOT, NOT A LEAN FORMULA.
   They swing the FRONT CONTACT about the rear one by
   sin(wheelieAngle) * -pi/4 and rebuild matBikeRoot from the swung
   pair, so the pitch lives in the geometry.  The port already does
   this - racer_wheelie_swing_contact.

   THE LEAN ITSELF NEVER FORKS ON THE WHEELIE.  It is the same
   slip-angle lean either way.  What differs is WHICH FRAME its yaw
   is read from, and that is the whole bug - see 9.18.40.1.

   THE STEERING HALF IS ALREADY RIGHT: racer_steer_torque halves the
   torque on wheelieAngle != 0 (STEER_HALF at 0x004664d6).

     quotient before the blend, and the port does - but
     Anim_BlendAngleFrames clamps internally at both ends

9.18.41.1.  FOUND BY HOOKING THE BLEND: THE STANCE SATURATION SPEED
            WAS HALVED

   Anim_BlendAngleFrames has FIFTEEN call sites in UpdatePose, not
   four.  0x0045db88 - the LEA immediately before the one at
   0x0045db8f - is hookable and has all three arguments already
   pushed, so ESP+0, +4 and +8 give (a, b, t) directly.

   THE STATIC READ HAD PICKED THE WRONG PATH INTO IT.  Reading
   0x0045db6f..0x0045db8f cold says the pushes are STATIC then
   STATIC_HAUT; the hook says arg0 is 0x51d678, which is index 4,
   STATIC_PIED.  Several paths converge on that call and the
   disassembly around it belongs to only one of them.  Below the
   speed gate it is STATIC_PIED -> STATIC_HAUT; above it, arg0 reads
   0x51d584 and arg1 0x51d2a8, which is STATIC_HAUT -> STATIC as
   expected.

   THE WEIGHT IS 2.5x TOO LARGE, at every sample [verified]:

9.18.41.2.  AND THE FLAG BEHIND IT IS PersoAnim, NOT "LOCAL PLAYER"

   The stance constant looked swapped.  It was not - the SELECTOR was
   wrong, and it selects four other things too.

   0x0045d864 loads ECX 0xf0, takes the flag into EDI at 0x0045d869
   and overwrites ECX with 0x78 only when the flag is non-zero.  The
   flag comes from the CALLER: maybe_Racer_UpdateSkeleton at
   0x00467bbe pushes [EAX*4 + 0x509398] with EAX = 73*bikeIndex -
   g_bikeCarac[bikeIndex].PersoAnim, read by the same expression the
   ground fit uses (9.29.9).

   THE PORT PASSED isPlayer.  Every SPEED bike reads PersoAnim 0 and
   isPlayer is 1 for the rider, so on a road track the port ran FOUR
   branches the original leaves off.  ON MOTOCROSS THE ORIGINAL TAKES
   THEM TOO, because those eight read 1 - so the port's error there
   is not that it runs them but that it runs them for the AI as well:

   +----------+-----------------------------+------------------------+
   | branch   | original, PersoAnim 0       | port, isPlayer 1       |
   +==========+=============================+========================+
   | stance   | sat (0xf0/2)/3.6 = 120 km/h | (0x78/2)/3.6 = 60      |
   +----------+-----------------------------+------------------------+
   | lean     | the SPEED curve             | the angle curve        |
   +----------+-----------------------------+------------------------+
   | pitch    | skipped entirely            | JUMP / DESCENT blended |
   +----------+-----------------------------+------------------------+
   | pre-race | never runs                  | runs                   |
   | stunt    |                             |                        |
   +----------+-----------------------------+------------------------+

   MEASURED [verified]: hooking 0x0045df8f, the angle path, took ZERO
   hits through a full turn while 0x0045df40, the speed path, took
   eight with arg0 0x51d860 (SLIDE_R) and arg1 0x51d490 (LEAN_R).
   EDI reads 0 in every register dump from every hook placed in this
   function.

   THE PITCH ROW IS THE DOWNHILL REPORT.  0x0045db94 is
   TEST EDI,EDI / JZ 0x0045dc44 and 0x0045dc44 is past the whole
   pitch block, so the original never blends JUMP or DESCENT for a
   PersoAnim-0 bike.  The port did, which is the rider standing up on
   the pegs going downhill.

   THE FIX IS THE ARGUMENT, NOT THE BRANCHES.  Flipping the tests one
   at a time was the first attempt and it corrected three sites while
   leaving pre-race and stunt wrong, because "the flag is the inverse
   of isLocal" is only true while PersoAnim happens to be 0.  Passing
   bc->persoAnim is right for every bike including one that sets it.

   PersoAnim HAS NOW GATED THREE SEPARATE THINGS - the ground fit's
   per-probe segment search (9.29.9), the atan lean in UpdateLean
   (9.18.40), and all five of these.  It reads 0 on every shipped
   bike, so ANY branch it guards is dead code that a port will
   silently run if it substitutes something else.  Check the caller
   before naming a flag.

9.20.2.  The transcription check, and the bug it found

   tools/check_physics.py mirrors src/physics.c value for value and
   runs it against the captures in 9.17, 9.18 and 9.19. It executes
   nothing from build/ - rule 9 - and it is not a unit test of the C
   so much as a check that the C and the measurements still agree.

   IT FAILED ON ITS FIRST RUN, and usefully. Grip and drag passed;
   every engine-gain prediction came out high by 1.1000 exactly.

   o A CLEAN RATIO NAMES ITS OWN CAUSE. 1.1 is the multiplier
     Engine_TorqueAt applies when g_codeCheckPassed is set AND
     racer+0x88 is zero (9.11), and physics.h had told callers to
     pass it on the reasoning that a legitimate copy always earns
     it.

   o READING BOTH TERMS OUT OF THE LIVE GAME SETTLED IT.
     g_codeCheckPassed at 0x004dc1f8 reads 1 - the protection is
     satisfied - but racer+0x88 reads 1 as well, and the condition
     needs it ZERO. SO THE MULTIPLIER IS NOT APPLIED IN NORMAL PLAY,
     and the thirteen captured acceleration frames confirm it: all
     of them match the curve without it. [verified]

   o WHICH REFRAMES THE "PROTECTION BONUS". 9.11 reads the 1.1 as
     something a cracked copy loses. It is that, but it is ALSO
     gated on a second flag that was set every time anybody looked,
     so the branch a legitimate copy actually takes is the same one.
     A port that passes the bonus accelerates ten per cent harder
     than the game does.

   o THE CHECK ONLY CAUGHT IT BECAUSE THE FLAG WAS A PARAMETER.
     Folding the 1.1 into the curve - the tidier-looking option -
     would have hidden a flat ten per cent inside the one function
     whose output had been verified, and the check would have gone
     on passing grip and drag while silently failing here.

9.20.3.  racer+0x88 is not what its neighbours suggest

   It sits in the middle of the gearbox block and the obvious
   reading is "a shift is in progress":

       +0x7c  gear            read 1
       +0x80  shift timer     read 0
       +0x84  shift state     read 0 - IDLE
       +0x88  the gate        read 1
       +0x8c  pending down    read 0

   o SO IT IS NOT A SHIFT FLAG. The bike was stationary in first
     gear with the shift state idle and the gate still set. What
     writes it remains unknown, and it now matters more than it did:
     it is the difference between the engine the game has and one
     ten per cent stronger. [open]

9.20.4.  What the next increment is

   o THE BRAKE PATH, and it needs measuring before it is written.
     Frein clamped to [10, 140] scaled by the brake-hold counter is
     an outline, not a model, and the same hook technique that
     settled acceleration would settle it in one run.

   o THE BODY-SPACE LONGITUDINAL VELOCITY. physics_tick_drivetrain
     lerps road speed toward d->speed, which is right on the

9.25.  THE SCAFFOLDING TRANSCRIBED - src/racetick.c

   The control-flow spine physics.c hangs off: the clock, the
   per-racer loop and the human/AI fork. Unlike the drivetrain it
   needed no measurement - it is branches, a countdown and a loop -
   so this section is about SCOPE and about the details that are easy
   to get subtly wrong, not about numbers.

9.25.1.  What was written, and what was deliberately not

   THE SOLO RACE TICK, and nothing else. Named exclusions, each
   flagged at the point it belongs in the source:

   o THE DirectPlay START HANDSHAKE in maybe_Race_TickClock - mark
     our net slot 4, wait for every peer to reach 4, ping each,
     qsort by latency, busy-wait on timeGetTime so all machines
     leave the grid together.

   o THE TIME-ATTACK AND GHOST BRANCH in Race_TickAllRacers. Worth
     noting because of what it implies: it ticks racer 1, plays the
     ghost back into slot 2 and RETURNS - SO NO AI RUNS IN TIME
     ATTACK AT ALL. The main loop is not entered.

   o THE REMOTE-PEER PATH for slots somebody else owns.

   o ALL OF maybe_Game_Tick's OUTER SHELL - the menu fork, the
     vtable dispatch to collision and render, the font reloads, the
     race-finish jingles. That is a game-loop interface rather than
     a race tick and belongs with whatever drives the port's main
     loop.

9.25.2.  Two timers in one variable

   g_raceTimer (0x004dc0a8) counts down by the frame delta and is the
   GRID COUNTDOWN first and the RACE TIME LIMIT afterwards. Nothing
   in the variable says which; g_bPreRace disambiguates it, and the
   changeover is the single moment the timer goes negative while
   still on the grid.

   o SO MR_RaceClock KEEPS THE FLAG BESIDE THE COUNTER rather than
     leaving a caller to remember. A reader who assumes the variable
     means one thing is wrong for the other phase.

   o AND THE TEST IS < 0, NOT == 0. The timer is meant to go
     negative; the limit-expired branch then allows a further 500
     units of grace before it writes raceState 3.

9.25.3.  The order is not the obvious one

       1  decrement the timer
       2  advance every eligible racer's clock, but only if
          !preRace
       3  test the transition

   o SO THE FRAME THE GRID CLEARS ADVANCES NO LAP TIME, because
     preRace was still set when step 2 ran and is only cleared in
     step 3. Reordering these to "check first, then tick" loses a
     frame off the first lap.

   o THE DECREMENT IS UNCONDITIONAL IN SOLO. The original guards it
     with a four-term disjunction whose first term is
     g_nSoloMode != 0; the rest exists to hold every machine on the
     grid until the handshake completes.

   o AND THE WHOLE FUNCTION IS GATED on DAT_004dc0ac, which wraps
     the entire body. What writes it is unread, so racetick.c makes
     it a field rather than assuming it set. [open]

9.25.4.  The three loops do not agree on where to start

   +----------------------------+----------------+------------------------------------------+
   | Loop                       | Walks          | In                                       |
   +============================+================+==========================================+
   | the lap clocks             | racers 0..24   | maybe_Race_TickClock, 25 entries         |
   +----------------------------+----------------+------------------------------------------+
   | the start reset            | racers 0..24   | maybe_Race_TickClock, 25 entries         |
   +----------------------------+----------------+------------------------------------------+
   | the raceState assign       | racers 0..N-1  | g_racerCount entries, from 0             |
   +----------------------------+----------------+------------------------------------------+
   | THE PER-RACER TICK         | racers 1..24   | Race_TickAllRacers, 24 entries           |
   +----------------------------+----------------+------------------------------------------+

   o THE CLOCK WALKS FROM 0 AND THE TICK WALKS FROM 1. Slot 0 is
     unused so it costs only a wasted iteration on an inactive
     record - but a port that tidies all four to the same index has
     changed one of them, and the one it would change is the one
     that matters.

   o THE raceState ASSIGNMENT DOES NOT TEST active AT ALL, unlike
     the two loops above it in the same function.

9.25.6.  One deliberate divergence

   THE ORIGINAL INDEXES g_lapTimes BY THE RAW 16-BIT lapCounter with
   no bounds test, so a ninth lap would write into the next racer's
   row. racetick.c guards it and says so in place.

   o THAT IS A DIVERGENCE AND IT IS FLAGGED AS ONE. Rule 5 says
     match the original including the ugly parts, but reproducing an
     out-of-bounds write reproduces a crash rather than a behaviour,
     and the
     original is protected by lap counts that never reach eight
     rather than by the code. If a race format ever allows nine
     laps, the original has a bug and we do not. [deliberate]

9.27.  DRIVE AND STEERING TRANSCRIBED, and an ORIGIN convention

   Two more of the seventeen calls in 9.26.2, and they are the two
   that write the fields physics.c already consumes. Both went into
   src/racer.c rather than physics.c, deliberately - see 9.27.4.

9.27.1.  racer_apply_drive closes the input-to-physics loop

   maybe_Racer_ApplyDrive (0x00465da0) turns this tick's buttons into
   the two numbers the drivetrain branches on, and it settles two
   things that were previously read from the other end.

   o THE DIRECTION IT BUILDS IS THE SLOPE TERM'S dirY.
     matOrientation[9..11] + [6..8], normalised, is handed straight
     to the drivetrain, and its Y component is what 9.22 measured as
     -0.0257 and -0.2632 rolling down a hill. The two halves of that
     now meet.

   o AND ITS NUMERATOR IS THE DRIVETRAIN'S EBP. 9.21.4 read the
     brake gate as "EBP < 0 brakes, 0 coasts, > 0 accelerates" from
     inside the drivetrain; this is the caller that produces it, and
     it agrees.

   THE INPUT OVERRIDES ARE GAMEPLAY, not physics, and are worth
   listing because none of them is obvious from the drivetrain side:

   o A HUMAN WHO HAS FINISHED OR TIMED OUT GETS buttons = 2. Not
     "input ignored" - THE BRAKE IS HELD. raceState outside {0,1}
     forces it.
   o EVERYONE BRAKES ON THE GRID - preRace sets the brake bit for
     every racer.
   o A WHEELIE IMPLIES THROTTLE. If wheelieAngle is non-zero the
     accelerate bit is forced on, because the bike cannot be on its
     back wheel and coasting.
   o BRAKING CANCELS THE WHEELIE, clearing both stuntOrOverspeed and
     stuntHeld. This is the THIRD place that latch is enforced.

   o THE ANALOG AXIS IS NOT SYMMETRIC and this is read, not inferred:
     n = 0x8000 - axis0, then n >= 0 passes through while n < 0 is
     DIVIDED BY 2000 (the IDIV at 0x00465ef9). A full deflection one
     way is worth about 0.0005 of the digital unit. Whether that is
     deliberate or a defect is open, but it is transcribed as-is.
     [open]

9.27.2.  racer_apply_steering, and three constants that are ratios

   maybe_Racer_ApplySteering (0x00466240). Three paths - analog,
   digital and centring - chosen by whether axis1 is centred. axis1
   is the STEERING axis; axis0 is throttle.

   +----------------+--------------------+----------------------------------------------+
   | Address        | Value              | What it really is                            |
   +================+====================+==============================================+
   | 0x004d10c0     | 0.5555556          | 2/3.6 EXACTLY - the minimum speed to steer   |
   |                |                    | is 2 km/h                                    |
   +----------------+--------------------+----------------------------------------------+
   | 0x004d10c8     | 3.0517578e-05      | 1/0x8000 - normalises the axis offset to +-1 |
   +----------------+--------------------+----------------------------------------------+
   | 0x004d10d0     | 0.1666667          | 1/6 - scales the self-centring               |
   +----------------+--------------------+----------------------------------------------+
   | 0x004d10d8     | 0.004              | the AI's flat steering-torque factor         |
   +----------------+--------------------+----------------------------------------------+
   | 0x004d10e0     | 1.6                | the upper clamp on the steering grip term    |
   +----------------+--------------------+----------------------------------------------+

   o YOU CANNOT STEER BELOW 2 km/h, and the threshold is 2/3.6
     exactly. It gates all three paths: analog zeroes its own input
     and both digital paths fall through to centring.

   o THE DIGITAL PATH HAS NO COAST-OVER. If the bars are already
     over the other way the angle is SNAPPED to zero rather than
     driven through it, so every direction change restarts from
     centre.

   o THREE OF THE FIVE CONSTANTS ARE EXACT RATIOS - 2/3.6, 1/0x8000
     and 1/6 - rather than tuned numbers. Worth noticing before
     anyone rounds them.

9.27.3.  Two open questions closed on the way past

   o racer+0x30c1 IS crashLatch, and that closes physics.h's note
     about maybe_Racer_UpdateWheelie's mystery reset path. THE
     WHEELIE RESET IS THE CRASH RESET: a crash clears the angle, the
     timer, the request (stuntOrOverspeed, +0x2e85) and the button
     latch (stuntHeld, +0x31f9) together. The full request chain is
     now readable end to end - button sets stuntHeld,
     maybe_Racer_Tick copies it to stuntOrOverspeed for a human in
     solo, UpdateWheelie ramps on that, ApplyDrive clears both on
     the brake.

   o AND THE ApplySteering PLATE COMMENT STILL CARRIED THE STALE
     turnBrakeFlag CLAIM - "written ONLY for a human on MotoType 1"
     and "what consumes it is not read yet". 9.21.4 corrected both
     in docs a while ago and the DB was not updated with it. It is
     now. That is exactly the divergence CLAUDE.md rule 5b exists to
     prevent, and it survived because the correction was written
     where it was FOUND rather than everywhere it was RECORDED.

9.27.4.  Where the code went, and why

   BOTH FUNCTIONS WENT INTO racer.c, NOT physics.c, even though
   their output feeds the drivetrain. physics.c is the module held
   to measured numbers by tools/check_physics.py; letting raceState,
   preRace and button words into it would put gameplay branching
   inside the one file whose correctness is checkable arithmetic.

   o SO THE BOUNDARY IS: racer.c turns input and race state into
     numbers, physics.c turns numbers into speeds. MR_DriveDemand is
     the handoff.

9.27.5.  Every transcribed function now names its original

   A greppable ORIGIN tag sits above each one:

       /* ORIGIN  Engine_TorqueAt @ 0x0048ee30 */
       /* ORIGIN  maybe_Physics_TickDrivetrain @ 0x0048ef30,
        *         at 0x0048f962 */
       /* ORIGIN  NONE - ours */

   o THE ADDRESS IS ALWAYS GIVEN, even when the names match, so a
     rename in the DB cannot orphan the tag.

   o THE ENCLOSING FUNCTION COMES FIRST when the C is a SLICE of a
     bigger original. Six of physics.c's functions are pieces of one
     4284-byte function and the tag says which piece.

   o "NONE - ours" IS EXPLICIT so that an untagged function reads as
     an oversight rather than as ours by default.

   o 31 FUNCTIONS TAGGED across physics.c, race.c and racer.c, and
     audited to confirm none landed on a forward declaration. The
     count is checkable: grep -c "^/\* ORIGIN" src/*.c against the
     function count.

9.32.  THE BIKE COULD NOT PULL AWAY - two omissions, both flagged

   The drive path was wired up and the bike steered but would not
   move. Both causes were pieces of maybe_Physics_TickDrivetrain that
   9.20 had recorded as [open] and written around, and together they
   made a standstill self-sustaining.

9.32.1.  Zero engine speed makes zero torque

   At rest the gearbox reports engineSpeed 0, so rpm 0, so power 0.
   THE ORIGINAL BREAKS THAT CIRCLE WITH AN IDLE FLOOR at 0x0048f388:

       idle = moteurVitesseMax / regimeMax * 1200
       if (engineSpeed < idle) engineSpeed = idle

   o 9.17.7.4 HAD ALREADY MEASURED THIS - engineSpeed pinned at
     exactly 0.7000 for the first two frames off the line, and 0.7 is
     what the expression gives for the road bike. The observation was
     recorded and the code was never written.

   o It is applied only when accelerating and on the ground, which
     physics_tick_drivetrain is by construction.

9.32.2.  And a zero divisor skipped the gain entirely

   9.20 wrote `if (gearboxRoadSpeed > 0) { ... }` around the engine
   term, with a comment saying the original's low-speed guard was
   unread and that this was NOT it. IT IS NOW READ, at 0x0048f403:

       if (roadSpeed > 0.0001)  a = power / roadSpeed
       else                     a = power * 10000.0

   o THE MULTIPLIER IS EXACTLY 1/THRESHOLD. So it is "clamp the
     divisor to 0.0001", written as a multiply to dodge the
     division - not a separate low-speed model, just an
     unconditional way to write one.

   o THE STAND-IN WAS WORSE THAN THE THING IT STOOD IN FOR. Guarding
     with `> 0` looks safer than dividing, and it silently removed
     the only branch that can start the bike. A guard that skips work
     is not a neutral placeholder.

9.32.3.  The acceleration cap is the launch limiter

   With the low-speed branch in place the raw gain at a standstill is
   power times ten thousand - enormous. What actually decides how hard
   a bike leaves the line is the cap:

       gain = min(gain, dtMs * 0.03)

   o 9.17.1 RECORDED THIS AS "NEVER OBSERVED BINDING" and that was
     true of every capture taken - all of them were of a bike ALREADY
     MOVING, because the hooks were armed mid-race to avoid burning
     hits on the countdown (9.17.6). IT BINDS AT EXACTLY THE MOMENT
     NOBODY MEASURED.

   o SO A NEGATIVE OBSERVATION WAS AGAIN A STATEMENT ABOUT THE
     METHOD, not about the code - the third time in this subsystem
     after 9.18.2 and 9.19.3.

9.32.4.  Constants

   The 4 constants are #defined with their addresses in
   src/physics.c.

9.32.5.  What now runs

   src/physics.c applies the idle floor and the real low-speed guard;
   tools/check_physics.py gains four launch cases, including that the
   gain from a standstill is positive AND equal to the cap, and that
   the rolling numbers are unchanged.

   o THE ROLLING CAPTURES STILL MATCH, which is the important part:
     both fixes only alter behaviour below road speed 0.0001 or
     below idle rpm, and every measured frame was above both. The
     model that was verified is untouched.

9.46.10.  THE PLAYER COULD NEVER LEAVE THE GROUND

   Driving straight off the dirt arena's start gate, throttle only,
   70 steps:

       player airborne frames   MOTO 26   ours 0
       AI airborne samples      MOTO 218  ours 173

   MOTO's rider took three jumps - steps 4-9, 40-48 and 52-62, with
   flAirTime running to 57 - and ours took none.  THE AI JUMPED ON
   BOTH SIDES, which is what narrows it: the AI tick does its own
   ground snap inside racer_tick_ai and never comes through the
   player's path.

   THE CAUSE IS ONE MISSING GUARD.  race_tick_physics ends with a
   snap-to-terrain block that assigned position.y and onGround = 1
   UNCONDITIONALLY, after racer_update_ground_contact had already
   decided the question and written onGround 0 on its airborne
   branch.  Every airborne frame was undone at the end of the tick
   that produced it.

   racer_is_airborne - the test at 0x00469427 - had NO CALLER AT ALL,
   which is the tell that should have been noticed earlier: a
   transcribed, documented function that nothing reaches is either
   dead in the original or unwired in the port, and it is worth
   knowing which.  Here it was reached through
   racer_update_ground_contact and its answer was then thrown away.

   THE BLOCK'S OWN COMMENT SAID SO: "a bike ON the ground has no
   independent height, and only the airborne branch does".  The guard
   is what makes that sentence true.

   THAT GUARD WAS NECESSARY AND NOT SUFFICIENT.  With it in, the
   player still took zero jumps, because the MOVER was pressing the
   bike down every frame: race_tick_physics called racer_integrate
   with a literal 0 for the lift flag, and that arm adds a constant
   downward step to the displacement unconditionally.  position.y can
   then never rise the 32 units the airborne test needs.

   THE LIFT GATE IS PersoAnim AND A STRAIGHT, read off
   0x0045d748..0x0045d762:

       EBX = 0
       if (PersoAnim)                             0x0045d74a
           if (!Racer_AiIsCorner(this, seg, 8))   0x0045d759
               EBX = 1                            0x0045d762
       ModelInstance_Integrate(..., EBX)          0x0045d770

   A ROAD BIKE IS NEVER ALLOWED TO LIFT, and neither is a dirt bike
   in a corner - only a dirt bike on a straight.  THAT IS WHY THE
   JUMPS EXIST ON THE DIRT ARENA AND NOWHERE ELSE, and it is a
   design decision rather than a property of the terrain: the same
   crest under a road bike does nothing.

   AND IT IS THE SAME CORNER TEST THE AI USES.  Racer_AiIsCorner was
   imported for maybe_Racer_TickAI; maybe_Racer_Tick calls it for the
   PLAYER with the same eight-segment look-ahead.  Importing the AI
   is what put the function in reach of this fix.

   BOTH HALVES IN, THE BIKE FLIES.  Placed on the dirt arena's first
   ramp at 33 m/s in gear 4, stepped frame by frame:

       n |  MOTO      y     grnd |  ours      y     grnd
       0 |       -3.42     0    |       -3.42     0
       1 |      -20.36     0    |      -20.85     0
       2 |      -50.51     0    |      -52.91     0
       3 |      -93.61     0    |      -99.91     0
       4 |     -149.37     0    |     -161.55     0
       5 |     -217.54     0    |     -265.24     1

   THE ARC AGREES TO A COUPLE OF UNITS for the first four frames, so
   the launch, the gravity and the airborne integrator are all right.

   TWO THINGS ARE NOT, and both are in what happens next:

     o OURS LANDS A FRAME EARLY, at step 5 where MOTO is still up.
     o MOTO RE-LAUNCHES OFF THE NEXT CREST at step 7 and ours does
       not, so the run is 13 airborne frames against 5.

   THE WHOLE REMAINING DIFFERENCE IS THE SPEED THROUGH THE FLIGHT:

       MOTO  32.28 -> 31.68 -> 31.18 -> 30.79 -> 30.49 -> 30.29
       ours  33.48 -> 35.23 -> 36.96 -> 38.34 -> 39.33 -> 40.00

   MOTO LOSES SPEED IN THE AIR AND OURS GAINS IT.  The horizontal
   step per frame goes 267 -> 240 on MOTO and 293 -> 348 on ours, so
   we overshoot the ramp, meet the descending terrain early, land a
   frame sooner and are in the wrong place for the second crest.

   OUR AIRBORNE ARM IS NOT THE BUG.  0x0048d790 in
   ModelInstance_Integrate really does FSQRT the velocity and store it
   into instance+0x100, so speed = |v| is the original's own
   behaviour and a projectile's magnitude genuinely grows as it
   falls.  MOTO's speed falls anyway because the 1.6 AIRBORNE DRAG is
   applied afterwards and outweighs it - 0.3 on the ground against
   1.6 in the air.

   IN THIS PORT THE DRAG LANDS ON THE DRIVETRAIN MIRROR and reaches
   the racer through r->speed = s_race_drive.speed at the end of the
   tick.  THAT COPY WORKS: read together, r->speed and
   s_race_drive.speed are the same number to six figures, 40.430 and
   40.430, with gearboxRoadSpeed agreeing at 40.353.  So the |v| the
   airborne arm computes is DISCARDED every frame and what survives
   is the drivetrain's own value - which means the growth is the
   DRIVETRAIN accelerating the bike in mid-air.

   THE GROUND PLANES ARE FINE, which kills the first theory.  Read
   out of the loaded dirt arena, ground[0] normals are (0, 0.9462,
   -0.3236) on segment 18 and (0, 0.9853, -0.1711) on 20, 22 and 40,
   so trk_segment_has_surface is true and nothing is gated off.

   THE DRAG IS TOO SMALL TO BE THE WHOLE STORY EITHER.  With
   motoMasse 150 the airborne drag is only 0.383 a frame at 33 m/s
   against 0.072 on the ground, while the observed GAIN is about 1.7
   a frame - so roughly 2.1 a frame of torque is arriving that should
   not be.

   physics_drivetrain_frame HAS THE RIGHT SHAPE FOR IT: at
   physics.c:870 airborne is (d->airTime != 0.0f), and the airborne
   arm free-revs the engine instead of pushing gearboxRoadSpeed into
   the speed.  d->airTime reads 5.184 and upward during the flight,
   so that arm ought to be taken.  It evidently is not, or not
   everywhere.

   HOOKED, AND IT NARROWS TO ONE BLOCK.  Logging d->airTime and
   d->speed on entry to physics_drivetrain_frame across a flight:

       airTime   0  2.592  5.184  7.776 10.368 ... 28.512   0
       speed    33  32.93  33.48  34.36  35.23 ...  39.80  40

   THE airborne FLAG IS CORRECT - airTime is non-zero from the second
   frame - and the first airborne frame even LOSES speed, 33 to
   32.93, which is the drag working.  From the third it gains about
   0.87 a frame.

   THE MIRROR MACHINERY IS INNOCENT.  Sampled DURING the flight, not
   after, r->speed and s_race_drive.speed agree to six figures every
   frame, and gearboxRoadSpeed tracks both.  So the |v| the airborne
   arm of racer_integrate computes is discarded as intended, and the
   growth is inside the drivetrain.

   IT IS THE AIRBORNE ARM AT physics.c:889-899:

       if (skipSync || airborne) {
           engineSpeed *= dtMs * K_COAST_DECAY + 1.0
           clamps
           if (airborne)
               gearboxRoadSpeed = physics_engine_to_speed(
                       c, gear, &engineSpeed, gearboxRoadSpeed)
       }

   gearboxRoadSpeed IS RECOMPUTED FROM THE ENGINE THROUGH THE GEAR on
   every airborne frame, and it is what speed then follows.  Measured
   across the same flight, engineSpeed moves only 6.16 to 6.21 - well
   under one per cent - while gearboxRoadSpeed goes 33.47 to 40.35,
   twenty per cent.  A ratio conversion cannot do that from a
   near-constant input unless the GEAR it is converting through is
   changing underneath it.

   THE TWO GEAR FIELDS AGREE, so that was not it: across the flight
   s_race_gearbox.gear and r->gear both read 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 5.

   THE ENGINE IS BEING DRIVEN IN MID-AIR.  Measured on the same
   flight, engineSpeed goes 6.16 -> 6.48 -> 6.79 while airborne -
   RISING - and the coast arm that would decay it is only reached
   when the throttle asks for less than there is:

       if (num >= 0) {
           target = num/den * moteurVitesseMax
           if (target < engineSpeed || num < 1) {
               if (skipSync || airborne)  ...coast, -2 per cent...
           } else {
               ...THE DRIVE...
           }

   WITH THE GAS HELD, target IS ABOVE engineSpeed AND THE DRIVE RUNS
   however high the bike is.  physics_engine_to_speed then converts
   the revved engine into a climbing gearboxRoadSpeed, and speed
   follows it.  That is the whole 0.87 a frame.

   THE FLAG ITSELF IS RIGHT.  0x0048ef63..0x0048ef75 computes it the
   same way ours does - TEST EAX,0x7fffffff then SETNZ - the only
   difference being that the original masks the sign bit, so its
   airborne is false for -0.0 where ours is true.  Worth matching,
   but not the bug.

   THE HOLE, READ.  0x0048ef60..0x0048f1a0 is not two speed clamps -
   it is the entry clamps AND THE WHOLE AIRBORNE ARM:

       0048efaf  if (speed > realSpeedMax) speed = realSpeedMax
       0048efd2  airTime & 0x7fffffff non-zero -> jump to 0x0048f0fb,
                 skipping every bit of the grounded velocity work

       0048f0fb  THE AIRBORNE ARM
                 clamp gearboxRoadSpeed to +-realSpeedMax
                 if (EDI == 0)
                     engineSpeed = SpeedToEngine(&gearboxRoadSpeed,
                                                 gear, engineSpeed)

   *** IT RUNS THE CONVERSION THE OTHER WAY FROM OURS. *** In the air
   the ENGINE FOLLOWS THE ROAD SPEED - maybe_Gearbox_SpeedToEngine at
   0x0048deb0 - so the road speed is only clamped and the bike
   coasts, losing the 1.6 drag and nothing else.  Our airborne arm
   calls physics_engine_to_speed, the INVERSE, so the road speed
   follows the engine instead.

   AND IT SITS BEFORE THE THROTTLE TEST.  The original handles
   airborne at the top of maybe_Physics_TickDrivetrain, independent
   of what the rider is asking for; ours is buried inside the coast
   arm, which holding the gas never reaches.  Those two together are
   the whole defect: the drive revs the engine in mid-air and the
   road speed is then computed from it.

   TRANSCRIBED.  physics_drivetrain_frame now carries the entry
   clamps and the airborne arm at the top, before the throttle test,
   in the original's order:

       if (speed > realSpeedMax) speed = realSpeedMax
       if (airborne) {
           clamp gearboxRoadSpeed to +-realSpeedMax
           engineSpeed = physics_speed_to_engine(&gearboxRoadSpeed,
                                                 gear, engineSpeed)
       }

   IT DOES NOT RETURN.  0x0048f14e falls into 0x0048f154 and the
   throttle logic still runs; the engine it revs is simply
   overwritten at the top of the next frame.  Written as the original
   has it rather than as an early exit.

   EDI AT 0x0048f137 IS STILL UNIDENTIFIED.  It gates the conversion
   and has the shape of the clutch or disengaged; the port passes
   `disengaged`, which is the flag it already carries for that gate
   and which physics_speed_to_engine takes anyway.  [open - what EDI
   is]

   THE COAST ARM'S OWN engine_to_speed AT physics.c:896 IS LEFT
   ALONE.  It is the inverse conversion and it is reached only off
   the throttle, which is a different case from this one; removing it
   on suspicion would be a second change riding on an untested
   first.

   THE TRANSCRIPTION CHANGED NOTHING - 33.48 -> 40.43 again, to the
   decimal.  Bracketing the drivetrain says why.  Logging on entry to
   physics_drivetrain_frame and again on entry to
   physics_drivetrain_tail:

       FRM  air= 5.184  speed=33.4838  gbrs=33.474
       TAIL air= 5.184  speed=33.4838  gbrs=34.7524
       FRM  air= 7.776  speed=34.3624  gbrs=34.324

   speed IS UNTOUCHED BY THE FRAME and rises between the TAIL and the
   next frame.  THE GROWTH IS IN THE TAIL, not in the entry block and
   not in the drive.

   AND MOTO SHOWS WHAT THE AIR IS SUPPOSED TO LOOK LIKE.  Gas held,
   through a jump:

       n | grnd | engineSpeed | gearboxRoadSpeed | speed | airTime
       0 |   0  |      0.3168 |           1.7424 | 32.28 | 41.47
       1 |   0  |      0.6336 |           3.4848 | 31.68 | 46.66
       2 |   0  |      0.9504 |           5.2272 | 31.18 | 51.84
       5 |   0  |      1.9008 |          10.4544 | 30.29 | 67.39
       6 |   1  |      5.4545 |          30.0000 | 30.00 |  0.00

   IN THE AIR gearboxRoadSpeed IS NOT THE BIKE'S SPEED.  It is a free
   ramp - exactly +1.7424 a frame, 5.5 times the engine's +0.3168,
   which is the gear ratio - running up from near zero while the real
   speed DECAYS on drag alone.  The two are completely decoupled, and
   on touchdown gbrs snaps to the speed (30.0000) and the engine to
   5.4545.

   THE TAIL'S LERP IS NOW GATED ON THE GROUND, and that gate is read
   off MOTO rather than off the disassembly: in the air the two
   quantities are decoupled, so a lerp that pulls gearboxRoadSpeed
   toward the longitudinal velocity has no business running.

   BUT IT IS NECESSARY, NOT SUFFICIENT, and the direction of that
   lerp is worth stating because it was mis-stated once already:
   it writes gearboxRoadSpeed FROM the speed, not the other way.
   So it explains why our gbrs shadowed the speed instead of
   ramping, and it does NOT explain the speed itself climbing.

   AND THE LAST COUPLING IS FOUND.  Hooking drive_load to see what
   the racer carries into each tick:

       r->speed   r->gbrs    r->eng
        32.9349   32.9349   6.00182
        33.4838   33.8780   6.16022
        34.3624   34.7492   6.31862
        35.2306   35.6204   6.47702
        36.0979   36.4916   6.63542

   THE ENGINE RAMPS LINEARLY at +0.1584 a frame, gearboxRoadSpeed
   follows it through the ratio, AND speed FOLLOWS gbrs one frame
   behind.  MOTO does the first two and NOT the third: its engine
   ramps +0.3168, its gbrs +1.7424, and its speed decays on drag.

   SO THE DEFECT IS NOT THE RAMP - the free-rev is correct - IT IS
   THAT OUR speed IS STILL TIED TO IT.  Every coupling in
   physics_drivetrain_frame and physics_drivetrain_tail is now gated
   on the ground.  The one that is not is INSIDE the drive branch:
   physics_tick_drivetrain, the accelerate-path slice, which its own
   comment says "applies its own drag and its own coupling" and which
   takes no airborne flag at all.  With the gas held that branch
   runs, and its coupling pulls speed onto the ramp.

   DONE.  physics_tick_drivetrain takes an `airborne` flag and skips
   BOTH halves of the coupling under it:

       step 5   speed = gearboxRoadSpeed          grounded only
       step 6   gearboxRoadSpeed += (speed - gbrs) * grip
                                                  grounded only

   The torque, the rev and the road-speed ramp still run, which is
   what keeps MOTO's +0.3168 engine and +1.7424 gbrs in the air; only
   the two lines that tie the bike's real speed to that ramp are
   suppressed.

   check_physics.py PASSES, 0 failures.  Every capture behind that
   harness is a GROUNDED frame, so its mirror models the airborne == 0
   path and the gate does not change what it verifies - noted in its
   header so a future jump capture does not silently skip it.

   AND IT MADE NO DIFFERENCE EITHER.  Re-measured, ours still runs
   33.48 -> 42.50 through the flight.  THE TELL IS gearboxRoadSpeed:

       MOTO  gbrs   1.74   3.48   5.23   6.97   8.71  10.45
       ours  gbrs  33.88  35.62  37.36  39.26  41.31  43.37

   MOTO'S gbrs RESTARTS FROM NEAR ZERO AT TAKEOFF and ramps; ours
   carries the pre-jump value and keeps tracking the speed.  So the
   gate is either not firing or not the whole coupling - and the
   larger thing it exposes is that SOMETHING ZEROES THE ENGINE AND
   THE ROAD SPEED WHEN THE BIKE LEAVES THE GROUND, which this port
   does not do at all.  MOTO's engine is ~0.3 on the first airborne
   frame against ours at ~6.0.

   THAT READING WAS WRONG AND IS WITHDRAWN.  Nothing resets the
   engine on takeoff.  A watchpoint on racer1+0xec through a MOTO
   flight shows the engine ramping 6.08, 6.40, 6.72, 7.03, 7.35 -
   +0.3168 a frame - and gearboxRoadSpeed with it, 33.45 to 40.42,
   exactly like ours.  The "1.74, 3.48, 5.23" ramp recorded above was
   a different run that happened to take off with the engine near
   zero; comparing two runs at different points of the same track is
   what made it look like a reset.

   SO THE COUPLING DIAGNOSIS STANDS: gbrs climbs on both sides and
   only the SPEED differs.

   AND A WATCHPOINT NAMES EVERY WRITER OF IT.  racer1+0x124 through
   the same flight has exactly TWO:

       0048d798   ModelInstance_Integrate, the speed = |v| store
       004900a0   maybe_Physics_TickDrivetrain, the drag

   NOTHING ELSE TOUCHES THE SPEED IN THE AIR.  No coupling from
   gearboxRoadSpeed, which is what our physics_tick_drivetrain step 5
   was doing before the gate.  MOTO's speed then decays 33.97, 32.75,
   31.67, 30.70, 29.87 - about 1.1 a frame, well above the 0.383 the
   drag alone accounts for, so the |v| store must be REDUCING it too.

   THE INTEGRATOR IS NOT THE PROBLEM EITHER, and the arithmetic says
   so on both sides.  Predicting the next speed from the frame's own
   moveDir and the gravity step - sqrt of (speed*md + (0, -dt*G, 0))
   with dt 0.033 and G 24.525, the constants being identical -

       MOTO   speed  mdY      predicted  actual next
              32.282 -0.0499     32.333     31.677
              31.677 -0.1005     31.769     31.181
              30.492 -0.2527     30.706     30.289

       ours   33.484 -0.0242     33.513     35.231
              35.231 -0.0706     35.297     36.964
              38.844 -0.1561     38.979     40.870

   MOTO LANDS BELOW ITS OWN PREDICTION by 0.4 to 0.65 a frame, which
   is a loss of roughly the right size for the airborne drag.  OURS
   LANDS ABOVE IT BY 1.7.  So the gravity step and the sqrt are right
   on both sides and something in our tick ADDS about 1.7 a frame to
   the speed after the integrator has set it.

   IT IS NOT THE DRIVE SLICE.  Hooked, physics_tick_drivetrain is
   called exactly ONCE in the whole run - on the first, grounded
   frame, with airTime 0 - and never again while the bike is up.  The
   airborne gate added to it is therefore not even reached, and
   neither is the coupling it was meant to suppress.

   WHICH LEAVES NO IDENTIFIED WRITER.  Every remaining store to speed
   - the entry clamp, the drag floor, the |v|, the realSpeedMax clamp,
   the landing resync and the end-of-tick mirror copy - either
   reduces it or is accounted for.  The one that found the answer on
   MOTO's side was a hardware data watchpoint on racer+0x124, and THE
   PORT HAS NO EQUIVALENT: mracer_set_hook traps function entries
   only, and these are inline stores.

   THE GAP IS CLOSED - agent.c has SET_DATABP now, four hardware
   slots on the game thread - and it named the writer in one run.

   OURS, watching racer1+0x124 through a flight:

       drive_store+0x5B            12
       racer_integrate+0x9BE        6

       drive_load+0x5B             12

   AND ONE OF THOSE JUMPS: 4205E55D to 420B01DF in a single store -
   33.224 to 34.752, +1.53 - from physics_set_long_speed.

   IT IS THE FIRST SetLongSpeed IN THE TAIL, physics.c:1176, which
   assigns the speed straight FROM gearboxRoadSpeed.  While the bike
   is up and gbrs is free-ramping off the engine, that drags the real
   speed onto the ramp, and +1.53 a frame is the whole error.

   MOTO'S OWN WATCHPOINT SAYS IT SHOULD NOT RUN THERE.  racer+0x124
   through a MOTO flight has exactly two writers - the integrator's
   speed = |v| at 0x0048d798 and the drag at 0x004900a0.
   SetLongSpeed is not among them.  Gated on the ground;
   check_physics.py still passes.

   AND A LESSON ABOUT THE NEW TOOL.  The first run used `changed` on
   a ONE-BYTE watch and reported only two writers, because two
   different floats can share a low byte and the filter hid the
   third.  On a byte watch of a float, leave `changed` off.

   THERE ARE TWO SetLongSpeed CALLS IN THE TAIL AND BOTH COUPLE.
   physics.c:1176 assigns the speed straight from gearboxRoadSpeed;
   the second, at the end, assigns it from v = gearboxRoadSpeed +
   slope and then stores v back into gbrs.  Gating only the first cut
   the writer count from 12 to 7 and left the error - both are now
   grounded-only, and `d->gearboxRoadSpeed = v` still runs, because
   the RAMP is the original's behaviour and only the speed must stay
   off it.

   AND THE WATCHPOINT PERTURBS WHAT IT MEASURES.  Every hit is a
   kernel round trip, which stretches the frame; a run with one armed
   on a per-frame field showed the speed DECAYING while the same run
   without it showed the speed climbing.  TRUST THE WRITER LIST FROM
   AN ARMED RUN AND THE NUMBERS FROM AN UNARMED ONE - never both from
   the same run.

   drive_load ALSO RUNS TWICE A TICK, which is not obviously wrong -
   the second is the post-crash reload - but it is worth knowing when
   reading a trace of it.  [open]

9.46.14.1.  Dead gates removed

   physics_tick_drivetrain briefly took an `airborne` argument and
   gated steps 5 and 6 on it.  IT CANNOT BE REACHED AIRBORNE:
   physics_drivetrain_frame calls it only from the `else` of

       if (target < engine || num < 1)   ...
       else if (skipSync || airborne)    free rev, 0x0048f6f0
       else                              THE DRIVE BRANCH

   so the gate is the CALLER'S and the parameter was unreachable.
   Removed.  An unreachable gate is worse than none: it reads as
   though the binary tested at that level, and the next reader has no
   way to tell a modelled branch from an invented one.

9.46.14.2.  The end-of-tick snap in race.c is ours

   maybe_Racer_Tick has NO ground snap at that seam.  The original
   snaps in exactly two places - the grounded branch of
   UpdateGroundContact at 0x0046947d, and the wheel contacts inside
   UpdateLean on the ROAD arm only.

   Measured over 40 frames of the arena straight including a jump,
   r->segment equalled r->segmentStart every frame, and nothing
   between the contact call and the snap moves position - so the
   block recomputes a height already written.  Kept for now because
   the two fields have different maintainers and no corner or wall
   clip has been checked.  [open - delete once they are shown to
   agree everywhere]

9.46.14.3.  mr_place now resets the air state

   A placement that sets position and speed but leaves flAirTime and
   onGround alone lands a bike FLYING on one side and GROUNDED on the
   other.  racer_integrate forks on flAirTime and the drivetrain's
   airborne arm skips two speed couplings, so one stale frame is
   worth about a metre a second - which then compounds the length of
   the straight and reads as a physics difference.  Two runs of the
   same jump differed by 25 units of height and by 1.0 m/s from this
   alone.  OFF_AIRTIME and OFF_ONGROUND are in the reset on both
   sides now.

9.46.14.4.  MOTO's frames-per-command is NOT stable - check it

   The pairing "MOTO SEND_INPUT 1 1 runs TWO frames, so pair MOTO 1
   against port 2" (9.18.15) holds only sometimes.  Within one session
   MOTO went from two frames a command to ONE, with no restart and
   nothing sent to it in between, and every row of the comparison then
   looked like a physics divergence.

   flAirTime IS THE CHEAP TELL.  physics_air_time steps it by exactly
   2.592 a frame, so through a jump:

       airTime 5.2, 10.4, 15.6 ...    MOTO ran TWO frames a command
       airTime 2.6,  7.8, 13.0 ...    MOTO ran ONE

   Read it before trusting a row-by-row diff.  DO NOT "fix" it with
   frames=2: MOTO wedges on the larger batch and simply stops
   advancing - seen here at the sixth row, the same overrun 9.18.15
   records.  The fix is a MOTO restart, which is the user's to do.

9.46.16.  THE RACE CLOCK TIMES A SESSION OUT - mr_place guards it now

9.46.17.  THE TACHO'S 1.2x - A HARDCODED FULL SCALE

   race.c filled racer+0x11c itself:

       tachoRpm = engineSpeed / MoteurVitesseMax * 12000.0

   and 12000 was a constant in race.c.  The original scales by the
   CARAC'S RegimeMax, field 29, and that is PER BIKE: 12000 on the
   road bikes and 10000 on the dirt ones.  So the stand-in was exact
   on the street and 1.2x high on the dirt - 12000/10000 - which is
   why earlier sessions recorded the tachos as matching and this one
   did not.  Same code, different track.

       MOTO 10000/8 * 5.2220 = 6527      read 6527
       ours 12000/8 * 5.2220 = 7833      read 7832

   IT WAS NOT ONE FIELD BUT THREE.  speedKmhRaw and speedKmhNeedle
   were filled in the same place, from s_race_drive.speed * 3.6.
   Racer_UpdateDash owns all three, it was imported whole, and it was
   only ever CALLED FOR THE AI - racer_tick_ai's one call site.  The
   player never reached it, so every field it owns was a stand-in.

   THE SPEEDO IS MEASURED, NOT REPORTED, and that is the difference
   that matters beyond the scale: the original takes |rearWheelContact
   - rearWheelContactPrev| over the frame delta, so a bike being
   dragged sideways or spinning its wheel shows what it is doing over
   the GROUND.  speed * 3.6 cannot show either.

   The fix is one call at maybe_Racer_Tick's own place for it -
   0x0045d7eb, immediately before maybe_Trk_LaneOfRacer - and the
   deletion of all three stand-ins.  The player takes the function's
   early return (humanPlayer and soloMode both set), so it gets the
   contact-based speedo and the engine-based tacho and never reaches
   the gearbox_auto_select arm, which is the AI's.

   VERIFIED after the change, 32 frames from rest on the dirt arena:

9.46.18.  THE SHIFT EDGE - ONE FRAME OF CALL ORDER

   The gearbox state machine is BIT-IDENTICAL through a change.  Gear,
   shift timer, state, pending and gearboxRoadSpeed all agree exactly:

       step | gear tmr st pnd |  MOTO engine |  ours engine
         11 |    1   0  0   0 |      6.96622 |      6.96622
         12 |    1 136  1   1 |      7.41395 |      7.42121
         13 |    1  72  1   1 |      7.73075 |      7.73801
         14 |    1   8  1   1 |      8.00000 |      8.00000
         15 |    2 -24  0   0 |      4.01860 |      4.01860

   ONLY engineSpeed MOVES, and only on the FIRST PENDING FRAME.  The
   step 12-13 increment is +0.31680 on both - the free-rev rate,
   MoteurVitesseMax * dtMs * 0.0006 - so once pending, the two agree
   exactly.  The whole error is made in the transition.

   IT IS NOT A CALL-ORDER DIFFERENCE.  An entry hook on
   maybe_Physics_TickDrivetrain (0x0048ef30, capturing ECX+0x60 state,
   ECX+0x5c timer, ECX+0xc8 engine, ECX+0x108 road speed) splits
   step 12's two game frames on MOTO:

       frame | state | timer |  engine in | engine out | arm
           1 |     0 |     0 |    6.96622 |    7.25550 | DRIVE
           2 |     1 |   168 |    7.25550 |    7.41395 | free-rev

   THE ORDER IS DRIVE THEN FREE-REV, WHICH IS OURS TOO.  An earlier
   reading of this section claimed MOTO took free-rev first; that was
   inferred from the increments alone and the hook disproves it.  The
   request lands between the two frames on both sides.

   SO THE WHOLE DIFFERENCE IS IN FRAME 1'S DRIVE INCREMENT: from the
   same entry engine of 6.96622, MOTO adds 0.28928 and ours 0.29659.
   Frame 2's free-rev step is +0.1584 on both, to the digit, and so
   is every drive increment before this frame.

9.46.18.1.  And the cause: the gearbox resync that was dropped

   A second hook, at 0x0048f4ee - the instruction after the drive
   branch's two engine clamps - on that one drive frame:

       engine after the clamps   7.25550
       road speed, still         12.7099
       throttle target cap       8.0  (= MoteurVitesseMax)
       shift state               0

   The plain formula on 12.7099 gives 7.26281, and THAT IS WHAT THE
   PORT HAD.  The difference is the round trip the drive branch does
   and the port did not:

       0048f4a6  engine    = SpeedToEngine(roadSpeed)
       0048f4ba  clamp to carac field 6, MoteurVitesseMax
       0048f4d9  clamp to the throttle target
       0048f502  roadSpeed = EngineToSpeed(engine)

   THE OLD NOTE CALLED THIS STEP OMITTED "AS A MEASUREMENT RATHER
   THAN AN OVERSIGHT" - gearboxRoadSpeed came back byte-identical on
   8 of 8 frames, so the round trip was judged lossless and dropped.
   It IS lossless for the road speed.  It is not lossless for the
   ENGINE, and the engine it leaves behind is the rounded one that
   the next frame builds on.  The note even said the step "does
   change engineSpeed, a lot"; what it missed is that this makes the
   step necessary, not skippable.

   IT ONLY SHOWS AT A SHIFT because on an ordinary frame the tail
   rebuilds the engine from the road speed and erases the residue.
   On the frame a shift is requested the NEXT frame takes the
   free-rev arm, which ADDS to the engine instead of rebuilding it -
   so the residue survives to the frame boundary, and that is the
   only place a capture ever saw it.

   Restored, with the two clamps, and physics_tick_drivetrain now
   takes gear, the throttle target and disengaged to do it.

   AND IT DID NOT MOVE THE NUMBER EITHER.  After the restore the
   difference is still 0.007266 at steps 12-13 and 0.010451 at 24-25,
   the same values to the digit, from a Debug binary confirmed newer
   than the source.  The restore is right on its own evidence - the
   round trip is in the binary and the port lacked it - but it is not
   the cause.

   THREE STRUCTURAL FIXES, NONE OF THEM THE CAUSE.  The shift request
   moved past the tail, the request gated on onGround, and now the
   gearbox resync restored: each was demanded by the binary, each
   landed, and the residual is unchanged to six decimal places
   through all three.  A difference that survives three correct
   changes untouched is not being produced where any of them looked.

9.46.18.2.  Both sides hooked, and the answer

   Hooking physics_drivetrain_frame's entry on the port, against the
   0x0048ef30 entry hook on MOTO, gives the same four numbers per
   game frame for the first time.  Frame 1 of the request step, the
   frame the whole difference is made in:

       side | engine in | engine out | road speed out
       MOTO |   6.96622 |    7.25550 |        12.6971
       ours |   6.96622 |    7.26281 |        12.6972

   THE ROAD SPEED AGREES AND THE ENGINE DOES NOT, and the arithmetic
   says why in one line.  The gear ratio here gives
   MoteurVitesseMax / (rapport * VitesseMaxBoite) = 8 / (0.35 * 40) =
   0.571427, and:

       7.25550 / 12.6971 = 0.571427     MOTO is self-consistent
       7.26281 / 12.7099 = 0.571427     ours is consistent with a
                                        HIGHER road speed - 12.7099,
                                        the value BEFORE the drag

   So MOTO ends the frame with an engine that matches the road speed
   it is actually leaving, and the port ends it with an engine that
   matches the road speed it had BEFORE the drag was taken off.

   THE CAUSE IS THE SPLIT ITSELF.  maybe_Physics_TickDrivetrain does
   drive, brake, drag/roll/lerp, clamp, SpeedToEngine - in that
   order, one function.  The port cut it into physics_drivetrain_
   frame and physics_drivetrain_tail, and THE FINAL SpeedToEngine
   ENDED UP IN THE WRONG HALF: it sits at the bottom of the frame
   function, which race.c calls BEFORE the tail, so our engine is
   resynchronised from a road speed the drag has not yet touched.

   It is invisible on an ordinary frame because the next frame's
   drive step rebuilds the engine from the road speed anyway.  It
   survives only across a shift request, where the following frame
   takes the free-rev arm and ADDS to the engine instead of
   rebuilding it.  That is why one seam shows up as two frames of
   0.1 per cent once per gear change and nowhere else.

   DONE.  The clamp and the SpeedToEngine now sit at the END of
   physics_drivetrain_tail, which is where the original has them, and
   the tail takes gear, skipSync and disengaged to do it.

9.46.18.3.  And the seam is gone

9.46.20.2.  THE WALL CRASH IS A COIN FLIP - it cannot be diffed

   A single run of a rail impact showed MOTO latching a crash and the
   port not, and that was written up here as a port defect.  IT IS
   NOT.  Three identical repeats gave (0,0), (13,13) and (0,13) crash
   frames - the outcome varies on BOTH sides, independently, from the
   same placement.

   THE DECISION IS `(rand() & 3) == 0`.  At the angles these spots
   produce the impact does not reach the always-crash arm, which
   needs |cos| > 0.98 AND speed > 100 km/h together; it lands in the
   one-in-four arm instead.  MOTO draws from Game_RandNext and the
   port from the CRT rand(), two independent streams, so agreement on
   any single run is chance and disagreement proves nothing.

   Measured at the moment of contact with a hook on
   racer_wall_impact_cos: normal (1, -0, 0), travel (0.9426, 0, 0),
   so |cos| = 0.9426 - under the 0.98 the hard arm needs, even from
   the "square" spot, because the bike drifts over the run-up.

   WHEN BOTH DO CRASH THE PATH AGREES: speed within 0.0912 m/s over
   the down frames, and both decay at about 0.81 a frame.

   SO A CRASH REGRESSION NEEDS A DETERMINISTIC IMPACT, and none of
   the current spots gives one.  It wants |cos| > 0.98 and more than
   27.78 m/s AT CONTACT, which means a much shorter run-up so the
   heading does not drift.  Until such a spot exists, compare the
   crash PATH on runs where both sides happened to latch, and never
   read a single run as evidence either way.  [open - add a
   square-hard spot]

   THE LESSON IS THE SAME ONE AS 9.46.18.2, in a new costume: one
   sample of a random process looked like a structural difference.
   Repeat before writing it down.


9.46.21.  THE RIDER KEPT POSING AFTER PAUSE

   Reported from the dirt track: press pause on a hill and the rider
   goes on settling into whatever pose he was heading for, for a
   fraction of a second.

   racer_pose_pitch IS A QUARTER-STEP FILTER WITH NO dt:

       poseBlend += (target - poseBlend) * POSE_PITCH_SMOOTH

   so it converges by a fixed fraction PER CALL, and it is reached
   from the DRAW - race_draw_racers -> race_pose_racer ->
   racer_update_pose - which runs on every rendered frame.  Pausing
   freezes the target and the filter walks the rest of the way there
   in the ten-odd frames it needs, which at 60 fps is about a sixth
   of a second.  Exactly the reported length.

   HOOKED AND CONFIRMED, with the game paused and no input sent:
   eight calls to racer_pose_pitch, the wheel contacts identical on
   every one.

   THE dt > 0 GUARD IN race_update DOES NOT REACH IT.  That guard
   covers the TICK; this runs in the DRAW, which has no such test and
   should not need one.

   THE ORIGINAL CANNOT HAVE THIS BUG.  maybe_Racer_UpdateSkeleton is
   what calls UpdatePose, and all FOUR of its callers are ticks:

       0045d7d8  maybe_Racer_Tick
       0046c783  maybe_Racer_TickAI
       0045f12c  maybe_Racer_TickRemote
       0047d8df  maybe_Game_Tick

   It never poses from the draw, so its filter can only advance on a
   simulation step.

   THE FIX SPLITS THE ONE PIECE OF STATE OUT.  racer_update_pose is a
   pure function of its inputs apart from poseBlend, which is why
   nothing else drifted; the advance moved to racer_pose_pitch_tick,
   called from racer_tick_dispatch for every ticked racer, and the
   draw now READS the field.  The port still poses from the draw
   because step 6's far fade needs the camera distance and the draw
   is the only place that has it.  The gates are UpdatePose's own -
   PersoAnim, and no wheelie - because the original writes poseBlend
   only on that branch.

   IT IS racer_ai_lean_step's BUG ONE SUBSYSTEM OVER, and worth
   stating as a class: AN ACCUMULATOR WITH NO dt IS A BUG WHEREVER IT
   IS REACHED FROM A NON-STEP PATH.  Two found so far; the way to
   find the rest is to look for `x += (t - x) * k` and `x *= k` with
   no dt, then ask what calls them.


9.46.22.  THE AIRBORNE STUNTS - two blockers, both closed

   The dirt bikes have stunts on a jump: press the stunt button in the
   air and the rider kicks his legs out, waves, and so on.  MOTO loads
   NINE of them on the KTM - g_nStuntPoseCount at 0x005cf4fc reads 9
   live - and the port did none.

   racer_pose_stunt WAS ALREADY WRITTEN, whole, with the right gates.
   Two things upstream of it were missing.

   1.  stuntPressed WAS NEVER SET.  maybe_Input_ReadDevice writes TWO
       fields on a change of action6Down (racer+0x31fd):

           00411e77  JZ 00411e8c          fell  -> stuntHeld = 0
           00411e7e  [ECX+0x31f9] = 1     stuntHeld, the LEVEL
           00411e84  [ECX+0x3201] = 1     stuntPressed, a ONE-SHOT

       race_poll_input set the level only, so racer_pose_stunt's
       rising-edge test could never pass.  The port had never written
       action6Down either.  race_stunt_edge does both now.

   2.  durationMs WAS NEVER PARSED.  Each stunt entry in the bike
       manifest carries two integers after the name, and
       maybe_Bike_ReadManifest reads them as DURATION then HAND
       SKELETON:

           0042a1e1  record+0x08 = the '_N_' digit - 0x30
           0042a1ef  record+0x04 = the pose index
           0042a1fe  Settings_ReadInt -> record+0x0c   duration
           0042a209  Settings_ReadInt -> record+0x00   hand skeleton

       With duration 0 racer_pose_stunt_step returns 0 and the stunt
       ends on the frame it starts, so fixing the input alone would
       have produced a one-frame flicker and looked like a different
       bug.  Both are needed.

       THEY ARE READ EVEN WHEN THE TABLE IS FULL, because they are in
       the STREAM: skipping them at STUNT_POSE_MAX would desynchronise
       every record after it.

   WHICH STUNT IS CHOSEN IS THE CLOCK, NOT THE RANDOM.  UpdatePose
   calls Rand_Next, stores it, and immediately overwrites with
   g_framePrevMs % count - so the rand is a dead store.  Already
   transcribed that way.

9.46.22.1.  And a third: the race clock is dead, so nothing expired

   With the first two fixed the stunt FIRED and then never stopped -
   the rider held the pose through the landing and through a crash -
   and it was always the same stunt.  Both from one field.

       raceClockMs = 0      read live, mid-race

   race_clock_tick exists and NOTHING CALLS IT (race.c's own [open -
   wire MR_RaceClock]), so racer+0x2ddd sits at 0 for the whole race.
   MR_PoseEnv.nowMs was fed from it, so:

       elapsed = nowMs - stuntStartStamp = 0 - 0 = 0

   which never reaches the duration, and

       stuntNext = nowMs % stuntCount = 0

   which is why only the FIRST of the nine ever played.  One dead
   field, both symptoms, and neither looks like a clock problem from
   the outside.

   THE NOTE THAT MADE IT PLAUSIBLE said any monotonic millisecond
   source works because every timer nowMs feeds is a delta against a
   stamp on the same racer.  That is true and it is not the whole
   test: the source also has to ADVANCE.  It now comes from tm_now,
   which is monotonic, is closer to the original besides - UpdatePose
   picks the next stunt with g_framePrevMs, a free-running frame
   stamp, not the race clock - and stops while paused, which a pose
   timer should.

   THE CLEAR IS A DURATION TEST AND NOTHING ELSE.  0x0045e19f compares
   the record's +0x0c against the elapsed and clears racer+0x305d when
   it is past; there is no landing test and no height test in it.  A
   short jump does not cut a stunt short in the original either - the
   durations are simply short.

   o BUT THE RECORD IS INDEXED BY racer+0x3065 THERE, not by the
     stunt slot: 0x0045e196 loads it, shifts by 4 and reads
     0x580d14.  Ours indexes by stuntPose - 1 and stores the POSE
     index in stuntPoseId, which reads 33 live - not a stunt slot.
     The two are self-consistent on our side, so this is not
     currently wrong, but the field's meaning does not match.
     [open - settle what racer+0x3065 is]

9.46.22.3.  Frame 0 blends from the pose so far, not from itself

   That single-frame majority is why the next symptom looked like a
   missing animation: the rider snapped into the pose, held it, and
   snapped back.

   0x0045e15d is a JLE that SKIPS the store at 0x0045e171:

       0045e15f  ECX = k * 244            sizeof(MR_AnimFrame)
       0045e16a  EDX = base + (k-1)*244
       0045e171  [ESP+0x20] = EDX         ONLY when k > 0
       0045e179  ECX = base + k*244
       0045e185  EAX = [ESP+0x24]         the same slot, past a push
       0045e191  blend(dst, w, EAX, ECX)

   So on frame 0 the 'from' side is whatever that slot already held -
   the pose the earlier passes built - and the weight carries the
   rider INTO the stunt.  The port passed the stunt's own frame 0 on
   both sides, so the blend was a constant and w was discarded.

   w IS A 0 -> 1 -> 0 HUMP, sin(elapsed/duration * pi), which is the
   ease-in and the ease-out in one number.  That shape only makes
   sense against a fixed 'from', and it IS the whole animation for a
   one-frame stunt.  Reading it as a within-frame fraction and then
   blending a frame against itself threw away both halves of the
   move.

   [open - untested; needs a jump with the button pressed]


9.46.23.  THE DONUT - the brake gate that had no caller

   Reported: on the dirt arena, retail holds gas and brake and a
   direction together and does endless donuts; the port just stops.

   THE BRAKE IS NOT GATED ON THE BUTTON ALONE.  0x0048f904:

       0048f915  turnBrakeFlag  != 0 -> run
       0048f91f  driveInterlock != 0 -> run
       0048f927  preRace        != 0 -> run
                 otherwise the counter is zeroed and the whole
                 deceleration block is SKIPPED

   and maybe_Racer_ApplySteering clears turnBrakeFlag (racer+0xac)
   for a HUMAN on a MotoType 1 bike that is braking AND turning at
   the same time.  So on a dirt bike that combination does not brake
   at all - the bike keeps its speed and slides, which is the donut.
   Under 2 m/s the flag is forced back on, so a nearly stopped bike
   can always brake.

   physics_brake_gated WAS ALREADY TRANSCRIBED, with the three
   conditions and their addresses in its comment, and NOTHING CALLED
   IT.  physics_drivetrain_frame braked on `if (braking)`.

   THAT IS THE THIRD OF THIS SHAPE IN ONE SESSION - stuntPressed
   written by nothing, racer_pose_pitch advancing in the draw, and
   now a gate with no caller.  A transcribed function with no caller
   is not neutral: it reads as done and behaves as absent.  WORTH A
   SWEEP: every non-static function in src/ that nothing calls is
   either dead or a missing wire, and the two look identical from
   inside the file.

9.46.23.1.  Wiring it was not enough - the third condition

   With the gate called, turnBrakeFlag measured 0 correctly under gas
   + brake + right on the dirt bike, and the bike STILL stopped:
   19.7 -> 9.3 m/s over ten commands.

   THE GATE HAS THREE CONDITIONS AND ONLY ONE WAS RIGHT.  Off the
   disassembly, not the decompiler:

       0048f904  TEST EBP,EBP        the drive demand
       0048f906  JL 0048f915         num < 0 -> run the three tests
       0048f915  EAX = [ESI+0x88]    instance+0x88 = racer+0xac,
                                     which IS turnBrakeFlag
       0048f91f  EAX = [ESP+0x7c]    a STACK LOCAL
       0048f927  EAX = g_bPreRace
       0048f930  [ESI+0x8c] = 0      else zero the counter and skip

   The port passed driveInterlock as the middle condition and the
   caller hands that a literal 1, so the gate was UNCONDITIONAL and
   turnBrakeFlag could not matter.  Passing 0 instead is what makes
   the flag reach anything.

   THE NOTE THAT CAUSED IT: physics.h said "driveInterlock is
   racer+0x88".  The +0x88 is the INSTANCE's, and the instance is
   racer+0x24, so the field is racer+0xac - the same one
   racer_apply_steering writes as turnBrakeFlag.  One field, two
   names, and the offset written against the wrong base.  Corrected
   in place.

   [ESP+0x7c] IS STILL UNIDENTIFIED and is passed as 0.  That is the
   reading that lets turnBrakeFlag do anything at all; if it is
   non-zero in some state the brake returns there.  [open]

9.59.3.  The skeleton walk, hooked

   Skl_BuildPartMatrices @ 0x00463d90 IS CALLED ONLY ON THE TWO
   ROOTS. Hooking its entry catches parts 0 and 3 and nothing else,
   however long it runs. The tree is walked by FUN_00464190, which
   its two tail calls enter.  [verified]

   THE PARENT MATRIX IS AT ESP+8 FROM ONE CALL SITE AND ESP+12 FROM
   THE OTHER, so a fixed offset reads one of them as garbage. Dump
   the whole frame and find the 3x4 by testing for orthonormality
   instead - and use ONE capture, because two overlapping capture
   windows concatenate into something that can look like a valid
   matrix and is not. That produced a false result here once.

   PART 1 IS HANDED PART 2'S MATRIX. Its parent matrix reads
   (0, +4.91, -4.56), which is part 2's own, where the tree in
   Skl_BuildPartMatrices' comment makes part 1 a child of part 0
   and so identity. That is the OPEN note in that comment answered
   the wrong way round: it doubted both recursive calls could
   receive the same matrix, and they appear to.  [verified]

   IT IS ENTERED EXACTLY SEVEN TIMES A FRAME because ONLY HALF THE
   RECURSION IS A CALL. Its tail:

       0046447b  MOV  ECX,[ESI]      the sibling
       0046447f  JZ   00464486
       00464481  CALL 00464190       SIBLING - a real call
       00464486  MOV  ESI,[ESI+8]    the child
       0046448b  JNZ  0046419a       CHILD - a JUMP, past the
                                     prologue, reusing the frame
       00464491  POP EDI / POP ESI / ADD ESP,0xb4 / RET

   The compiler turned the child recursion into a loop. A hook on
   the function ENTRY therefore sees sibling descents and never
   child ones - which is the whole of the "walk stops at seven
   nodes" puzzle. TO SEE THE CHILDREN, HOOK 0x0046419a.  [verified]

   AND IT SETTLES THE OPEN NOTE in Skl_BuildPartMatrices' comment,
   which doubted that both recursive calls could receive the same
   matrix. They share a frame because one of them is not a call:
   the CALL at 0x00464481 pushes NO arguments, so the sibling reads
   the matrix out of its caller's frame - and by then that frame
   holds the matrix just computed for the part whose sibling it is.
   Measured: part 1 is handed part 2's matrix, (0, +4.91, -4.56).

   THAT LOOKS LIKE "A SIBLING IS COMPOSED UNDER THE PREVIOUS
   SIBLING", AND IT IS NOT. Tested by recomposing the original's
   OWN tree from its own angles and pivots, both ways, and
   comparing against the matrices it stores:

       sibling under PARENT     worst 59.65 at part 6
       sibling under PREVIOUS   worst 96.09 at part 11

   Neither reproduces them, and the "previous sibling" reading is
   the WORSE of the two. So the frame observation is real - part 1
   genuinely receives part 2's matrix at the call - but it does
   not generalise into a composition rule. Something else in that
   shared frame is being read, or it is not the world matrix at
   all.  [verified negative]

   DO NOT CHANGE skl.c ON THE STRENGTH OF THE FRAME CAPTURE. The
   port's composition already reproduces every .rot-driven part
   exactly (9.59.2). Testing the rule against the original's own
   data costs nothing and settled it in one pass; changing the
   port first would have broken working code.

   HOOKING 0x0046419a CATCHES THE WHOLE WALK - the prologue falls
   through to it and the child loop jumps to it, so both paths are
   covered. Fifteen non-root parts per frame, in order:

       2, 1, 15, 16, 4, 9, 7, 5, 6, 8, 13, 12, 10, 11, 14

   and the tree read from the part records accounts for every one.
   [verified]

   PART 15'S MATRIX IS NOT BUILT BY THE WALK. Its parent matrix,
   captured from the live frame, is part 1's - identity, zero
   translation - so its world matrix should just be its own local:

       pivot (2.69, -33.55, 76.81), angle pure y 0.03375
       pivot - pivot*E   =  (-2.59,  0.00, +0.13)
       stored matrix     =  (-2.83, +5.29, -3.40)

   A y rotation about a pivot cannot move anything in y. With the
   parent proven identity there is nowhere else for +5.29 to come
   from. A hardware watchpoint on that matrix names both writers
   and their order:

       004643c0   inside FUN_00464190      the skeleton walk
       004613b6   inside maybe_Racer_Draw  the OVERRIDE

   THE WALK WRITES IT AND THE DRAW OVERWRITES IT, every frame. So
   the matrix sitting in the part record is the draw's, not the
   pose's, which is why it cannot be reproduced from the angle and
   pivot no matter how the tree is composed.  [verified]

   THE OVERRIDE IS WHY THE FRONT BODYWORK SITS WRONG. Part 15 owns
   body-mesh vertices 71..94 - it is front-end bodywork, not the
   separate wheel instance - and the two matrices differ by a pure
   translation:

       walk      (-2.59,  0.00, +0.13)
       override  (-2.83, +5.29, -3.40)
       delta     (-0.24, +5.29, -3.53)

   The original's front bodywork is therefore 5.29 units HIGHER
   than ours, which is exactly the reported symptom: a gap above
   the front wheel in the original against the wheel touching the
   bodywork in the port.  [verified]

   WHAT IT COMPOSES AGAINST IS STILL UNREAD, but two candidates
   are eliminated. Part 15's parent is part 1, and part 1's matrix
   is written ONLY by the walk - a watchpoint on it caught
   0x004643c0 six times and no draw writer - so the parent really
   is the identity the record holds. And the rotation is a pure y,
   which cannot move anything in y about any pivot. So the
   override adds an explicit translation from somewhere; the delta
   is not a vector stored anywhere in the racer, so it is
   computed.

   THE STORE IS PART-15-ONLY. Hooked at 0x004613af over 24 hits,
   every one writes part 15 - EBP resolves as slot*2000 +
   part*100 and part is 15 each time. It is a dedicated override,
   not a loop that happens to include it.  [verified]

   It addresses racer + slot*2000 + part*100 + 0x654, the slot
   coming from racer+0x638.

   WHAT IT COMPUTES, traced instruction by instruction:

       Vec3_Set(ESP+0x12c, -pivot)        0x004610e4, pivot
                                          from EDI = part+0x48
       rotate that by the part's own      0x004610e9..0x0046114a
         euler, built from its angles
         through the 0x4000 table
       + [ESP+0x1fc .. 0x204]             0x00461153, 0x00461174,
                                          0x00461182

   SAME PIVOT, SAME ANGLES, SAME RULE AS THE WALK - and one
   difference: where Skl_BuildPartMatrices adds the PIVOT back
   after rotating, this adds a different vector. Read live:

       added V        (+2.4208, -28.2615, +73.2790)
       part 15 pivot  (+2.6900, -33.5500, +76.8100)

   and V predicted from the stored matrix - override.trans plus
   pivot*R - is (+2.4505, -28.2600, +73.2755), matching the
   captured value to 0.03. So the mechanism is settled: the
   override rotates about the pivot and re-anchors to V instead of
   to the pivot.  [verified]

   WHAT V IS, IS THE LAST UNKNOWN, and these are ruled out:

     o not any pivot in the skeleton;
     o not the front wheel instance offset (0, -27.84, +84),
       though its y is close;
     o not the pivot under the part's own rotation or its
       inverse - pivot*R is (+5.28, -33.55, +76.68);
     o NOT ANYWHERE IN THE RACER. A scan of all 0x3209 bytes finds
       none of its three components even individually, so it is
       computed in maybe_Racer_Draw's frame rather than read.
       [verified]

   It sits at ESP+0x1fc at the store, with three zeros below it and
   a 1.0 above, which looks like a field of something larger.

   A WATCHPOINT CANNOT REACH IT - SET_DATABP refuses stack
   addresses - so the writer was found by searching the function's
   operands for 0x1fc instead. Two hits: the FADD that reads it,
   and the LEA that fills it.

   V IS BUILT AT 0x0045fdde..0x0045fe15:

       0045fdde  CALL Vec3_Scale   -> [ESP+0x180]
       0045fde4  MOV  ECX,[EBX+0x638]        the slot
       0045fdea  ..fdfa  ECX*5*5*5 << 4      = slot * 2000
       0045fdfd  ADD  EAX,EBX                + racer
       0045fdff  ADD  EAX,0x6e8
       0045fe08  CALL Vec3_Add
       0045fe15  CALL Vec3_Copy    -> [ESP+0x1fc]   = V

   AND 0x6e8 IS PART 1'S PIVOT. The pose array starts at 0x63c,
   0x6e8 - 0x63c is 172, and 172 is one 100-byte part stride plus
   the 0x48 pivot field - so the address is part 1 + 0x48,
   (-0.25, +24.12, +38.32).  [verified]

   SO THE OVERRIDE RE-ANCHORS PART 15 TO ITS PARENT'S PIVOT PLUS A
   SCALED VECTOR, not to its own pivot. That is why no rotation
   about its own pivot could ever reproduce the stored matrix, and
   it is the +5.29 that lifts the front bodywork clear of the
   wheel.

   AND THE SCALED VECTOR IS FORK TRAVEL. Vec3_Scale at 0x0045fdde
   is handed, captured live:

       direction  (+0.0424, -0.8310, +0.5546)   a UNIT vector
       length      63.035862
       product    (+2.6744, -52.3851, +34.9595)

   The direction is exactly normalize(pivot15 - pivot1):
   (2.94, -57.67, 38.49) over its length 69.40 gives
   (0.0424, -0.8310, 0.5546) to four places. So the override places
   part 15 at

       pivot1 + 63.04 * forkAxis

   where its REST position is pivot1 + 69.40 * forkAxis, which is
   pivot15 itself. Feed it 69.40 and the override collapses into
   the walk's rule exactly; it is fed 63.04, which is 90.8 per
   cent of rest.  [verified]

   Reassembled: V = pivot1 + 63.0359 * dir = (+2.4244, -28.2651,
   +73.2795) against a captured V of (+2.4208, -28.2615, +73.2790).

   SO THE FRONT END SLIDES ALONG THE FORK AXIS BY THE SUSPENSION
   STATE, and the port draws it at rest length because it bakes the
   static .rot pose. That is the whole of the reported symptom -
   the original's front bodywork sits clear of the wheel because
   the fork is extended past where the pose alone would put it.

   HOW THE LENGTH IS BUILT, 0x0045fd94..0x0045fdcd:

       Vec3_Sub    -> [ESP+0x178]     a fork delta
       Vec3_Length -> L
       FMUL [ESP+0x180]               * A
       FDIV [ESP+0xac]                / B
       -> [ESP+0x12c], the scalar
       Vec3_Normalize                 the unit fork axis

   Captured live:

       A = 69.405312   the REST fork length, |pivot15 - pivot1|
       B = 76.418365
       L * A / B = 69.405 * 69.405 / 76.418 = 63.031

   against the 63.0359 the scale actually receives - 0.008 per
   cent, so the formula is right and L is the rest length too.
   ESP+0xb0 reads -57.678, exactly the y of pivot15 - pivot1,
   which confirms that frame region holds the fork delta.
   [verified]

   THE DIVISOR IS WHEEL GEOMETRY. It is written at 0x0045fc87
   from

       Vec3_Length(...)                    0x0045fc62   -> L
       g_bikeCarac[racer+0x78] index 15    0x0045fc78
       * 96.0  (0x004d102c)                0x0045fc7f
       FSUBP                               0x0045fc85
       -> D = L - 96 * RoueRayonAv

   Carac index 15 is RoueRayonAv, 0.29 on this bike, so
   96 * 0.29 = 27.84 is the FRONT WHEEL RADIUS in world units, and
   D = 76.4184 gives L = 104.2584. The carac base in the
   instruction is 0x5092b4, which is 0x3c past carac[0] - fifteen
   floats - with a 73-float stride.  [verified]

   SO THE FORK LENGTH IS restLen * restLen / (L - wheelRadius).
   That is suspension geometry: the front end is placed so a wheel
   of that radius sits against a surface at distance L. It is
   static on page 10, which is why the offset shows with no dive
   and why calling it "fork travel" was an over-reading.

   L IS THE DISTANCE TO THE FRONT WHEEL. Built at 0x0045fc4d:

       A = [ESP+0x70]  (-0.2536, +65.8834, +38.3071)
       B = [EBP]       (-0.0000, -27.8404, +83.9753)  EBP =
                                                      racer+0x1b4
       L = |A - B| = 104.2584

   racer+0x1a8 is the FRONT WHEEL INSTANCE and +0x1b4 is its
   position - and that value is exactly the front wheel offset
   maybe_Racer_PlaceWheels' comment records from 23 captures,
   (0.0000, -27.8404, 83.9753).  [verified]

   THE WHOLE CONSTRUCTION, end to end:

       L       = |anchor - frontWheelCentre|
       D       = L - 96 * RoueRayonAv
       forkLen = |pivot15 - pivot1| ^ 2 / D
       part15  = pivot1 + forkLen * normalize(pivot15 - pivot1)

   so the front bodywork is placed by where the WHEEL is, which is
   itself placed from the carac. Every input exists in the port.

   A's x and z are pivot1's to four decimals and its y is 65.88
   against pivot1's 24.12, so A is the upper fork anchor rather
   than the steering-head pivot itself.

   WHERE A COMES FROM IS THE ONE THING STILL UNREAD, and one
   candidate is eliminated: the Vec3_Add at 0x0045fc0f does NOT
   write it. Its addends are (+2.9447, -57.6784, +38.4920) - the
   untransformed fork delta - and (0, 0, 0), which sum to the
   delta and not to A, so ESP+0x70 is not that call's
   destination. The remaining candidate in range is the single
   FSTP [ESP+0x70] at 0x0045f584, which writes one float.
   [open]

   IT DOES NOT BLOCK THE PORT. A is one endpoint of a distance,
   and every other term in the construction is identified and
   available - the pivots, the wheel instance position,
   RoueRayonAv and the 96 scale. The placement can be built now
   and A pinned when it is read.

   AND THAT IS THE VISIBLE BUG. The port bakes the rest pose and
   never does this placement, so its front wheel sits where the

   THE PORT ALREADY HAS THE STATE. racer_update_fork_dive and
   racer_decay_fork_dive run in race.c; what is missing is feeding

   DO NOT APPLY THE DELTA AS A CONSTANT. 5.29 is this bike on this
   page; it is an output of something, and pasting it in would be

   THAT IS WHY A BAKED POSE CANNOT MATCH. The port bakes the
   walk's result into vertices; the original overwrites some part
   matrices afterwards and draws from those. Any vertex comparison
   against an overwritten part measures the override, not our

       part  sibling  parent  child      part  sibling parent child

9.59.6.  The race placement is a matrix round trip, not a lift

   THE MENU AND THE TRACK ARE NOT THE SAME PROBLEM. On page 10 the
   racer sits at the origin with an identity orientation, so the
   placement collapses to a scalar and 96*hauteurCadre - 48*rayonAv
   reproduces it. ON THE TRACK IT DOES NOT: measured lifts of 78.08
   and 77.69 on consecutive frames, varying, with non-zero x and z.

   THE MATRIX THAT PLACES THE PIVOTS IS BUILT BY A ROUND TRIP:

       0045ff73  FUN_0046d270(ESP+0x29c, ESP+0x10)   matrix -> euler
       0045ff80  FUN_0046d4f0(ESP+0xac, euler)
       0045ff99  FUN_00403740(ESP+0x180, euler, pos) euler -> matrix
       0045ffc8  FUN_00410010(out, record[1].pos, ESP+0x180)

   FUN_0046d270 IS Matrix34_ToEuler - it takes a 3x3 and writes
   three angles, guarding the pole with SQRT(1 - m[6]^2) against the
   epsilon at 0x004d0e50.  [verified from the decompile]

   THE SOURCE MATRIX IS NEITHER racer+0xbc NOR racer+0x2e55.
   Captured on two cornering frames:

       source row2      ( 0.85461, 0.00000, -0.51927)
       matBikeRoot row0 (-0.51927, 0.00000, -0.85461)

   so source.row2 is matBikeRoot.row0 turned 90 degrees in xz - the
   bike's LATERAL axis. Rows 0 and 1 carry a further rotation about
   it whose cosine reads 0.61160 and 0.61190 on the two frames, so
   about 52.3 degrees and near constant while the yaw changes. The
   source matrix's translation is ZERO.  [verified]

   WHAT THE PORT DOES IS STRUCTURALLY WRONG, not just off by a
   number: race.c passes matBikeRoot plus a scalar lift. Two
   different lifts, in fact - 2*96*rayonAr for the draw position and
   96*hauteurCadre + 48*rayonAr for the fork - where the original
   has one matrix for both. The bike is drawn about 16 units low as
   a result, which reads on screen as the wheels sunk into the road.

   THE 52.3 DEGREES IS THE LEAN. ESP+0x29c is assembled at
   0x0045f5b4 by Matrix34_Multiply from a rotation built on the
   NEGATED lean - the FCHS at 0x0045f57b - so the source matrix is
   the bike's heading with the lean rolled in. The euler that comes
   back out confirms it: captured (-180.000, 53.691, 127.727) deg,
   and asin(source.row2.x) with row2.x = -matBikeRoot.row0.z =
   0.80583 is 53.69 degrees exactly.  [verified, four frames]

   SO ONE MATRIX SERVES BOTH, and the port now matches: race.c rolls
   bikeWorld by -lean exactly as it already rolled the draw matrix.
   Before that the two disagreed inside our own code the moment the
   bike leaned - the body drew rolled and the fork was measured
   against an unrolled frame.

   THE POSITION IS STILL OPEN. Captured at 0x0045ff89:

       pos - racerPos   (-33.0098, 43.1268, -44.9199)
                        (-34.9307, 43.1295, -43.3926)

   Its world y is CONSTANT at 43.13 across frames while the bike
   yaws and leans, and in matBikeRoot's frame the first component is
   stable at 55.74 against 96 * hauteurCadre = 55.68.

   DO NOT FIT THAT. Four formulas have been fitted to these totals
   and discarded in this section already, each surviving until the
   bike or the context that disproved it. 55.74 against 55.68 is a
   0.06 gap on two frames of one bike. Capture the ADDENDS that
   build ESP+0x70, the way the lift was finally settled, rather than
   another expression that reproduces the total.

   READING THIS FUNCTION: hook an instruction BEFORE any pushes, or
   address a capture off a register rather than ESP. maybe_Racer_Draw
   reuses its stack slots heavily and ESP shifts through it; four
   different slot readings gave four different answers before a
   register-addressed capture settled it.


9.61.  The lean sign is inverted in the port, and why

   MEASURED SIDE BY SIDE, same input, both games running [verified]:

   +----------------------+------------+------------+
   | after accel + LEFT   | MOTO.EXE   | mracer     |
   +======================+============+============+
   | yaw(matOrientation)  |  +0.934579 |  +0.000000 |
   +----------------------+------------+------------+
   | yaw(matBikeRoot)     |  +0.665232 |  +0.216609 |
   +----------------------+------------+------------+
   | d = orient - bikeRoot|  +0.269347 |  -0.216609 |
   +----------------------+------------+------------+
   | lean                 |  -0.506737 |  positive  |
   +----------------------+------------+------------+

   RIGHT is the mirror of this on both sides, so the flip is
   systematic and not a one-off.

   THE CONTROL THAT PINS IT: from one reset, on the same straight,
   50 frames of accelerate alone leaves MOTO's yaw(matOrientation) at
   +0.000000; 50 frames of accelerate plus LEFT leaves it at
   +0.934195. STEERING ROTATES THE BODY. In the port that yaw is
   0.000000 in both cases, because racer_set_track_pos puts
   r->matrix on the rail and nothing else writes it.

   So the port computes (fixed rail) - (travel), where the original
   computes (leading body) - (travel). Only the travel term moves,
   and it moves toward the turn, so the difference carries the
   opposite sign. The rider leans out of the corner.

9.61.1.  The dependency, which is bigger than the sign

   The obvious repair - rotate r->matrix with the steering - does
   not stand on its own. The +0.934 rad above is 53 degrees, and it
   is a REAL HEADING CHANGE, not a slip angle: MOTO's bike has left
   the racing line and is heading across the track. A railed bike
   that points 53 degrees off the rail while still sliding along it
   would look worse than the wrong lean does.

   The original reaches that heading through the steering TORQUE at
   the end of maybe_Racer_ApplySteering @ 0x00466240:

       factor = humanPlayer ? RotationTransfert * (1 - fade(speed))
                            : 0.004                 0x004d10d8
       if (wheelieAngle != 0) factor *= 0x004d1018
       if (fxGate) grip = clamp(|SlipSpeed()|*k + k2, 1.0, 1.6)
                          and 1.6 flat while braking AND turning
       FUN_0040fd20(0, grip * factor * dt * K * steering, 0)
       FUN_0040fd40(...)  FUN_0045cb00()

   a PURE YAW vector, applied to the body every frame. The fade is
   off VitesseMaxBoite, NOT RealSpeedMax - a different fade from the
   visual one in maybe_Racer_Draw, and the two must not be merged.

   DONE, and the port now steers a body. race.c calls
   racer_apply_steering, racer_steer_torque returns the yaw, and the
   bike moves along its heading instead of along the rail.

   TWO THINGS COULD NOT BE READ OFF THE DECOMPILATION and were
   measured against MOTO.EXE instead. Ghidra loses the scale inside
   FUN_0040fd40 - it shows a bare __ftol into g_cosTable with the
   arguments gone - so neither the unit nor the sense of the yaw
   vector is recoverable statically:

   o THE YAW IS IN RADIANS. Reading it as cosTable units (pi/8192)
     makes the whole term truncate to zero: 0.0028 * 3.3 * 0.01 * 600
     is 0.055, and __ftol(0.055) is 0. As radians the same expression
     gives 0.0513 rad a frame against a measured 0.0467, the
     difference being the first frames of bar ramp-up averaged in.

   o THE SIGN IS NEGATED. Holding left drives steer to -600, the lock
     stop, and MOTO's body yaw RISES.

   THE POSITION STAYS IN TRACK COORDINATES - segPos and lateral -
   rather than becoming a free world position. The segment lookup,
   the ground snap and the wall clip already work in those, and
   locally the two are the same thing. The heading is kept in WORLD
   space and resolved against the current segment each frame, so a
   curving track carries the bike round without re-basing it.

   ACCELERATION IS ABOUT 25 PER CENT HIGH. From a standing start,

       0.50 s   MOTO 10.567 m/s    port 13.573 m/s   +28.4%

9.61.3.  What is still missing: the slip model

   The sign is now right, but the MAGNITUDE will not match yet, and
   the reason is worth stating before someone tunes it.

   MOTO's slip angle in the measurement above is 0.269 rad, 15
   degrees - the velocity is a long way behind the heading because
   the tyres are sliding.

   IN THE PORT IT IS EXACTLY ZERO, not merely small, and the reason
   is structural rather than a matter of degree. The same yawRel
   moves the position and rotates the body in the SAME frame, so the
   displacement is along the new heading by construction and moveDir
   equals the body forward to five decimals. Measured after the free
   body landed:

   +----------------------+------------+------------+
   | accel 30f + LEFT 20f | MOTO.EXE   | mracer     |
   +======================+============+============+
   | yaw(matOrientation)  |  +0.934579 |  +0.734855 |
   +----------------------+------------+------------+
   | yaw(matBikeRoot)     |  +0.665270 |  +0.734851 |
   +----------------------+------------+------------+
   | d                    |  +0.269308 |  +0.000003 |
   +----------------------+------------+------------+
   | lean                 |  -0.356543 |  -0.000005 |
   +----------------------+------------+------------+

   THE INVERSION IS GONE - d no longer carries the wrong sign - and
   the body genuinely steers, reaching 79 per cent of the original's
   yaw over the same input. What is left is that a bike whose
   velocity is welded to its heading HAS no slip angle, so it has
   nothing to lean from.

   THE LEAN IS THEREFORE ZERO UNTIL THE SLIP MODEL LANDS. Closing
   the gap needs the lateral velocity to be integrated against grip
   rather than snapped to the heading - maybe_Racer_SlipSpeed and the
   force it feeds. DO NOT SCALE THE LEAN UP TO COMPENSATE: that would
   fight the slip model on the day it lands, which is exactly the
   trap CLAUDE.md rule 5a describes. [open]

   +----------------------+------------+------------+--------+
   |                      | MOTO.EXE   | mracer     | ratio  |

   o THE LOCAL PLAYER WAS NOT MARKED HUMAN. racer_place_from_grid

9.61.5.  g_frameDeltaSmoothed IS NOT THE FRAME DELTA - it is
         smoothed, and the harness inflates it

   The lean divides by g_frameDeltaSmoothed (0x004dc150), and an
   earlier session recorded it as "exactly 33.0 - a 30 fps frame".
   IT IS NOT A CONSTANT. Read
   live during a stepped comparison it is 48.751 ms, while the frame
   delta is 33.0.

   IT IS A SMOOTHED REAL-TIME MEASUREMENT, so driving MOTO.EXE one
   frame at a time through SEND_INPUT inflates it - each frame takes
   as long as the pipe round trip. That makes the lean, and only the
   lean, harness-dependent.

   THE FORMULA IS CONFIRMED CORRECT once the right value goes in:

       ac   = 0.2691 * 2                        = 0.5382
       x    = (0.5382 / 48.751) * -30           = -0.3312
       lean = x * 1.4 + 0.12                    = -0.3437
       MOTO measured                              -0.3436

   THE HARNESS NO LONGER INFLATES IT. mrhook frame-locks the game
   clock, so the smoothed delta reads exactly 33.000 on every frame
   of a stepped run, resumed frames included - see frame-loop.txt
   1.4 for the mechanism and why a boundary correction was not
   enough. LEAN IS THEREFORE COMPARABLE BETWEEN THE TWO BUILDS,
   which it was not when this section was first written.

   COMPARE THE SLIP ANGLE ANYWAY. It is what the physics produces
   and it is dt-independent, so it stays the sounder of the two.

   ONLY THE LEAN WAS EVER AFFECTED. The drivetrain reads the raw
   delta, not the smoothed one, so the acceleration curve is
   unchanged by any of this - a straight-line run measured before
   and after the clock fix produced identical speeds to five
   decimal places at 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 and 60 frames. Straight-
   line figures recorded in earlier sessions stand.  [verified]

   THE TWO DO AGREE under the frame-locked harness, measured: the
   global reads 15.9999962 and a hook on racer_lean_atan reports our
   divisor as exactly 16 on every call. The lean defect that remains
   on the dirt bike is in yawDelta, not in the divisor - journal 534.

   through EDI, so a displacement search for [reg + 0x18c] structurally

9.66.  THE REMOUNT - TIMING, AND WHY OURS CREPT

9.66.1.  The delay is three seconds, with a four-second ceiling

   flCrashTimerMs starts at 400.0 (CRASH_TIMER_START, 0x43c80000 at
   0x00467e6f) and maybe_Racer_UpdateCrash subtracts frameDelta from
   it every tick.  There are TWO exits:

       timer < 0                        forced remount
       riderSpeed < 1 km/h AND
       timer <= 100 (CRASH_HOLD_MS)     the normal one

   g_frameDeltaScaled IS 3.3 PER TICK at 33 ms a tick (9.17.2), so:

       normal   400 -> 100  = 300 units = 90.9 ticks = 3.00 s
       ceiling  400 ->   0  = 400 units =  121 ticks = 4.00 s

   THE NAME IS WRONG AND IT MATTERS.  flCrashTimerMs is NOT in
   milliseconds - 3.3 units per 33 ms is ONE UNIT PER 10 ms, which
   CRASH_DT_SCALE confirms independently: frameDelta * 0.01 is the
   tick in SECONDS, so a unit is a centisecond.  Reading 400 as
   milliseconds gives 0.4 s and a delay four hundred per cent short.

   So the wait is a FIXED three seconds in every ordinary crash - the
   rider has long stopped sliding by then, and the timer is what the
   remount is actually waiting on.  Only a rider still moving after
   three seconds is held to the four-second ceiling.

   THE PORT CAN SHORTEN IT for testing: [OPTIONS_CUSTOM]
   CrashRecoveryMs replaces the 400 with its own value in ms, -1
   keeping the built-in.  It sets the CEILING, so the normal remount
   stays a second earlier.  config-file.txt.

9.66.2.  The bike comes back dead, and why ours crept

   Racer_ResetCrash's human arm is five writes and three calls
   [verified, 0x004681f6..0x0046822a]:

       f_0180 = 0                          racer+0x180
       speed  = 0                          racer+0x124
       Racer_SetLongSpeed(instance, 0)     0x00468208
       Gearbox_SyncRoadSpeed(instance)     0x0046820f
       engineSpeed = Gearbox_SpeedToEngine(&gearboxRoadSpeed, gear,
                                           engineSpeed)
       flCrashTimerMs = 0
       crashLatch     = 0
       g_nCrashResetSignal = 1

   THE MIDDLE CALL IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS.  Zeroing speed on its own
   does not survive: physics_drivetrain_frame rebuilds speed FROM
   gearboxRoadSpeed on the following frame, so a stale road speed
   hands the entire crash speed straight back and the bike creeps off
   under the rider.  Gearbox_SpeedToEngine reads the same field, so
   the engine comes back off idle with it.

   THE PORT HAD MADE THIS EXACT OMISSION ONCE BEFORE, in the
   wall-impact tail - 9.18.23.3 - where it gave back seven metres a
   second across a glancing strike.  ANY path that moves speed
   without going through the gearbox needs the sync after it; a
   missing one is the first thing to suspect whenever the port zeroes
   a speed and it returns.

   IT MUST LAND ON THE RACER, NOT ON MR_Drivetrain.  The port's
   mirror is OUR invention - Racer_ResetCrash writes the racer and the
   model instance, and has nothing to mirror.  Adding the sync to the
   mirror alone changed nothing at all, because the reset now runs
   AFTER race_tick_physics has done its drive_store, and the
   drive_load on the next line reloads the mirror FROM the racer.  The
   zeroes were discarded twice over and the remount still handed back
   18.3.

   MEASURED on the port across the remount frame:

        n | latch  crashTmr   r.speed   r.gbRoad
       47 |   1     79.90      0.0014   18.3000
       51 |   1     53.50      0.0006   18.3000
       52 |   0      0.00     18.1617   18.1617

   r.speed decays correctly to nothing while r.gbRoad sits FROZEN for
   the whole crash - the drivetrain is skipped, so nothing decays it -
   and the first post-remount frame rebuilds speed straight out of it.

   THE GENERAL RULE, and this is the second time it has cost a
   session: WHENEVER A FIX WRITES A DRIVE FIELD, CHECK WHICH SIDE OF
   drive_store IT RUNS ON.  Before it, write the mirror; after it,

9.66.3.  The rider slide drag is CORRECT - the gap is his launch height

   Traced frame by frame through a shallow-angle crash, walls on both
   sides [verified]:

        n |  MOTO riderSpd |  ours riderSpd
       13 |     10.49011   |    10.49000    airborne, matched
       14 |     10.21511   |    10.48393    LANDING
       15 |      9.94011   |    10.20893
       16 |      9.66511   |     9.93393

   THE CONSTANT IS RIGHT.  Both shed exactly 0.275 a frame once down -
   two ticks of dt * CRASH_GROUND_DRAG, 0.033 * -4.1667 = -0.1375 -
   and the whole airborne arc agrees to 0.0002.  There is nothing
   wrong with the drag.

   OURS LANDS ONE TICK LATE, and keeps the 0.269 that tick would have
   taken for the rest of the slide.  The ground drag is 7.5x the air
   drag (-4.1667 against -0.5556), so WHEN he lands dominates, and the
   landing is picked by riderPosition.y >= (int)groundY + 8.

   THE HEIGHT IS THE CAUSE, AND IT IS SEEDED AT THE CRASH.  Measured
   on the crash frame itself, with the two BIKES in the same place to
   three decimals (-1248.000, 0, 11013.3):

       rider.y above the bike    MOTO 50.083    ours 48.323

   1.76 units, which compounds to a constant 4.43 across the arc -
   same shape, same launch direction to five decimals, just offset -
   and so crosses the 8-unit clearance a tick later.

   SO THIS IS NOT A CRASH DEFECT AT ALL.  It is the rider's world
   height under a FULL WHEELIE, which is the skeleton, and the bike
   itself is exactly where MOTO's is.  Not worth chasing from the
   crash side.  [open - rider height at full wheelie]
