MotoRacer PC Reverse Engineering                           Analysis Note


                       THE RACE CAMERA


Abstract

   How the game aims the view during a race. Covers the camera
   record, the mode dispatch, and what is and is not yet known
   about the chase camera itself.

   The short version: the camera record is bigger than
   MR_ModelInstance.txt 3.3 described, the race uses MODE 7, and the
   camera's famous "settle" is three different lerp rates applied to
   different components of the eye and the target.

0.  CORRECTION: the rotation matrix in 4.5 is TRANSPOSED

   The composed matrix recorded at 0x0046fbfe as

       [  ca, 0, sa ]        [ ca, 0, -sa ]
       [   0, 1,  0 ]   is   [  0, 1,   0 ]
       [ -sa, 0, ca ]        [ sa, 0,  ca ]

   read the other way round. Under the engine's row-vector
   convention the recorded form mirrors the camera about the track
   axis.

   IT IS INVISIBLE IN THE MATHS. y passes through either way, a
   rotation cannot change the boom length, and both offsets the
   camera rotates - the eye (0, 64, boom) and the target
   (0, -160, 1680) - have x = 0. So the only component the error can
   reach is X, and a camera sitting on the wrong side of the bike
   still tracks it, still sits at the right height and still holds
   the right distance.

   CAUGHT AGAINST MOTO.EXE, both after the same 20-frame left from a
   reset, relative to the bike:

   +----------+---------------------------+----------+
   |          | eye                       | distance |
   +==========+===========================+==========+
   | MOTO.EXE | (+306.43, +216.72, -373.8)|   529.68 |
   +----------+---------------------------+----------+
   | port     | (-386.17, +254.45, -284.8)|   543.11 |
   +----------+---------------------------+----------+

   Same length, X negated. The target showed it too: -1115 against
   +1352.

   THE LESSON IS ABOUT WHAT A LIVE READ PROVES. 4.5 was right that
   the numbers came out of the running game rather than off a
   whiteboard, and still had them the wrong way round, because
   reading a matrix out of memory does not record which way it is
   indexed. A rotation matrix and its transpose differ only in signs
   that a symmetric input cannot expose.

1.  The camera record

   An array of 0x238-byte records based at g_cameraRecords
   (0x005161c0), indexed by DAT_004dc0f8 [verified]. Section 3.3 of
   structs/MR_ModelInstance.txt had the last two of these; the rest
   come from reading FUN_004700e0 and FUN_0046f840 and are confirmed
   against a live race.

   +--------+-----------+-------------------------------------------+
   | Offset | Type      | Meaning                                   |
   +========+===========+===========================================+
   | +0x00  | ptr       | the racer record being followed           |
   +--------+-----------+-------------------------------------------+
   | +0x04  | int       | that racer's index                        |
   +--------+-----------+-------------------------------------------+
   | +0x08  | int       | THE CAMERA MODE - 3, 6 or 7               |
   +--------+-----------+-------------------------------------------+
   | +0x68  | float[12] | view matrix, 3x3 then translation at +0x8c|
   +--------+-----------+-------------------------------------------+
   | +0x98  | float[3]  | the eye. This is g_cameraPos              |
   +--------+-----------+-------------------------------------------+
   | +0xa4  | float[3]  | the look-at TARGET                        |
   +--------+-----------+-------------------------------------------+
   | +0xc8  | float[3]  | the PREVIOUS eye, kept for the smoothing  |
   +--------+-----------+-------------------------------------------+

   o READ LIVE, mid-race, to check all of the above at once:

         +0x00  0x00584445 = 0x0058123c + 1 * 0x3209
         +0x04  1
         +0x08  7
         +0x98  (-128.00, 254.45, 1578.18)
         +0xa4  (-128.00, -160.00, 3738.00)

     The +0x00 pointer resolving to exactly racer 1 confirms both the
     racer base (0x0058123c) and the 0x3209 stride, and +0x98 matches
     an independently captured camera position. [verified]

   o THE TARGET IS BELOW THE GROUND. -160 on y, against an eye at
     +254. That is not a bug; the camera aims at a point under the
     track surface well ahead of the bike, which is what tips the
     view down without pointing it at the bike.

1.1.  The view variant tables - three arrays and one index

   The record carries EIGHT CAMERA PLACEMENTS, selected by one index
   at +0x21c, and both the chase camera and the replay director read
   them. This is 228 bytes of the record, and it was the largest
   single unmapped block in it.

   +--------+--------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | Offset | Type         | Meaning                                 |
   +========+==============+=========================================+
   | +0x11c | float[8][3]  | camera OFFSET, in bike-local space      |
   +--------+--------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | +0x17c | float[8][3]  | camera ANGLES, euler radians            |
   +--------+--------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | +0x1dc | float[8]     | a scalar per variant, all 64.0          |
   +--------+--------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | +0x21c | int          | THE VARIANT, 0..7 - indexes all three   |
   +--------+--------------+-----------------------------------------+

   All four are code-derived, not inferred from the data [verified]:

       0046f390   MOV  ECX,[ESI+0x21c]              the variant
       0046f396   LEA  EDX,[ECX+ECX*2]              variant * 3
       0046f399   FLD  [ESI+EDX*4+0x17c]            angle[v].x
       0046f3a0   FMUL [0x004d13a0]                 radians -> index
       0046f3ad   CALL __ftol
       0046f3b4   SUB  EAX,0x1000                   the cos->sin shift
       0046f3b9   AND  ECX,0x3fff                   index g_cosTable

       0046fbfe   MOV  EDX,[ESI+0x21c]              Camera_UpdateChase
       0046fc04   FLD  [ESI+EDX*4+0x1dc]            scalar[v], stride 4
       0046fc0b   LEA  EBX,[EDX+EDX*2]
       0046fc0e   MOV  EAX,[ESI+EBX*4+0x11c]        offset[v].x

   0x4000 cosTable entries span 2pi, so 0x1000 is pi/2 - the shift
   that turns a cosine lookup into a sine. Three angles through that
   path is a euler-to-matrix build.

1.2.  The eight placements, read live

   +---+----------------------+---------------------+---------------+
   | v | offset  (x, y, z)    | angles (x, y, z)    | what it is    |
   +===+======================+=====================+===============+
   | 0 | (   0, -160, 1680)   | (0, 0, 0)           | far ahead,    |
   |   |                      |                     | below, facing |
   |   |                      |                     | forward       |
   +---+----------------------+---------------------+---------------+
   | 1 | (   0,  160,  -20)   | (0, pi, 0)          | on the bike,  |
   |   |                      |                     | looking BACK  |
   +---+----------------------+---------------------+---------------+
   | 2 | (-250,  150,    0)   | (0, pi/2, 0)        | left side     |
   +---+----------------------+---------------------+---------------+
   | 3 | ( 250,  150,    0)   | (0, -pi/2, 0)       | right side    |
   +---+----------------------+---------------------+---------------+
   | 4 | ( -70,  120,  200)   | (0, pi, 0)          | rear left     |
   +---+----------------------+---------------------+---------------+
   | 5 | (  70,  120,  200)   | (0, pi, 0)          | rear right    |
   +---+----------------------+---------------------+---------------+
   | 6 | (   0,  400,  400)   | (-pi/4, pi, 0)      | high behind,  |
   |   |                      |                     | pitched down  |
   +---+----------------------+---------------------+---------------+
   | 7 | (   0,  500,  500)   | (-pi/4, pi, 0)      | higher, same  |
   +---+----------------------+---------------------+---------------+

   o THE TWO TABLES AGREE WITH EACH OTHER, which is what makes this
     more than a pair of coincidental arrays. A camera 250 units to
     the LEFT is yawed +pi/2 to look right; the one 250 to the RIGHT
     is yawed -pi/2. The two placed 400 and 500 units up and behind
     are both pitched down a quarter turn. Nothing had to be assumed
     to line them up - the code indexes both with the same value.

   o THE OFFSETS ARE BIKE-LOCAL. Shot 5 loads the bike root matrix
     from racer+0x2e55 with Matrix34_Copy2, transforms
     offset[v] through it, and adds the bike position. So these
     are a boom, not a world position.

   o SHOT 5 CANNOT PICK VARIANTS 0 OR 1. Its chooser is

         do { v = rand() & 7; } while (v < 2);

     so the two on-bike placements are reserved for something else.
     CLOSED IN 1.17: they are the player LOOK placements, v0 forward
     and v1 backward, and camera+0x21c is the state of the look-back
     key. The cockpit guess was wrong (1.13) and the answer was in
     the input layer, not the camera.

   o THE 64.0 ARRAY IS UNIFORM and therefore says nothing about what
     it means. Eight identical values is what a table looks like
     before anything varies it, so it may be per-variant data the
     shipped tracks never differentiate. [open]

   o SLOTS 2 AND 3 ARE A USABLE SIDE CAMERA and nothing in the
     game reaches them - only 0 and 1 have a key. Writing 2 into
     camera+0x21c is the whole hack: mrhook puts it on "1" and
     re-asserts it, and tools/mr_cheats.py --on cam90 does it
     once. It is the only angle the fork steering and the wheelie
     pivot can be watched from, and it is what our port draws for
     its own "1" key.

     THE PORT MUST NOT ROUTE IT THROUGH THE CHASE CAMERA, and this
     is why the slot has no lerp and no boom. Camera_UpdateChase
     settles its eye at 0.2 a frame and then re-normalises the
     distance; the lag that produces points BACKWARD along the
     direction of travel, so a sideways offset fed through it is
     dragged round behind the bike as speed rises until only the
     rear wheel is in frame. The look slot places the eye outright
     from the bike every frame, which is what keeps it locked on.

     DO NOT REACH FOR camera+0x3c INSTEAD. It looks like the view
     angle because Input_OnKeyDown writes pi into it in the same
     breath as it writes 1 into +0x21c, and it is not: the look
     branch never reads it, and on the chase path it shortens the
     boom. Setting it to pi/2 gives slot 1 - a flat 180 - with
     the pi/2 discarded.

1.3.  Four more scalars from the shot chooser

   +--------+---------------------------------------------------+
   | +0x28  | ORBIT YAW, radians. The shot chooser writes pi or |
   |        | -pi here on a coin flip for shot 2, pins it to pi |
   |        | for shot 3, and zeroes it for shots 1 and 5.      |
   +--------+---------------------------------------------------+
   | +0x3c  | zeroed when shot 1 is entered                     |
   +--------+---------------------------------------------------+
   | +0x58  | ORBIT RADIUS. Shots 2 and 3 multiply it by the    |
   |        | cosTable pair to build the orbit offset.          |
   +--------+---------------------------------------------------+
   | +0x220 | zeroed at the top of Camera_UpdateTrackside       |
   +--------+---------------------------------------------------+

   o WHICH CLOSES THE "orbit, angle out of g_cosTable" LINE of 5.2.
     Shot 2 orbits at the angle in +0x28 and the radius in +0x58;
     shot 3 is the same code with the angle pinned. The coin flip
     between pi and -pi is why an orbit can start from either side.

1.4.  Coverage after the variant tables

   The record is 0x238 = 568 bytes. Section 1 had 96 of them. With
   1.1 to 1.3 it is 340, or 60 per cent. What is left is mostly the
   +0xd4 block, +0x100..+0x11c, and the tail past +0x220 - where
   +0x228, +0x22c and +0x230 read 128.0, 256.0 and 512.0, a
   power-of-two ladder that looks like distance thresholds. [open]

1.5.  The four frustum planes at +0xd4

   Sixty-four bytes at +0xd4 are FOUR PLANES, each a unit normal
   followed by a distance [verified by arithmetic on a live record]:

   +--------+------------------------------------+--------------+
   | Offset | normal (x, y, z)                   | d            |
   +========+====================================+==============+
   | +0xd4  | (-0.039617, -0.084618, -0.995626)  |    7653.947  |
   +--------+------------------------------------+--------------+
   | +0xe4  | (-0.039617, -0.084618, -0.995626)  |  -10346.052  |
   +--------+------------------------------------+--------------+
   | +0xf4  | (-0.548060, -0.072559, -0.833286)  |   -6113.086  |
   +--------+------------------------------------+--------------+
   | +0x104 | ( 0.480118, -0.072559, -0.874198)  |   19239.498  |
   +--------+------------------------------------+--------------+

   Evaluating N.eye + d for each, with the eye read from +0x98 of
   the same record:

       +0xd4       0.000        passes through the eye
       +0xe4  -17999.999        18000 units in front of it
       +0xf4       0.000        passes through the eye
       +0x104      0.000        passes through the eye

   THREE PLANES THROUGH THE EYE AND ONE 18000 AHEAD IS A VIEW
   FRUSTUM - near, left, right, far. Nothing else has that shape.

   o AND THE NEAR/FAR NORMAL IS THE VIEW MATRIX'S THIRD COLUMN, bit
     for bit. Column 2 of the matrix at +0x68 is
     (-0.039617, -0.084618, -0.995626) and so is the normal at
     +0xd4 - not approximately, identically. That is the camera's
     forward axis, which is exactly what a near and a far plane
     share.

   o THERE ARE NO TOP OR BOTTOM PLANES. Four is the whole set, so
     the culling is horizontal only. For a game whose tracks are
     long ribbons that is the cheap and correct choice, and a
     reconstruction should not "fix" it into six.

   o THE ARITHMETIC IS THE EVIDENCE HERE, not a disassembly. Three
     independent dot products landing on 0.000 and one on exactly
     -18000.000 is not something a wrong reading produces.

   o THE CONSUMER IS maybe_Racer_CullSphere (0x0046e8b0), which
     tests all four with Plane_Distance (0x004b10c0) against the
     object radius scaled by -4.0:

         0x0046e945   +0xd4      0x0046e96c   +0xe4
         0x0046e993   +0x104     0x0046e9af   +0xf4

     NOTE THE ORDER - the third test is +0x104 and the fourth is
     +0xf4, which is not address order. The first two reject
     outright; the last two must BOTH pass.  [verified]

     The producer is still unlocated. FUN_0046d680 is the camera
     constructor and default-constructs four plane objects here via
     FUN_004b0f10, which confirms the block is four planes, but it
     does not compute them.  [open]

1.6.  Camera_InitRecord - the authority on which fields exist

   Camera_InitRecord (0x0046d760, __fastcall) fills a fresh record,
   writing every dword by hand. That makes it a better field
   inventory than any sweep: a dword it never writes is per-frame
   state, and a dword it writes is a field with a default.

   It builds the eight placements of 1.2 as alternating pairs - a
   Vec3_Copy of the offset, then a call of FUN_0046d4f0 with the
   angles, then the 64.0 scalar - and every constant matches what
   the live record reads back.

   Defaults worth recording [verified]:

   +--------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   | +0x0c  | 8       | reads 2 in a race [open]               |
   +--------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   | +0x30  | -1      | [open]                                 |
   +--------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   | +0x114 | 5500    | an INT, not a float [open]             |
   +--------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   | +0x118 | 3500    | an INT [open]                          |
   +--------+---------+----------------------------------------+
   | +0x228 | 128.0   | a power-of-two ladder. No reader found |
   | +0x22c | 256.0   | by displacement search, and record 0's |
   | +0x230 | 512.0   | absolute addresses have no xrefs.      |
   |        |         | [open]                                 |
   +--------+---------+----------------------------------------+

   o WHAT IT DOES NOT WRITE IS AS USEFUL. The view matrix at +0x68,
     the eye at +0x98, the target at +0xa4, the previous eye at
     +0xc8 and the four planes at +0xd4 are absent from the
     constructor - all per-frame state, which is consistent with
     everything section 4 established about the settle.

1.8.  A poison sweep on LIVE gameplay

   1.7 said most of the remainder reads zero in every sample, and
   that the technique of structs/MR_Racer.txt 21 applied. It does.
   116 zero bytes were filled with 0xA5 while an autoplay demo was
   running with the trackside director cutting between shots, and
   read back after several cuts [verified, 2026-09-05].

   96 OF 116 SURVIVED. Five dwords were written, and two of them
   were already documented - +0x28, the orbit yaw of 1.3, and +0x3c,
   which shot 1 zeroes. The other three are new.

   o THIS IS THE FIRST POISON SWEEP THIS PROJECT HAS RUN ON LIVE
     PLAY rather than a replay. There is no determinism to protect,
     so the only cost of a wrong guess is a visibly broken camera -
     which is itself the reader test. Nothing looked wrong, and 96
     bytes carried garbage through several cuts with no effect.

1.9.  camera+0x10 .. +0x1f is a screen bounding rectangle

   A watchpoint on +0x1c caught sixteen hits across six instructions
   in one function, and the function is unambiguous [verified]:

       +0x10   int   min X
       +0x14   int   min Y
       +0x18   int   max X
       +0x1c   int   max Y

   maybe_Camera_BuildScreenBounds (0x0046e620, __fastcall) seeds min
   and max from one projected point, folds in three more with a pair
   of compares each, adds 1 to both maxima, clamps both minima to
   >= 0, then swaps any pair that came out inverted.

   o THE ORDER IS LOAD-BEARING. The clamp runs BEFORE the swap, so
     the swap can put max+1 back into the min slot and a negative
     survives. That is not hypothetical: the sweep read +0x10 at
     -42255 and +0x14 at -2212 in a live frame. A reconstruction
     that clamps after swapping produces different numbers.

   o IT RUNS DURING RENDERING, NOT CAMERA SELECTION. The only caller
     is FUN_0046e320 and its only caller is maybe_Game_RenderFrame.

   o WHAT THE RECTANGLE IS FOR IS NOT ESTABLISHED. Clamping to >= 0
     and adding one to the maxima is the shape of a clip or dirty
     rectangle, and a camera is a plausible owner of a viewport -
     but no consumer has been found and 0x18 is far too common a
     displacement to search for. FUN_0046e320 is where to look.
     [inferred, weakly - do not build on it]

1.9.9.  THE CRASH CAMERA - the eye stops, the aim follows

   Camera_UpdateChase wraps its ENTIRE chase path - boom, settle,
   pull-back, Camera_LookAt - in a test of the viewed racer's
   crashLatch at 0x0046f88a (racer+0x30c1) [verified].  The else arm
   is three calls:

       Vec3_Add   (t, racer+0x30 position, racer+0x30b5 riderPosition)
       Vec3_Divide(t, t, 2.0)                      0x0046d590
       Camera_LookAt(camera, t)

   THE EYE IS NEVER WRITTEN on that arm.  It stays wherever the last
   chase frame left it - at the impact - and only the aim moves,
   tracking the MIDPOINT of the bike and the rider as the two
   separate.  The camera stops at the crash and watches it happen.

   MEASURED over sixteen frames after a shallow-angle crash:

       MOTO   eye held (-1172.6, 10513.1) TO THE DIGIT while the bike
              slid from 506 to 1819 units away from it
       port   chased, holding a constant 483 units behind, the eye
              travelling 1302 units

   Vec3_Divide computes 1.0/k once and multiplies.  1.0/2.0 is exact
   in binary, so a port writing * 0.5f has the same number and not an
   approximation.

   VERIFIED with the gate in place - both eyes hold to the digit for
   fourteen frames, MOTO at (-1174.4, 10537.8) and the port at
   (-1168.7, 10510.0), and the distance-to-bike curves stay within
   about 25 units of each other the whole way out.  The 28 units
   between the two freeze points is the run-in drift, not the camera:
   they reach the rail at slightly different places for the reason
   racers.txt 9.65.1 gives.

1.10.  camera+0xc0 is the camera's yaw

   Sixteen hits, every one at the same instruction [verified]:

       0046e2ff   CALL FUN_00490220
       0046e304   FSTP float ptr [ESI+0xc0]

   It is the LAST thing Camera_LookAt does - build the view matrix
   into +0x68, then store an angle. Matrix34_YawOf is the same helper
   Camera_UpdateChase and maybe_Racer_BeginCrash call, and ECX here is
   camera+0x68 - the VIEW MATRIX, not the camera. Values read -3.1018,
   1.4400 and -0.5749 across three samples, always inside [-pi, pi].

   IT IS THE VIEW'S HEADING, NEGATED. Recomputing Matrix34_YawOf's
   general form offline from the stored matrix matches the stored
   angle to five decimals at four headings: 0.00000, -0.61447,
   -1.29248, -1.99184. Each is minus atan2(row2.x, row2.z) to within
   a tenth of a degree, the difference being the pitch.

   THE CONSUMER IS THE PARTICLE BILLBOARD. maybe_Fx_DrawParticle reads
   it at 0x004b0dd1 and builds RotY of it, which - because the angle
   is negated - CANCELS the view's yaw and leaves only the pitch, so
   the quad faces the camera. See fx-particles.txt 7.4.

1.11.  Two fields nothing reads, confirmed twice

   +0x114 (5500) and +0x228 (128.0) were watched in mode=rw for the
   whole sweep and took ZERO HITS - not read, not written, through
   several camera cuts.

   o WHICH MATTERS BECAUSE THE STATIC SEARCH HAD ALREADY FAILED to
     find a reader for either, and a failed search proves nothing on
     its own: a read through a pointer is invisible to a
     displacement search. The watchpoint is the instrument that CAN
     see one, and it saw nothing. Two methods with different blind
     spots agreeing is worth more than either alone.

   o SO THE POWER-OF-TWO LADDER AT +0x228 IS DEAD DATA in a race.
     Written once by Camera_InitRecord and never looked at again.

1.12.  Coverage, and what is still zero

   About 460 of 568 bytes, or 81 per cent.

1.13.1.  F5 IS A DISTANCE LADDER - the four chase views

   Camera_UpdateChase takes its boom from a four-entry table in the
   record, indexed by a slot the player cycles [verified]:

       0046f926   MOV EAX, [ESI + 0x220]              nViewSlot
       0046f92f   FLD  float ptr [ESI+EAX*4 + 0x224]  pViewBoom[slot]
       0046f936   FADD float ptr [ESI + 0x58]         + flOrbitRadius

   Camera_InitRecord seeds the table, and Input_OnKeyDown case 0x74
   is the only other writer of either field:

       nViewSlot  +0x220  int       0
       pViewBoom  +0x224  float[4]  0, 128, 256, 512

       00432096   MOV ECX, [EAX + 0x5163e0]    camera[active]+0x220
       0043209c   INC ECX
       0043209d   AND ECX, 0x3
       004320a0   MOV [EAX + 0x5163e0], ECX

   With the flOrbitRadius of -484 a live race reads - 4.4 - the four
   booms are

       slot 0   -484     the default chase
       slot 1   -356
       slot 2   -228
       slot 3    +28

   o SLOT 3'S BOOM IS POSITIVE, and that is the interesting one. The
     pull-back is eye = pos - normalise(eye - pos) * boom, so a
     positive boom puts the eye on the FAR side of the bike origin -
     about 28 units in front of it, plus the usual +191 lift. Retail's
     fourth view shows neither the rider nor the bike, which is what
     a camera slightly ahead of the rider's head looks like
     [observed], and it is NOT mode 6: F6's first person is a fifth,
     separate view.

   o THE WRITE HIDES FROM AN OFFSET SEARCH. It is encoded as
     [EAX + 0x5163e0] with g_abCameraRecords (0x005161c0) folded into
     the displacement, so a hunt for "+0x220" returns only
     Camera_InitRecord, Camera_UpdateTrackside and the read in
     Camera_UpdateChase - which is how F5 looked like it had no
     writer at all.

   o IT SURVIVES A RACE. Camera_InitRecord is reached only from
     FUN_0046d680, off the Decor_Draw / FUN_00403190 / FUN_00413d60 /
     FUN_00413d80 init chain and not from a race start, and the only
     other zeroing writer is Camera_UpdateTrackside. So the player's
     chosen distance persists across restarts.

   o BOTH KEYS END IN maybe_Camera_SignalCut (0x0046d750), which
     sets DAT_004dc138 = 1 - a cut notification read by
     maybe_Snd_TickRace and maybe_Game_Tick [inferred].

   RECONSTRUCTED: race_camera_cycle_view in src/race.c, on '5'
   because F5 is the port's frame stepper. CAM_BOOM_TABLE there is
   this table.

1.14.  camera+0x34 is nSelectFlag - THE FINISH CAMERA

   It is a boolean that says the viewed racer has FINISHED, and
   under it the chase camera pulls in and swings round as the bike
   coasts to a stop.  That is the pan a retail recording shows after
   the line [verified].

   TWO WRITERS, BOTH IN Camera_SelectView (0x004583e0), and both on
   an arm that goes on to select mode 7 - so the finish uses the
   SAME camera a live race does, with one field flipped:

       raceState == 2    nSelectFlag = 1      finished
       otherwise         nSelectFlag = 0      racing, and the grid

   A live race therefore never sets it, which is why an earlier
   sweep found it 0 at all sixteen hits and recorded one writer and
   a guess.  The set path needs a lap to be completed first.

   THREE TERMS IN Camera_UpdateChase (0x0046f840) ARE GATED ON IT:

     1  THE BOOM PULLS IN, over time rather than at once.
        flLookYaw (+0x3c) ramps by the frame delta while it is
        above [0x004d13e8], stepping by [0x004d13f0], and the boom
        is then shortened by

            flOrbitRadius * [0x004d1400] * flLookYaw * [0x004d13f8]

     2  THE AIM POINT CHANGES.  The racing arm builds the target
        from pLookOffset[nLookBackKey] rotated into world space;
        under the flag it is matrix row 1 scaled by [0x004d1354],
        added to the racer position.

     3  THE EYE DROPS, by

            flLookYaw * [0x004d13f8] * flUnk54 * [0x004d1410]

        subtracted after the usual flUnk54 lift.

   o flLookYaw IS NOT AN ANGLE HERE.  It is the ramp the first and
     third terms share, and the name is a leftover - Camera_
     UpdateChase's plate comment already says so.  Input_OnKeyDown
     writes pi into it beside the look-back key, which is what made
     it look like a view angle.

   o AND THIS CLOSES camera+0x54.  flUnk54 holds 191.0 and 1.3 had
     it beside the orbit radius with nothing established.  It is
     the EYE LIFT - added unconditionally - and term 3 scrubs a
     fraction of it back off as the ramp runs.  So the camera
     settles DOWN toward the bike as it closes in, which is what
     the shot looks like.

   THE CONSTANTS, AND ALL FIVE ARE CLOSED FORMS [verified]:

   +--------------+-------------------------+--------------------+
   | Address      | Value                   | What               |
   +==============+=========================+====================+
   | 0x004d13e8   | -3pi/4    (double)      | the ramp's floor   |
   +--------------+-------------------------+--------------------+
   | 0x004d13f0   | -pi/300   (double)      | the ramp's step    |
   +--------------+-------------------------+--------------------+
   | 0x004d13f8   | -4/(3pi)  (double)      | the shared scale   |
   +--------------+-------------------------+--------------------+
   | 0x004d1400   | -0.5                    | the boom's share   |
   +--------------+-------------------------+--------------------+
   | 0x004d1410   | 2/3                     | the eye drop's     |
   +--------------+-------------------------+--------------------+
   | 0x004d1354   | 64.0                    | term 2's scale -   |
   |              |                         | the eye's own lift |
   +--------------+-------------------------+--------------------+

   THE FIRST THREE ARE ONE NUMBER.  -3pi/4 * -4/(3pi) is EXACTLY 1,
   so at the end of the ramp both gated terms reduce to a plain
   multiple: the boom by -0.5 and the eye by 2/3.  That identity is
   the check that says these were read rather than fitted.

   SO THE SHOT, IN NUMBERS.  The boom runs from -484 to -726, half
   again as far back; the eye settles from +191 down to about +64,
   which is the bike's own height; and the aim moves from 1680
   units ahead of the rider onto the machine itself.  The ramp
   takes about 135 frames - 2.2 seconds at 60fps - which is the
   length of the pan in a retail recording [observed].

   IMPLEMENTED IN THE PORT.  MR_Camera carries selectFlag and
   lookYaw, race.c's camera block sets the flag from the VIEWED
   racer's raceState the way Camera_SelectView does, and
   camera_update carries the three terms.

   ONE SIMPLIFICATION.  Term 2 is matrix ROW 1 scaled by 64 plus
   the matrix translation; the port's frame is Ry alone, so row 1
   is (0,1,0) and the translation is zero, and it writes the lift
   directly.  If the unread orientation block in Camera_UpdateChase
   is ever recovered, this is one of the two places it changes.

   AND THERE IS A FOURTH TERM - THE ORBIT [inferred].  The ramp
   swings the eye ROUND the bike as well as pulling the boom out
   and dropping the eye, which is the "swings to the side and
   front" a retail recording shows.  In the original it falls out
   of that same unread composed matrix rather than standing on its
   own, which is why the first three were found and this one was
   not.

   THE EVIDENCE IS A MEASUREMENT.  With both games paused on the
   same 4th-place finish, every magnitude already agreed:

   +---------------+------------+------------+
   | Quantity      | MOTO       | port       |
   +===============+============+============+
   | boom length   |     726.8  |     726.2  |
   +---------------+------------+------------+
   | eye y         |     127.0  |     127.3  |
   +---------------+------------+------------+
   | aim y         |      64.0  |      64.0  |
   +---------------+------------+------------+
   | eye bearing   |   46.8 deg |  182.3 deg |
   +---------------+------------+------------+

   Only the bearing differed, by 135.5 degrees - and lookYaw had
   ramped to -2.3646 rad, which is -135.5 degrees.  Adding it to
   the eye's rotation reproduces MOTO's bearing to a tenth of a
   degree, and at lookYaw 0 it is the identity, so a live race is
   untouched.

   IT REMAINS AN INFERENCE.  The real block may apply the rotation
   somewhere else, or fold in a pitch this does not have.  The
   three __ftol operands are recoverable with a trace hook the way
   Dd_DrawHorizon's were, and that is what would settle it.
   [open - the composed matrix in Camera_UpdateChase]

3.  Mode 6 - THE FIRST-PERSON CAMERA, F6

   It is inline in Camera_Update rather than a function of its own,
   and it is what HELP.GEN calls "1RST PERSON VIEW" - 1.13.

3.1.  It is not the mode a race STARTS in

   Kept because mode 6 was mistaken for the chase camera once, and
   ruling it out is what found mode 7.

   A captured start grid has the eye 256 up and 480 back. Mode 6
   cannot produce that from any bike state, and reading camera+0x08
   out of a live race gives 7 directly. So mode 6 is not the race
   camera - it is the camera a race REACHES, the moment the rider
   presses F6.

3.2.  The model, read out of 0x00470190

   +----------------------------------------------------------------+
   |   k      = PersoAnim ? 10.0 : 5.0                              |
   |   eye    = pos + M * (0, 170 - k * wheelieAngle,  0)           |
   |   target = pos + M * (0, 170 + k * wheelieAngle, 40)           |
   |   roll   = lean * 0.25, in trig-table units                    |
   +----------------------------------------------------------------+

   M is racer+0xbc, matOrientation - rows then translation, ROW 1 the
   up axis and ROW 2 the forward one. So the eye sits 170 units up
   the bike's OWN up axis and the aim point sits the same 170 up plus
   40 forward: a rigid placement in bike space with no boom, no eye
   lerp and no settle. 170 is a rider's head height, and it is the
   only reason the view reads as first person at all.

   +--------------+---------------+----------------------------------+
   | Constant     | Value         | What it is                       |
   +==============+===============+==================================+
   | 0x004d1418   | 170.0         | eye height on the bike's up axis |
   +--------------+---------------+----------------------------------+
   | 0x004d141c   | 40.0          | how far ahead the aim point sits |
   +--------------+---------------+----------------------------------+
   | 0x004d1424   | 5.0           | wheelie term, target, PersoAnim 0|
   +--------------+---------------+----------------------------------+
   | 0x004d1428   | -5.0          | wheelie term, eye,    PersoAnim 0|
   +--------------+---------------+----------------------------------+
   | 0x004d1414   | 10.0          | wheelie term, target, PersoAnim  |
   +--------------+---------------+----------------------------------+
   | 0x004d1420   | -10.0         | wheelie term, eye,    PersoAnim  |
   +--------------+---------------+----------------------------------+
   | 0x004d1430   | 10/13, double | the y lerp, PersoAnim 0          |
   +--------------+---------------+----------------------------------+
   | 0x004d1408   | 0.5           | the y lerp, PersoAnim != 0       |
   +--------------+---------------+----------------------------------+
   | 0x004d13e0   | 0.25          | share of the lean the view rolls |
   +--------------+---------------+----------------------------------+
   | 0x004d13a0   | 2607.594588   | radians to trig units, double    |
   +--------------+---------------+----------------------------------+

   o THE WHEELIE PITCHES THE VIEW UP, and it is the one thing that
     moves in bike space. The eye sinks by k * wheelieAngle while the
     aim rises by the same amount, so the two effects add and the
     rider watches the sky come down as the front wheel lifts. The
     field is racer+0x307d, wheelieAngle - NOT speed. An earlier
     reading of this section said speed, which was inferred from an
     unnamed DAT rather than read.

   o IT DOES NOT DEGENERATE. At wheelieAngle 0 the eye and the aim
     differ by a pure 40 * forward, which is horizontal and non-zero,
     so Camera_LookAt's zero-distance guard never fires. This section
     used to claim a start grid under mode 6 would keep a stale
     matrix; that followed from reading the 40 as belonging to the
     eye rather than the target.

   o ONLY Y IS SMOOTHED, against the previous frame's eye and target.
     The original lerps all three components of both and two of the
     three factors are 1.0, so the compiler emitted (v - prev) + prev
     for x and z. That is a DIFFERENT scheme from mode 7's three
     rates - do not carry one into the other.

   o TWO CODE BLOCKS, ONE MODEL. The PersoAnim arm builds the
     bike-local vector and calls Vec3_TransformByMatrix34; the other
     unrolls the same product by hand. Only k and the y lerp differ,
     which is why src/race.c folds them into one path.

   o 0x004d13a0 IS THE COS TABLE'S OWN 2pi. It reads
     2607.594587618272, which is 0x4000 / 6.283185307178 to the last
     bit and not 0x4000 / the true 2pi (2607.5945876176133). Same
     typed constant Trig_BuildCosTable uses - trig.h.

3.2.1.  THE ROLL IS APPLIED TO THE VIEW MATRIX, not to the eye

   After Camera_LookAt, 0x00470554 onward post-multiplies the view by
   a rotation and then re-reads the yaw:

       view = view * Rz(lean * 0.25)
       camera+0xc0 = Matrix34_YawOf(view)

   so the picture rotates and the eye does not move. A QUARTER of the
   lean, not all of it - the rider's head stays more upright than the
   bike.

   o THE ORIGINAL BUILDS A FULL THREE-ANGLE EULER THERE and two of
     the three angles are literally zero. The tell is the folding: it
     reads g_cosTable[0] (1.0) and g_cosTable[0x3000] (sin of 0) as
     bare absolute addresses instead of indexing. What survives is a
     plane rotation, and nothing says the other two axes are reachable
     - do not generalise the surviving form.

   o THE YAW READS THE SAME EITHER WAY. Rz mixes x and y and leaves
     every z alone, and the yaw is atan2 of two z components, so the
     order Camera_LookAt -> roll -> Matrix34_YawOf is reproduced for
     fidelity rather than because it changes a number.

3.2.2.  Two things suppress it, and both are in the CALLER

   +----------------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | Gate                 | Where                                     |
   +======================+===========================================+
   | viewed racer crashed | Camera_SelectView 0x004583e1 - it only    |
   |                      | asks for mode 6 when racer+0x30c1,        |
   |                      | crashLatch, is zero                       |
   +----------------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | TAB held             | Camera_Update 0x004701b5 - nLookBackKey   |
   |                      | non-zero falls THROUGH to mode 7          |
   +----------------------+-------------------------------------------+

   So a crash hands the rider the chase camera's crash shot and first
   person comes back when they get up; and holding the rear-view key
   in first person gives the chase camera looking back. Neither is a
   mode change - mode 6 simply is not selected, or is not taken.

3.3.  Reconstructed in src/race.c

   camera_first_person is the mode 6 arm and camera_apply_roll is the
   0x00470554 block. race_camera does Camera_SelectView's crashLatch
   test and zeroes MR_Camera.roll on every other path, which the
   original does not need to do because it holds no such field -
   Camera_LookAt rebuilds the whole matrix each frame.

   ON '6' AND NOT F6. F5 is the port's frame stepper, so '5' and '6'
   stand in for the two camera keys until that moves.

   NOT RECONSTRUCTED: the rear-view key, so the TAB gate of 3.2.2 has
   nothing to test yet. [open]

4.  Mode 7 - the race chase camera

   FUN_0046f840. This is the one. It is a large function and is NOT
   yet fully decoded; what follows is the shape, not a transcription.

   o param_1[0x87] SELECTS A VARIANT - the in-race view toggle. Zero
     takes the chase path below; non-zero takes a different path that
     copies a matrix from the racer at +0x2e31 and composes it, which
     is presumably the cockpit or bumper view.

   o THE BOOM LENGTH is param_1[param_1[0x88] + 0x89] + param_1[0x16]
     - a table of distances indexed by a selector, plus a base. So
     the chase distance is a SETTING, not a constant.

   o IT IS PULLED BACK ALONG A NORMALISED DIRECTION at the end:

         d   = normalise(eye - racer.pos)
         eye = racer.pos - d * boom

     which is the classic boom, and explains why the camera keeps a
     fixed distance rather than a fixed offset.

   o A WHEELIE OR BRAKE TERM. When param_1[0xd] is non-zero the boom
     is shortened by a term in param_1[0xf], which is itself ramped
     each frame by param_2 (the frame delta) - a camera that pulls in
     under some condition and eases back out.

4.1.  The settle, measured

   THE CAMERA'S SETTLE IS THREE DIFFERENT LERP RATES, not one. Every
   one of them is a plain "move this fraction of the way there each
   frame" against the value saved from last frame [verified]:

   +------------------+------------+-----------------------------+
   | Component        | Constant   | Rate                        |
   +==================+============+=============================+
   | eye x, y, z      | 0x004d1404 | 0.2                         |
   +------------------+------------+-----------------------------+
   | target x, z      | 0x004d1408 | 0.5                         |
   +------------------+------------+-----------------------------+
   | target y         | 0x004d140c | 0.333333                    |
   +------------------+------------+-----------------------------+

   The eye is the slowest, so the camera drifts into place while the
   aim has already mostly arrived.

   o THIS IS THE OBSERVED SETTLE. A retail recording shows the view
     easing over roughly ten frames, finishing before the announcer
     has said "three" [observed]. An eye lerping at 0.2 a frame is
     within a pixel of its target in about ten frames, which is the
     same number from the other direction.

   o MODE 6 SMOOTHS ONLY Y, at 0.769231 (0x004d1430, a double) or
     0.5 (0x004d1408) depending on the branch. Different scheme,
     different constants - do not carry one into the other.

4.2.  The three angles, and the geometry they sit in

   THE ANGLES ARE PLAIN RECORD FIELDS, not lost temporaries. The
   decompiler shows them as bare __ftol() calls only because it
   cannot track the FPU stack; the disassembly names them outright:

       0046f998   FLD [ESI+0x38]  FMUL [0x004d13a0]  CALL __ftol
       0046f9c6   FLD [ESI+0x3c]  FMUL [0x004d13a0]  CALL __ftol
       0046f9f8   FLD [ESI+0x40]  FMUL [0x004d13a0]  CALL __ftol

   0x004d13a0 is 2607.5947, the project's usual 0x4000/(2*pi) trig
   scale, so all three are RADIANS. A fourth angle - the negated
   return of FUN_00490220, called with the eye - joins them at
   0x0046faf0. [verified]

   o +0x3c IS DOUBLE-DUTY. The same field is the wheelie/brake ramp
     that shortens the boom at 0x0046f947. One float, two readers.

   READ LIVE AT THE GRID, ALL THREE ARE ZERO. That is not a failed
   measurement - it is what pins everything else down, because with
   the rotation neutral the remaining terms can be solved directly.

   +--------+------------------+---------------------------------+
   | Offset | Live value       | Meaning                         |
   +========+==================+=================================+
   | +0x34  | 0                | wheelie/brake flag              |
   +--------+------------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x38  | 0.0              | angle 1                         |
   +--------+------------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x3c  | 0.0              | angle 2, also the wheelie ramp  |
   +--------+------------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x40  | 0.0              | angle 3                         |
   +--------+------------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x54  | 191.0            | added to eye.y, unconditionally |
   +--------+------------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x58  | -484.0           | the boom BASE                   |
   +--------+------------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x11c | (0, -160, 1680)  | per-variant target offset       |
   +--------+------------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x1dc | 64.0             | per-variant scalar              |
   +--------+------------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x21c | 0                | view variant - 0 is the chase   |
   +--------+------------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x220 | 0                | index into the boom table       |
   +--------+------------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x224 | {0,128,256,512}  | THE BOOM TABLE - four distances |
   +--------+------------------+---------------------------------+

   o THE BOOM TABLE IS THE IN-RACE CAMERA DISTANCES, the four the
     view key cycles through. boom = table[+0x220] + (+0x58), which
     at index 0 is -484.

   o +0x54 IS ADDED ON BOTH PATHS. The JZ at 0x0046ffda skips only
     the extra wheelie subtraction; both edges reach the same FSTP
     at 0x0046fffc. So eye.y always gains 191.

   THE WHOLE THING SOLVED AGAINST ONE LIVE FRAME. With the bike
   parked at the grid on racer 1:

       racer.pos              (-128,    0,    2058)
       target offset +0x11c   (   0, -160,    1680)
       target = pos + offset  (-128, -160,    3738)   <- reads +0xa4

       eye (read at +0x98)    (-128,  254.45, 1578.18)
       eye - racer.pos        (   0,  254.45, -479.82)
       less the +0x54 lift    (   0,   63.45, -479.82)
       magnitude                             484.00   <- = |boom|

   Both lines close exactly, which is as good a confirmation of the
   record layout as it is of the formula. [verified]

   o AND IT EXPLAINS A LONG-STANDING FUDGE. src/menu.c's p1_camera
     was fitted to this same frame as "480 back, 256 up". It is
     neither: it is 484 along a direction, and a SEPARATE 191 lift
     applied afterwards. Close enough to look right at the grid,
     wrong everywhere else.


4.3.  The angles are ZERO in gameplay, and the yaw does the work

   THREE SAMPLES, THREE STATES, ALL ZERO. The camera was probed on
   the start grid, in a wheelie, and mid-corner with the bike
   leaned over. +0x34, +0x38, +0x3c and +0x40 read 0 in every one.

   So the three angles are NOT bike lean, NOT track banking and NOT
   the wheelie - the wheelie did not even set the +0x34 flag, which
   means whatever gates the boom-shortening path is not what riding
   on one wheel does. They are most likely a replay or cinematic
   camera's offsets.

   o WHICH IS GOOD NEWS FOR THE PORT. They were called "the whole
     of the camera's orientation and the only real gap"; they turn
     out not to block anything. Mode 7 can be implemented with all
     three at zero and be correct for ordinary play.

   o NOT PROOF THEY ARE ALWAYS ZERO. Three samples of normal riding
     is not every state. Replay, podium and the finish sequence are
     unsampled.

   THE FOURTH ANGLE IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS. Racer_GetYaw
   (0x00490220), negated at 0x0046f996, is the bike's heading, and
   with the other three at zero it is the ONLY thing orienting the
   boom while riding. It takes racer+0x98 - the racer's own 3x4
   matrix - and NOT the camera's +0x98, which is a trap worth
   naming: ECX still holds the racer record from 0x0046f881.

   Read live, mid-corner, the racer matrix is a clean rotation with
   ZERO translation, every row squaring to 1.00005:

       row0  (-0.97988,  0.0,     -0.19990)
       row1  (-0.05208,  0.96550,  0.25525)
       row2  ( 0.19301,  0.26051, -0.94601)   forward
       T     ( 0,        0,        0      )

   The zero translation collapses the function to atan2(row0.z,
   row2.z) - the textbook yaw extraction. See its plate comment.

4.4.  The boom, checked twice in different places

   +----------------+---------------------+---------------------+
   | Sample         | On the straight     | Mid-corner          |
   +================+=====================+=====================+
   | racer.pos      | (-128, 0, 19963)    | (-25553,-995,47503) |
   +----------------+---------------------+---------------------+
   | eye            | (-128, 212, 19481)  | (-25485,-852,47979) |
   +----------------+---------------------+---------------------+
   | less +0x54     | (0, 21.0, -482.6)   | (68.0,-48.3,475.6)  |
   +----------------+---------------------+---------------------+
   | magnitude      | 483.1               | 482.9               |
   +----------------+---------------------+---------------------+

   against a predicted |boom| of 484 both times. The second sample
   is the valuable one: different altitude, real lateral offset,
   bike heading -z. The first two probes were both on the same
   straight with x pinned at -128, where the model could have been
   coincidence.

   o THE 1-UNIT RESIDUAL IS NOT EXPLAINED. Most likely the eye
     sitting one 0.2-lerp step short of converged, but that is a
     hypothesis, not a measurement. An earlier note in this file
     claimed the grid sample closed EXACTLY at 484 - that used a
     racer position assumed from our own grid table rather than
     read out of the game, so the exactness was partly circular.
     Reading racer+0x0c directly is what turned it into 483.1.

   o AND THE PRE-BOOM OFFSET IS LOCAL (offset.x, +0x1dc, boom) =
     (0, 64, -484): 64 up and 484 back, before the matrix, the
     renormalisation and the +191.


4.5.  The second matrix, and the whole camera closed

   THE SECOND MATRIX IS Ry(-yaw), CONFIRMED BY READING IT. Its angle
   was traced through the FPU stack to the negated Racer_GetYaw
   result: at 0x0046fac6 an FXCH ST2 brings the parked -yaw back to
   the top, 0x0046fac8 scales it by 2607.5947 and 0x0046faf0 turns
   it into a table index. The matrix built from it is then combined
   with the three-angle matrix by Matrix34_Multiply at 0x0046fbf9.

   A hook on the 6-byte MOV at 0x0046fbfe - immediately after that
   multiply returns - captured the composed result at [ESP+0x98]:

       m00  0.728993   m01  -4e-13    m02   0.684521
       m10  ~0         m11   1.0      m12  ~0
       m20 -0.684521   m21  ~0        m22   0.728993
       t    0, 0, 0

   Every off-diagonal is ~1e-12, which is zero to float precision,
   and m11 is literally 0x3f800000. So the three-angle matrix IS
   identity when the angles are zero, and the composition is a pure
   Y rotation that leaves the local y untouched. [verified]

   o ONE HAND-TRACED SIGN WAS WRONG. The convention is
     [cos,0,sin; 0,1,0; -sin,0,cos]; a by-hand read of the FPU
     stores had m02 = -sin. That error is the reason the hook was
     used instead of more staring at FXCH.

   THE CONTRADICTION WAS THE ORDER OF OPERATIONS, NOT THE MATRIX.
   Section 4.3 recorded that rotating (0, 64, -484) by the yaw
   predicts a local y of +64 while the corner sample measured
   -48.3, and concluded the matrix was doing something unaccounted
   for. It was not. The boom renormalisation runs AFTER the 0.2
   lerp, so the direction it normalises is the LAGGED eye, not the
   raw one. The raw y is always 64; the final y equals it only once
   the camera has converged.

   PROVED ON THE ONE SAMPLE THAT HAD CONVERGED - the start grid,
   bike stationary:

       |(0, 64, -484)|        = 488.213
       boom scale 484/488.213 =   0.991368
       y = 64 * 0.991368      =  63.447
       + the +0x54 lift       = 254.447

       measured eye.y         = 254.45   (racer.pos.y = 0)

   Exact. The two moving samples missed by 42 and 112 for the same
   reason they measured 483.1 and 482.9 against a boom of 484: the
   eye was mid-lerp. [verified]

4.6.  The chase camera, whole

       boom   = boomTable[+0x220] + (+0x58)            = -484
       M      = Ry(-Racer_GetYaw(racer + 0x98))

       raw    = racer.pos + (0, +0x1dc, boom) * M
       eye    = lerp(prevEye, raw, 0.2)

       target = racer.pos + (+0x11c) * M
                lerped 0.5 on x and z, 0.333 on y

       Camera_LookAt(eye -> target)

       eye    = racer.pos - normalise(eye - racer.pos) * boom
       eye.y += (+0x54)                                 = 191

   with (+0x11c) = (0, -160, 1680) and (+0x1dc) = 64, both
   initialised from the constant block at 0x004d134c..0x004d1354.

   The three angles at +0x38/+0x3c/+0x40 are zero in every sampled
   gameplay state, so Ry(-yaw) is the entire orientation. Nothing
   in the above is fitted: every constant is read out of the binary
   or the live process.


4.7.  What is NOT reconstructed

   Everything between the boom length and the final pull-back. The
   middle of FUN_0046f840 builds a rotation from three angles that
   come out of FUN_00490220 and a run of __ftol calls the decompiler
   has lost the operands of, multiplies it by a second matrix built
   from the cosine table, and uses the result to offset the eye. That
   block has not been read.

   So the port cannot yet drive its camera from racer state. What it
   CAN do, and should, is stop pretending: src/menu.c's p1_camera
   reproduces ONE captured frame and says so.

4.8.  Reconstructed in src/race.c

   MR_Camera carries eye, viewEye, target and prevEye across frames;
   camera_update takes a racer position and a racer matrix and does
   the whole of 4.6. src/menu.c's p1_camera now feeds it the grid
   slot for racer 1 and returns what it produces.

   WHAT IT REPLACED IS THE POINT. p1_camera held three constants
   fitted to ONE paused frame - 480 back, 256 up, 1975 ahead - under
   a comment admitting they reproduced a measurement rather than
   reconstructing anything. The real camera needs none of them, and
   it explains why the fit was close but never right: the eye is 484
   along a direction and THEN lifted 191, which at the grid happens
   to land near "480 back, 256 up".

   o CHECKED AGAINST THE GAME BEFORE A LINE WAS WRITTEN. Our own
     grid slot for racer 1 is (-128, 0, 2058) with forward +Z, so
     the yaw is zero and M is identity:

         raw    = (-128, 0, 2058) + (0, 64, -484)
         eye    = pos + 484 * normalise(0, 64, -484)
                = (-128,  63.447, 1578.18)
         + 191  = (-128, 254.447, 1578.18)
         target = (-128, -160, 3738)

     against the game's (-128.00, 254.45, 1578.18) and (-128.00,
     -160.00, 3738.00). Both exact, from our own grid data.

   o AND THE BUILT CODE LOGS EXACTLY THAT:

         page1: track view - eye (-128 254 1578) at (-128 -160 3738)

     against the game's (-128.00, 254.45, 1578.18) and (-128.00,
     -160.00, 3738.00). All six values, from our own grid data
     through the reconstructed camera. [verified]

   o ONE FRAME, ONE STEP - AND THIS WILL REGRESS. The horizon and
     the 3D pass both need the camera, and while both of them
     STEPPED it every lerp ran at double rate. The target logged
     (-128, -89, 3318) where one step gives (-128, -53, 2898) and
     two give precisely -89 and 3318.

     The eye hid it completely: it snaps on the first frame and the
     raw value is static, so double-stepping a converged number is a
     no-op. Only the still-easing target showed anything.

     The original has the SAME split and no problem, because
     Camera_UpdateChase runs in the game tick and
     maybe_Game_RenderFrame only reads the record. p1_camera now
     takes an advance flag. Worth re-checking when the real game
     loop lands and page01_tick stops being the only per-frame hook.

   o TWO DEVIATIONS, both deliberate and both commented in place.
     The first frame SNAPS rather than lerping, because the original
     seeds its record in maybe_Game_InitRace and we have nowhere
     else to get a first eye. And the grid slot stands in for a
     racer record, because page 1 has no moving racer yet - same
     shape, static contents.

   o THE LOOK-AT/BOOM ORDER IS PRESERVED. camera_update hands back
     viewEye as well as eye, and page01_draw_3d builds the view
     matrix from viewEye while positioning the frame at eye. That
     reproduces the original's own inconsistency rather than tidying
     it away.


5.  THE REPLAY CAMERA - who cuts, and on what

   Section 2 listed modes 3, 6 and 7. THERE IS A FOURTH, MODE 1,
   and it is the whole replay camera. It was missed because nothing
   in a normal race ever sets it.

5.1.  Camera_SelectView picks the mode, and names the replay

   Camera_SelectView (0x004583e0) runs every frame and ends with
   two calls to Camera_SetMode (0x0046da70) [verified]:

       if (DAT_004dc014)                    target=viewed, mode 3
       else if (g_dwCockpitView active)     target=viewed, mode 6
       else if (racer[+0x2de5] == 2)        target=viewed, mode 7
       else if (countdown or DAT_004dc020)  target=viewed, mode 7
       else                                 mode 1
       Camera_SetMode(mode);

       if ((DAT_004e9830 || DAT_00504ea0 || DAT_004e8968 ||
            DAT_004dc134 || g_dwReplayMode == 1)
           && maybe_g_trkCameraCount > 0) {
           Camera_SetMode(1);
           Camera_UpdateTrackside(dt, g_viewedRacerIndex);
       }

   THE REPLAY IS NAMED EXPLICITLY. g_dwReplayMode == 1 is one of
   five conditions that switch the trackside director on, and the
   whole thing is gated on maybe_g_trkCameraCount > 0 - the count of
   CAMERA_xxx entries the track file places as decor. So a track
   with no cameras in its decor gets no trackside shots at all.

   o WHICH CLOSES subsystems/replay.txt 6.2. That section observed
     the replay cutting between an orbit and fixed trackside shots
     and connected it to the CAMERA_000 decor by name alone. This
     is the code that reads the count.

   o AND IT EXPLAINS MODE 6. DAT_004dc00c is toggled by
     Input_OnKeyDown case 0x75 (F6), and that case is gated on
     g_dwReplayMode != 1 - so the cockpit view is a live-play
     toggle a replay cannot use. Mode 6 also drops out when the
     viewed racer's crash latch racer+0x30c1 is set: you do not get
     the cockpit while you are off the bike.

5.2.  Camera_UpdateTrackside is the shot director

   Camera_UpdateTrackside (0x0046e9f0) owns two globals:

   +---------------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | g_nCameraShot       | 0x004ea5a8 - the current shot type, 0..5  |
   +---------------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | g_nCameraShotTimer  | 0x004dc13c - dt units left on it          |
   +---------------------+-------------------------------------------+

   The timer is counted down at the end of the function.  When it
   goes NEGATIVE the top of the function chooses a new shot and
   reloads it [verified]:

   IT COUNTS DOWN BY dt, NOT BY ONE, and the durations below are
   therefore in dt UNITS rather than frames [verified, 0x0046f7c8]:

       FILD  [g_nCameraShotTimer]
       FSUB  [esp+0xc0]            ; the frame delta
       __ftol, store

   So a shot lasts the same WALL-CLOCK time whatever the frame rate,
   and at the 1.667 a 60fps frame delivers, the 250 of a fixed shot
   is 150 frames - two and a half seconds.

   READING THEM AS FRAMES MAKES EVERY SHOT 1.67x TOO LONG at 60fps
   and frame-rate dependent into the bargain.  The symptom is
   specific: the camera holds a fixed trackside shot long after the
   bike has gone behind a building, because nothing tests the view
   (5.3) and only the timer can end it.

   +------+----------+------------------------------------------+
   | Shot | Duration | What it is                               |
   +======+==========+==========================================+
   |  0   |   250    | a fixed trackside camera                 |
   +------+----------+------------------------------------------+
   |  1   |   600    | plain follow - sets record mode 7        |
   +------+----------+------------------------------------------+
   |  2   |   300    | orbit, angle out of g_cosTable           |
   +------+----------+------------------------------------------+
   |  3   |   400    | orbit, angle pinned to pi (0x40490fdb)   |
   +------+----------+------------------------------------------+
   |  4   |   260    | fixed, with a computed projection zoom   |
   +------+----------+------------------------------------------+
   |  5   | 400/300  | a matrix shot off the bike root matrix   |
   +------+----------+------------------------------------------+

5.3.  It is a timer AND a position test - both, not either

   This is the part worth getting right, because either half alone
   gives the wrong model.

   THE TIMER DECIDES WHEN TO CUT. THE TRACK DECIDES WHAT TO CUT TO.
   When the timer expires the director calls Trk_FindNearestCamera
   (0x0040d4f0) and asks maybe_Vec3_Differs (0x00470860) whether
   the answer came back non-zero:

       a camera was found -> shot 0 or 4, a FIXED trackside camera
       none               -> rand() picks 0..11, and only three of
                             the twelve are an orbit

   "FOUND" IS PURE PROXIMITY ALONG THE TRACK, and it is worth being
   precise because the obvious guess is wrong. Trk_FindNearestCamera
   walks a window of 23 SEGMENTS from the bike's current one, walks
   each segment's decor list, strncmp's the entry name against
   "CAMERA" and "camera" (six characters, both cases), and keeps
   the nearest by

       d2 = (decor.x - bike.x)^2 + (decor.z - bike.z)^2

   THERE IS NO LINE OF SIGHT TEST, no frustum, no occlusion, and Y
   IS IGNORED ENTIRELY. A camera counts as available if a decor
   object named CAMERA* exists within the next 23 segments, full
   stop. The segment index wraps modulo the segment count, so the
   search crosses the start line rather than stopping there.

   o SO A TUNNEL ALWAYS ORBITS. A rider watching a replay reported
     exactly that - "there is a tunnel that it goes through and
     always does an orbit shot". The reason is not that the tunnel
     blocks the view, because nothing tests the view: it is that no
     CAMERA decor is PLACED within 23 segments of it. The orbits
     are the fallback, and a tunnel is simply the longest stretch
     of track where the fallback always fires.
     [verified as to the code path, observed as to the tunnel]

   THE FALLBACK IS MOSTLY THE ORDINARY CHASE, and the scale is
   what says so.  The pick is (rand() & 0xffff) * 1/65535 * 12.0
   truncated - 0x004d13c4 and 0x004d13c8, two multiplies the
   decompiler drops - so it runs 0..11:

   +--------+------------------------+----------+
   | pick   | shot                   | odds     |
   +========+========================+==========+
   | 0      | orbit, running         | 1 in 12  |
   +--------+------------------------+----------+
   | 1      | orbit, pinned          | 1 in 12  |
   +--------+------------------------+----------+
   | 2      | a bike-local slot      | 1 in 12  |
   +--------+------------------------+----------+
   | 3..11  | the ordinary chase     | 9 in 12  |
   +--------+------------------------+----------+

   READ AS THIRDS - which is exactly what the decompiler's shape
   suggests, since it tests 0, 1 and 2 and then has an else - the
   chase becomes unreachable and the camera orbits at every cut.
   That is a different-looking replay, and it is wrong.

   o THE RANDOM CHOICE IS WHY IT DOES NOT REPEAT. rand() is
     FUN_004b1160, which is above 0x004b0000 and so CRT [inferred
     from the address range and from every use being masked - & 7,
     & 1, & 0xffff]. So two replays of the SAME race do not
     necessarily cut the same way, even though the physics is
     bit-exact. That is worth knowing before anyone treats the
     camera as part of the deterministic replay.

5.3.1.  THE ZOOM BELONGS TO ONE SHOT AND MUST NOT OUTLIVE IT

   Shot 4 sets the projection zoom to frame the bike at a constant
   size, up to 3.0.  EVERY OTHER SHOT SETS IT BACK TO 1.0 - case 1,
   the plain chase, included - and Camera_SelectView resets it once
   more at the top of every frame, before any mode is chosen
   [verified, 0x0045841e].

   TWO RESETS FOR ONE VALUE IS NOT REDUNDANCY.  The director can
   hand the frame back to the CHASE, and the chase is not part of
   the director - so leaving each shot to restore what it changed
   is not enough.  Without the per-frame reset a zoom of up to 3x
   survives the cut and the chase camera draws from its usual place
   magnified, which reads as an impossibly close shot from just
   behind the helmet rather than as a zoom.  It comes and goes with
   whether shot 4 happened to run first, so it looks like the shot
   choice is at fault and it is not.

5.4.  What the watchpoints actually saw, and one correction

   Four watchpoints on the record's first sixteen bytes filled
   16/16 in about five frames, catching the per-frame churn rather
   than a cut [verified]:

       +0x08  3 -> 7 at Camera_SetMode, 7 -> 1 at Camera_SetMode,
              1 -> 3 at 0x0046f2f7 inside Camera_UpdateTrackside
       +0x04  -1 -> 1 -> -1
       +0x00  0 -> racer1+0x24 -> 0

   That sequence IS Camera_SelectView running: mode 7 chosen, then
   overridden to 1 by the replay branch, then the director settling
   it to 3 for a shot. Every frame.

   o A CORRECTION TO SECTION 1. An earlier reading of this record
     - taken by hooking Camera_Update's ENTRY and dumping 32 bytes
     - reported +0x00 through +0x10 as "constant across every hit"
     and +0x14/+0x18/+0x1c as the camera position. Both were
     wrong. The fields are rewritten several times a frame; the
     hook simply always sampled the same instant, when the record
     is at rest. The eye is +0x98, as section 1 already had it.

     SAMPLING ONE INSTRUCTION DOES NOT SHOW THAT A FIELD IS
     CONSTANT. It shows what the field reads at that instruction.
     A watchpoint is the instrument for the other question.

   o AND CAPS MUST SUIT THE FIELD. Catching an actual CUT needs a
     filter or a much larger cap, because the resting churn will
     exhaust any small budget in a few frames. Not yet done.
     [open]

5.5.1.  Both selection paths, seen firing

   The sixteen writes land on ten distinct instructions inside
   Camera_UpdateTrackside, and they divide exactly along 5.3's two
   paths [verified]:

   +------------+-------+--------------------------------------+
   | 0x0046ebdb |   0   | camera FOUND - shot 0                |
   +------------+-------+--------------------------------------+
   | 0x0046ebe9 |   4   | ... then a coin flip promotes it to 4|
   +------------+-------+--------------------------------------+
   | 0x0046eb2a |   2   | none found, rand() -> 0              |
   +------------+-------+--------------------------------------+
   | 0x0046eb66 |   5   | none found, rand() -> 2              |
   +------------+-------+--------------------------------------+
   | 0x0046eb1b |   1   | none found, rand() -> anything else  |
   +------------+-------+--------------------------------------+

   o THE 0-THEN-4 PAIR IS ONE DECISION, NOT TWO. Hits 3 and 4 fired
     back to back with identical registers: the code stores 0 and
     then overwrites it with 4 when rand() & 1 is clear. A reader
     of the trace who counted writes would double-count the cuts.

   o AND rand() IS CONFIRMED. ECX read 0x7fff on every one of the
     sixteen hits - RAND_MAX. Section 5.3 called FUN_004b1160 rand()

   o THE SHOT SEQUENCE OVER TWO LAPS was 0, 1, 0, 4, 1, 0, 1, 1, 0,
     4, 2, 1, 4, 5, 2, 0 - so the fallback orbits (1, 2, 5) came up
     about as often as the fixed cameras (0, 4). Nine of sixteen
     selections found no camera within 23 segments, which says the
     placed cameras cover well under half this track.

6.  THE LOOK BRANCH - TAB, and the pan it leaves behind

   nLookBackKey (camera+0x21c) is an INDEX, and a non-zero one takes a
   whole different path from the chase - Camera_UpdateChase forks on it
   at 0x0046f8a4 and Camera_Update's mode 6 arm falls THROUGH to the
   chase on it at 0x004701b5. So the rear view pre-empts both cameras,
   and it suppresses the cockpit meshes as well: Racer_DrawCockpit's
   gate at 0x0046213e tests the same field.

6.1.  The placement, measured twice

   +------------------------------------------------------------------+
   |   eye  = racerPos + matOrientation * pLookOffset[slot]           |
   |   view = transpose(euler(pLookAngles[slot]) * matOrientation)    |
   |   target  IS NOT WRITTEN                                         |
   +------------------------------------------------------------------+

   No boom, no settle, no lerp. Slot 1 was forced into the live record
   and read back at two headings [verified]:

   +---------------+----------------------+-----------------------+
   | State         | racerPos             | eye                   |
   +===============+======================+=======================+
   | grid, yaw 0   | (-128, 0, 2058)      | (-128, 160, 2038)     |
   +---------------+----------------------+-----------------------+
   | corner, yaw   | (-1224.68, 0,        | measured  (-1206.35,  |
   | ~111 deg      | 2924.14)             | 160, 2931.15)         |
   |               |                      | predicted (-1206.03,  |
   |               |                      | 160, 2931.36)         |
   +---------------+----------------------+-----------------------+

   o THE VIEW IS THE TRANSPOSE of the camera's world orientation, which
     is what a view matrix is. At the grid the record held Ry(pi)
     exactly; mid-corner it held the transpose of
     euler(0, pi, 0) * matOrientation to the digit. Taking the product
     without transposing mirrors the scene.

   o THE SCALAR AT +0x1dc IS 64.0 for all eight slots and does NOT
     reach the eye - both measurements match the offset alone. It is
     loaded at 0x0046fc04 into the same FPU block, so it does something;
     what, is unread. [open]

   o BOTH SAMPLES ARE PURE YAW and slots 6 and 7 carry a pitch. Ry
     rotations commute, so neither settles whether the original
     composes euler * matrix or matrix * euler. Slot 1 is unaffected.
     [open]

6.2.  THE RELEASE PAN IS THE TARGET IT DID NOT WRITE

   Releasing the key in COCKPIT view tilts the view and recovers over a
   few frames; releasing it on the chase does not [observed]. It is not
   an animation and nothing in the game plays one - it falls out of two
   facts that are already established:

     1  the look branch leaves camera+0xa4, the target, ALONE. Read
        back while slot 1 was live it still held the forward cockpit
        target, (-128, 170, 2098) [verified].
     2  mode 6 SMOOTHS ONLY Y, against the previous frame's eye and
        target - 3.2. x and z snap.

   So on release the eye's y lerps from the look placement's 160 to the
   cockpit's 170 while x and z snap, and the target - never disturbed -
   is already where it belongs, only 40 units ahead. Ten units of eye
   error against a 40-unit lever arm is atan(10/40) = 14 degrees of
   tilt, shed at 0.769 per frame.

   o AND THAT IS WHY THE CHASE DOES NOT DO IT. Mode 7's target sits
     1680 ahead and all three components lerp, so the same ten units is
     0.34 degrees. The cockpit's short aim distance is the whole
     amplifier.

   o SO A PORT GETS IT FOR FREE, and must not implement it. Reproduce
     the two facts - leave the target alone in the look branch, smooth
     only y in mode 6 - and the pan appears. Adding a tilt on top would
     double it.

Open: the camera keeps moving while the game is paused

   THE PORT'S CHASE CAMERA SETTLES DURING A PAUSE. Held paused at
   the end of a scripted straight-line run it drifts to rest,
   which means it is integrating against something other than the
   virtual clock.  [observed]

   That matters beyond the cosmetics. race.h says the camera steps
   in race_update and nowhere else, and race_update takes a zero
   step while tm_now is frozen - so if the camera still moves, one
   of those two statements is wrong. Anything reading tm_real_now,
   or timeGetTime directly, would do it.

   It also means a camera position sampled at a pause boundary is
   not necessarily the position that frame ended on, which would
   quietly corrupt any camera comparison against MOTO.EXE. Settle
   this before measuring the camera.  [open]


The camera advances only when time passed - RESOLVED

   THE CHASE CAMERA IS A FIRST-ORDER FILTER chasing a point
   rigidly attached to the bike, so stepping it on a frame the bike
   did not move pulls it IN toward that point for free. race_update
   runs on paused frames - it just takes a zero step - so the
   port's camera converged whenever the game sat paused. Visible as
   the view creeping in after a pause; race_camera(1) is now gated
   on dt > 0.  [verified]

   IT ALSO CORRUPTED EVERY STEPPED MEASUREMENT, which is the part
   worth remembering. A scripted comparison pauses between bursts,
   and the port's camera closed up during each pause while the
   original's stayed frozen. That produced a long hunt through
   constants, matrices and boom arithmetic, all of which turned out
   to be identical:

       raw eye radius      488.21 both, = sqrt(484^2 + 64^2)
       boom scale          -484.00 both
       eye lerp            0.2 both, 0x004d1404
       record constants    lift 191, base -484, up 64,
                           table {0,128,256,512}, slot 0
       yaw source          racer+0xbc, matOrientation, both

   The raw eye and the boom scale were read out of the RUNNING game
   with a hook at 0x0046fcf0 capturing ESP+1c, ESP+50 and the
   racer position through EBX - the stack locals could not be
   decomposed from the listing, and two wrong conclusions had
   already been drawn by trying.

   AFTER THE GATE, same run, no constant changed:

       frames   MOTO camAng   port camAng   diff
           10      +168.03       +167.62    0.41
           20      +143.53       +143.08    0.45
           30      +116.82       +116.44    0.38
           40       +90.47        +90.02    0.45

       eye minus bike, frame 40:
           MOTO  +482.9  +223.5    -3.9
           port  +482.8  +225.1    -0.2

   Tracking went from 0.98 of the bike's rotation to matching the
   original's 0.48-rising-to-0.83, and the height from 21 units
   out to under 2 - the eye now DESCENDS through a turn as the
   original's does.  [verified]

   THE LESSON IS ABOUT THE HARNESS, not the camera. The virtual
   clock freezes the simulation, and it was verified frozen; what
   was not checked is that everything downstream of it respects
   the same gate. Anything integrating outside the dt path keeps
   running through a pause and silently biases every stepped
   measurement taken of it.

Open: the port tracks higher and swings faster

   Measured side by side, both builds driven from the grid by the
   same scripted run with frame counts verified equal. Eye minus
   bike position, world axes, MOTO in camera mode 7 (record 0 at
   g_cameraRecords, the farthest vantage):

       frames        MOTO dx,dy,dz            port dx,dy,dz
           0     +0.0  +230.9  -482.4     +0.0  +251.5  -480.2
          10   +100.0  +229.5  -472.0   +189.9  +251.4  -441.1
          20   +286.8  +228.0  -388.1   +373.6  +250.9  -301.8
          30   +430.8  +225.4  -217.8   +470.6  +250.2   -96.2
          40   +482.9  +223.5    -3.9   +462.8  +249.8  +128.9

   THE BOOM LENGTH IS RIGHT - 482.4 against 480.2 at rest - so the
   distance is not the problem. Two things are:  [verified]

   o HEIGHT. The port sits about 21 units higher throughout, 251
     against 230, and the original DESCENDS slightly through the
     turn (230.9 to 223.5) where the port barely moves (251.5 to
     249.8). Whether 21 is a vantage-point difference or a
     constant is unread - retail offers several and this capture
     is mode 7 only.

   o SWING RATE. As an orbit angle atan2(dx, dz), the original
     travels 89.5 degrees over those forty frames and the port
     travels 105.6 - about 18 per cent more. The port's camera
     gets AHEAD of the turn, which is what puts the bike between
     the eye and the front wheel and why the fork cannot be seen
     from behind in the port but can in the original.

   THE BIKE HEADING IS EXACT between the two builds (racers-convergence.txt
   9.59.1) - measured at the same samples, +25.18 against +25.18,
   +108.37 against +108.46 - so this is the camera's own lag and
   not the thing it is following.

   THE PORT HAS ALMOST NO LAG AT ALL. Camera rotation as a
   fraction of the bike's, same run:

       frames   bike turned   MOTO      port
           10      25.18       0.48      0.98
           20      53.44       0.68      0.99
           40     108.37       0.83      0.995

   The original's camera starts less than half way round and
   catches up over about a second; ours is rigidly attached. That
   is the whole difference, and it is why the front wheel is
   visible from behind in the original and not in the port - a
   camera that swings with the bike keeps the body between the eye
   and the fork.  [verified]

   IT IS NOT THE CONSTANTS AND NOT THE MATRIX. Read live,
   0x004d1404 is 0.2, 0x004d1408 0.5, 0x004d140c 0.333333,
   0x004d1354 64.0, and the target offset (-160, 1680) - all
   matching the port. The camera calls Racer_GetYaw on ECX+0x98 at
   0x0046f991, which is racer+0xbc, matOrientation - the same
   heading the port uses, NOT the travel frame.  [verified]

   WHERE IT DIVERGES IS THE FILTER STATE. Read live mid-turn:

       MOTO  eye     - bike   len 531.73   angle +64.81
             prevEye - bike   len 985.97   angle +64.81

   The original keeps its previous eye at nearly TWICE the radius
   its eye ends up at, where the port keeps it at the pre-boom
   radius of about 484. 985.97 is close to 512 + 484 = 996, the
   FARTHEST boom slot, and the capture was taken with retail set
   to its farthest view.

   THE BOOM ITSELF IS CORRECT IN THE PORT - read at 0x0046ff60:

       0046ff76  MOV [ESI+0xc8],EAX    prevEye = eye, PRE-boom
       0046ff88  ...                   eye = racerPos - d * s
       0046ffba  FLD [ESI+0x54]        then the 191 lift on y

   and the live numbers agree: eye - bike is
   (437.04, 222.42, 205.59), and taking the 191 lift back off y
   leaves a length of 484.00 EXACTLY. Final radius, lift order and
   the pre-boom prevEye store all match the port.  [verified]

   SO THE DIFFERENCE IS IN THE LERP TARGET, NOT THE BOOM. The
   original's PRE-boom eye sits 986 from the bike, about twice the
   484 it ends at, while the port's converges to the 488 its raw
   is built at - (0, 64, -484). A filter always pulling toward a
   point well outside the boom sphere, and renormalised back onto
   it every frame, is what produces a heavy swing; one pulling
   toward a point already on the sphere tracks rigidly.

   MEASURED ON BOTH, same turning state:

                 prevEye radius   eye radius less the lift
       MOTO           985.97               484.00
       port           569.38               484.00

   RULED OUT, all read live from the record at 0x005161c0: lift
   +0x54 = 191.0, boom base +0x58 = -484.0, local up +0x1dc = 64.0,
   boom table +0x224 = {0,128,256,512}, slot +0x34 = 0, mode 7 -
   every one matching the port. The lerp is the same shape too,
   eye = prev + (raw - prev) * 0.2 at 0x0046fcf0, with prev in
   stack locals and the result stored to +0xc8 pre-boom. And the
   port steps the camera once a frame, gated on race_camera(1).

   A TRAP WORTH MARKING: prevEye.y reads exactly 64.00, which
   looks like the filter having converged onto raw. It has not.
   racerPos.y is 0 and the up offset is 64, so the y term
   converges trivially and says nothing about the horizontal -
   where the lag actually lives.

   So the filter is identical and its state still ends up nearly
   twice as far out in the original. What differs has to be raw
   itself, built at 0x0046fc60 as a matrix-vector accumulation
   through stack locals that cannot be decomposed from the
   listing. THE NEXT STEP IS A HOOK, not more reading: dump
   [ESP+0x1c..0x24] and [ESP+0x10] at 0x0046fcf0 and compare the
   raw eye directly.  [open]

2.  The side view rolls with the bike, and one immediate fixes it

   The look-slot path of Camera_UpdateChase builds its view from the
   racer matrix it loads at 0x0047000f:

       0047000f  ADD EAX, 0x0058406d       g_racers + 0x2e55

   and racer+0x2e55 is matBikeRoot, WHICH CARRIES THE WHEELIE PITCH.
   Measured through a wheelie at wheelieAngle pi/2 [verified]:

   +---------------------------+---------+
   | racer+0x2e55  matBikeRoot | m[7] =  |
   |                           | 0.70711 |
   +---------------------------+---------+
   | racer+0x00bc  matOrient   | 0.00000 |
   +---------------------------+---------+

   THE TWO ARE DIFFERENT MATRICES, which is easy to miss because both
   are the identity at rest. matOrientation holds the heading only;
   matBikeRoot holds the whole attitude.

   POINTING THE ADD AT +0x00bc GIVES A LEVEL VIEW, and the A/B is
   clean. Same wheelie, the camera view matrix at record+0x68:

       unpatched   m[4]  0.8522 -> 0.7074 -> 0.7071   rolls to 45 deg
       patched     m[4]  1.0000 throughout            stays level

2.1.  Patch the camera read, not the field

   LEVELLING racer+0x2e55 ITSELF DOES NOT WORK AND IS NOT SAFE.

     o It does not work from a script. The field is rewritten every
       frame by maybe_Racer_UpdateLean at 0x0046a5b2, before the
       camera reads it, so a poll that writes it between frames is
       gone by the time it matters - measured, the pitch came back
       every frame.

     o It would not be safe even if it stuck. maybe_Racer_Draw copies
       the same matrix at 0x0045f2e5 into the slot that becomes
       rearMat, and maybe_Racer_ChassisAttitude reads it at
       0x0045ca35 - the suspension target is built from it. The bike
       and its physics would tilt along with the camera.

   The single immediate at 0x00470010 is read by nothing else.

2.2.  Where it lives

   it. The write goes through hook_suspend_game because it happens

   tools/mr_levelcam.py is the probe that established the field and
