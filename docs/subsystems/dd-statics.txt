MotoRacer PC Reverse Engineering                           Analysis Note


                 STATIC OBJECTS AND TEARDOWN IN DD.CPP


Abstract

   screen3d\dd.cpp builds two static C++ arrays at startup and tears its
   DirectDraw objects down in two modes. Reading the constructors
   settled the exact base, stride and count of both arrays - and
   corrected a four-byte error this project had been carrying in
   MR_DisplayMode.

1.  Two static arrays

   Both are built with _eh_vector_constructor_iterator_ and registered
   for destruction with _atexit, which gives base, element size, count
   and both element functions in one instruction each [verified]:

   +----------------+------------+--------+-------+-------------------------------+
   | Array          | Base       | Stride | Count | Ctor / Dtor                   |
   +================+============+========+=======+===============================+
   | g_displayModes | 0x005d8950 | 0x24   | 50    | maybe_DisplayMode_Ctor / Dtor |
   +----------------+------------+--------+-------+-------------------------------+
   | texture banks  | 0x005d3a20 | 0x149  | 7     | maybe_DdCaps_Ctor / Dtor      |
   +----------------+------------+--------+-------+-------------------------------+

   +-------------------------+------------+-----------------------+
   | Function                | Address    | Role                  |
   +=========================+============+=======================+
   | DisplayModes_StaticCtor | 0x004737a0 | builds the mode array |
   +-------------------------+------------+-----------------------+
   | DisplayModes_StaticDtor | 0x004737d0 | its atexit handler    |
   +-------------------------+------------+-----------------------+
   | maybe_DdCaps_StaticCtor | 0x004738a0 | builds the bank array |
   +-------------------------+------------+-----------------------+
   | maybe_DdCaps_StaticDtor | 0x004738d0 | its atexit handler    |
   +-------------------------+------------+-----------------------+

   The bank array settles an open question in subsystems/dd-init.txt,
   which had recorded 'two struct arrays with a 329-byte stride' and
   asked for the loops to be checked against the disassembly. 0x149 is
   329, and the count is 7.

2.  Why the constructor is the authority

   MR_DisplayMode had its base taken from the first field
   Cdd_EnumModesCallback writes, 0x005d8954. The constructor says
   0x005d8950. Every offset in that note was four bytes low and the
   record's first member was invisible, because the enumeration callback
   never touches it [verified].

   That member is constructed and destroyed per element, both wrapping a
   call to 0x004c4e12 - the helper the Cdd error reporters use before
   CString::Format - so it is very likely a CString holding the mode
   name [inferred].

   The general lesson, and it is worth applying before the next struct:
   A RECORD DOES NOT NECESSARILY START WHERE ITS FIRST WRITE LANDS. For
   a C++ codebase the static-constructor cluster gives base, stride and
   count exactly, and costs one function read. Look there before
   deriving a base from a write site.

   A second tell was available and missed: a 0x24 stride with only 0x20
   of writes should have prompted the question, and the answer was not
   'a dead field at the end' but 'a live field at the front'.

3.  Two teardown modes

   maybe_Cdd_ReleaseSurfaces at 0x004742f0 takes one argument that
   chooses how far to unwind [verified]:

   +----------+---------------------------------+-------------------------------+
   | Argument | Releases                        | Keeps                         |
   +==========+=================================+===============================+
   | non-zero | the six surface objects         | primary, clipper, IDirectDraw |
   +----------+---------------------------------+-------------------------------+
   | zero     | those, plus primary and clipper | nothing, if DAT_004e8948      |
   +----------+---------------------------------+-------------------------------+

   The partial mode is the interesting one. It drops the drawing
   surfaces while keeping the device and the primary alive, which is
   exactly what a resolution change needs - and it is how
   maybe_Screen3D_UpdateScreen can rebuild surfaces at a new size
   without dropping DirectDraw. See subsystems/mode-change.txt.

   Every release nulls its global immediately, so calling it twice is
   safe. maybe_Cdd_Dtor uses the full form.

4.  The texture bank record

   Before releasing anything, maybe_Cdd_ReleaseSurfaces frees texture
   banks by walking the 0x149 array BACKWARDS, twice - pass 0 for
   entries whose +0x0c is zero, pass 1 for the rest [verified]:

   +--------+------------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | Offset | Holds                        | From                           |
   +========+==============================+================================+
   | +0x00  | screen-mode selector         | maybe_DdCaps_ShouldLoad        |
   +--------+------------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | +0x04  | enable flag                  | maybe_DdCaps_ShouldLoad        |
   +--------+------------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | +0x08  | in-video-memory flag         | maybe_Cdd_CreateTextureSurface |
   +--------+------------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | +0x10  | a predicate callback         | maybe_DdCaps_ShouldLoad        |
   +--------+------------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | +0x1c  | a name or path string        | maybe_Cdd_CreateTextureSurface |
   +--------+------------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | +0x20  | surface size, pitch * height | same, summed by TotalSize      |
   +--------+------------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | +0x24  | a flag gating video memory   | maybe_Cdd_CreateTextureSurface |
   +--------+------------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | +0x2c  | set to 1 on reset            | maybe_DdCaps_Reset             |
   +--------+------------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | +0x30  | the DirectDraw surface       | maybe_Cdd_CreateTextureSurface |
   +--------+------------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | +0x34  | texture handle, reset to -1  | maybe_DdCaps_FreeTexture       |
   +--------+------------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | +0x38  | entry count, max 0x80        | maybe_DdBank_AllocSprites      |
   +--------+------------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | +0x3c  | entry array, 0x4e each       | maybe_DdBank_AllocSprites      |
   +--------+------------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | +0x40  | requested width              | maybe_Cdd_CreateTextureSurface |
   +--------+------------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | +0x44  | requested height             | maybe_Cdd_CreateTextureSurface |
   +--------+------------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | +0x48  | name buffer, 0x101 bytes     | maybe_DdCaps_SetName           |
   +--------+------------------------------+--------------------------------+

   CORRECTION. The offsets first recorded here - +0x0c, +0x18, +0x1c -
   were wrong. They came from assuming the backward walk's pointer was
   element+0x0c, which required the element base to be 0x005d41ee. It is
   0x005d41d6, so the walk pointer is element+0x24 and every offset was
   0x18 too low. The element bases follow from the constructor:
   0x005d3a20 plus i * 0x149.

   The record's shape is now bounded at both ends. The name buffer at
   +0x48 is 0x101 bytes, and 0x48 + 0x101 = 0x149 exactly, which
   accounts for the whole record and confirms the stride independently
   [verified].

   The handle at +0x34 goes to the same releaser D3D_FreeBankTextures
   uses, which ties this array to subsystems/d3d-textures.txt. Seven
   banks.

   Why two passes in that order has not been established. Freeing one
   group before the other suggests a dependency - system-memory copies
   before video-memory ones, or shared banks last [guess].

   CORRECTION. +0x30 was recorded here as a loaded flag, from
   maybe_DdCaps_FreeTexture's test of it before freeing the handle at
   +0x34. It is the DirectDraw SURFACE POINTER.
   maybe_Cdd_CreateTextureSurface passes bank+0x30 straight to
   CreateSurface as the output slot, so the free path was reading 'do I
   have a surface' rather than a separate flag [verified].

   The same function confirms +0x20 as the surface's byte size, pitch
   times height - which is exactly the field maybe_DdCaps_TotalSize adds
   up, so the two readings agree from opposite ends.

4.1.  What the constructor initialises

   maybe_DdCaps_Ctor at 0x00477550 is the ctor argument to the vector
   iterator, and it touches every dword from +0x00 to +0x44 plus the
   name buffer. That accounts for the whole record and closes the first
   open question of section 8 [verified]:

   +----------------+-------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | Offset         | Ctor leaves | Note                                    |
   +================+=============+=========================================+
   | +0x00 .. +0x14 | 0           | six dwords, cleared                     |
   +----------------+-------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | +0x18          | 1           | a non-zero default                      |
   +----------------+-------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | +0x1c          | CONSTRUCTED | not cleared - see below                 |
   +----------------+-------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | +0x20 .. +0x28 | 0           | three dwords, cleared                   |
   +----------------+-------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | +0x2c          | 1           | matches 'set to 1 on reset'             |
   +----------------+-------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | +0x30          | 0           | the surface pointer, null               |
   +----------------+-------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | +0x34          | -1          | the texture handle, matches FreeTexture |
   +----------------+-------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | +0x38 .. +0x44 | 0           | four dwords, cleared                    |
   +----------------+-------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | +0x48          | 0           | 0x101 bytes, cleared                    |
   +----------------+-------------+-----------------------------------------+

   Only two fields get a non-zero default, +0x18 and +0x2c, and both get
   1. +0x34 starting at -1 agrees with maybe_DdCaps_FreeTexture
   resetting it to -1, so -1 is 'no texture' at both ends of the
   lifetime.

4.1.1.  +0x1c is a constructed object, and the name buffer is exactly 0x101

   The ctor does not clear +0x1c. It constructs it [verified]:

       0047756d  MOV  [EBP-0x10], ECX
       00477570  MOV  ESI, ECX
       00477572  LEA  ECX, [ECX + 0x1c]
       00477575  CALL 0x004c4e12

   0x004c4e12 is the same helper section 2 found wrapped around
   MR_DisplayMode's first member and identified as very likely a CString
   constructor. Section 4's table already had +0x1c as 'a name or path
   string' from maybe_Cdd_CreateTextureSurface. The two readings agree:
   it is a CString, constructed here and used there.

   The tail is settled by construction rather than inference:

       004775b2  LEA    EDI, [ESI + 0x48]
       004775be  MOV    ECX, 0x40
       004775c3  REP    STOSD          ; 0x40 dwords = 256 bytes
       004775c5  STOSB                 ; one more byte

   256 + 1 = 0x101, cleared explicitly, so the name buffer really is
   char[257] and not char[256] with a stray byte after it. That also
   explains why maybe_DdCaps_SetName can strncpy 0x100 bytes without
   terminating: the 257th byte is zeroed at construction and never
   written again, so the buffer is permanently NUL-terminated
   [verified].

   o For the reconstruction this is the field that must be declared
     char[257]. Declaring char[256] lets the compiler pad the record to
     332 and silently breaks the pointer arithmetic in every loop that
     walks the array by 0x149.


5.  The bank accessors

   Six small functions operate on one bank record or on the whole array.
   None self-identifies; all were named from what they touch [verified]:

   +------------------------------+------------+------------------------------+
   | Function                     | Address    | Does                         |
   +==============================+============+==============================+
   | maybe_DdCaps_SetName         | 0x004776b0 | copy 0x100 bytes to +0x48    |
   +------------------------------+------------+------------------------------+
   | maybe_DdCaps_FreeTexture     | 0x00477b00 | free one bank's texture      |
   +------------------------------+------------+------------------------------+
   | maybe_DdCaps_FreeAllTextures | 0x00474de0 | the two-pass walk            |
   +------------------------------+------------+------------------------------+
   | maybe_DdCaps_Reset           | 0x00477930 | free +0x3c, clear the record |
   +------------------------------+------------+------------------------------+
   | maybe_DdCaps_TotalSize       | 0x00474fe0 | sum +0x20 across banks       |
   +------------------------------+------------+------------------------------+
   | maybe_DdCaps_Ctor / Dtor     | 0x00477550 | the element pair             |
   +------------------------------+------------+------------------------------+

   maybe_DdCaps_FreeAllTextures and the opening of
   maybe_Cdd_ReleaseSurfaces are the same two-pass walk, one written out
   and one called. Whether the compiler inlined it or the source repeats
   it cannot be told from here.

   Two more general helpers came out of the same batch and belong to
   dd.cpp rather than to the banks: maybe_GetSurfaceDesc at 0x00474fb0,
   which zeroes a DDSURFACEDESC, sets dwSize to 0x6c and calls
   GetSurfaceDesc; and Cdd_MessageBoxF at 0x00472bf0, a printf-style
   wrapper over Cdd_MessageBox using a 0x800-byte stack buffer.


5.1.  +0x34 is a bank INDEX, and it links the two arrays

   maybe_DdCaps_FreeTexture at 0x00477b00 is fourteen instructions
   [verified]:

       if (this->surface /* +0x30 */) {
           D3D_FreeBankTextures(this->f34);
           this->f34 = -1;
           this->surface = 0;
       }
       return 1;

   Section 4 records +0x34 as a 'texture handle'. It is more specific
   than that, and the callee settles it. D3D_FreeBankTextures indexes
   its argument by 0x338 [verified]:

       piVar3 = &g_bankPaletteSlots + param_1 * 0x338;
       if (*(int *)(&DAT_004f95d4 + param_1 * 0x338) != 0) ...

   0x338 is 824, the stride of g_bankRecords - the array based at
   0x004f92f8 that holds a DirectDraw surface at +0x2e0 and its locked
   bits at +0x330, indexed by face+0x36. So +0x34 of a texture bank is
   an INDEX INTO g_bankRecords, not an opaque handle.

   +--------------------------+--------+-------+----------------------------------+
   | Array                    | Stride | Count | Reached by                       |
   +==========================+========+=======+==================================+
   | texture banks, this note | 0x149  | 7     | screen-mode selection            |
   +--------------------------+--------+-------+----------------------------------+
   | g_bankRecords            | 0x338  | many  | index in bank+0x34, or face+0x36 |
   +--------------------------+--------+-------+----------------------------------+

   That is a real ownership relation between two arrays that are easy to
   confuse. A texture bank is the resolution-variant DESCRIPTOR - name,
   screen mode, predicate, size - and it points at the g_bankRecords
   slot that holds the actual surface and its sixteen sub-textures.
   Reading them as two names for one thing, which the shared word 'bank'
   invites, is wrong in both directions.

   o -1 in +0x34 therefore means 'owns no g_bankRecords slot', which is
     consistent with the constructor starting it at -1 and with
     FreeTexture returning it to -1.

5.1.1.  It does not release the DirectDraw surface

   FreeTexture nulls +0x30 without calling Release on it. Its callee
   does not release it either - D3D_FreeBankTextures walks the bank's
   sixteen sub-textures, freeing D3D texture slots and releasing
   IDirectDrawPalette objects, and never touches the surface at
   bank+0x30 [verified].

   RESOLVED in section 5.1.2, and the answer is the second one.

   o Worth checking against the -DoRealLock family too. If a surface can
     be dropped while a page's bits pointer is still cached at
     g_bankRecords+0x330, that is the stale-pointer case section 47.3 of
     rasterizer-pipeline.txt warns about.


5.1.2.  Nothing releases the texture surface

   maybe_Cdd_ReleaseSurfaces at 0x004742f0 frees texture banks before
   any COM release, and its loop is maybe_DdCaps_FreeTexture written out
   inline. The walk pointer is entry+0x24, so its piVar1[3] and
   piVar1[4] are entry+0x30 and entry+0x34 [verified]:

       if (entry->f30 != 0) {
           D3D_FreeBankTextures(entry->f34);
           entry->f34 = -1;
           entry->f30 = 0;
       }

   Identical to the standalone function, including nulling +0x30 without
   calling Release on it. So both teardown paths drop the pointer the
   same way, and D3D_FreeBankTextures does not release it either - it
   frees D3D texture slots and IDirectDrawPalette objects and never
   touches +0x30.

   The creator settles ownership. maybe_Cdd_CreateTextureSurface passes
   &bank->f30 to IDirectDraw::CreateSurface as the output slot, twice -
   the second is a retry after DDERR_OUTOFVIDEOMEMORY (0x8876017c),
   falling back to system memory [verified]:

       00477a10  LEA  EAX, [ESI + 0x30]
       00477a22  CALL [EAX + 0x18]        ; CreateSurface
       00477a2b  CMP  EBX, 0x8876017c     ; DDERR_OUTOFVIDEOMEMORY
       00477a35  LEA  EAX, [ESI + 0x30]   ; retry

   So the bank owns a surface it created, and every path that stops
   using it nulls the pointer without releasing. On the evidence
   available that is a LEAK [inferred - two independent teardown paths
   and the creator have been read, but the field is reached by computed
   addressing throughout, so no cross-reference can prove that nothing
   anywhere releases it].

   If it is a leak, the cost is not confined to shutdown:

   +---------------------+-------------------------------+------------------+
   | Trigger             | Path                          | Surfaces dropped |
   +=====================+===============================+==================+
   | a race load         | maybe_Track_SetHorizonBanks   | up to 3          |
   +---------------------+-------------------------------+------------------+
   | a resolution change | ReleaseSurfaces, partial mode | up to 7          |
   +---------------------+-------------------------------+------------------+
   | shutdown            | ReleaseSurfaces, full mode    | up to 7          |
   +---------------------+-------------------------------+------------------+

   Only the last is harmless. The partial mode exists precisely so a
   resolution change need not tear down the device, and the horizon
   banks are refreshed on every race load, so both of the repeatable
   paths run often.

   o Worth one more check before treating it as fact: a scan for any
     Release reached from a pointer loaded out of a bank's +0x30. That
     cannot be done by cross-reference and needs a small dataflow pass.

   o For the port it hardly matters whether the original leaked - the
     reimplementation must release the surface either way. It matters
     for FIDELITY testing: a port that correctly releases will show
     different video-memory behaviour from the original, and that is a
     difference to expect rather than to chase.


5.2.  The creator, and what it does not do

   maybe_Cdd_CreateTextureSurface at 0x00477980 is __thiscall(bank,
   width, height). The two arguments go into a DDSURFACEDESC with
   dwFlags 7 - CAPS, HEIGHT, WIDTH - at +0x08 and +0x0c, which is what
   identifies them [verified]. On success both are stored back into the
   bank, and on failure both are zeroed:

       if (hr == 0) { bank->f40 = width; bank->f44 = height; }
       else         { bank->f40 = 0;     bank->f44 = 0;      }

   Section 4 had +0x40 as 'set on success' without saying what it was
   set to. It is the requested width, and +0x44 the requested height -
   the dimensions the bank was created at, which is why the failure path
   clears them rather than leaving a stale size.

   The rest of the function is already described in section 4: video
   memory first with a system-memory retry after DDERR_OUTOFVIDEOMEMORY,
   then GetSurfaceDesc to confirm which memory class was really granted,
   setting +0x08 and accumulating maybe_g_texVidMemTotal.

5.2.1.  It overwrites +0x30 without releasing it

   One detail matters for section 5.1.2. CreateSurface receives
   bank+0x30 as its output slot directly, and nothing in this function
   reads or releases whatever was there before [verified]. So the create
   path does not release either.

   That is the third independent path over the same field, and all three
   treat it the same way:

   +--------------------------------+-------------------------------+
   | Path                           | What it does to +0x30         |
   +================================+===============================+
   | maybe_Cdd_CreateTextureSurface | overwrites with a new surface |
   +--------------------------------+-------------------------------+
   | maybe_DdCaps_FreeTexture       | nulls it                      |
   +--------------------------------+-------------------------------+
   | maybe_Cdd_ReleaseSurfaces      | nulls it, inlined             |
   +--------------------------------+-------------------------------+

   None of them calls Release. Callers do pair them -
   maybe_Track_SetHorizonBanks frees three banks before loading one - so
   the sequence in practice is null, then overwrite, and the interface
   pointer is simply dropped at each step. That strengthens the reading
   in section 5.1.2 without proving it, since the same caveat applies:
   computed addressing means no cross-reference can close it.

   o DAT_004ee9ec appears here as an alternative to bank+0x24 in
     choosing video memory, and again in maybe_DdCaps_TotalSize as an
     alternative to bank+0x24 in deciding whether an entry counts. The
     same global overriding the same field in two places reads as a
     global 'video memory is available' or 'use video memory' switch.
     Unnamed, and worth one lookup when its writer is found.


6.  The sprite record, 0x4e bytes

   Each texture bank owns an array of these, at bank+0x3c with the count
   at bank+0x38. maybe_DdBank_AllocSprites builds it: operator new of n
   * 0x4e + 4, the count in the leading dword, then
   _eh_vector_constructor_iterator_ over the elements. The count is
   CLAMPED TO 0x80, so a bank holds at most 128 entries [verified].

   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | Offset         | Holds                                     |
   +================+===========================================+
   | +0x00          | frame count                               |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | +0x04, +0x08   | hotspot, subtracted from the destination  |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | +0x0c, +0x10   | origin, added to the destination          |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | +0x14, +0x18   | per-frame step in x and y                 |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | +0x1c .. +0x28 | the source rect: left, top, right, bottom |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | +0x2c          | unique id; 0 means the slot is free       |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | +0x30          | back-pointer to the owning bank           |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | +0x34          | colour key low, 3 bytes                   |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | +0x37          | colour key high, 3 bytes                  |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | +0x3a          | flags passed through to the blit          |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | +0x3e          | the current frame                         |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | +0x42          | an embedded CString name                  |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | +0x4a          | CLIP MODE bitfield; see dd-blit.txt 2.1   |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+

   The two 3-byte fields at +0x34 and +0x37 both receive the same three
   bytes from 0x004f0df4, which are 00 ff 00. CONFIRMED as a colour-key
   low and high pair: maybe_Cdd_DrawSurface takes exactly such a pair,
   packs each through the runtime pixel-format globals and sets
   DDBLT_KEYSRCOVERRIDE. See subsystems/dd-blit.txt section 3
   [verified].

   maybe_DdBank_FindSprite searches all seven banks for an entry whose
   +0x2c matches its argument, walking from base+0x2c and subtracting
   0x2c on a hit. Its bank loop bound of 0x005d431f admits exactly seven
   banks, which independently confirms the count that
   maybe_DdCaps_StaticCtor gives [verified].

   +---------------------------+------------+--------------------+
   | Function                  | Address    | Does               |
   +===========================+============+====================+
   | maybe_DdSprite_Ctor       | 0x004772f0 | one sprite         |
   +---------------------------+------------+--------------------+
   | maybe_DdSprite_Dtor       | 0x004773b0 | one sprite         |
   +---------------------------+------------+--------------------+
   | maybe_DdBank_AllocSprites | 0x004776d0 | the array, max 128 |
   +---------------------------+------------+--------------------+
   | maybe_DdBank_FindSprite   | 0x00477270 | search all banks   |
   +---------------------------+------------+--------------------+


   IDENTIFIED. The 0x4e record is a SPRITE. maybe_DdSprite_Draw reads a
   source rectangle out of it, steps that rectangle by a fixed stride
   per frame, offsets the destination by an origin and a hotspot, and
   hands both colour keys to the blit. maybe_DdBank_AddSprite fills the
   same fields from its arguments and returns a unique id [verified].

   The family was renamed accordingly - maybe_DdSprite_Ctor,
   maybe_DdSprite_Dtor, maybe_DdBank_AllocSprites,
   maybe_DdBank_FindSprite. The earlier names described where the record
   lived rather than what it is.

   Ids come from maybe_g_nextSpriteId, a monotonic counter, so they are
   unique across every bank. That is what makes
   maybe_DdBank_FindSprite's search of all seven banks meaningful: a
   sprite handle identifies a sprite anywhere, not a slot within a bank.

   A slot with +0x2c == 0 is free, which is both how
   maybe_DdBank_AddSprite finds space and why the counter must never
   issue 0.

   One detail worth keeping for the port: maybe_DdSprite_Draw has an
   interlace adjustment. When DAT_00624b14 is set it nudges the
   destination y by one to correct parity, which pairs with the scanline
   fill in subsystems/dd-present.txt section 3. Both would be dropped
   together.

6.1.  Three details of the draw

   maybe_DdSprite_Draw at 0x00477400 is 327 bytes and its plate comment
   covers the shape. Three details are worth pinning [verified].

6.1.1.  It confirms bank+0x40 and +0x44 from a second consumer

   The source rectangle is clamped against the owning bank before the
   blit:

       if (bank->f40 <= right)  right  = bank->f40 - 1;
       if (bank->f44 <= bottom) bottom = bank->f44 - 1;

   Right is clamped against +0x40 and bottom against +0x44, which is
   only meaningful if those are the surface width and height. Section
   5.2 established that from the producing side -
   maybe_Cdd_CreateTextureSurface stores the requested width and height
   there - and this is the same reading from a consumer that never sees
   the creation. Two independent directions, so the identification no
   longer rests on one function.

   Note the clamp is to size MINUS ONE, so the rectangle's right and
   bottom are treated as inclusive coordinates rather than as the
   exclusive bounds a RECT normally carries. Worth keeping for the port;
   an off-by-one here is a column of missing pixels.

6.1.2.  The interlace rule is a parity match, not a nudge

   Section 6 describes the adjustment as nudging y by one on the wrong
   parity. The test is more specific than that:

       if (DAT_00624b14 && ((srcTop + dstY) & 1)) dstY++;

   It forces srcTop + dstY to be EVEN - that is, it makes the
   destination row parity agree with the source row parity. That is what
   a field-based or alternate-scanline blit needs: a sprite row that
   came from an even source line must land on an even destination line,
   or the interlace pattern breaks up. Pairs with the scanline fill in
   dd-present.txt section 3, and both would be dropped together.

6.1.3.  +0x3e is the last REQUESTED frame

   The frame is stored before it is validated:

       sprite->f3e = frame;
       if (0 < frame && frame < sprite->frameCount) ...step the rect...

   So an out-of-range frame is recorded in +0x3e and then ignored for
   the stepping, leaving the rectangle at frame zero. The field is the
   last frame asked for, not the frame actually drawn, and a reader
   cannot use it to tell what is on screen.


6.2.  Three details of the allocator

   Section 6 records that the family was renamed to the sprite
   vocabulary. Four references had not been updated with it - two field-
   table cells, the accessor table and the paragraph above - and have
   been reconciled against the database.

   Three behaviours are worth recording beyond the allocation itself
   [verified]:

   o A COUNT OF ZERO IS A NO-OP. The whole body is inside if (n != 0),
     so calling it with zero leaves +0x38 and +0x3c untouched rather
     than clearing them. It cannot be used to empty a bank.

   o THE CLAMP IS SILENT. n > 0x80 is truncated to 0x80 with no error
     and no report, so a caller asking for more entries than a bank can
     hold gets 128 and no indication that the rest were dropped.

   o IT DOES NOT FREE AN EXISTING ARRAY. +0x3c is overwritten directly.
     If a bank is allocated twice, the previous operator new block is
     dropped.

   That last one is the fourth field in this subsystem handled the same
   way, after the three paths over +0x30 in section 5.2.1. The pattern
   is consistent enough to be a house style rather than an oversight:
   resources are dropped by overwriting the pointer, and the pairing
   with a free is left to the caller.

   o maybe_DdCaps_Reset is what frees +0x3c, per section 4. Whether
     every allocation path is preceded by a Reset has not been checked,
     and that is the same open question as section 5.1.2 in a second
     place.

   On failure it is careful in the other direction: if operator new
   returns null, +0x3c is set to null AND +0x38 is reset to 0, so the
   count never describes an array that does not exist. The success and
   failure paths are not equally defensive.


6.3.  AddSprite, and a field it never sets

   maybe_DdBank_AddSprite at 0x00477790 takes sixteen arguments and
   every one lands in a named field, so the record's layout and the call
   signature confirm each other [verified]:

       int DdBank_AddSprite(bank, CString name,
                            left, top, right, bottom,   /* +0x1c.. */
                            hotspotX, hotspotY,         /* +0x04, +0x08 */
                            originX,  originY,          /* +0x0c, +0x10 */
                            stepX,    stepY,            /* +0x14, +0x18 */
                            frameCount,                 /* +0x00 */
                            flags,                      /* +0x3a */
                            keyLow, keyHigh);           /* +0x34, +0x37 */

   It scans bank+0x3c for the first entry whose +0x2c is zero, stamps it
   with maybe_g_nextSpriteId post-incremented, writes the back-pointer
   at +0x30, and returns the id - or 0 if the bank is full, which
   section 6 already records.

6.3.1.  A null colour key means black, not 'no key'

   Either key argument may be null, and both fall back to the same blob
   [verified]:

       if (keyLow  == NULL) keyLow  = &DAT_004ee938;
       if (keyHigh == NULL) keyHigh = &DAT_004ee938;

   DAT_004ee938 is eight zero bytes. So omitting a key does not disable
   keying - it installs the key 00 00 00, and black becomes transparent
   for that sprite. Distinct from the 00 ff 00 at 0x004f0df4 that
   section 6 cites, which is what one particular caller passes.

   o For the port that is a behavioural default worth preserving
     exactly. A reimplementation that treats a null key as 'no colour
     key' would draw black pixels the original drops.

6.3.2.  +0x4a is set for exactly one sprite

   AddSprite does not write +0x4a, and maybe_DdSprite_Ctor zeroes it.
   Across the whole binary the field has exactly two instructions
   touching it [verified]:

   +---------------------------------+--------+-----------------------+
   | Site                            | Access | What                  |
   +=================================+========+=======================+
   | FUN_004121e0 @0x00412508        | write  | MOV [EAX + 0x4a], ESI |
   +---------------------------------+--------+-----------------------+
   | maybe_DdSprite_Draw @0x00477517 | read   | MOV EBX, [EDX + 0x4a] |
   +---------------------------------+--------+-----------------------+

   The writer reaches its sprite through maybe_DdBank_FindSprite and
   stores 1, 4 or 6 depending on g_cfgScreenMode, g_cfgWidescreen169 and
   g_fullscreenResolved - then a few lines later tests the same value
   with & 4. A quantity written as 1, 4 or 6 and then bit-tested is a
   flags word, not a scale.

   The reader is Draw's fallback, if (scale == 0) scale = sprite->f4a,
   feeding maybe_Cdd_DrawScaled. So for every sprite except the one
   FUN_004121e0 patches, +0x4a stays 0 and the fallback substitutes 0
   for 0 - a no-op. The field is not a general default; it is a special
   case for a single sprite.

   o RESOLVED in section 6.3.3 by reading maybe_Cdd_DrawScaled: +0x4a is
     not a scale.


6.3.3.  +0x4a is not the scale, and the argument map proves it

   maybe_Cdd_DrawScaled at 0x004788b0 does take a scale, and it is a
   FLOAT - the fast path tests it against 0x3f800000, the bit pattern of
   1.0f, and otherwise runs both dimensions through __ftol [verified].
   The question is whether +0x4a is the argument that lands there. It is
   not.

   Mapping maybe_DdSprite_Draw's push sequence at 0x0047750f onto
   DrawScaled's prologue settles it. Pushes are in reverse order, so the
   last push is the first argument [verified]:

   +------+--------------------+-----------------------------+
   | Arg  | Pushed from        | Used by DrawScaled as       |
   +======+====================+=============================+
   | 1, 2 | ESI, EDI           | destination x and y         |
   +------+--------------------+-----------------------------+
   | 3    | LEA [ESP+0x24]     | the source rect             |
   +------+--------------------+-----------------------------+
   | 4    | [ESP+0x48]         | THE SCALE, compared to 1.0f |
   +------+--------------------+-----------------------------+
   | 5    | sprite+0x3a        | flags                       |
   +------+--------------------+-----------------------------+
   | 6    | the +0x4a value    | passed through untouched    |
   +------+--------------------+-----------------------------+
   | 7, 8 | sprite+0x34, +0x37 | the colour key pair         |
   +------+--------------------+-----------------------------+

   DrawScaled's prologue confirms the alignment: it loads [ESP+0x24] and
   [ESP+0x2c] into the destination x and y, [ESP+0x30] as the rect and
   [ESP+0x34] as the value it compares against 1.0f. The +0x4a value is
   one slot further along and is handed straight to
   maybe_Cdd_DrawSurface without being read.

   So the scale reaching DrawScaled is a separate caller-supplied
   argument to maybe_DdSprite_Draw, and +0x4a defaults a DIFFERENT
   parameter - one that only the blit core interprets. That agrees with
   the writer in FUN_004121e0 storing 1, 4 and 6 and bit-testing with &
   4, and it removes the contradiction: a float scale could never hold
   those values meaningfully, because as float bit patterns 1, 4 and 6
   are denormals indistinguishable from zero.

   o Two places carried the wrong reading and are corrected: the field
     table above, and the plate comment on maybe_DdSprite_Draw, which
     said 'scale defaults to entry+0x4a when the caller passes 0'. The
     defaulting is real; calling the defaulted parameter a scale is not.

   o RESOLVED. It is a CLIP MODE bitfield, arriving as
     maybe_Cdd_DrawSurface's param_5 and choosing what bounds the draw.
     See dd-blit.txt section 2.1.


7.  Banks are resolution-variant

   maybe_DdCaps_ShouldLoad at 0x00474e40 decides whether a given bank is
   wanted, and the answer depends on the display mode [verified]:

       if (a menu is up)                       return 0
       banks 4, 5, 6: load only when bank+0x00 == g_cfgScreenMode
       the others:    bank+0x00 against 2, together with
                      g_cfgSmallFonts and g_cfgScreenMode
       then the predicate at bank+0x10, if present
       finally the name at bank+0x48 must be non-empty

   So the engine keeps texture banks in per-resolution variants and
   loads only the matching set. The deciding pair is g_cfgScreenMode and
   g_cfgSmallFonts - exactly the pair that picks between the LowRes and
   HiRes halves of every font id in structs/MR_Font.txt. The same idea
   runs through both subsystems.

   maybe_DdCaps_ReloadAll at 0x00474f10 is the operation that follows
   from it: free every bank's texture in a two-pass backward walk, then
   walk forward and reload each bank that ShouldLoad accepts.

   ON THE SPINE. That is the second half of what a resolution change
   costs. maybe_Cdd_ReleaseSurfaces' partial mode re-creates the
   surfaces; this re-uploads every texture bank, re-selected against the
   new g_cfgScreenMode. A port that changes resolution without tearing
   down the device needs both halves, and both already exist.

   One detail worth keeping: the free walk runs backwards and the load
   walk forwards, and their two-pass group order is inverted as well, so
   the group freed last is loaded first. Whether that matters or is
   incidental has not been established [verified as to the order,
   unexplained as to the reason].


7.1.  Banks 4, 5 and 6 are the horizon, one per resolution

   Section 7 established that banks are resolution-variant without
   saying which banks or what they hold. FUN_00410350, called from
   maybe_Game_LoadRace with a track index, answers both [verified]:

       off = track * 0x130;
       sprintf(scratch, "%sBank\HorizHi.raw",  path(off));
       DdCaps_SetName(&bank[6], scratch);
       sprintf(scratch, "%sBank\HorizMed.raw", path(off));
       DdCaps_SetName(&bank[5], scratch);
       sprintf(scratch, "%sBank\HorizLow.raw", path(off));
       DdCaps_SetName(&bank[4], scratch);
       DdCaps_FreeTexture() x3;
       switch (g_cfgScreenMode) { 0, 1, 2 -> one LoadRawToSurface }

   The three addresses are 0x005d3f44, 0x005d408d and 0x005d41d6, which
   are exactly base + 4, 5 and 6 times 0x149. So the reserved trio of
   section 7 is the HORIZON backdrop, held per track and per resolution,
   renamed on every race load and reloaded for whichever screen mode is
   current.

   +-----------------+-------------------+-------+
   | g_cfgScreenMode | Bank loaded       | Index |
   +=================+===================+=======+
   | 0               | Bank\HorizLow.raw | 4     |
   +-----------------+-------------------+-------+
   | 1               | Bank\HorizMed.raw | 5     |
   +-----------------+-------------------+-------+
   | 2               | Bank\HorizHi.raw  | 6     |
   +-----------------+-------------------+-------+

   That is independent corroboration of the mode numbering.
   structs/MR_DisplayMode.txt derives g_cfgScreenMode as a size class
   from the mode dimensions and records that 2 is 640x480 rather than
   320x240; the filenames agree from a completely different direction,
   with 2 taking the Hi variant and 0 the Low.

   The path prefix comes from a per-track record array based at
   DAT_004dc088 with a stride of 0x130, the string sitting at +0x24 of
   each record. That array is read by a dozen functions outside dd.cpp
   and is not part of this subsystem; noted as an edge, not followed.

   o Only ONE of the three is ever resident. The other two are named but
     freed, so a resolution change costs a reload of the horizon exactly
     as section 7 describes for banks generally.


7.2.  The horizon is a SCROLLING 2D PANORAMA, not geometry

   FUN_00480930 registers the sprites and Dd_DrawHorizon
   Between them they are the whole sky: there is no dome, no
   skybox and no 3D at all.

   THE ART IS ONE 256x256 PAGE CUT IN HALF. FUN_00480930 allocates
   two sprites on bank 6 and names them from rectangles:

   +--------------------+-----------------------+------------------+
   | Sprite             | Rect (x0,y0,x1,y1)    | Mode             |
   +====================+=======================+==================+
   | Horizon 1 HiRes    | 0, 0, 255, 127        | 2, 640x480       |
   | Horizon 2 HiRes    | 0, 128, 255, 255      | 2                |
   | Horizon 1 MedRes   | 0, 0, 203, 101        | 1                |
   | Horizon 2 MedRes   | 0, 102, 203, 203      | 1                |
   | Horizon 1 LowRes   | 0, 0, 255, 63         | 0, one only      |
   +--------------------+-----------------------+------------------+

   so the high and medium modes get a PAIR of half-height strips and
   the low one a single short strip.  [verified]

   THE DRAW TILES THEM ALTERNATELY ACROSS THE SCREEN. The camera's
   yaw comes out of the view matrix with fpatan, becomes a pixel
   offset, and the two strips are laid side by side until the
   viewport is covered:

       for (; x + w < 0; x += w)  parity ^= 1;   /* wrap left */
       if (parity) swap(A, B);
       do {
           DdSprite_Draw(A, x,     y);
           DdSprite_Draw(B, x + w, y);
           x += 2 * w;
       } while (x <= g_viewMaxX);

   THE PARITY FLIP IS THE INTERESTING PART. Wrapping the offset back
   onto the screen can land on either strip, so the loop counts how
   many strip-widths it wrapped and swaps A and B when that count is
   odd. Without it the two halves would change places whenever the
   camera passed a multiple of the strip width and the seam would
   jump.  [verified]

   ABOVE AND BELOW ARE FLAT FILLS. Once the band is drawn, two
   Screen3D_QueueScreenQuad calls cover the rest of the viewport -
   one from the top of the screen down to the band, one from the
   band to the bottom. Their uv is a SINGLE TEXEL taken from the
   sprite's own corners: piVar4[7]/[8], the top-left, for the sky
   above, and piVar4[9]/[10], the bottom-right, for the ground
   below. So the sky colour and the ground colour are whatever the
   horizon strip's corner pixels happen to be, and no separate
   colour is stored anywhere.

   o WHICH EXPLAINS A SYMPTOM WORTH RECORDING. On a build where the
     band does not draw, the two fills still do - and they present
     as a pair of flat rectangles with nothing between them. That is
     exactly what MOTO.EXE shows on this machine under the 16-bit
     colour problem: "2 small skybox squares and all the geometry
     missing" [observed]. The fills are the last thing standing.

   o AND THE SCALE IS RESOLUTION-DERIVED, not stored:
     (g_viewMaxX * 6) / 640.

7.2.1.  The scroll arithmetic, and the six scalars behind it

   Every constant Dd_DrawHorizon uses is now read rather than
   inferred. The .rdata four came out of the Ghidra database and the
   .data four out of a running race through the mrhook agent, so the
   two halves corroborate each other rather than sharing a source.

   +--------------+-------------+------------------------------------+
   | Address      | Value       | Role                               |
   +==============+=============+====================================+
   | 0x004d00d8   | 100*pi      | added until a negative heading     |
   |              |             | goes positive                      |
   +--------------+-------------+------------------------------------+
   | 0x004d00e0   | 2*pi        | the fmod modulus, and the heading  |
   |              |             | span of one period once over S     |
   +--------------+-------------+------------------------------------+
   | 0x004d00e8   | pi/2        | the quarter turn the strip index   |
   |              |             | is offset by                       |
   +--------------+-------------+------------------------------------+
   | 0x004d00d0   | 2607.5947   | 0x4000/(2*pi), the trig table      |
   |              |             | scale - the project's usual one    |
   +--------------+-------------+------------------------------------+
   | 0x004d00f0   | 50000.0     | how far out the horizon point sits |
   +--------------+-------------+------------------------------------+
   | 0x004db2b4   | 0.25        | g_horizonPitchScale, the scale on  |
   |              |             | the projected offset               |
   +--------------+-------------+------------------------------------+
   | 0x004db2b0   | 5           | g_nHorizonYBias, subtracted from   |
   |              |             | the band's y                       |
   +--------------+-------------+------------------------------------+
   | 0x004e4f94   | 0           | -CorrectRatioTextures; subtracted  |
   |              |             | from 256 to give the uv divisor    |
   +--------------+-------------+------------------------------------+
   | 0x006d20c4   | 394.0       | g_projScaleX, the live projection  |
   |              |             | scale - see below                  |
   +--------------+-------------+------------------------------------+

   THE HORIZONTAL SCROLL, with w = 256 the strip width:

       Y    = fmod(heading, 2*pi)
       S    = (g_viewMaxX * 6) / 640          /* integer */
       kS   = 2*pi / S
       r    = Y - trunc(Y / kS) * kS
       x    = -(2w + trunc(r / kS * 2w))

   so one 512-px panorama period spans 2*pi/S radians of heading.
   S is INTEGER and therefore coarse: 2 at 320 wide, 4 at 512, 5 at
   640. At 640x480 that gives the sky a 90-degree horizontal field
   against the 3D pass's 78, and the mismatch is the binary's rather
   than a transcription error - worth knowing before it gets
   "fixed".

   THE VERTICAL IS A PROJECTED POINT, not a stored horizon line. A
   point 50000 away along the pitched view direction goes through
   the lens and lands the band's bottom edge on the horizon:

       yp   = -50000 * sin(pitch)
       zp   =  50000 * cos(pitch)
       y    = trunc(lens/zp * yp * 0.25) - h - 5 + vpA + vpB

   o THE LENS IS g_projScaleX, ALREADY NAMED. projection.txt has it
     as g_projBaseX * g_projZoom, so the horizon does not carry its
     own lens - it reads whatever the 3D pass is currently set to,
     which is why the two stay consistent when the camera zooms.

   o IT READ 394.0 LIVE, which is the number src/menu.c's
     P1_VIEW_LENS was calibrated to by hand from a single
     paused-frame measurement. Two independent derivations agreeing
     on 394 promotes the debug camera's lens from a fitted constant
     to a corroborated one. projection.txt records 480.0 for the
     same global from a different moment, which is consistent -
     it is derived and therefore varies.


7.2.2.  Reconstructed, and transcribed rather than substituted

   src/horizon.c draws it. The art resolves as
   "<track>BANK\HORIZHI.RAW" through track_asset_name and loads
   with bank_load mode 1 - the mode-0 skip of texture-banks.txt 4.2
   does NOT apply, because maybe_Track_SetHorizonBanks reaches the
   file through Cdd_LoadRawToSurface and not through
   Bankw_LoadRawTexture [verified, 0x00410350].

   Only the HiRes variant is built, because the port is 640x480,
   which is screen mode 2, which is HorizHi.

   NOTHING IN IT IS A SUBSTITUTION ANY MORE. Two were flagged when
   this was first written, and BOTH TURNED OUT TO BE MY ERROR rather
   than gaps in the binary. Recorded because the mistake is
   instructive:

   o THE HEADING LOOKED IMPOSSIBLE AND WAS NOT. 0x00402990 adds the
     camera record's translation at +0x8c to the third matrix row at
     +0x80 and takes atan2 of that z and x. Read cold, that is
     dominated by where the camera STANDS rather than where it
     POINTS, so it was called "not a heading at all" and replaced.

     It is a heading. MR_ModelInstance.txt 3.4 already recorded that
     Camera_LookAt writes the view matrix with TRANSLATION ZERO, so
     the addition contributes nothing and the atan2 is of a pure
     direction - the third row, the camera's forward vector. The
     answer was in docs/ before the objection was written.

   o THE VERTICAL WAS FOLDED AND SHOULD NOT HAVE BEEN. vpA and vpB
     were called unread and collapsed into "the viewport centre
     row". Screen3D_GetViewportRows (0x0049a010) is FOUR LINES and
     hands back g_viewTopMargin and g_viewCentreY unmodified - 0 and
     240 live. The -5 does not cancel, so the fold left the band
     five pixels low and it showed [observed].

   THE LESSON, since it has now cost time twice in this document: a
   formula that looks wrong is a reason to go and read the record it
   operates on, not a licence to replace it. Both "impossible"
   readings were resolved by four lines of a callee and one sentence
   of an existing struct note.

7.2.3.  Confirmed against the running game

   A hook at 0x00402d62 - the 10-byte MOV [0x005f1310],1, where EDI
   already holds the finished band y - caught four identical frames
   of a live race on ile01:

       EDI  0x62        band top y = 98, so the horizon line is 226
       EBP  0x100       strip width 256, as the sprite rects say
       EBX  0xfffffd80  start x -640 = -(2w + 128)
       [ESP+0x4c]       pitch +0.10322 rad = 5.914 degrees

   THE PITCH SIGN IS OPPOSITE OURS. The game reports +0.1032 where
   the port's asin of the view direction gives -0.104 for the same
   camera. The magnitudes agree, and because the port also negates
   in -50000*sin(pitch), the two flips cancel and the arithmetic
   matches. Worth knowing before someone "fixes" one of them.

   THAT SAMPLE SETTLED THE DAMP. Against y = proj - h - 5 + 0 + 240
   the two candidates are

       with 0.25     proj = -10.2  ->  y = 97
       without       proj = -40.8  ->  y = 67

   and the game says 98. So the damp is applied and is not a
   misreading; it is ile01's own value rather than a fixed fourfold
   softening - 7.2.5.

   AND F IS THE CAMERA'S HEIGHT, which closed the last pixel. The
   binary multiplies two terms by the float at camera+0x9c, and
   MR_ModelInstance.txt 3.3 puts the record's position at +0x98, so
   +0x9c is position.Y. The point is therefore HZ_DIST out along the
   pitched view direction LIFTED TO THE EYE, which is the true
   horizon. With F = 256.23 at the trace's pitch:

       y' = 256.23*cos - 50000*sin = -4897.8
       z' = 256.23*sin + 50000*cos =  49760.3
       proj = 394/49760.3 * -4897.8 * 0.25 = -9.695  ->  -9
       y    = -9 - 128 - 5 + 0 + 240 = 98

   exact. Dropping F gives -10 and so 97, which is precisely the
   one-pixel residual that stood before the record was read.

7.2.4.  The camera record stores its basis in COLUMNS, and reading
        it as rows scrolled the sky backwards

   Reported symptom: the panorama slides the wrong way round a
   corner - turn left and the sky goes further left.

   THE SCROLL ARITHMETIC WAS NEVER AT FAULT.  7.2.1's formula is
   transcribed correctly and 7.2.3's trace confirms it - but that
   trace caught the band's Y.  Nothing had checked which way the
   panorama moved, and the heading feeding it was wrong.

   0x00402990 reads +0x80..0x88, adds the translation at +0x8c
   (zero, LookAt writes it so) and takes atan2 of that z against
   that x.  This document and horizon.c both read +0x80..0x88 as the
   camera's FORWARD row.  It is not; it is the third row of a matrix
   whose COLUMNS are the basis:

   +---------+------------------------------+------------------+
   | reading | forward vector               | dot with         |
   |         |                              | (bike - camera)  |
   +=========+==============================+==================+
   | columns | (m[2], m[5], m[8])           |          +0.935  |
   +---------+------------------------------+------------------+
   | rows    | (m[6], m[7], m[8])           |          -0.662  |
   +---------+------------------------------+------------------+

   The column reading points at the bike; the row reading points
   away.  [verified]

   WHY THE ROW READING LOOKED RIGHT: row1 comes out (0, +0.995,
   -0.099), near world up, and that fits EITHER layout - as
   (right.y, up.y, fwd.y) or as the up row.  It is not a
   discriminator, and it was the only check made.

   SO +0x80 IS right.z AND +0x88 IS fwd.z.  With right = (fwd.z, *,
   -fwd.x), checked against the measured basis, the binary computes

       atan2(m[8], m[6]) = atan2(fwd.z, right.z)
                         = atan2(d.z, -d.x)

   which is pi minus the heading horizon.c had - a REFLECTION, so it
   runs the opposite way as the camera turns.

   MEASURED on MOTO turning right, eight steps [verified]:

   +----------+-------------+---------------+
   | step     | bike yaw    | horizon yaw   |
   +==========+=============+===============+
   | settled  |     +90.000 |       +90.458 |
   +----------+-------------+---------------+
   | 1        |      82.046 |        94.792 |
   +----------+-------------+---------------+
   | 4        |      64.556 |       109.841 |
   +----------+-------------+---------------+
   | 7        |      47.153 |       126.452 |
   +----------+-------------+---------------+

   The two run in opposite directions at the same rate, and pi minus
   the bike's heading tracks the horizon's to within the camera's
   own lag.  horizon.c now takes atan2(d[2], -d[0]).

   THE VERTICAL IS UNAFFECTED - it uses the pitch, which comes from
   the same third row's y and is a different component.  7.2.3's
   pixel result stands.

7.2.5.  The damp and the Y bias come from the TRACK, not the code

   Both are read by Trk_Load, four bytes each, under a version > 2
   gate [verified]:

   +------------+-------------------+-------+------------------------+
   | Read at    | Global            | Type  | Used by Dd_DrawHorizon |
   +============+===================+=======+========================+
   | 0x0040e0a5 | 0x004db2b0        | int   | the Y bias, 0x00402d37 |
   +------------+-------------------+-------+------------------------+
   | 0x0040e0b9 | 0x004db2b4        | float | the damp, 0x00402d2a   |
   +------------+-------------------+-------+------------------------+

   Both globals are ZERO in the image, so a version 2 track leaves
   them at zero rather than at any built-in default.

   THE DAMP MULTIPLIES THE PROJECTION, so it is not a fixed fourfold
   softening of the pitch response - it is whatever the track says.
   Read live, with the band top each binary draws at race start:

   +------------+-------+-------+----------+---------------------+
   | Track      | bias  | damp  | band top | with ile01's pair   |
   +============+=======+=======+==========+=====================+
   | ile01      |     5 | 0.25  |       98 | 98, exact           |
   +------------+-------+-------+----------+---------------------+
   | Red City   |   -25 | 1.0   |      100 | 98, two pixels high |
   +------------+-------+-------+----------+---------------------+

   So a reconstruction that hardcodes one track's pair is pixel-exact
   on that track and wrong everywhere else, by an amount that looks
   like a camera fault rather than a data one.  That is the trap this
   section exists to mark.

7.2.6.  The period divisor is a MODE SELECTOR, not a width

   The scroll period comes from

       s  = (viewMaxX * 6) / 640
       kS = 2pi / s

   and the original has exactly three screen modes, so that integer
   divide is picking one of three answers: s is 2 at 320 wide, 4 at
   512 and 5 at 640.  One 512-pixel period spans 2pi/s radians of
   heading.

   WIDESCREEN IS OURS AND BREAKS THE ASSUMPTION.  Fed an 854-wide
   frame the same expression returns 7, where 640 returns 5 - so kS
   falls from 1.2566 to 0.8976 and the whole panorama re-phases.  On
   screen that reads as the sky sliding sideways by roughly the width
   the frame gained, which looks like the band being anchored to the
   right edge rather than like a period change.

   One period is 512 AUTHORED pixels at any aspect.  A wider frame
   shows more of it; it does not stretch it.  So the divisor is taken
   from the design width and the draw extent from the real one -
   horizon.c.

7.2.7.  The panorama anchors to x = 0; the world anchors to its centre

   The phase above places the panorama's origin column at screen
   x = 0.  The 3D pass projects about g_viewCentreX.  At the design
   width those agree - 320 is half of 640 - and the original, which
   has no frame wider than its design, never has to care.

   OUR 16:9 IS 854 WIDE AND Hor+ (options.h), so g_viewCentreX
   becomes 427 while the sky's anchor stays at 0.  A world ray
   straight ahead moves from x 320 to x 427; the sky column that was
   ahead stays at 320.  The two part by 107, which is half the width
   gained, and reads on screen as the sky sliding sideways when the
   aspect is toggled.

   So horizon.c adds g_viewCentreX - FB_DESIGN_W / 2 after the parity
   wrap and renormalises, which is zero at 4:3 and leaves that path
   byte-identical.

   o THE SCROLL RATES ALREADY AGREE and are not the problem.  The
     sky runs 512 px per 2pi/5 of heading, 7.111 px per degree; the
     world runs g_projScaleX * pi/180, 6.876 at a 394 lens.  The 3.4
     per cent mismatch is the binary's own - 7.2.6 - and it is the
     same in both aspects, so what the widening breaks is the ANCHOR
     and nothing else.
