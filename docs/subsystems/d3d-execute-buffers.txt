MotoRacer PC Reverse Engineering                           Analysis Note


                    THE DIRECT3D EXECUTE BUFFER PATH


Abstract

   The hardware path is Direct3D Immediate Mode driven by EXECUTE
   BUFFERS. This note records the direct evidence, since the claim
   shapes the whole modernization plan: execute buffers have no modern
   equivalent and cannot be ported mechanically.

   Nothing here is inferred from the era or from the API's reputation.
   It is the opcodes, the structure layouts and the interface GUIDs in
   this binary.

1.  Which interface generation

   Every GUID below was found by scanning .rdata and .data for the
   interface's first dword [verified]:

   +----------------------------+------------+------------+
   | GUID                       | Address    | Introduced |
   +============================+============+============+
   | IID_IDirect3D              | 0x004d1650 | DX2/DX3    |
   +----------------------------+------------+------------+
   | IID_IDirect3D2             | 0x004d1660 | DX5        |
   +----------------------------+------------+------------+
   | IID_IDirect3D3             | absent     | DX6        |
   +----------------------------+------------+------------+
   | IID_IDirect3DDevice        | 0x004d16b0 | DX2/DX3    |
   +----------------------------+------------+------------+
   | IID_IDirect3DDevice2       | 0x004d16c0 | DX5        |
   +----------------------------+------------+------------+
   | IID_IDirect3DExecuteBuffer | 0x004d16f0 | DX2/DX3    |
   +----------------------------+------------+------------+
   | IID_IDirect3DTexture       | 0x004d16d0 | DX2/DX3    |
   +----------------------------+------------+------------+
   | IID_IDirect3DTexture2      | 0x004d16e0 | DX5        |
   +----------------------------+------------+------------+
   | IID_IDirect3DViewport      | 0x004d1700 | DX2/DX3    |
   +----------------------------+------------+------------+
   | IID_IDirect3DViewport2     | 0x004d1740 | DX5        |
   +----------------------------+------------+------------+
   | IID_IDirectDraw2           | 0x004d15d8 | DX2        |
   +----------------------------+------------+------------+
   | IID_IDirectDraw4           | absent     | DX6        |
   +----------------------------+------------+------------+

   So the binary carries both the DX2/DX3 interfaces and their DX5
   successors, and stops there: no IDirect3D3 and no IDirectDraw4, which
   are the DirectX 6 generation. It probes for whichever is present,
   which is the usual 1997 arrangement.

   That refines the common description of this game as a DirectX 3
   title. The rendering STYLE is DirectX 3 era, but the binary is
   prepared to talk to a DirectX 5 runtime through the ...2 interfaces
   [verified].

   It matters because DirectX 5 introduced DrawPrimitive as the
   replacement for execute buffers, and this binary queries
   IDirect3DDevice2 yet still builds execute buffers. The old path was
   kept.

2.  The instruction stream

   D3D_EmitRenderStates at 0x0041a0a0 appends instructions to the buffer
   by hand. Each is a D3DINSTRUCTION header, three fields in four bytes,
   followed by that many payload records [verified]:

       BYTE  bOpcode      8 = D3DOP_STATERENDER
       BYTE  bSize        8 = sizeof(D3DSTATE)
       WORD  wCount       1, then 12 or 13
       ... wCount records of { DWORD stateType; DWORD value; }

   The state numbers it writes decode cleanly against
   D3DRENDERSTATETYPE, which is the confirmation that this really is the
   D3D instruction encoding and not some private format:

   +------+-----+--------------------------------+
   | Hex  | Dec | D3DRENDERSTATE_                |
   +======+=====+================================+
   | 0x02 | 2   | ANTIALIAS                      |
   +------+-----+--------------------------------+
   | 0x03 | 3   | TEXTUREADDRESS                 |
   +------+-----+--------------------------------+
   | 0x04 | 4   | TEXTUREPERSPECTIVE             |
   +------+-----+--------------------------------+
   | 0x07 | 7   | ZENABLE                        |
   +------+-----+--------------------------------+
   | 0x08 | 8   | FILLMODE                       |
   +------+-----+--------------------------------+
   | 0x09 | 9   | SHADEMODE, 1 flat or 2 gouraud |
   +------+-----+--------------------------------+
   | 0x0e | 14  | ZWRITEENABLE                   |
   +------+-----+--------------------------------+
   | 0x0f | 15  | ALPHATESTENABLE                |
   +------+-----+--------------------------------+
   | 0x15 | 21  | TEXTUREMAPBLEND                |
   +------+-----+--------------------------------+
   | 0x17 | 23  | ZFUNC, set to 4 LESSEQUAL      |
   +------+-----+--------------------------------+
   | 0x1a | 26  | DITHERENABLE                   |
   +------+-----+--------------------------------+
   | 0x1b | 27  | ALPHABLENDENABLE               |
   +------+-----+--------------------------------+
   | 0x1d | 29  | SPECULARENABLE                 |
   +------+-----+--------------------------------+
   | 0x21 | 33  | STIPPLEDALPHA                  |
   +------+-----+--------------------------------+

   SHADEMODE taking 1 or 2, and ZFUNC taking 4, are the enum values
   D3DSHADE_FLAT, D3DSHADE_GOURAUD and D3DCMP_LESSEQUAL. The
   DITHERENABLE state is set from a global that the command-line
   documentation at 0x004dc664 exposes as a dithering switch.

   INCOMPLETE. That table was drawn from D3D_EmitRenderStates alone,
   which is the bulk block written once per execute buffer. Six further
   emitters append D3DOP_STATERENDER instructions from inside the queue
   walk, one per primitive, and between them add four more states -
   0x11 TEXTUREMAG, 0x12 TEXTUREMIN, 0x13 SRCBLEND, 0x14 DESTBLEND
   [verified]. With those the catalogue is complete: every state number
   and every value in the stream decodes against a D3D enum.

   The six are D3D_SetBlendMode 0x0041dde0, D3D_SetBlendModeFlatAlpha
   0x0041e000, D3D_SetBlendModeSrcAlpha 0x0041e1b0,
   D3D_SetTextureFilter 0x0041e2b0, D3D_SetDither 0x0041e380 and
   D3D_InvalidateBatch 0x0041e730. See d3d-render-state.txt.

3.  The geometry opcodes

   Four opcodes are emitted in total, from four functions, all in the
   Dsi_d3d.cpp range [verified]:

   +--------+-----------------------+-------+------------------------------------+
   | Opcode | Name                  | bSize | Emitted by                         |
   +========+=======================+=======+====================================+
   | 0x02   | D3DOP_LINE            | 4     | D3D_EmitLine 0x00419ee0            |
   +--------+-----------------------+-------+------------------------------------+
   | 0x03   | D3DOP_TRIANGLE        | 8     | D3D_EmitTriangle 0x00419d60        |
   +--------+-----------------------+-------+------------------------------------+
   | 0x08   | D3DOP_STATERENDER     | 8     | D3D_EmitRenderStates 0x0041a0a0    |
   +--------+-----------------------+-------+------------------------------------+
   | 0x09   | D3DOP_PROCESSVERTICES | 0x10  | D3D_EmitProcessVertices 0x00419ff0 |
   +--------+-----------------------+-------+------------------------------------+
   | 0x0b   | D3DOP_EXIT            | 0     | D3D_CloseExecuteBuffer 0x0041a310  |
   +--------+-----------------------+-------+------------------------------------+
   | 0x0c   | D3DOP_BRANCHFORWARD   | 0x10  | D3D_EmitProcessVertices            |
   +--------+-----------------------+-------+------------------------------------+

   No matrix opcodes, no light opcodes, no D3DOP_SPAN or
   D3DOP_SETSTATUS. The engine transforms nothing through Direct3D
   beyond the one PROCESSVERTICES call and issues no matrix state at
   all, which fits a game that keeps its own camera and its own software
   rasterizer.

3.1.  Vertices are transformed but not lit

   D3D_EmitProcessVertices writes a D3DPROCESSVERTICES whose dwFlags is
   2, D3DPROCESSVERTICES_TRANSFORM - not TRANSFORMLIGHT [verified].

       +0x00  dwFlags    = 2
       +0x04  wStart     = 0
       +0x06  wDest      = 0
       +0x08  dwCount    = vertex count
       +0x0c  dwReserved = 0

   So Direct3D transforms and the engine lights. That is the same
   lighting the software path carries per vertex at +0x0c and burns into
   the Full and Half rasterizer chains. Both back ends are fed pre-lit
   vertices, which is a good sign for a port: a modern pipeline takes
   pre-lit colours without argument.

3.2.  Triangles are batched under one header

   D3D_EmitTriangle is the most interesting of the four. It does not
   emit one instruction per triangle [verified].

   When the texture handle differs from the previous triangle it emits a
   D3DOP_STATERENDER carrying D3DRENDERSTATE_TEXTUREHANDLE, then opens a
   D3DOP_TRIANGLE header with wCount 0 and remembers that header's
   address. Every subsequent call increments the wCount in the saved
   header in place and appends an 8-byte D3DTRIANGLE.

   So a run of triangles sharing a texture costs a single header, and
   the thing that closes a batch is a texture change. Texture sorting,
   done inline by the emitter, in 1997.

   The wFlags field distinguishes three cases:

   +-------+-------------+--------+-------------------------------------+
   | Mode  | Vertices    | wFlags | Meaning                             |
   +=======+=============+========+=====================================+
   | 2     | v, v+1, v+2 | 0x300  | EDGEENABLE1 | EDGEENABLE2           |
   +-------+-------------+--------+-------------------------------------+
   | 1     | v, v+2, v+3 | 0x1f   | D3DTRIFLAG_EVEN, strip continuation |
   +-------+-------------+--------+-------------------------------------+
   | other | v, v+1, v+2 | 0x700  | EDGEENABLETRIANGLE, all three       |
   +-------+-------------+--------+-------------------------------------+

   Modes 2 and 1 in sequence emit a quad as two triangles sharing an
   edge, with the shared edge's antialiasing disabled so the seam does
   not show. The default mode enables all three edges for a standalone
   triangle.

   D3D_EmitLine is the same machinery for D3DOP_LINE with a 4-byte
   D3DLINE payload, presumably for wireframe or debug output.

4.  Buffer layout, vertices and the viewport

4.1.  Two buffers, created once

   maybe_D3D_InitDevice creates the execute buffers through
   IDirect3DDevice::CreateExecuteBuffer, vtable +0x18, passing a
   D3DEXECUTEBUFFERDESC with dwSize 0x14 = 20 and dwFlags 1 =
   D3DDEB_BUFSIZE [verified].

   It creates TWO, the second only when a global is set. Every function
   that touches a buffer takes an index and picks between them, so the
   engine alternates: while one is executing the other is being filled.

   The size is maxVerts * 64 plus 48 per triangle, rounded down to a 2
   KB boundary, where maxVerts is clamped against a per-device cap read
   from the device capability table.

4.2.  The buffer is vertices then instructions

   D3D_BeginExecuteBuffer locks the buffer, vtable +0x10, and splits it
   in two [verified]:

       lpData                          vertices, D3DTLVERTEX each
       lpData + maxVerts * 0x20 + 0x28 instructions

   0x20 is sizeof(D3DTLVERTEX). Two independent write pointers advance
   towards each other's regions, one for vertices and one for
   instructions, and the instruction emitters bounds-check theirs
   against the buffer end.

4.3.  The vertex format

   D3D_WriteVertex fills a standard D3DTLVERTEX and advances by 0x20
   [verified]:

   +-------+----------+------------------------------------------------+
   | Float | Field    | Source                                         |
   +=======+==========+================================================+
   | [0]   | sx       | vertex +0x10, screen X                         |
   +-------+----------+------------------------------------------------+
   | [1]   | sy       | vertex +0x14, screen Y                         |
   +-------+----------+------------------------------------------------+
   | [2]   | sz       | 1.0 with no z-buffer, else vertex +0x08 scaled |
   +-------+----------+------------------------------------------------+
   | [3]   | rhw      | vertex +0x18                                   |
   +-------+----------+------------------------------------------------+
   | [4]   | color    | computed by D3D_DrawScene                      |
   +-------+----------+------------------------------------------------+
   | [5]   | specular | likewise                                       |
   +-------+----------+------------------------------------------------+
   | [6]   | tu       | from the caller                                |
   +-------+----------+------------------------------------------------+
   | [7]   | tv       | from the caller                                |
   +-------+----------+------------------------------------------------+

   rhw taking vertex +0x18 is worth pausing on. Section 6 of
   subsystems/rasterizer-pipeline.txt verifies that field as 1/z from
   the software clipper, entirely independently, and rhw is by
   definition the reciprocal homogeneous w. Both back ends read the same
   field for the same purpose, which is about as strong a confirmation
   of a vertex layout as this project can get without running the game.

   Colours are computed in D3D_DrawScene through the shade table at
   DAT_005d43b0 - now g_d3dShadeRamp - indexed by the vertex's own
   intensity at +0x0c. That is the same intensity the software path
   thresholds at 0x3dfff to pick its Full or Half chain. The engine
   lights; Direct3D does not.

   THE RAMP IS NOW DECODED: it is 32 levels by 256 channel values of
   table[level][channel] = channel * level / 32, built at run time by a
   loop at the end of Cdd_ctor2 and empty in the image. See
   structs/MR_Vertex.txt section 6.4, and note that full brightness
   comes out 247 rather than 255.

   The SPECULAR at [5] is written with the same shaded RGB and is
   INERT - SPECULARENABLE is emitted from a global Cd3d_Ctor zeroes and
   nothing else touches [verified].

4.4.  The viewport is a formality

   D3D_CreateViewport does the expected three calls -
   IDirect3D::CreateViewport at +0x1c, IDirect3DDevice::AddViewport at
   +0x24, IDirect3DViewport::SetViewport at +0x14 - and the D3DVIEWPORT
   it passes is the correct 0x2c = 44 bytes.

   It is also entirely zero apart from dwSize. No x, y, width, height,
   scale or z range is ever supplied [verified].

   That is consistent, because D3D_ExecuteBuffer calls Execute with flag
   2, D3DEXECUTE_UNCLIPPED. The engine has already transformed and
   clipped in its own code, so there is no viewport transform for
   Direct3D to apply and nothing for it to clip against. The viewport
   object exists only because Execute requires the parameter.

   For a port this is good news twice over: there is no viewport mapping
   to reproduce, and the vertices arriving at the API are already in
   screen space with 1/z attached.

4.5.  Submitting

   D3D_ExecuteBuffer restores the main DirectDraw surface if lost, then
   [verified]:

       IDirect3DDevice::BeginScene   vtable +0x4c
       IDirect3DDevice::Execute      vtable +0x20
             (device, executeBuffer, viewport, D3DEXECUTE_UNCLIPPED)
       IDirect3DDevice::EndScene     vtable +0x50

5.  Closing and submitting a buffer

   D3D_CloseExecuteBuffer at 0x0041a310 finishes a buffer in four steps
   [verified]:

       1  append D3DINSTRUCTION { 0x0b D3DOP_EXIT, 0, 0 }
       2  vtable +0x14  IDirect3DExecuteBuffer::Unlock
       3  zero 48 bytes and fill a D3DEXECUTEDATA:
            +0x00  dwSize = 0x30 = sizeof(D3DEXECUTEDATA)
            +0x08  dwVertexCount
            +0x0c  dwInstructionOffset
            +0x10  dwInstructionLength
       4  vtable +0x18  IDirect3DExecuteBuffer::SetExecuteData

   The dwSize of 0x30 is decisive on its own. 48 bytes is exactly
   sizeof(D3DEXECUTEDATA) in the DirectX 3 and 5 headers, and the three
   fields written land at the offsets that structure defines.

   Overflow is caught by comparing the write pointer against a limit and
   reporting "Error in D3DUnLockExecuteBuffer lpBufEnd OverFlow", which
   is also where the name in the original source leaks.

6.  Bringing the device up

   D3D_InitDevice at 0x0041e8f0 is the whole hardware bring-up. It has
   no callers; Direct3D reaches it as a callback [verified].

6.1.  Choosing a device, and the 16-bit lock

   After EnumDevices the selection loop demands three things of a
   candidate, and the first one matters for any modernization plan
   [verified]:

   +-------------------------------------------+------------------------------------------+
   | Test                                      | Meaning                                  |
   +===========================================+==========================================+
   | dwDeviceRenderBitDepth & 0x400            | DDBD_16, renders 16 bits per pixel       |
   +-------------------------------------------+------------------------------------------+
   | g_devIsHardware                           | not the reference or software rasterizer |
   +-------------------------------------------+------------------------------------------+
   | DoesTexturesTransparency or a config flag | colour-keyed textures                    |
   +-------------------------------------------+------------------------------------------+

   So the 16-bit restriction is not only a display-mode filter. A
   Direct3D device that cannot render at 16 bits per pixel is rejected
   during selection, before a surface or a texture is ever created.
   Anything that removes the depth lock has to address this test as well
   as the mode enumeration.

   Among qualifying devices the loop prefers one whose desc +0x08 has
   bit 0 set over the first candidate found; that field is
   dcmColorModel, so it is preferring one colour model over another
   [inferred].

6.2.  PowerVR is detected by a FourCC surface

   The PowerVR check is a neat trick. The engine asks DirectDraw to
   create a surface whose pixel format carries the FourCC 0x43525650,
   which spells PVRC, and treats success as proof of a PowerVR board
   [verified]. Setting g_cfgUsePowerVr then forces several other globals
   on, including the infinite-z-buffer flag.

6.3.  The vertex budget

   The execute buffer is sized from a fixed budget, not from the scene
   [verified]:

       g_maxVertices = ((budget * 0xf000) / 0x2580) * 3
       budget = 0x20 normally, 8 when PowerVR is active
       then clamped to g_devMaxVertexCount

   +---------+----------+-----------------------------------------------+
   | Case    | Vertices | Buffer bytes                                  |
   +=========+==========+===============================================+
   | normal  | 612      | (204+1)*0x30 + 612*0x40, rounded down to 2 KB |
   +---------+----------+-----------------------------------------------+
   | PowerVR | 153      | the same formula                              |
   +---------+----------+-----------------------------------------------+

   0x40 per vertex is twice the 0x20 a D3DTLVERTEX needs; the surplus
   and the 0x30 per triangle are the instruction stream's share. When
   the buffer fills mid-scene D3D_DrawScene closes it, executes it and
   resumes into the next one, so the budget caps latency rather than
   scene complexity.

6.4.  What else it does

   EnumTextureFormats is issued here, vtable +0x38, with
   D3D_EnumTextureFormatsCallback at 0x00419980 as the callback. That
   function fills g_texFormats and is the answer to the open question in
   subsystems/d3d-textures.txt section 6.5 about where the descriptor
   array comes from - it has no callers because Direct3D calls it
   [verified].

   Both execute buffers are created and then primed by a Begin and Close
   pair before any drawing. Finally the initialiser walks 20 texture
   banks and preloads every marked sub-texture, choosing the source
   depth from the bank flags: bit 0 is 8-bit palettised, bit 5 4-bit
   palettised, bit 1 16-bit and bit 6 8-bit. Exhausting the slots
   reports "Not enough video memory" exactly once.

6.5.  The z buffer

   D3D_CreateZBuffer at 0x0041e580 is called from the initialiser with
   the display size when g_cfgUseZBuffer is set. It releases any
   existing surface, then creates one attached to the main surface
   [verified]:

   +-------------------------+------------------------------------------------+
   | Field                   | Value                                          |
   +=========================+================================================+
   | +0x04 dwFlags           | 0x47 = CAPS | HEIGHT | WIDTH | ZBUFFERBITDEPTH |
   +-------------------------+------------------------------------------------+
   | +0x18 dwZBufferBitDepth | the best the device offers, see below          |
   +-------------------------+------------------------------------------------+
   | +0x68 ddsCaps           | 0x24000 ZBUFFER | VIDEOMEMORY                  |
   +-------------------------+------------------------------------------------+
   |                         | 0x20800 ZBUFFER | SYSTEMMEMORY if not hardware |
   +-------------------------+------------------------------------------------+

   The depth is picked from D3DDEVICEDESC.dwDeviceZBufferBitDepth,
   testing DDBD_32, DDBD_24, DDBD_16 then DDBD_8 in that order and
   taking the first available - so it asks for the deepest z buffer the
   hardware has, unlike the colour depth, which is pinned at 16.

   The surface is then attached with
   IDirectDrawSurface::AddAttachedSurface, and the code records whether
   the request for video memory was actually honoured.

6.5.1.  Two ways to have no z buffer

   The control flow distinguishes them, and the distinction matters
   [verified]:

   +-------------------------------+-----------------------------+
   | Situation                     | Returns                     |
   +===============================+=============================+
   | g_cfgUseZBuffer clear         | 0, and nothing is created   |
   +-------------------------------+-----------------------------+
   | device reports no z buffer    | 1, SUCCESS, with no surface |
   +-------------------------------+-----------------------------+
   | no usable depth bit set       | 0                           |
   +-------------------------------+-----------------------------+
   | CreateSurface or attach fails | 0, both surfaces released   |
   +-------------------------------+-----------------------------+

   So a device with no z buffer is a supported configuration rather than
   an error. What happens then is visible in D3D_WriteVertex: when
   g_zBufferEnabled is clear it writes sz = 1.0 into every D3DTLVERTEX
   rather than a real depth, and the hardware path falls back entirely
   on draw order.

   That is the same discipline the software path enforces through the
   span buffer, which also resolves visibility by order rather than by
   depth. Both back ends are built to work without a z buffer, and the z
   buffer is an accelerator rather than a requirement [inferred].

7.  Why this cannot be translated

   The shape of the API is visible in the code and it is worth stating
   plainly, because it decides the porting strategy.

   To change a render state the engine does not call a function. It
   writes a byte, a byte and a word into a memory buffer, then eight
   bytes per state, advancing a raw pointer it has to bounds-check
   itself. Vertices and drawing commands go into the same buffer.
   Nothing happens until the buffer is unlocked and handed back through
   SetExecuteData.

   So the driver receives a small program to interpret rather than a
   sequence of calls. Every error is a buffer-layout error, and the
   compiler can check none of it.

   There is no modern counterpart to port to. The replacement path,
   DrawPrimitive, arrived in DirectX 5 and even that is long gone. The
   conclusion recorded in CLAUDE.md stands: this path must be REPLACED,
   not translated.

   The good news is that the surface is small. The instruction emission
   is concentrated in the Dsi_d3d.cpp range 0x00419000 to 0x0041b000,
   and the game's own software rasterizer already expresses the same
   scene through a completely different mechanism - see
   subsystems/rasterizer-pipeline.txt. Two independent renderers means
   the geometry interface between the engine and its back end is already
   a real boundary.

8.  Open questions

   o ANSWERED. All six opcodes the engine emits are in section 3.

   o Whether the DX5 interfaces are used for anything beyond being
     queried is unestablished. Only four calls in the whole binary use
     vtable +0x74, the DrawPrimitive slot on IDirect3DDevice2, and two
     of those are in MFC. Neither of the other two has been confirmed to
     be a device call.

   o The viewport is done; see section 4.4. No material or light state
     is ever set - there are no D3DOP_STATELIGHT or matrix opcodes at
     all, which follows from the engine doing its own transform and
     lighting.


9.  The emitters now carry MR_Cd3d * on this

   Every function in this note that writes the execute buffer is a
   __thiscall method on the Cd3d object, and all of them now declare it
   [verified]. The cursor arithmetic they perform is written

       this->pInstrCursor

   in the decompiler rather than *(int *)(param_1 + 8), so the opcode
   layouts described here can be read against the code directly.

   MR_Cd3d is 12 bytes:

       0x00  undefined4  dwUnknown00
       0x04  void *      pVertexCursor    stepped 0x20 per D3DTLVERTEX
       0x08  void *      pInstrCursor     stepped 4 for a header, 8 per
                                          state or triangle record

   The method was a GhidraClass named to match the structure; see
   the bulk signature pass. The class holds twelve
   functions - the two geometry emitters, the two execute-buffer
   bracket calls, the two texture entry points, the five state
   emitters, and the constructor.

   o dwUnknown00 at +0x00 is still unidentified, and it is now the only
     unnamed field in the object the entire hardware path runs through.
