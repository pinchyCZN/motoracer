MotoRacer PC Reverse Engineering                           Analysis Note


                              THE REPLAY


Abstract

   MotoRacer records every race and offers to play it back. IT
   RECORDS INPUTS, NOT POSITIONS, and replays them through the real
   physics - which is why the playback lands on the same lap times
   to the millisecond.

   The whole format is eight bytes a frame in a static array. It was
   decoded in one sitting by watching the game replay a race that
   had just been ridden, which is the only way it could have been:
   the buffer is indistinguishable from any other array until
   something is seen to write it.

1.  The buffer

   +--------------+----------------------------------------------+
   | 0x00558aa8   | g_abReplayBuffer - the records, 8 bytes each |
   +--------------+----------------------------------------------+
   | 0x004e9838   | g_dwReplayCount - how many records exist,    |
   |              | and the WRITE index                          |
   +--------------+----------------------------------------------+
   | 0x004e983c   | g_replayFrame - the PLAYBACK position        |
   +--------------+----------------------------------------------+
   | 0x004e9834   | g_dwReplayMode - 0 live, 1 playback, 2 demo  |
   +--------------+----------------------------------------------+
   | 0x004e9814   | g_dwReplayTotalMs - accumulated recorded ms  |
   +--------------+----------------------------------------------+

   THE COUNT AND THE FRAME ARE DIFFERENT THINGS and they read the
   same only when a playback has run to the end. Reading 0x004e9838
   as "where the replay is up to" would be wrong every other time.

   IT IS A STATIC ARRAY, NOT A HEAP ALLOCATION. Capacity is 0x4fb0 =
   20,400 records, tested in maybe_Replay_WriteInput and nowhere
   else. At the ~30 frames a second the deltas show that is about
   eleven minutes twenty of racing, and a longer race records only
   its first 20,400 frames - no wrap, no error, the rest of the
   replay is simply missing.

   o THE CAP IS SMALLER THAN THE SPACE. 20,400 * 8 = 0x27d00 ends at
     0x005807a8 and g_racers is at 0x00581218, so 0xa70 bytes lie
     between the buffer's real end and the next known global and
     belong to something not yet identified. [open]

   o WHICH IS WHY IT COULD NOT BE FOUND BY LOOKING FOR ONE. A sweep
     for heap pointers by megabyte bucket returned hundreds of
     candidates and none was this, because this is not on the heap.
     A sweep for an index near the frame count missed it too: the
     replay frame index counts REPLAY frames, not game frames, and
     the two had drifted far apart.

2.  The record

   Eight bytes, read live out of a replay in progress [verified]:

   +--------+------+----------------------------------------------+
   | +0x00  | 3    | THE INPUT WORD, the low 24 bits. Bits 0..5   |
   |        |      | are the bindable actions: 1 accel, 2 brake,  |
   |        |      | 4 left, 8 right, 0x10 gear up, 0x20 gear     |
   |        |      | down. Bits 6 and 7 are NOT keys - see 2.1    |
   +--------+------+----------------------------------------------+
   | +0x03  | 1    | THE FRAME DELTA in milliseconds. Observed    |
   |        |      | 0x1c, 0x21, 0x22, 0x23, 0x24 - 28 to 36 ms   |
   +--------+------+----------------------------------------------+
   | +0x04  | 2    | a joystick axis, 0x8000 centred              |
   +--------+------+----------------------------------------------+
   | +0x06  | 2    | the other axis, 0x8000 centred               |
   +--------+------+----------------------------------------------+

   maybe_Replay_ReadInput (0x004578a0) does the unpacking:

       EDX = g_replayFrame;
       EAX = g_abReplayBuffer[EDX];    the 8-byte record
       EAX &= 0xffffff;                mask off the frame delta
       *inputOut = EAX;

   o THE TWO AXES ARE THE JOYSTICK. maybe_Input_ReadDevice writes
     0x8000 into DAT_0051bcf4 and DAT_0051bcf8 as the centred value
     for exactly these, so a keyboard race records 0x80008000 in
     every frame - which is what a keyboard race reads back.

2.1.  The top two bits are the stunt, and they are synthesised

   maybe_Replay_WriteInput (0x004577f0) folds two racer fields into
   the button word on its way into the buffer [verified]:

       racer[+0x31f9] ? (w | 0x80) : (w & ~0x80)    STUNT HELD
       racer[+0x3201] ? (w | 0x40) : (w & ~0x40)    STUNT PRESSED

   WHICH IS NECESSARY, because Turbo/Stunts is the ONE action that
   sets no input bit. Input_OnKeyDown's seven bindable actions are
   indices 0..6, and cases 0 to 5 OR bits 1, 2, 4, 8, 0x10 and 0x20
   into racer+0x31e9 - but case 6 writes racer+0x31f9 and +0x3201
   directly and touches the word not at all. Without this fold-in a
   recording could not reproduce a wheelie.

   o SO BIT 7 IS A LEVEL AND BIT 6 IS AN EDGE, not two keys. Bit 7
     stays up for the whole wheelie; bit 6 marks the press and is
     cleared by maybe_Racer_UpdatePose. A decoder that lists them
     alongside accel and brake is describing the wrong thing.

   MEASURED OVER ONE 5,956-FRAME RACE [verified], read straight out
   of the buffer with tools/mr_replay.py:

   +-------------+---------+-------+--------------------------------+
   | bit         | frames  | pct   |                                |
   +=============+=========+=======+================================+
   | 0x01 accel  |   5037  | 84.6% | held almost throughout         |
   +-------------+---------+-------+--------------------------------+
   | 0x08 right  |   1359  | 22.8% |                                |
   +-------------+---------+-------+--------------------------------+
   | 0x04 left   |    900  | 15.1% |                                |
   +-------------+---------+-------+--------------------------------+
   | 0x80 STUNT  |    330  |  5.5% | 32 bursts, longest 87 frames   |
   |             |         |       | = 2.9 s                        |
   +-------------+---------+-------+--------------------------------+
   | 0x02 brake  |    252  |  4.2% |                                |
   +-------------+---------+-------+--------------------------------+
   | 0x40 edge   |     67  |  1.1% |                                |
   +-------------+---------+-------+--------------------------------+

   o THE TWO GEAR BITS NEVER FIRED. 0x10 and 0x20 are Higher Gear
     and Lower Gear, bound to Z and A, and this rider used neither
     across three and a half minutes - which is consistent with the
     gearbox running automatically for a human in solo.

   o AND ONE OF THE THREE CRASHES WAS A WHEELIE. The crash at
     140218 ms has bit 7 up - accel+left+STUNT - while the crashes
     at 112963 and 123367 read plain accel. That is the first
     evidence in this project for the stunt costing stability, and
     it came out of the recording rather than out of the physics.

3.  How playback works

   The replay feeds the recorded input into the racer's own input
   word at racer+0x31e9 and lets the ordinary physics run. Watched
   live [verified]:

   +--------+---------------------------------------------------+
   | racer  | written by maybe_Replay_ReadInput at 0x004578e0   |
   | +0x31e9| - eight hits, all from that one site              |
   +--------+---------------------------------------------------+
   | racer  | written by the ordinary physics integrator at     |
   | +0x30  | 0x0048dc3d, with ESI = racer+0x24 - the same      |
   |        | writer as during a real race                      |
   +--------+---------------------------------------------------+

   THAT IS THE WHOLE ANSWER. The input word is driven by the replay
   and the position is not driven at all - it falls out of the
   physics, exactly as it does when a human is riding. Sampled
   across four seconds of playback the input word read 9, 8, 1, 0, 5
   - accel+right, right, accel, nothing, accel+left - while the gear
   shifted 3, 5, 5, 6, 4 and the speed moved with it.

   o AND THE PLAYER IS LOCKED OUT. g_dwReplayMode reads 1 during
     a replay, which is the same gate mrhook's SEND_INPUT refuses
     to drive through. The game is not merging the two input
     sources; it is switching between them.

   o MODE 2 IS NEITHER. Racers_Init forces two racers when
     g_opponentCount is 0 and the mode is 2 - most likely the
     attract demo, which would need racers without a player
     [inferred]. See subsystems/racers.txt.

4.  Why it stays in sync

   Because the frame delta is recorded alongside the input. A
   replay does not re-time the race, it re-runs it with the same
   dt sequence, so an integration that was done in 33 ms steps is
   redone in 33 ms steps whatever the machine is doing now.

   PROVEN BY A CHECKPOINT [verified]. A watchpoint on the split
   table caught the replay writing 0x00004d90 - 19856 ms - into
   entry 0. That is the SAME first checkpoint time, to the
   millisecond, that the original ride recorded. The physics was
   re-run from inputs and arrived at the identical answer.

   o SO A PORT CANNOT REPLAY OLD FILES WITHOUT BIT-EXACT PHYSICS.
     That is the real cost of this design and it is worth knowing
     before any of the physics is rewritten: a reconstruction whose
     integrator differs in the last bit will drift, and a .Sav from
     the retail game will not play back. Positions would have been
     forgiving; inputs are not.

5.  Saving, and -saverecord

   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | -saverecord    | an undocumented command-line flag at      |
   |                | 0x004dc4c0. Main_InitAndParseCommandLine  |
   |                | sets 0x004e8924 to 1 for it               |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | *.Sav          | the file type - the dialog filter reads   |
   |                | "Record Files (*.Sav)|*.Sav|All Files..." |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+

   THE FLAG ONLY GATES WRITING TO A FILE. The game offered a replay
   after a race with 0x004e8924 reading 0, so recording into
   g_abReplayBuffer happens always and -saverecord is about
   persisting it.

   THE RECORDER ITSELF IS maybe_Replay_WriteInput (0x004577f0), and
   it is nine lines: clamp the delta to 0xff, fold in the two stunt
   bits, pack dt<<24 | buttons and axis0<<16 | axis1, bump
   g_dwReplayCount. g_dwReplayTotalMs accumulates the UNCLAMPED
   delta, so a frame longer than 255 ms makes that global and the
   sum of the recorded deltas disagree - and the replay plays that
   frame back short.

   A version check exists - REPLAY VERSION ERROR and REPLAY
   COMPATIBILITY ERROR, in six languages - so the file carries a
   header. FUN_00437b70 reads the flag four times and holds one
   malloc against four frees; it is the save/load path and it has
   not been read.

6.  DETERMINISM, PROVEN BIT FOR BIT

   Section 4 argued from the recorded frame delta that the replay
   must re-simulate exactly, and offered one checkpoint time as
   evidence. Letting a whole replay run with a hook on
   maybe_Racer_BeginCrash settles it much harder.

   The live race recorded three player crashes. The replay of that
   race produced [verified]:

   +--------+-----------+------------+---------+
   | racer  | speed     | race clock | segment |
   +========+===========+============+=========+
   |   1    |  94.896   |   25086 ms |   423   |
   |   4    |  22.222   |   27507 ms |   426   |
   |   2    |   0.916   |   27507 ms |   426   |
   |   1    | 109.567   |  112963 ms |   427   |
   |   1    | 109.559   |  123367 ms |   534   |
   |   1    | 108.486   |  140218 ms |     8   |
   |   7    |  22.222   |  186887 ms |    38   |
   +--------+-----------+------------+---------+

   THE THREE PLAYER CRASHES THE LIVE RUN CAPTURED - 109.567 at
   112963, 109.559 at 123367, 108.486 at 140218 - CAME BACK
   IDENTICAL. Not close: the same IEEE bit patterns, 4e22db42,
   671edb42 and e0f8d842, at the same millisecond.

   o A CRASH IS THE HARSHEST TEST AVAILABLE. It is the end of a
     long chain of integration, collision and grip, and any
     divergence anywhere upstream moves it. Three of them landing
     on identical bits says the re-simulation is exact, not merely
     close.

6.1.  The AI are re-simulated too

   Racers 4, 2 and 7 crashed during the playback. So the field is
   not a recording of twelve bikes - the replay re-runs ALL of them
   from the same starting state and they crash where they crashed.

   o WHICH ANSWERS AN OPEN QUESTION FROM SECTION 6 and raises the
     cost of the port again. It is not enough for the PLAYER's
     physics to be bit-exact; the AI's decisions have to be too,
     and the RNG at FUN_00458ce0 has to produce the same sequence
     from the same seed.

   o AND IT CAUGHT A PILE-UP. Racer 1 went down at 25086 ms in
     segment 423; racers 4 AND 2 went down together at 27507 ms in
     segment 426, two and a half seconds later and three segments
     on. The rider described exactly that from memory - "it was me
     that crashed and the rider crashed when it hit my bike" -
     without knowing the trace would show two of them.

6.2.  The replay uses the trackside cameras

   Watched rather than measured [observed]: the replay camera
   orbits the player, then cuts to a fixed position and pans as the
   bike goes past, like a broadcast camera.

   THOSE FIXED POSITIONS ARE ALREADY IN THE DATA. The decor table
   at DAT_004dc0e4 carries entries named CAMERA_000 and
   maybe_Trk_CountCameras (0x00473240) reads the same table - see
   subsystems/decor.txt section 3. So the trackside cameras a
   replay cuts between are placed by the track file as decor, not
   hardcoded.

   o THAT IS WHY CAMERA IS A DECOR CLASS WITH NO MESH. It exists to
     be a position, and this is what reads it. Neither half was
     worth much alone.

   THE PORT DOES IT NOW.  Camera_SelectView lists five flags that
   switch the director on and g_dwReplayMode == 1 is one of them, so
   a playback takes the trackside shots rather than the chase - the
   single condition that makes a replay look like the attract demo
   rather than like a race.  race.c's camera block tests
   race_replay_mode() beside race_auto_ride().

   o AND IT CUTS ON THE FIRST FRAME.  camera_reset leaves the shot
     timer at zero, which is not yet EXPIRED, so the director would
     hold a stale eye for one frame before choosing anything.
     Starting a playback arms it at -1.

6.3.  A race recorded under mr_freeze cannot be played back

   A race was ridden with tools/mr_freeze.py holding the checkpoint
   clock, so the rider could take his time; he finished last. The
   freeze was then stopped and the recording played back, and the
   replay ABORTED PART WAY THROUGH - the checkpoint timeout expired
   at a checkpoint the original race had passed. [verified,
   2026-09-05]

   THAT IS NOT A BUG, IT IS THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE YET FOR SECTION 3.
   If the buffer held positions, or matrices, or anything about where
   the bike WAS, freezing a clock during recording could not change
   what plays back. The playback diverged because it RE-SIMULATES
   from the inputs, and the input stream says nothing about the race
   clock.

   +---------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | in the buffer             | NOT in the buffer              |
   +===========================+================================+
   | the button word           | the checkpoint countdown at    |
   | the frame delta           | 0x004dc0a8                     |
   |                           | anything a debug tool wrote    |
   +---------------------------+--------------------------------+

   o SO AN OVERRIDE APPLIED WHILE RECORDING MUST BE APPLIED WHILE
     PLAYING BACK. mr_freeze is a loop over GET_MEM and SET_MEM
     against 0x004dc0a8; it is not part of the game state the replay
     restores. Run it during playback too, or record without it.

   o AND THE PHYSICS DID NOT DIVERGE. The bike followed the same
     path from the same inputs - section 6 proved that bit for bit,
     twice. What ran out was an external gate the replay does not
     restore, so the race was cut short rather than played wrong.

   o WHICH GENERALISES TO EVERY DIAGNOSTIC OVERRIDE WE HAVE. A
     SET_MEM poke, a frozen value, a hook that alters state - none
     of them are recorded, so any replay taken while one was active
     is only reproducible with it active again. Worth stating
     because the symptom is a replay that "stops early" rather than
     an error.

7.  The port

   race.c carries it: the buffer, the recorder, the reader and the
   dt override.  It is small because this document had already
   decoded the format - the work was wiring, not discovery.

   +-------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | race_replay_write       | maybe_Replay_WriteInput        |
   +-------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | race_replay_begin_frame | maybe_Replay_ReadInput, plus   |
   |                         | handing back the recorded dt   |
   +-------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | race_replay_apply       | the input switch in            |
   |                         | race_poll_input                |
   +-------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | race_replay_start       | arms it; the caller restarts   |
   +-------------------------+--------------------------------+

   THE RECORD IS BYTE-FOR-BYTE THE ORIGINAL'S, deliberately:
   tools/mr_replay.py decodes MOTO's buffer and reads ours unchanged
   because of it.

   THREE THINGS WORTH KNOWING:

   o RECORDING IS ONCE A FRAME, NOT ONCE A TICK.  race_poll_input
     runs per SUBSTEP in this port (frame-loop.txt 1.3.9), so
     recording there would write several records for one frame and
     the playback would run long.  The write happens after the
     substep loop, off the racer's own input word.

   o A REPLAY IS THE RACE RUN AGAIN, from the grid.  YES arms the
     playback and restarts; the arm survives race_load's reset,
     which is the one thing that would otherwise wipe the recording
     on its way to playing it.

   o AND THE dt COMES FROM THE RECORD.  race_update takes its step
     from the recorded millisecond delta instead of the clock while
     a playback runs.  Without that the race is re-TIMED rather than
     re-run and drifts - section 4.

   o THE LIVE STEP IS QUANTISED TO WHOLE MILLISECONDS, and that is
     not tidiness - it is what makes the record EXACT.  At 60fps the
     frame is 16.667 ms; recording (int)(dt * 1000) stores 16 while
     the physics integrates 16.667, so a playback runs about four
     per cent short.  That looks like nothing for the first seconds
     and has the bike turning early by the end of a lap, which reads
     as a frame-rate problem and is not one.  The remainder is
     CARRIED, so the sequence runs 17, 17, 16, 17 ... and averages
     the true frame time - truncating without the carry would make
     the race itself run four per cent slow.

     The original never faces this: its frame delta IS an integer
     millisecond count, and the replay records that very value.

   o ONE RECORD IS ONE SUBSTEP, NOT ONE FRAME, and that is what
     makes fast-forward work.  The physics already integrates in
     slices of at most 20 ms whatever the frame rate or the clock
     scale (frame-loop.txt 1.3.9), so recording at that level makes
     the recording paced in SIMULATED time: about fifty records a
     simulated second, from a 17 ms frame or from a 640 ms one.

     A 40x frame is 32 substeps of 20 ms - which fit the record's
     one byte, where the 640 did not.  And a playback then spends
     whatever the wall clock is really worth: race_replay_peek_steps
     counts how many recorded steps fit in the frame that actually
     elapsed, so a race recorded under TAB plays back at NORMAL
     speed.  Recording at frame level could not do either.

     The buffer holds about 5.8 minutes of racing at 60fps on this
     pacing, against eleven at the original's frame-level ~30fps.

   o EVERY STEP IS A WHOLE NUMBER OF MILLISECONDS.  The frame's
     millisecond budget is shared out across its substeps with the
     remainder going one each to the first few, so the record can
     express exactly the step the simulation took.  A substep of
     dt/steps generally cannot be written down in the record, and a
     record that disagrees with the simulation is a playback that
     drifts.

   NOT PORTED: the .Sav file, so a replay lives only as long as the
   process.  Retail files could not be played back anyway without
   bit-exact physics (section 4).

7.1.  Open questions

   o THE FILE FORMAT. Only the in-memory record is decoded. What a
     .Sav wraps around it - the header, the version, the track and
     bike identifiers - is unread.

   o ANSWERED, BOTH OF THEM - see section 6. The AI are
     re-simulated and they crash where they crashed, and the
     player's crashes come back bit-identical.
