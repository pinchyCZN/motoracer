MotoRacer PC Reverse Engineering                           Analysis Note


                           THE FX PARTICLES


Abstract

   One pool of 128 records drives every puff of dust in the game:
   the wheel spray, the crash smoke and the rider's landing. This
   note is the whole chain - the record, the allocator, the burst
   helper, the per-frame tick and the draw.

   The port has none of it. racer_fx_gate, racer_fx_in_range and
   racer_fx_particle_count are written and have no callers; nothing
   else exists. So a crash produces no smoke and a slide leaves no
   spray. See port-divergences.txt 2.22.

1.  The pool

   +---------------------------+------------+-------------------------+
   | Item                      | Address    | Note                    |
   +===========================+============+=========================+
   | g_fxParticles             | 0x0052c498 | 128 x 260 bytes         |
   +---------------------------+------------+-------------------------+
   | maybe_Fx_SpawnParticle    | 0x00457620 | find a slot, construct  |
   +---------------------------+------------+-------------------------+
   | maybe_Fx_InitParticle     | 0x004afe90 | the constructor         |
   +---------------------------+------------+-------------------------+
   | maybe_Fx_FreeParticle     | 0x004afe30 | clears the in-use flag  |
   +---------------------------+------------+-------------------------+
   | maybe_Fx_ResetParticlePool| 0x00413dd0 | frees all 128           |
   +---------------------------+------------+-------------------------+
   | maybe_Fx_TickAllParticles | 0x00457590 | walk, tick the live     |
   +---------------------------+------------+-------------------------+
   | maybe_Fx_TickParticle     | 0x004b0730 | one particle, one frame |
   +---------------------------+------------+-------------------------+
   | maybe_Fx_DrawAllParticles | 0x004575c0 | walk, draw the live     |
   +---------------------------+------------+-------------------------+
   | maybe_Fx_DrawParticle     | 0x004b0be0 | queue two quads         |
   +---------------------------+------------+-------------------------+
   | maybe_Fx_Burst            | 0x00467c40 | spawn N in a frame      |
   +---------------------------+------------+-------------------------+

   THE POOL IS A MEMBER OF g_game, at 0x0052c478 + 0x20, and that
   matters for anyone hunting the readers: the tick and the draw
   index off the game pointer and never mention 0x0052c498, so a
   displacement search finds only the three functions that take the
   base directly. Both were found with a hardware watchpoint on a
   live slot instead.

   ALLOCATION IS A LINEAR SCAN for the first record whose +0xfc is
   zero, and a full pool SILENTLY DROPS the request. With 24
   particles per player frame from the wheel spray the pool does
   fill - measured, 122 of 128 busy two frames after a crash - so a
   burst arriving on a full pool is ordinary behaviour rather than
   an error case.

2.  The record

   260 bytes, and all of it is now accounted for. Offsets are
   bytes; the constructor and the tick both index in dwords.

   +--------+------+--------------+---------------------------------+
   | Offset | Size | Field        | Note                            |
   +========+======+==============+=================================+
   | +0x000 |   4  | kind         | 5 for smoke and spray; 1..4 is  |
   |        |      |              | a second family the same tick   |
   |        |      |              | and draw handle                 |
   +--------+------+--------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x004 |  12  | position     | world                           |
   +--------+------+--------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x010 |  12  | velocity     | the burst's matrix term         |
   +--------+------+--------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x01c |  12  | rotation     | accumulated, zeroed at spawn    |
   +--------+------+--------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x028 |  12  | rotation rate| added to the above each frame   |
   +--------+------+--------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x038 |   4  | life, int    | as spawned, never decremented   |
   +--------+------+--------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x03c |   4  | life, float  | THE COUNTDOWN                   |
   +--------+------+--------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x040 |  64  | MR_Primitive | the front quad                  |
   +--------+------+--------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x080 |  64  | MR_Primitive | the back quad                   |
   +--------+------+--------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x0c0 |  48  | corners      | four xyz, model space, Z = 0    |
   +--------+------+--------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x0f0 |   4  | size         | an INT, not a float             |
   +--------+------+--------------+---------------------------------+
   | +0x0fc |   4  | in use       | the allocator's only test       |
   +--------+------+--------------+---------------------------------+

   THE TWO PRIMITIVES LIVE IN THE RECORD, which is what makes it 260
   bytes for something that is conceptually a point and a velocity.
   maybe_Fx_DrawParticle passes +0x40 straight to
   Screen3D_CullAndQueueFacesEx with a count of 2.

   +0x0f0 IS AN INT AND READS AS 0.0 IF YOU DUMP IT AS A FLOAT. The
   values are 40 and 80, whose bit patterns are denormals. The
   corners are built from it as +-2 * size, so a puff is 160 or 320
   units across.

   THE LIFETIME PAIR IS A COUNTDOWN AND ITS ORIGINAL. Only the float
   is decremented; the int keeps the spawn value. That settles the
   question maybe_Fx_InitParticle's plate comment left open.

3.  Spawning

3.1.  maybe_Fx_Burst

       void maybe_Fx_Burst(int count, float x, float y, float z,
                           int lifeBase, uint lifeMask, float m[12])

   THE MATRIX IS PASSED BY VALUE, and that is why the argument list
   reads six long at both call sites. Each does SUB ESP,0x30 and
   fills the 48 bytes with Matrix34_Copy before pushing the scalars.

   Per particle:

       life    = (rand() & lifeMask) + lifeBase
       pos     = (x, y + _DAT_004d10a0, z)      THE SAME FOR ALL
       vel     = f3*m.row0 + f2*m.row1 + f1*m.row2 + m.row3
                 each f is a fresh rand() & 3
       rotRate = three rand()s sign-extended from 5 bits, times
                 _DAT_004d1150 - DRAWN ONCE, BEFORE THE LOOP
       size    = ((rand() & 1) * 5 + 5) * 8     40 or 80
       colour  = Color_Pack16(4, 4, 4)
       kind    = 5

   THE THREE COMPONENTS OF THE MATRIX TERM ARE THE VELOCITY, NOT A
   POSITION OFFSET, and that is worth stating because the shape of
   the expression invites the other reading. Every particle in a
   burst starts at the same point and separates because it is
   thrown differently.

   IT IS THROWN IN THE CALLER'S OWN FRAME. A 0..3 cube along the
   matrix's rows, so a bike going down sideways throws its smoke
   sideways rather than along the world axes.

   THE ROTATION RATE IS SHARED BY THE WHOLE BURST. Its three rands
   are drawn before the loop, so every particle spins the same way
   and only the throw and the life differ - which is what makes a
   burst read as one puff coming apart rather than as a firework.

   CONFIRMED AGAINST FIVE LIVE SLOTS. A crash on ile01 with the bike
   at (1248, 0, 2058) heading +x, matOrientation rows (0,0,-1)
   (0,1,0) (1,0,0), filled slots 123..127 with rotation rates all
   exactly (5, 14, 3)/32 and velocities whose x and -z components
   were the integers 0..3 the formula predicts.

3.2.  Where the crash calls it

   +------------+---------------------------+---------------------------+
   | Site       | Call                      | When                      |
   +============+===========================+===========================+
   | 0x004681b3 | (6, position, 0xf, 0x10,  | the bike goes down        |
   |            |  matOrientation)          |                           |
   +------------+---------------------------+---------------------------+
   | 0x004686f6 | (4, riderPosition, 7, 4,  | the rider lands, and only |
   |            |  matRiderRoot)            | when crashLaunchDir.y is  |
   |            |                           | over 0.05                 |
   +------------+---------------------------+---------------------------+

   So a crash is six particles at the bike with lives 16..31, then
   four more where the rider hits with lives 4..10. The landing puff
   is smaller, shorter and gated - a rider already sliding when the
   crash starts never produces it.

   The wheel spray is the third client, from maybe_Racer_EmitWheelFx
   at 0x0046790b - 24 particles a frame for the human player, 4 for
   an AI. See racers.txt 9.12.2.

4.  The tick

   THE CONSTANTS, read out of the running binary. Watch the widths:
   the two the tick integrates with are DOUBLES whose low dwords read
   as absurd floats.

   +----------------+-----------+------------------------------------+
   | Address        | Value     | Use                                |
   +================+===========+====================================+
   | 0x004d2070     | -0.0864   | particle gravity, a DOUBLE         |
   +----------------+-----------+------------------------------------+
   | 0x004d2078     | 0.8       | horizontal drag per frame, DOUBLE  |
   +----------------+-----------+------------------------------------+
   | 0x004d2088     | 1.0       | float, in the billboard frame      |
   +----------------+-----------+------------------------------------+
   | 0x004d10a0     | 10.0      | float, the burst's y bias - but    |
   |                |           | see the open question              |
   +----------------+-----------+------------------------------------+
   | 0x004d1150     | 0.03125   | 1/32, the rotation-rate scale, a   |
   |                |           | DOUBLE                             |
   +----------------+-----------+------------------------------------+

   maybe_Fx_TickParticle(p, dt), called for every live slot. Kind 5:

       size = (int)p.size                    re-truncated
       rebuild the four corners from it
       rebuild BOTH quads, UVs (224,160) (255,160)
                               (255,191) (224,191)
       colour Color_Pack16(0x80,0x80,0x80) on both

       vel.y  += dt * _DAT_004d2070          gravity
       pos.y  += vel.y * dt
       vel.x  *= _DAT_004d2078               drag, a plain multiply
       pos.x  += vel.x * dt
       vel.z  *= _DAT_004d2078
       pos.z  += vel.z * dt
       rot    += rotRate
       life   -= dt
       if (life < 0) free the slot

   ONLY GRAVITY IS INTEGRATED. X and Z are damped by a per-frame
   multiply and never accelerated, so a burst drifts with its launch
   velocity, sinks and stops. There is no ground collision: a
   particle spawned below the road falls through it and is only ever
   removed by its life running out.

   THE QUADS ARE REBUILT EVERY FRAME WITH DIFFERENT UVs FROM THE
   ONES THE CONSTRUCTOR WROTE - (225,0) (255,31) at spawn against
   (224,160) (255,191) here. The tick runs before the draw, so the
   spawn UVs are never seen. Both are constants; there is no frame
   animation, whatever the eight stacked puff frames in Rect A
   suggest (d3d-textures.txt 5.2.1).

5.  The draw

   maybe_Fx_DrawParticle(p, ?, camera):

       build a billboard frame from the particle's rotation and the
         camera, translation = position - camera+0x98
       Matrix34_Multiply(M, frame, camera + 0x68)
       verts = Screen3D_TransformVertsFlat(M, &corners, 4, 0x1f)
       Screen3D_CullAndQueueFacesEx(verts, &primitives, 2, 1)

   FOUR VERTICES, TWO FACES - the same quad wound 0,1,2,3 and
   3,2,1,0, so the billboard is double-sided. A port that relies on
   backface culling alone loses half of every particle.

   The two arms of the function, kind 1..4 and kind 5, are the same
   work; the compiler duplicated the block rather than sharing it.

5.1.  It is a detail-level feature

   +------------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | g_cfgDetailLevel | Effect                                  |
   +==================+=========================================+
   | 0, 1             | nothing is drawn at all                 |
   +------------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | 2                | DAT_004f258c counts to 8 - one call in  |
   |                  | eight draws                             |
   +------------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | 3 and up         | every call draws                        |
   +------------------+-----------------------------------------+

   THE COUNTER IS GLOBAL, NOT PER PARTICLE. At detail 2 it is every
   eighth particle across the whole pool, not every eighth frame of
   one, so the effect thins the spray evenly rather than making
   individual puffs flicker.

6.  The texture

   Both quads take texId 0x44 through maybe_Prim_InitQuad, and the
   UVs the tick writes are a 32 x 32 texel patch at u 224..255,
   v 160..191.

   bnk00_01.raw is the bike's 256 x 256 particle atlas and its Rect
   A holds eight stacked 34 x 32 frames of a dust puff, luminance
   keyed. See d3d-textures.txt 5.2.1. How 0x44 resolves to that
   page is not established here. [open]

7.  What a port still needs

   THE SIMULATION HALF IS NOW WRITTEN - src/fx.c and the spray loop
   in racer.c. The pool, the free-slot scan, the constructor, the
   kind-5 tick and the 24-particle emitter are all imported whole,
   with the tick wired into race_update at GAME level rather than
   into the racer loop. What is left is the draw.

   THE DRAW HALF NEEDS ONE MORE READ. The billboard frame is inlined
   into maybe_Fx_DrawParticle and Ghidra renders it with
   extraout_ST0 / extraout_ST1 phantoms. Those names are guesses,
   and reading them as variables is exactly what produced a wrong
   mat34_from_euler once already (model.c). It wants a symbolic
   simulation of the x87 block, stack and all, before it is
   transcribed - not a transcription of the decompiler's output.

   Everything below that maps onto the port as it stands:
   Screen3D_TransformVertsFlat is model.c's transform,
   Screen3D_CullAndQueueFacesEx is cull.c plus queue_primitive, and
   MR_Primitive is already the same 64-byte record.

7.1.  The brake spray, measured live - and three corrections

   Triggered by accelerating briefly and braking for one frame on
   ile01, then reading the pool [verified].  48 slots went live, all
   kind 5.

   THE SPAWN HEIGHT QUESTION IN SECTION 8 IS ANSWERED, and it was
   asking about the wrong client.  The spray reads

       pos.y  6.85 .. 7.02      with the bike and the rear contact
                                both at y = 0
       vel.y -0.57 .. -0.64     life 23.4 of 30, so 6.6 ticks in

   and -0.0864 * 6.6 is -0.570.  So the spray DOES spawn at
   y + 10.0 - _DAT_004d10a0 straight - and integrates down from
   there exactly as the tick says.  The -29.6 that section 8 could
   not explain was a CRASH burst, which spawns at the bike matrix
   rather than the rear contact.  Two clients, two spawn points.

   THE TICK AND THE DRAW ARE GAME-LEVEL, NOT RACER-LEVEL.
   maybe_Fx_TickAllParticles has ONE caller, maybe_Game_Tick at
   0x0047d887, and maybe_Fx_DrawAllParticles has ONE, 
   maybe_Game_RenderFrame at 0x00480279.  Only the SPAWN is inside
   the racer tick, through maybe_Racer_EmitWheelFx.  A port that
   hangs the tick off the racer will run it once per racer.

   THE PARTICLE GROWS, WHICH SECTION 4 DOES NOT SAY.  Watching one
   slot for its whole life [verified]:

   +------+------+----------+-------+
   | tick | size | corner.x | life  |
   +======+======+==========+=======+
   |    0 |   16 |   -30.00 | 23.40 |
   +------+------+----------+-------+
   |    1 |   18 |   -34.00 | 16.80 |
   +------+------+----------+-------+
   |    2 |   20 |   -38.00 | 10.20 |
   +------+------+----------+-------+
   |    3 |   22 |   -42.00 |  3.60 |
   +------+------+----------+-------+
   |    4 |   24 |        - | freed |
   +------+------+----------+-------+

   size rises by 2 every tick and the corners follow it, so a puff
   EXPANDS as it fades.  The spray spawns with 14 and the first
   readable value is already 16.

   THE CORNERS ARE +-2*size AFTER ALL - AN ORDERING ARTEFACT, and the
   disassembly settles it:

       004b094b  EAX = [ESI+0xf0]            size, read FIRST
       004b0951  FST  [ESP+0xa0]             ST0 = dt
       004b0958  FMUL [0x004d2058]           dt * 0.6, a DOUBLE
       004b095e  EDI = EAX*2                 the corners take THIS
       004b0969  FILD size
       004b096d  FADDP                       size + dt*0.6
       004b096f  __ftol -> [ESI+0xf0]         and only now store it

   so the corners are built from the PRE-increment size and the
   +-2*(size-1) that every sample fitted is that one step of lag.

   AND THE GROWTH IS +1 A TICK, NOT +2. dt is about 3.3, so
   3.3 * 0.6 = 1.98 and truncation takes it to +1: 14 -> 15.98 -> 15,
   then 16.98 -> 16. The +2 in the table above is MOTO's SEND_INPUT
   overrun - frames=1 runs two - so each row is two ticks apart. That
   reconciles every number: sizes 16, 18, 20 two ticks apart with
   corners -30, -34, -38 from 15, 17, 19.

   LIFE FALLS 3.3 A TICK - 6.6 per two-tick step - and the slot is
   freed on the tick life goes NEGATIVE, not the one it reaches zero.
   The test is on the float's sign bit, so -0.0 does not free it.

7.2.  What the spray passes, read whole

   maybe_Racer_EmitWheelFx's loop at 0x0046760a..0x0046790b, once per
   particle, count 24 for a human and 4 for an AI:

       rot rate    three rand()s sign-extended from 5 bits * 1/32,
                   DRAWN PER PARTICLE here - unlike maybe_Fx_Burst,
                   which draws them once for the whole burst (3.1)
       throw       a = rand() & 3, b = rand() & 3, and b is NEGATED
                   unless buttons & 4 - steering flips the spread
       velocity    m[0]*b + m[6]*a + m[9], and the same for y and z
                   with rows 1,7,10 and 2,8,11.  The m[3..5] term is
                   multiplied by 0.0 and drops out.
       position    lerp BACK along rear -> rearPrev by i * K1158,
                   so the 24 particles lay a trail behind the wheel
                   rather than stacking on one point
       life        ((rand() & 1) * 2 + 2) * 15   ->  30 or 60
       colour      Color_Pack16(4, 4, 4)
       call        Fx_SpawnParticle(14, pos, vel, rot, life, 0,
                                    colour, 5)

   THE FIRST ARGUMENT IS 14 AND IT IS THE SIZE, not the kind - the
   kind is the LAST argument and is 5.  14 grows to 16 by the first
   readable tick.

7.3.  The port verified against the pool

   TWO TESTS, because the RNG cannot be shared.  MOTO and the port
   each run their own CRT rand(), so slot values can never match and
   an exact pool diff is meaningless.  What is testable splits in
   two: the SHAPE of a spawn, and the ARITHMETIC of the tick.

   THE SPAWN, after one brake frame [verified]:

   +-------------------+-------------------+-------------------+
   | property          | MOTO (2 frames)   | port (1 frame)    |
   +===================+===================+===================+
   | live slots        | 48                | 24                |
   +-------------------+-------------------+-------------------+
   | kinds             | [5]               | [5]               |
   +-------------------+-------------------+-------------------+
   | lifetimes         | [30, 60]          | [30, 60]          |
   +-------------------+-------------------+-------------------+
   | pos.y - rear.y    | 6.69 .. 9.06      | 8.82 .. 9.06      |
   +-------------------+-------------------+-------------------+
   | rot rate, max     | 0.156 .. 0.969    | 0.313 .. 0.969    |
   +-------------------+-------------------+-------------------+

   24 a frame, kind 5 only, exactly the two lifetimes, the spawn lift
   landing under 10.0 and falling, and rotation rates on the 1/32
   grid.  Every difference is MOTO's SEND_INPUT overrun running two
   frames to the port's one.

   THE TICK IS BIT-EXACT.  Seeding ONE identical particle into slot 0
   of both pools takes the generator out of the picture entirely;
   integrated for twelve ticks, position, velocity, rotation, life,
   size and the corners agree to the last digit at every step, with
   max |dpos| = 0 throughout [verified].

   A HARNESS TRAP WORTH KEEPING: SET_MEM caps the command length and
   a 260-byte record exceeds it.  The first run of this test wrote
   NOTHING, said nothing about it, and compared two leftover
   particles - which looked like a real 11-unit divergence.  Write
   the record in chunks and read it back before trusting the result.

7.4.  The billboard frame, read and then measured

   Section 7 said this needed a symbolic read of the x87 block rather
   than a transcription of the decompiler's extraout_ST0/ST1
   phantoms.  Both were done and the phantoms were a FALSE ALARM.

   THE THREE SUBTRACTIONS, 0x004b0c52..0x004b0c6f, traced on the x87
   stack:

       FLD  pos.x                        (x)
       FLD  pos.y                        (y, x)
       FLD  pos.z                        (z, y, x)
       FSUB cam[0xa0]                    (dz, y, x)
       FXCH ST2                          (x, y, dz)
       FSUB cam[0x98]                    (dx, y, dz)
       FXCH                              (y, dx, dz)
       FSUB cam[0x9c]                    (dy, dx, dz)

   so the camera position is +0x98/+0x9c/+0xa0 and the three deltas
   are the ordinary ones.  Ghidra assigned one of them to the WRONG
   matrix slot, which is what made the row look scrambled.

   THE ROTATION COLLAPSES TO A Y-AXIS TURN.  The compiler emitted a
   full three-axis euler build, but two of the three angles are
   CONSTANT - g_cosTable[0] at 0x005fcb80 is 1.0 and
   g_cosTable[0x3000] at 0x00608b80 is 0.0 - so nearly every term
   vanishes:

       row0  ( cos, 0, -sin)
       row1  (   0, 1,    0)
       row2  ( sin, 0,  cos)

   with the angle from camera+0xc0 * _DAT_004d2080 through the
   0x4000-entry table.  Everything else in that block multiplies by
   1.0 or 0.0.

   MEASURED AT THE CALL, hooking 0x004b0ed9 - the LEA after
   Matrix34_Multiply in the KIND-5 arm [verified].  Two live smoke
   particles:

       matrix rows passed to Screen3D_TransformVertsFlat
           ( 0.00000,  0.00000,  1.00000)
           ( 0.02454,  0.99970, -0.00000)
           (-0.99970,  0.02454,  0.00000)
           (142.53331, -149.38609, 237.11681)

   That sample was taken LOOKING STRAIGHT DOWN THE TRACK, where
   camera+0xc0 is 0 and the turn is degenerate - so the rotation came
   back as the view matrix unchanged.  Recaptured with the camera 30
   degrees off axis it is not:

       ( 1.00000 -0.00001 -0.00007)
       ( 0.00000  0.99623 -0.08677)     the view's PITCH, alone
       ( 0.00007  0.08677  0.99623)

   camera+0xc0 IS THE CAMERA'S YAW, NEGATED - see camera.txt 1.10 -
   so RotY of it CANCELS the view's yaw and leaves the pitch.  That is
   the billboard: the quad ends up facing the camera.

   TREATING THE TURN AS IDENTITY MAKES SMOKE GO EDGE-ON as the camera
   swings, and vanish near 90 degrees.  Nothing shows on a
   straight-line brake test, which is why the degenerate sample looked
   conclusive.

   Solving the translation back through the view rotation gives
   (237.12, -145.84, -146.16) against a pos - camera of
   (237.12, -145.84, -114.04): x and y to the digit, and z apart by
   exactly the distance the camera travelled between reading it and
   the hook firing.  So the translation is pos - camera, plainly.

   THE HOOK MUST GO IN THE KIND-5 ARM.  0x004b0c44 sends kind 5 to a
   DUPLICATED block at 0x004b0dae, so a hook in the first arm - which
   is what reads naturally from the decompiler's output - takes zero
   hits forever.

   KINDS 1..4 ARE THE TYRE TRAIL, not dead code.  They reach the pool
   through a DIFFERENT constructor - maybe_Fx_SpawnTrail (0x004576a0)
   into FUN_004b00e0 - so counting the callers of
   maybe_Fx_SpawnParticle does not enumerate the kinds.  Section 11
   has the whole path.

   SO THE DRAW IS:

       frame          = RotY(camera[0xc0] * K2080)
       frame.transl   = particle.pos - camera.pos
       M              = frame * camera[0x68]
       verts          = TransformVertsFlat(M, particle.corners, 4, 0x1f)
                        CullAndQueueFacesEx(verts, particle.quad, 2, 1)

7.5.  The quads, and what a live record actually holds

   maybe_Prim_InitQuad (0x00422d20) was already read - the DB comment
   on it has the full byte layout, and it matches MR_Primitive as the
   port already declares it.  What it does NOT tell you is what the
   record holds once the two calls after it have run, and that is
   where the useful facts are.  Read off a live smoke particle
   [verified]:

   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | field          | value                                     |
   +================+===========================================+
   | kind, effKind  | 0x44                                      |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | colorIndex     | 0xffdf                                    |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | bankIndex      | 1                                         |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | flags          | 0                                         |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | flatColor      | 0x00f8f8f8                                |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | vertexIndex    | 0,1,2,3,0   and   3,2,1,0,3               |
   +----------------+-------------------------------------------+

   0xffdf IS Color_Pack16(0x80,0x80,0x80) SATURATED.  The packer is
   (r << 11) | (g << 6) | b on FIVE-bit channels, so 0x80 clamps to
   31 in all three: 0xf800 | 0x7c0 | 0x1f.  Deriving it as 0x80 >> 3
   gives 0x8410 and a visibly darker puff.

   AND THE ALPHA BYTE IS ZERO, unlike the ribbon and the shadow which
   both carry 0xff there.  0x00f8f8f8 is the bare 5-5-5 unpack
   shifted left three.

   THE UVs IN THE LIVE RECORD ARE NOT THE ONES PASSED.  The tick hands
   InitQuad (224,160)(255,160)(255,191)(224,191), but the record
   afterwards reads 1.0 in the first eight slots with only the ninth
   pair holding (224,160).  FUN_00423060, called immediately after
   each InitQuad, rewrites them.  What it computes is unread.

   THAT DOES NOT BLOCK THE PORT and should not be transcribed
   blindly: CLAUDE.md puts the primitive queue on the REPLACE list,
   the DB comment on InitQuad says the same, and our renderer
   consumes MR_Primitive.uv directly in texels.  So the port writes
   the texel UVs the tick passes and lets its own queue read them.
   [open - what FUN_00423060 does to the UV block, and whether it
   matters once the texture is bound]

7.6.  Black smoke, and why an unbound bank is worse than none

   The draw went in and the particles appeared BLACK where retail has
   white.  Everything in the record was right - read back off a live
   port particle, kind 0x44, colorIndex 0xffdf, flatColor 0x00f8f8f8,
   texel UVs and both windings, all matching MOTO.

   THE BANK INDEX IS NOT PORTABLE, and copying the original's literal
   1 is what did it.  MOTO's bank table has stride 824 with the name
   at +76, and reading it out gives

       bank 0   data\speed\bnk00_00.raw
       bank 1   data\speed\bnk00_01.raw     <- the particle atlas
       bank 2   data\ile01\bank\bnk00_02.raw

   so the page is loaded whatever track is running - "SPEED" there is
   a shared asset directory, not the Speed Bay track.  THE PORT LOADS
   THE SAME FILE AT BANK 4: its level list starts at
   RACE_TRK_BANK_BASE = 8 and its contents differ from the
   original's, so bank 1 is simply empty.

   AND AN UNBOUND BANK DRAWS BLACK RATHER THAN UNTEXTURED, which is
   the part worth keeping.  prim_is_textured correctly returns 0 -
   the bank is not loaded - so the COLOUR comes out of the flat path,
   which is 0xfff8f8f8 shaded at intensity 31, a near-white.  But the
   blend ladder's texture bit is built from the KIND ALONE, without
   asking whether a page is bound (drawscene.c says so outright), and
   kind 0x44 is not one of the three untextured kinds.  So the device
   is told to modulate against a texture that is not there.

   The bank is now resolved BY NAME once, and a particle whose atlas
   is missing does not draw at all - which is right in a way that
   drawing black is not.

   ONE COMMENT CORRECTED: drawscene.c said "Nothing in the game is
   known to produce kind 0x44 or 0x45".  The smoke produces 0x44 at
   both InitQuad call sites.  0x45 is still unproduced.

7.7.  Still black - the atlas is nearly black, and the key was not
      being cut away

   Binding the right bank was necessary and not sufficient.  With
   s_fxBank resolving to 4 and the primitive carrying it, the smoke
   was still black.

   THE ATLAS IS DARK ON PURPOSE.  Sampling bank 4's page over the
   tick's UV patch - u 224..255, v 160..191 - gives RGB565 values of
   0x2124 and 0x0861, which unpack to (4, 9, 4) and (1, 3, 1) out of
   (31, 63, 31).  Rows 160 and 191 are entirely zero.  So the puff is
   a nearly-black smudge surrounded by pure black, and NOTHING
   modulated by it can come out white.

   THE BLACK IS THE COLOUR KEY, AND IT WAS NOT BEING CUT AWAY.
   Bank 4's flags are 0x0013 - SRC16 and KEYED both set - so those
   zero texels are meant to VANISH.  The original keys at the
   DirectDraw level: D3D_AllocTextureSlot sets a DDCOLORKEY of black
   with DDCKEY_SRCBLT, and a keyed texel is absent whatever blend
   mode is in force.

   THE PORT COUPLED THE TEST TO THE BLEND.  drawscene expressed the
   key as alpha in the texture and then turned the alpha test on with

       int keyed = (blend == R_BLEND_SRCALPHA);

   which is right for every kind that reaches the ladder's
   conditional row and wrong for the four that return before it:
   0x2c, 0x3e, 0x44 and 0x45 all take the half blend.  A keyed
   texture on one of those blended its key colour in at fifty per
   cent - and the particles are kind 0x44 on a keyed bank.

   IT IS NOW ASKED OF THE TEXTURE.  The depth rule still asks about
   the BLEND, deliberately: a half-blended surface is translucent and
   must not write depth, which is a different question from whether
   its texels are keyed.

   WHAT THIS PREDICTS ELSEWHERE: any other keyed texture drawn at
   kind 0x2c, 0x3e or 0x45 was showing its key colour too, and will
   now stop.  Nothing else in the port is known to use those kinds
   yet, so the smoke is the first thing to exercise the path.

   AND IT WAS STILL NOT ENOUGH.  The key cuts away the pure black
   AROUND each frame - 17.4 per cent of Rect A - and leaves the puff
   itself, which is genuinely dark.  See 7.8.

7.8.  The atlas is BRIGHTENED AT UPLOAD, and the port does not do it

   The particle UVs, u 224..255 and v 160..191, land inside RECT A of
   bnk00_01.raw - x 222..255, y 0..255 - and Rect A is not an
   ordinary keyed region.  d3d-textures.txt 5.2 has it:

       each texel is converted to HSV by Color_RgbToHsv (0x0041bb20),
       THE ALPHA IS TAKEN FROM THE RESULT, the value component is
       then SCALED BY A CONSTANT, and Color_HsvToRgb (0x0041bd30)
       converts back

   so what the original uploads is the BRIGHTENED colour carrying a
   BRIGHTNESS-DERIVED alpha.  The port's bank_texture uploads the raw
   texel with binary alpha - 0 on the key, 255 everywhere else.

   THAT IS THE WHOLE REMAINING GAP.  Rect A holds eight stacked
   34 x 32 frames of a dust puff "fading light to dark across the
   sequence", and v 160..191 is frame 5 of 8 - one of the darker
   ones.  Sampled raw it reads (4, 9, 4) and (1, 3, 1) out of
   (31, 63, 31).  Uploaded raw and drawn opaque, that is a black
   blob; uploaded the original's way it is a pale translucent wisp
   that fades as the sequence dims.

   RECT B IS THE SAME MECHANISM AND SIMPLER: a flat alpha of 8 out of
   15 over x 0..119, y 0..40, which is the bike's shadow silhouette.

   BOTH ARE NOW IMPLEMENTED in bank_texture, with the constants read
   off maybe_D3D_LoadRawToTexture at 0x0041cef7..0x0041cf7e:

       Rect A   alpha = (int)(v * 16), then v *= 5, then back to RGB
       Rect B   a flat alpha of 8, the MOV EDI,8 at 0x0041cf79

   and the four-bit alpha scaled to eight for our A8R8G8B8 pages.
   d3d-textures.txt 5.2 carries the derivation.

   THE COLOUR KEY STILL WINS INSIDE THE RECTANGLES.  The original
   keys at the surface with a DDCOLORKEY, which is independent of the
   per-texel alpha - so Rect B's black surround is cut away rather
   than drawn at its flat half-alpha.  Rect A needs no special case:
   a black texel has v = 0 and the brightness alpha is 0 anyway.

   WHICH PAGES GET IT is the original's four-way path match, not a
   test on the file name.  The literals are

       data\SpeedPkt\bnk00_01.raw     data\speed\bnk00_01.raw
       data\ktmcross\bnk00_01.raw     data\KTMpkt\bnk00_01.raw

   and only two distinct pages sit behind them: SPEED is byte-identical
   to SPEEDPKT, KTMCROSS to KTMPKT.  The four names are per bike
   DIRECTORY.

   WE COMPARE DIRECTORY AND FILE, CASE-INSENSITIVELY, and ignore any
   leading prefix, because our logical names are archive-relative where
   the original's are ini-configured paths.  The original needs four
   spellings only because its strcmp is case-sensitive.

   A BARE TAIL MATCH ON THE FILE NAME IS WRONG.  The title screen loads
   PAGES\TITLEDSI\BNK00_01.RAW, so a name-only test hands it the
   dust atlas's alpha - the right-hand 34 columns brightened and a
   half-alpha band across the top.

   THE HALF-SIZE PAGE HALVES EVERY BOUND, arm two at 0x0041c3b9, taken
   when bank flag 8 is set.  No stock data reaches it; the atlas ships
   full size.

       full   Rect A  x 222..255  y 0..255   Rect B  x 0..119  y 0..40
       half   Rect A  x 111..127  y 0..127   Rect B  x 0.. 59  y 0..20

   WHY THE CHASE TOOK THREE ROUNDS, recorded so the next one is
   shorter: the symptom "black where retail is white" had THREE
   independent causes stacked - an unbound bank (7.6), a colour key
   that was never cut away (7.7), and this. Each fix was necessary,
   none was sufficient, and after each one the symptom looked
   unchanged. A texture that draws black is worth suspecting of more
   than one fault.

7.9.  The crash burst - the other emitter

   maybe_Fx_Burst (0x00467c40) is the second and last thing that
   spawns a particle.  Both its call sites are in the crash, and both
   pass the matrix BY VALUE - SUB ESP,0x30 then Matrix34_Copy before
   the six scalars, which is why the argument list reads as eighteen.
   RET 0x48 confirms it.

   +----------------------------+-------+----------------+-----------+
   | Call site                  | Count | Position       | Frame     |
   +============================+=======+================+===========+
   | 0x004681b3 BeginCrash      |   6   | racer+0x30     | +0xbc     |
   |                            |       | position       | matOrien- |
   |                            |       |                | tation    |
   +----------------------------+-------+----------------+-----------+
   | 0x00468708 UpdateCrash     |   4   | racer+0x30b5   | +0x3085   |
   |                            |       | riderPosition  | matRider- |
   |                            |       |                | Root      |
   +----------------------------+-------+----------------+-----------+

   The bike puff is the last thing BeginCrash does.  The rider puff is
   gated on crashLaunchDir.y clearing 0.05 (_DAT_004d1198).

   LIFE IS TWO VALUES, NOT A RANGE.  It is (rand() & mask) + base, and
   the mask is a SINGLE BIT at both sites:

       bike    (rand & 0x10) + 0xf    ->  15 or 31
       rider   (rand & 4)    + 7      ->   7 or 11

   so a burst is half short-lived and half long-lived rather than
   evenly spread.  An earlier note gave these as 0x10..0x1f and
   4..0xa, which is what the arguments look like if base and mask are
   read the wrong way round.

   THE RATES ARE DRAWN ONCE, BEFORE THE LOOP.  Every particle in one
   burst spins together, and only the offset, the size and the life
   differ - which is what makes a burst read as one puff rather than
   as a firework.  THE WHEEL SPRAY IS THE OPPOSITE and draws its rates
   per particle; the two must not be copied to each other.

   THE SPREAD IS A 0..3 CUBE IN THE CALLER'S OWN FRAME, so a machine
   going down sideways throws its dust sideways.  All three rows count
   here, where the spray multiplies the middle row by zero.

   BOTH THE RATES AND THE THREE COEFFICIENTS ARE CONSUMED IN REVERSE
   of the order they are drawn - the third draw becomes rot[0] and
   scales row0.  Getting that backwards still looks like dust, so it
   shows up in a pool diff and not in a screenshot.

   THE RIDER PUFF IS NOT A ONE-SHOT.  It sets riderStateBits bit 0x40
   at 0x0046870d, but nothing tests that bit - the OR is the only
   access to racer+0x2dac in the whole of UpdateCrash - and
   crashLaunchDir.y is rewritten every frame at 0x0046856a.  So it
   fires on each frame the rider is coming down hard.

   EVERY CONSTANT IS THE SPRAY'S.  The rate scale (_DAT_004d1150,
   0.03125), the lift (_DAT_004d10a0, 10.0) and the colour
   (Color_Pack16(4,4,4) = 0x2104) are the same values, and the first
   two are the same addresses.

   THE OTHER TWO TRIPLES, and this is easy to get backwards:
   maybe_Fx_SpawnParticle's args 5..7 become the particle's VELOCITY
   and args 8..10 its ROTATION RATE.  Settled by the bit-exact pool
   diff of the spray, where the matrix-derived triple reads back as
   velocity and the sign-extended one as rotation rate.

9.  The skid ribbons - the other pool, and it is now in the port

   The particles are one of TWO fx pools and the smaller half of the
   work.  The ribbons are the black strip a sliding tyre leaves, and
   they are transcribed: src/fx.c carries the record, the
   initialiser, the appender and the draw.

   +---------------------------+------------+------------------------+
   | Item                      | Address    | Note                   |
   +===========================+============+========================+
   | g_fxRibbons               | 0x00542a28 | 16 x 5640 bytes        |
   | FUN_00457120              | 0x00457120 | init one, 64 faces     |
   | FUN_00457300              | 0x00457300 | init all sixteen       |
   | maybe_Fx_UpdateRibbon     | 0x00457390 | bounds-check the slot  |
   | maybe_Fx_AppendRibbonQuad | 0x00457200 | append two vertices    |
   | FUN_004573e0              | 0x004573e0 | transform and queue    |
   +---------------------------+------------+------------------------+

   FUN_004573e0 is called from maybe_Game_RenderFrame at 0x0048027e,
   beside maybe_Fx_DrawAllParticles.

9.1.  The first 4096 bytes are the PRIMITIVES

   racers.txt 9.16.1 records them as "NOT touched by the appender"
   and guesses at "32 bytes per vertex ... nothing read so far uses
   them".  THAT IS SETTLED AND THE GUESS WAS WRONG.  The draw passes
   ribbon+0 to Screen3D_CullAndQueueFacesEx as the FACE ARRAY, and
   4096 / 64 is 64 MR_Primitives.  The appender does not touch them
   because the INITIALISER builds them once:

       +0x0000   64 x MR_Primitive
       +0x1000  128 x vec3
       +0x1600  int quadCount, capped 62
       +0x1604  int vertexCount

   FUN_00457120 lays a TRIANGLE STRIP over the vertex pairs - 32
   iterations, two triangles each, indices advancing by two:

       Face_InitTriangle(0x0c, colour, i,     i + 1, i + 2)
       Face_InitTriangle(0x0c, colour, i + 1, i + 3, i + 2)

   so 64 faces cover all 128 vertices with no gaps.

9.2.  It is not textured, and the colour is derived

   Kind 0x0c is one of the three prim_is_textured rejects outright,
   so a ribbon draws as its flat colour and nothing else - no bank,
   no UVs.  That is why the record has no texture fields.

   The colour is Color_Pack16(8, 8, 8) = 0x4208, which
   Face_InitTriangle unpacks 5-5-5 and shifts left 3 into flatColor
   0x404040 - a dark grey.  Color_Pack16 is (r << 11) | (g << 6) | b,
   NOT true 565: green gets five bits at shift 6 and bit 5 is never
   written.

9.3.  What the port has, and the one thing it does not

   fx_ribbon_reset_all runs at race setup and each racer is handed
   two slots into fxSlot0 and fxSlot1 - sixteen over an eight-bike
   field is exactly two each.  fx_ribbon_draw_all runs beside
   fx_draw_all_particles, where the original draws it.

   NOTHING APPENDS YET.  maybe_Fx_UpdateRibbon's only callers are two
   sites inside maybe_Racer_EmitWheelFx (0x00467459 and 0x00467518),
   passing fxSlot0 and fxSlot1 with a wheel contact pair and
   segmentStart as the search hint.  That function is 2998 bytes and
   drives the spray as well as the ribbon, so it is its own import.
   The pool is live, drawn and empty until it lands.  [open]

10.  maybe_Racer_EmitWheelFx, imported whole

   2998 bytes, called once per racer per tick from all three tick
   variants and only when maybe_Racer_UpdateGroundContact set its
   flag.  It drives all three effects, which is why it could not be
   sliced for the ribbon alone.

   ORDER MATTERS AND IT IS NOT THE SHADOW'S.  maybe_Racer_Tick calls
   it at 0x0045d7e4, AFTER UpdateLean (0x0045d7c9), so it sees the
   SWUNG wheel contacts - a wheelie throws its spray from the raised
   wheel.  The shadow is built at 0x0045d7c2, BEFORE the lean, and
   sees the flat ones.  Two consumers of the same two fields, one
   step apart, wanting opposite things.  See racer-draw-audit.txt 62.

   THE STRUCTURE:

       1  the copy-protection check - NOT reconstructed, no payload
       2  the range gate, [viewed - 10, viewed + 30]
       3  the trail, gated on onGround and a positive surface id
       4  a speed floor of 5.0, and everything below sits behind it
       5  a fresh ribbon pair on the rising edge of brake or fxGate
       6  the two ribbons, rear to fxSlot0 and front to fxSlot1
       7  the spray

10.3.  The edge never fired - tickInputWord was never written

   First run after the import: no marks on screen, and the pool said
   why.  Every one of the sixteen ribbons held vertexCount 2 and
   quadCount 0, and the racer's slots walked 2/3 -> 8/9 -> 12/13
   across three brakes.

   So the emitter WAS running and the allocator WAS handing out
   slots - two per frame of braking rather than two per brake.  Each
   fresh ribbon got exactly one append before the next frame replaced
   it, and a ribbon needs TWO appends before it has a quad to draw.
   Zero quads means the draw skips it, so nothing was ever queued.

   THE CAUSE IS ONE FIELD.  The rising-edge test is

       (tickInputWord & 2) == 0 && (buttons & 2) != 0

   and tickInputWord was read in two places and written in NONE, so
   the left half was permanently true and every braking frame looked
   like the first.

   maybe_Racer_Tick stores the buttons into racer+0x2dc8 at
   0x0045d7fd - the last instruction of its call list, AFTER
   maybe_Racer_EmitWheelFx reads the old value at 0x004671fa.  That
   ordering is the whole mechanism: the field holds the PREVIOUS
   frame's word precisely because the latch trails the reader.

   Added at the end of race_tick_physics.  The port has no buttons
   argument there - race_poll_input leaves the word on the racer - so
   it latches inputWord | inputWord2, which is what the dispatch and
   the emitter both use.

   o AN UNWRITTEN FIELD IS NOT AN INERT ONE.  It read zero, which is
     a legal value, and it turned an edge into a level.  The tell was
     in the pool rather than the code: sixteen ribbons all stuck at
     the same tiny count is an allocation rate problem, not a
     geometry one.

   o AND A DIAGNOSTIC READ THE WRONG OFFSET. The first pass reported
     onGround 0 throughout, which would have meant the ribbon branch
     could never run; it was read at 0x2e2d where MR_Racer puts the
     field at 0x2db8. Read correctly it is 1. Nothing was concluded
     from the bad value, but it was one step from a second wrong
     diagnosis.

10.4.  The width reads velDir, not moveDir

   maybe_Racer_SlipSpeed is __thiscall on MODEL INSTANCE 0, racer+0x24,
   and reads instance+0x10c off it.  That is racer+0x130, velDir - NOT
   racer+0x18c, moveDir.  All six callers pass racer+0x24: the two
   verified here are maybe_Racer_ApplySteering, which loads
   LEA ECX,[ESI+0x24] at 0x00466249, and maybe_Racer_ApplyCornering,
   which already holds the instance in ESI at 0x0045d15c.

   THE TWO FIELDS ARE EQUAL WHILE THE BIKE GOES WHERE IT POINTS, so a
   straight-line comparison cannot separate them.  Measured in a
   braking left turn:

       +----------------+-------------+-------------+
       |                | MOTO        | port        |
       +================+=============+=============+
       | row0 . velDir  |   -0.009067 |    0.000014 |
       | row0 . moveDir |    0.224900 |    0.234270 |
       | skidWidthPrev  |    0.009067 |    3.923388 |
       +----------------+-------------+-------------+

   MOTO's stored skidWidthPrev matches |row0 . velDir| exactly and the
   port's matched |row0 . moveDir| exactly, which settles it without a
   hook.  Both call sites in the port passed moveDir and now pass
   velDir; the steering path at race.c is the same fix, and it feeds
   maybe_Racer_ApplySteering rather than the ribbon.

   THE FIX IS NECESSARY BUT NOT SUFFICIENT: THE PORT'S matOrientation
   IS A PURE YAW.  Both sides seed velDir the same way -
   ModelInstance_Integrate transforms (0,0,1) through matOrientation
   and stores it at 0x0048d6c5, and racer_integrate does the same - so
   that write is the original's and is not the defect.  What differs is
   the matrix it is seeded from:

       +--------+------------------------------------------+
       | MOTO   | row1 = (-0.023506, 0.999700, 0.006898)   |
       | port   | row1 = ( 0.000000, 1.000000, 0.000000)   |
       +--------+------------------------------------------+

   row0 . row2 is zero by construction, so against a yaw-only matrix
   physics_slip_speed returns a STRUCTURAL zero and the width can
   never widen.  MOTO reads -0.009770 where we read -0.000034.

   THE PITCH COMES FROM maybe_Racer_UpdateAirborne (0x00466660), 382
   bytes, which the port does not have.  On its grounded arm, gated on
   the brake bit and longSpeed > 0.1, it composes a nose-down PI/128
   into matOrientation through Racer_ComposeOrientation at 0x004666f4.
   FUN_0045d0c0 passes the angle through only while
   maybe_Racer_ChassisAttitude stays inside a band, so the dive
   saturates at one step instead of accumulating.

   [verified] live: MOTO on gas alone holds an exact identity, and
   four brake frames give row1 = (0, 0.999699, 0.024541) - and
   0.024541229 is sin(PI/128) to eight places.

   Racer_SetLongSpeed (0x0048cbe0) is also uncalled here against 14
   sites in MOTO, but wiring it in first changes nothing: it preserves
   the two body components it is not setting, and both are zero
   against a yaw-only matrix, so it returns the heading it was handed.

   maybe_Racer_UpdateAirborne IS NOW IMPORTED WHOLE, as
   racer_update_airborne, and runs in the tick at the original's own
   place - after ground contact, at 0x0045d7a6.  See racers.txt
   9.18.9.  matOrientation now carries the brake dive on both sides
   and, in a braking turn, agrees to 3e-5:

       +--------+-------------------------------------------+
       | MOTO   | row1 = (-0.014024, 0.999705, 0.019853)    |
       | port   | row1 = (-0.014055, 0.999703, 0.019896)    |
       +--------+-------------------------------------------+

   The ribbon LENGTH already matched before that: the same run laid a
   58-quad pair on both sides.

10.4.1.  Why the port's marks are thinner - still open

   THE SLIP DROPOUT IS NOT A DEFECT.  Single-frame stepping catches
   frames where velDir and row2 coincide and the slip is exactly zero
   in BOTH binaries - MOTO shows |velDir - row2| = 3.7e-09 with slip
   0.0000000.  MOTO's width is not pinned at the floor either.

   THE DEVIATION IS EXACTLY ONE TABLE INDEX ON BOTH SIDES.
   2*pi/16384 is 3.83495e-04, and that is the magnitude both binaries
   show - it is quantisation residue, not physics.  What differs is
   WHICH AXIS it lands on.  Eight frames of braking into a left turn,
   walls off, both sides stepped two frames:

   +------+---------------------------+---------------------------+
   |      | MOTO                      | port                      |
   +======+===========================+===========================+
   | d    | alternates - lateral on   | LATERAL EVERY FRAME, and  |
   |      | some frames, vertical on  | always the same sign      |
   |      | others                    |                           |
   +------+---------------------------+---------------------------+
   | slip | noisy: ~0, then +-0.011   | smooth monotone decay,    |
   |      | when the residue is       | -0.0084 down to -0.0049   |
   |      | lateral                   |                           |
   +------+---------------------------+---------------------------+
   | width| 5, 6, 10, 11, 12, 11, 12  | 2, 3, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1    |
   +------+---------------------------+---------------------------+

   The width rule widens by 2 whenever |slip| exceeds the previous
   frame's and narrows by 1 otherwise, so a NOISY slip widens and a
   smoothly decaying one narrows.  THAT is why the port's marks are
   thinner than retail.  The open question is what makes MOTO's
   residue move between axes.

   THREE EXPLANATIONS HAVE BEEN TESTED AND KILLED, all of them by
   measurement rather than by reading:

   o "THE TERRAIN FIT IS NOT TRANSCRIBED."  False.  It is
     racer_frame_from_forward, reached through racer_orient_grounded
     from racer_update_ground_contact, and it matches the original
     step for step - Vec3_Cross(worldUp, forward) at 0x004696b3,
     Vec3_Cross(forward, lateral) at 0x004696d1, then the nine stores
     at 0x004696f6..0x0046973c.  Both probes are snapped in their own
     segment on both sides.

   o "MOTO'S MATRIX IS OFF-INDEX AND OURS IS ON-INDEX."  Not
     established, and the test that suggested it was invalid: it took
     asin(row1.z), but row1.z is sin(yaw)sin(pitch)cos(roll) +
     cos(yaw)sin(roll), which is not the sine of any single quantised
     angle.  The port's value only looked clean because its yaw was
     near zero on a straight.

   o "MOTO QUANTISES LESS OFTEN THAN WE DO."  False.  A trace hook on
     0x004666f9 - the instruction after UpdateAirborne's
     Racer_ComposeOrientation call - took four hits in four braking
     frames, so the dive branch and its quantise run EVERY frame in
     the original too.

   WHAT WAS LEFT IN THAT WINDOW - AND IT WAS THE WHOLE ANSWER.
   maybe_Racer_Tick runs maybe_Racer_ApplyCornering at 0x0045d792,
   BETWEEN ModelInstance_Integrate seeding velDir at 0x0045d770 and
   UpdateGroundContact at 0x0045d79c.  The port had that function only
   as THREE SLICES in physics.c - physics_camber, physics_centripetal
   and physics_cornering_step - and NOTHING CALLED ANY OF THEM.  They
   were dead code, and turnRadius was only ever set to FLT_MAX.

   The function ends by calling Racer_SetSlipSpeed (0x0048d2c0), which
   is the exact mirror of Racer_SetLongSpeed with the incoming scalar
   paired against ROW 0 instead of ROW 2.  THAT IS THE ONLY THING IN
   THE GAME THAT PUTS A LATERAL COMPONENT INTO velDir.  Without it
   velDir can hold nothing but the heading Integrate seeded plus
   quantisation residue - which is exactly what the port's numbers
   showed, and why the residue always landed on the same axis: there
   was no lateral velocity for it to land beside.

   So the family that 10.4 started from is now complete:

   +--------------------------+------------+---------------------+
   | maybe_Racer_SlipSpeed    | 0x0048d0f0 | READS  the lateral  |
   | maybe_Racer_LongSpeed    | 0x0048ca10 | READS  the long.    |
   | Racer_SetLongSpeed       | 0x0048cbe0 | WRITES the long.    |
   | Racer_SetSlipSpeed       | 0x0048d2c0 | WRITES the lateral  |
   +--------------------------+------------+---------------------+

   racer_apply_cornering is imported whole and runs at the original's
   place in the tick.  [needs a Debug rebuild and a re-measure]

10.4.3.  The width on a STRAIGHT is denormal noise

   Re-measured on a harness that can be trusted - full placement,
   four-frame batches, walls off (racers-convergence.txt 9.18.15).

   THE RULE IS IDENTICAL.  0x00467532..0x0046757f:

       if (skidWidthPrev < |slip|) width += 2  else  width -= 1
       clamp [1.0, 12.0]

   which is racer_skid_width exactly.

   AND SO IS THE SLIP WHEN THERE IS ANY.  First braking frame,
   straight: MOTO -0.00849 against the port's -0.00850.  In a braking
   turn: -0.01293 against -0.01209, and +0.00171 against +0.00213.

   BUT ON A STRAIGHT MOTO'S SLIP IS DENORMAL NOISE.  A trace hook on
   the width update, capturing skidWidthPrev and skidWidth per frame:

       +---+---------------+---------------+-------+-----------+
       | n | |slip|        | prev          | width | decision  |
       +===+===============+===============+=======+===========+
       | 0 |   6.59e-13    |   1.43e-08    |  1.00 | narrow    |
       | 3 |   1.50e-18    |   5.81e-19    |  1.00 | WIDEN +2  |
       | 4 |   1.46e-19    |   1.50e-18    |  3.00 | narrow    |
       | 6 |   9.85e-19    |   7.48e-20    |  1.00 | WIDEN +2  |
       +---+---------------+---------------+-------+-----------+

   The rule is comparing this frame's noise against last frame's, so
   the width is a coin flip at the 1e-19 level and MOTO's streak
   flickers between 1 and 3 on a dead straight.

   THE PORT PRODUCES EXACT ZEROS THERE, so prev < |slip| is never
   true and the width pins at 1.  That is the whole of the
   "not quite as wide as retail" report on a straight: not the rule,
   not the slip, but whether the residue survives.  MOTO computes the
   dot product on the x87 stack in 80-bit; physics_slip_speed uses
   double and the terms cancel exactly.

   IN A CORNER IT DOES NOT MATTER - the slip is real, and the widths
   run in the same band on both, 2 to 7.

   [open] whether to chase the residue at all.  It is a numerical
   difference of 1e-19 with a visible consequence, and matching it
   means reproducing an x87 cancellation in double - which is the
   kind of thing CLAUDE.md 5a exists to stop being faked.  Worth
   deciding deliberately rather than drifting into.

10.4.4.  The brake ramp timer never reset

   Braking in a turn shed speed too fast - MOTO -0.594 m/s a frame
   against the port's -0.808 - while straight-line braking matched
   exactly.  The cause is not cornering.

   FOUND BY HOOKING, not by diffing endpoints.  A trace hook at
   0x0048f9f6, inside maybe_Physics_TickDrivetrain at the brake
   apply, capturing the step and both speeds every frame:

       +---+------------+--------------+-----------+
       | n | brakeStep  | gearboxRoad  | d(gbox)   |
       +===+============+==============+===========+
       | 0 |  0.15278   |    34.1608   |     -     |
       | 3 |  0.15278   |    33.5697   |  -0.1952  |
       | 6 |  0.15278   |    32.9868   |  -0.1938  |
       | 7 |  0.91667   |    32.7934   |  -0.1934  |
       +---+------------+--------------+-----------+

   0.15278 is 100/6 through the km/h and dt scaling - the RAMPED
   force.  The port, stepped one frame at a time over the same
   manoeuvre, read brakeHoldMs 1221, 1254, 1287 and a step of
   0.91667: full force from the first frame.

   brakeHoldMs ONLY EVER WENT UP.  physics_apply_brake adds the
   frame's milliseconds to it and nothing ever cleared it, so after
   the first brake of a race the counter was past the 200 ms ramp
   threshold forever.  The original clears it at 0x0048f930, on the
   frame all three brake conditions fail - driveInterlock at
   instance+0x88, the brake flag, and the countdown at 0x004dc0b4.

   Fixed: physics_drivetrain_frame now zeroes it on the non-braking
   branch.  STRAIGHT-LINE BRAKING HID THIS COMPLETELY, because by the
   time that test braked both sides were long past 200 ms and both
   used full force.

   AND THE METHOD IS THE POINT.  Endpoint diffing could see only that
   the port arrived slower; it took a per-frame capture of an
   INTERMEDIATE value - the brake step, which is never stored in the
   racer - to show that the two were applying different forces rather
   than the same force to different states.

10.4.5.  And the drag was being thrown away while braking

   With the ramp timer fixed, brakeHoldMs matched frame for frame -
   0, 33, 66, 99, 132, 165, 198, 231 on both - and the deceleration
   went from -0.923 to -0.159 a frame against MOTO's -0.195.  A
   second, smaller difference remained, and it DECAYED with speed on
   MOTO while the port's stayed flat: the classic shape of a v^2
   term.

       +--------+-------------+-------------+
       |        | MOTO        | port        |
       +========+=============+=============+
       | ramped |   -0.400    |   -0.319    |
       | full   |   -1.904    |   -1.846    |
       |        | decaying to |  flat       |
       |        |   -1.882    |             |
       +--------+-------------+-------------+

   Full brake alone is 0.91667 a frame, 1.833 over a two-frame row.
   MOTO loses about 0.036 a frame beyond it and the port 0.0065,
   against physics_drag's own prediction at 30 m/s of
   30*30*33*0.3/280*0.001 = 0.0318.

   THE DRAG WAS BEING COMPUTED AND DISCARDED.  The resync stand-in at
   the end of physics_drivetrain_frame copies gearboxRoadSpeed ONTO
   speed while braking and the other way while driving - so
   subtracting the drag from speed and then copying over it lost it,
   but only on the braking path.  Now subtracted from both.

   Both of these hid behind the same thing: the brake ramp.  While
   brakeHoldMs never reset, the port braked at full force from frame
   one and the missing drag was 0.7 per cent of a very large number.

   AFTER BOTH FIXES the curve has the right SHAPE - the port decays
   with speed where it used to be flat:

       +----+-------------+-------------+-----------+
       | n  | MOTO d      | port d      | delta     |
       +====+=============+=============+===========+
       |  5 |   -1.9038   |   -1.9159   |  -0.0120  |
       |  7 |   -1.8885   |   -1.9000   |  -0.0115  |
       |  9 |   -1.8753   |   -1.8863   |  -0.0109  |
       +----+-------------+-------------+-----------+

   WHAT IS LEFT IS CONSTANT, about 0.0055 a frame, and being
   speed-independent it is not drag.  physics_rolling_resistance at
   this bike and dt is 0.02 * 33 * 0.00981 = 0.00648 a frame, which
   is the right size - so the obvious move was to stop applying it
   while braking.

   THAT WOULD HAVE BEEN WRONG.  The site is 0x0048fe0d inside
   maybe_Physics_TickDrivetrain, calling Trk_RollResistanceAt
   (0x00459f90) with the bike's position, and a trace hook at
   0x0048fdf4 took FOUR HITS IN FOUR BRAKING FRAMES.  The original
   applies rolling resistance on a braking frame like any other, so
   race.c's unconditional application is right and the 0.6 per cent
   is NOT this.

   Trk_RollResistanceAt is eight bytes returning a constant 0.02 and
   reading none of the position it is handed - the twin of
   Trk_SurfaceGripAt eight bytes below it, which does the same with
   1.0.  Both were meant to make the surface matter and neither was
   finished.

   THE RESIDUAL, DECOMPOSED.  At 30 m/s the three known terms are

       brakeStep  0.91667      full brake, byte-identical both sides
       drag       0.03182      v*v*dt*0.3/motoMasse/1000
       roll       0.00647      0.02*dt*0.00981
       sum        0.95497

   against MOTO measured at 0.95190 and the port at 0.95795.  So MOTO
   loses 0.0031 LESS than the three terms predict and the port 0.0030
   MORE.  The port applying exactly those three cannot come out above
   their sum, which points at ORDER rather than magnitude.

   THE ORDERING CANDIDATE IS DEAD.  The thought was that the port
   drags the PRE-brake speed while the original drags the post-brake
   one.  Read out at 0x00490042:

       0049003e  TEST EBX,EBX            airborne?
       00490042  FLD  [ESI+0x100]        speed
       00490048  FMUL [ESI+0x100]        * speed
       0049004e  FMUL [ESP+0xa8]         * dtMs
       00490055  FMUL [0x004d1e28]       * 1.6   airborne
       00490070  FMUL [0x004d1e2c]       * 0.3   grounded
       0049007f  FDIV [carac + 0x1c]     / motoMasse, index 7
       00490086  FMUL [0x004d1c80]       * 0.001

   It reads instance+0x100 - speed - and the brake writes
   instance+0x108, gearboxRoadSpeed (0x0048fa06).  DIFFERENT FIELDS,
   so the brake never touches what the drag reads and BOTH sides drag
   the pre-brake speed.  The formula, the constants and the divisor
   all match physics_drag exactly.

   So the residual is not the drag's input, not the roll, not the
   brake step and not a double application.  It is in how the pieces
   are combined into the longitudinal speed - the assembly around
   [ESP+0x10] that the roll is applied to at 0x0048fe82, which the
   port does not have at all.  [open]

   AT 0.6 PER CENT WITH THE CURVE SHAPE CORRECT this is well past the
   point where it affects the ribbon, which is what the thread was
   for.

10.4.2.  BRAKING IS NOT DIVERGENT - a measurement trap

   An earlier reading here said the port braked 2.5x too weakly.  IT
   DOES NOT.  Braking matches:

       +--------+--------------------------------------------+
       | MOTO   | speedPrev 14.04704 -> speed 13.12387       |
       |        | one frame, delta -0.92317                  |
       | port   | one frame, delta -0.9225                   |
       +--------+--------------------------------------------+

   0.07 per cent apart, and both agree with the formula: frein 100,
   clamped, times 1/3.6 (0x004d1e10) times dtMs 33 times 0.001
   (0x004d1c80) is 0.916667.  The constants are byte-identical, the
   apply is a single subtraction from gearboxRoadSpeed at 0x0048fa06
   in both, brakeHoldMs advances 33 a frame in both, and there is
   exactly ONE brake block in maybe_Physics_TickDrivetrain - the
   clamp at 0x004d1df0 and the ramp at 0x004d1e08 each have a single
   xref.

   WHAT WENT WRONG IN THE MEASUREMENT: moto_send_input runs a MINIMUM
   OF TWO FRAMES for a one-frame request, so a stepper asking each
   side for one frame advances MOTO twice and the port once.  Every
   per-row delta came out doubled.  The tell was sitting in the same
   struct the whole time - speedPrev holds the previous FRAME's speed,
   and 14.04704 - 13.12387 is one brake step, not two.

   ANY per-frame comparison against MOTO must step both sides by two,
   or read the deltas out of speedPrev rather than out of successive
   samples.  Bulk sends are exact at two frames and above, so only
   single-frame stepping is affected.

   ON A STRAIGHT THE WIDTH IS DRIVEN BY FLOATING-POINT DUST.  The rule
   is widen when |slip| exceeds the previous frame's |slip| and narrow
   otherwise, so with skidWidthPrev at 0.0 any nonzero slip widens.
   MOTO's straight-line slip is around 2e-19 rather than a clean zero,
   which is why its width climbs while parked-and-braking; the port's
   was exactly 0.0 and narrowed instead.  This is not a defect on
   either side and there is nothing to match here - it is the reason a
   straight-line width comparison means nothing.

10.5.  A reset does not clear the marks

   [verified] live, both binaries, by reading all sixteen quadCount
   fields before and after the reset key:

       MOTO   (36,38) (36,38) (4,6) (4,6) (22,24) (22,24) ...
              unchanged across the reset
       port   all sixteen cleared to (0,0)

   MOTO leaves the pool alone, so marks laid before a reset stay on
   the track until the sixteen-slot ring recycles them.  The port
   clears them because its reset re-runs race setup, which calls
   fx_ribbon_reset_all.  A DIVERGENCE, not yet reconciled - the fix is
   to move fx_ribbon_reset_all off the reset path, and it is the
   user's call whether the harness reset should match.

11.  THE TYRE TRAIL - kinds 1..4, and why they are not dead

   The dirt specks a wheel leaves OFF THE ROAD.  Step 2 of
   maybe_Racer_EmitWheelFx, and the other half of the particle pool.

11.1.  The path, and the mistake that hid it

       maybe_Racer_EmitWheelFx  0x00466f5e
         -> maybe_Fx_SpawnTrail 0x004576a0   scans the SAME 128 slots
            -> FUN_004b00e0     0x004b00e0   the kinds-1..4 constructor

   maybe_Fx_SpawnTrail's scan starts at 0x0052c594, which is the
   particle pool's base 0x0052c498 plus 0xfc - the inUse field - with
   the same 260-byte stride and the same 128 slots.  ONE POOL, TWO
   CONSTRUCTORS.

   AN EARLIER NOTE CALLED KINDS 1..4 DEAD, reasoning that
   maybe_Fx_SpawnParticle is the only thing that sets a kind and both
   its callers pass 5.  Both halves of that were true and the
   conclusion was still wrong, because a second constructor writes the
   same records.  Counting the callers of ONE allocator does not
   enumerate what can appear in a shared pool.

11.2.  The kind IS the surface id

   FUN_004b00e0 switches on its last argument, which the emitter fills
   from Trk_SurfaceAtPoint, and has an arm for 1, 2, 3 and 4 only:

   +------+---------------------+--------------+---------------------+
   | kind | Color_Pack16        | atlas rect   | reads as            |
   +======+=====================+==============+=====================+
   |  1   | (9, 8, 6)           | 113,174 ..   | brown dirt          |
   |      |                     | 186,230      |                     |
   +------+---------------------+--------------+---------------------+
   |  2   | (0x14, 0x0d, 5)     | 2,109 ..     | sand                |
   |      |                     | 108,206      |                     |
   +------+---------------------+--------------+---------------------+
   |  3   | (0, 0x0b, 4)        | 113,109 ..   | grass               |
   |      |                     | 186,170      |                     |
   +------+---------------------+--------------+---------------------+
   |  4   | (0x13, 0x14, 0x15)  | 133,43 ..    | pale grey           |
   |      |                     | 216,105      |                     |
   +------+---------------------+--------------+---------------------+

   SURFACE 0 IS THE ROAD and takes the switch's default, which returns
   WITHOUT setting inUse - so the slot stays free and nothing is
   drawn.  That is the whole mechanism behind specks that stop on
   tarmac and resume on the verge; there is no separate road test.

   THE PRIMITIVE KIND IS 0x26, not the smoke's 0x44.

   THE TWO GREYS THE CALLER PASSES ARE NEVER READ.  The emitter builds
   Color_Pack16(9,9,9) and (8,8,8) and hands them over; the body uses
   the switch colour instead.

11.3.  The speck spreads and collapses

   Two counters, both truncated to int every frame [verified,
   0x004b0774..0x004b07ea]:

       while sizeSpawned*2 >= size and size <= 0x100
           size   += dt * 3.84        0x004d202c
           height += dt * 3.84
       otherwise
           height -= dt * 1.28        0x004d2030
           size   += dt * 1.28

   The quad stands on y = 0 and rises to `height`, width `size`, so it
   spreads sideways while flattening.  IT DIES WHEN height GOES
   NEGATIVE - there is NO life countdown on this arm, unlike kind 5.

   THE BALLISTICS ARE THE CRASH'S, not the smoke's: direction kept
   normalised with the magnitude in +0x34, gravity folded in and the
   pair renormalised each frame.

       v = dir * speed;  v.y += dt * -0.024525      0x004d2038
       speed = |v|;  dir = v / speed
       pos += dir * speed * dt * 0.01 * 128.0   0x004d2048, 0x004d2050

11.4.  The size, and the cadence

   Both __ftol operands were lost by the decompiler and are recovered
   from 0x00466f71..0x00467011 [verified]:

       n = (int)(speed / realSpeedMax * 64.0)        0x004d1060
           NOTHING is emitted unless n > 4
       A = matOrientation row2 + its translation - the image of the
           unit Z basis point, so A is the heading
       m = (int)(n * (1.0 - |dot(A, moveDir)|) * 5.0)
                                        0x004d0e70, 0x004d0f78
       size = (n + 2*m) / 3

   SO IT GROWS WITH SPEED AND WITH SLIP.  dot is 1 going straight,
   which zeroes m and gives the smallest speck.

   THE CADENCE IS A FREE-RUNNING PHASE, DAT_004ea508, advanced ONLY by
   the viewed racer so the whole field shares one rhythm:

       phase += frameDelta
       if (50.0 < phase) phase -= 50.0     0x004d1064
       emit only while phase < 30.0        the 0x41f00000 compare

   At 3.3 a tick that is about nine ticks emitting then six idle - the
   streak-then-pause the trail draws on the ground.

11.5.  What is implemented, and the one thing blocking it

   fx_init_trail, fx_spawn_trail, fx_tick_trail and the draw are all
   in, and the emitter carries the size formula and the cadence.

   THE GATE IS IN TOO, as of trk_surface_at_point.  What follows is
   how it was blocked and what unblocked it.

   Trk_SurfaceAtPoint (0x0040ec60) is nineteen lines
   and fully decoded, but it reads TWO SEGMENT FIELDS THE PORT DOES
   NOT LOAD - bandLo at +0xf0 and bandHi at +0xf4:

       d = dot(row0, point - origin) - dot(row0, row3)
       return (bandLo < d && d < bandHi) ? surfaceIn : surfaceOut

   Read live on ile01 they are -1024.0 and +1024.0 on every segment,
   with surfaceIn 0 (road) and surfaceOut 2 (sand) - which is exactly
   the beach.  They are NOT written by any instruction: the immediate
   -1024.0f appears ONCE in the whole image, in .rdata, used by a mesh
   builder.  They come from the FILE - 639 occurrences in ile01.trk at
   roughly 144-byte spacing - and our loader stops before them.

   THE LOADER WAS ALREADY READING THEM, as an unnamed `tail` pair,
   and nothing had looked at the values - see trk.txt 2.1.  Naming
   them was the whole fix.

   VERIFIED offline against MOTO's live segment data, sweeping the
   bike laterally along the segment's row 0 on ile01 segment 776:

       offset     0  ->  d  -715.2  -> surface 0, road
       offset   800  ->  d    84.8  -> surface 0, road
       offset  1500  ->  d   784.8  -> surface 0, road
       offset  -800  ->  d -1515.2  -> surface 2, SAND
       offset -2000  ->  d -2715.2  -> surface 2, SAND

   d tracks the lateral offset one for one, which is the check that
   row 0 really is the lateral axis and unit length.

11.6.  Verified against MOTO

   Both games placed at 1800, 0, 7168 on ile01 - off the road, band
   1024, surfaceOut 2 - and driven the same number of steps.

   THE ONE PARTICLE ALIVE IN BOTH POOLS AT THE SAME INSTANT MATCHES
   EXACTLY, in the shrink arm:

       MOTO slot 4   spawn 13   size 42 -> 50 (+8)   height 10 -> 0
       ours slot 1   spawn 13   size 42 -> 50 (+8)   height 10 -> 0

   A PARTICLE CROSSING THE CAP MID-FRAME LOOKS WRONG AND IS NOT.
   MOTO's slot 0 read size +16 with height +7 in the same frame -
   different deltas, where the grow arm moves both by the same k.
   Two ticks a frame is the answer: one grow tick then one shrink
   tick gives size +k+k' and height +k-k', which is 16.8 and 8.4
   before truncation.  Do not read it as a defect.

   THE CADENCE IS RIGHT.  Both phases advance 6.6 a step and wrap at
   50.0.  They sit at DIFFERENT OFFSETS, which is expected of a
   free-running counter, and that offset is most of why a live count
   differs between the two at any one moment.

   THE ROAD REALLY IS SILENT.  Placed at 0, 0, 7168 - on the road -
   and driven ten steps at speed 45, BOTH pools hold zero trail
   particles.  Kind 5 keeps spawning throughout, so the pool is live
   and it is the surface gate doing the work.

11.6.1.  Through a turn, which is where the slip term lives

   A STRAIGHT LINE TESTS HALF THE SIZE FORMULA.  dot(forward,
   moveDir) is 1 going straight, so m is 0 and size collapses to
   n / 3; the whole slip half is dead and a wrong constant there
   would not show.

   Driven through a left turn off-road, both games read the same
   inputs to the formula [verified]:

       dot(fwd, moveDir)   0.93966 on both
       1 - |dot|           0.06034 on both
       speed                47.274 on both

   and the formula predicts

       n    = int(47.274 / 80 * 64)      = 37
       m    = int(37 * 0.06034 * 5)      = 11
       size = (37 + 2*11) / 3            = 19

   THE NEWEST PARTICLE ON BOTH SIDES IS 19.  Older ones in the same
   pool read 16 and 18, spawned earlier in the turn at a different
   dot - which is why comparing the SET of spawn sizes proves
   nothing and comparing the newest against the formula proves it.

11.6.2.  The spray's throw flips with the bars

   maybe_Racer_EmitWheelFx picks -(rand & 3) or +(rand & 3) on
   buttons & 4, so the spray goes the OPPOSITE way to the steering.
   Recovered by projecting the particle's stored vector back onto the
   bike's lateral axis - lat = (vel - row3) . row0 - from a matched
   placement:

       brake + left    MOTO -0.19 .. 2.40    ours -0.27 .. 2.40
       brake + right   MOTO -2.40 .. 0.23    ours -2.40 .. 0.23

   Same sign, same bounds to the digit.  The live COUNTS differ by a
   few (114/108, 107/112) because the two rand streams are
   independent; that is not a signal.

11.6.3.  The particle counts, and why a saturated pool cannot be read

   A steady donut on the dirt bike once read MOTO {5:79, 4:26} against
   ours {5:103, 4:8} - a three-to-one gap on the trail.  IT WAS NOT A
   PARTICLE DEFECT.  Every bit of it came from the slide being wrong:
   fxGate never set (9.68), matBikeRoot yawed by the slip angle
   (9.69), and the lean on the dead branch (9.70).  With those fixed
   the same test reads, averaged over twelve frames:

       kind 4 (trail)   MOTO  16.75   ours  14.08
       kind 5 (spray)   MOTO 104.08   ours 106.50
       total            MOTO 120.83   ours 120.58

   THE TOTAL IS THE POOL, NOT THE EMITTERS.  128 slots, and both sides
   sit against the cap oscillating 112..128.  A SATURATED POOL CANNOT
   SHOW A SPAWN-RATE DIFFERENCE - only which emitter won the slots -
   so the 2.7-particle split between trail and spray is allocation
   order, and the totals agreeing to 0.2 per cent is the real result.

   MEASURE IT UNSATURATED INSTEAD.  Straight off-road, gas only, no
   slide - so the spray gate never opens and the trail has the pool to
   itself:

       kind 4 (trail)   MOTO 6.00   ours 5.50
       kind 5 (spray)   both 0.00

   Half a particle, which is one frame of the free-running cadence
   phase.  Nothing left to chase.

   WHAT WAS CHECKED AND MATCHED ALONG THE WAY: the per-frame count
   (24 human / 4 AI), the lifetimes ({30, 60} on both), the life
   decrement (6.6 a frame on both), the spawn order (trail in step 2,
   spray in step 5), and sizeSpawned (63..67 against 64..69, each
   matching its own side's formula from its own live speed and slip).

11.7.  The trap that made it look unimplemented

   The size term is speed / realSpeedMax * 64, reading racer+0xb4,
   and racer+0xb4 HAS NO WRITER IN THIS PORT - MOTO holds 80.0 and
   ours holds 0.0.  Transcribed literally it divides by zero, the
   n > 4 gate can never pass, and NOT ONE SPECK SPAWNS.  Everything
   downstream was correct and invisible.

   Every other limiter in the port already reads the CARAC's copy for
   an unrelated reason - racer+0xb4 is the field the copy protection
   zeroes - and this one has to as well.  physics.c around
   physics_set_long_speed carries the warning.

   THE SHAPE TO RECOGNISE: an effect that draws nothing at all, with
   no error and no wrong pixels, is usually a GATE that cannot open
   rather than a renderer that cannot draw.  Read the pool before
   reading the draw - 25 live particles, all kind 5, said at once
   that the emitter and not the billboard was at fault.
