MotoRacer PC Reverse Engineering                           Analysis Note


                   RACER AGAINST RACER COLLISION


Abstract

   Bikes push each other, and sometimes knock each other off.  It is
   one function, maybe_Game_CollideRacers (0x0047ecd0), 4794 bytes,
   run once a frame from the game object's vtable slot +0x18 - not
   from the per-racer tick, which is why nothing in the racer path
   leads to it.

   IMPORTED, whole, as race_collide_racers in src/race.c together
   with its three helpers.  It concerns more than one racer, which is
   what puts it in race.c rather than racer.c.

1.  Where it sits

   The game class vtable is nine entries at 0x004d04c8, installed by
   Game_StaticCtor [verified]:

   +--------+------------+------------------------------+
   | Slot   | Address    | What                         |
   +========+============+==============================+
   | +0x08  | 0x0047bf70 | maybe_Game_InitRace          |
   +--------+------------+------------------------------+
   | +0x14  | 0x0047d660 | maybe_Game_Tick              |
   +--------+------------+------------------------------+
   | +0x18  | 0x0047ecd0 | maybe_Game_CollideRacers     |
   +--------+------------+------------------------------+
   | +0x20  | 0x0047ff90 | maybe_Game_RenderFrame       |
   +--------+------------+------------------------------+

   Its one argument is the frame's time step: speeds inside are
   computed as distance / arg.

   THE CALL SITE IS NOT FOUND.  Tick and RenderFrame are both called
   through the same vtable from a loop that has not been read, and
   collision between them is the only order that makes sense - the
   positions it corrects are the ones the tick just wrote and the ones
   the draw is about to use.  [inferred]

   *** IT DOES NOTHING IN PRACTICE MODE. *** The entire body is under

       if (g_nSelectedMode != 0)

   so bikes pass through each other in mode 0 and collide in a single
   race and a championship.  That is the original's behaviour, and it
   is the first thing to check when a test appears to show no
   collision at all.

2.  The three gates

   A double loop over racers 1 .. g_racerCount-1 with a 25x25
   already-paired bitmap on the stack, so each pair is tested at most
   once a frame.  Then, cheapest first [verified]:

   +-----+------------------------------------------------------------+
   | #   | Gate                                                       |
   +=====+============================================================+
   | 1   | both racers active (+0x68)                                 |
   +-----+------------------------------------------------------------+
   | 2   | FOUR SEGMENTS apart at most, a forward distance            |
   |     | that wraps - FUN_0046c9f0 on the segmentStarts             |
   +-----+------------------------------------------------------------+
   | 3   | a SWEPT SPHERE test, radius 48 each, prev to               |
   |     | current, contact time in 0.05 .. 1.0                       |
   +-----+------------------------------------------------------------+

   So the contact diameter is 96 units and the test is swept, not
   instantaneous - two bikes closing fast cannot tunnel through each
   other inside one step.

3.  The response

   For each racer, its travel this step gives a SPEED and a unit
   DIRECTION:

       d     = position - positionPrev
       speed = |d| / dt * 0.78125
       n     = d / |d|,  or d unchanged when |d| is zero

   The pair is then ordered so the FASTER one is first, and both are
   moved:

       position = (position - positionPrev) * t + positionPrev - n

   which rewinds each to where it was at the contact time and pushes
   it one unit out along its own direction.  positionPrev is NOT
   touched.  Both racers then have their two wheel contact points
   rebuilt from the orientation - the same arithmetic
   maybe_Racer_PlaceWheels uses, inlined here because PlaceWheels also
   rewrites the position history.

   Both get flImpactBlend (+0x31d1) set to 200.0, and both run
   FUN_00466220, which clears stuntOrOverspeed and - for a human
   player in a solo game - stuntHeld.

   Finally both run maybe_Racer_UpdateLean.

4.  Which outcome

   One of four bits is ORed into each racer's riderStateBits
   (+0x2dac), and two of the four also call maybe_Racer_BeginCrash:

   +------+-----------+---------------------------------------------+
   | Bit  | Crash?    | When                                        |
   +======+===========+=============================================+
   | 0x04 | no        | same direction, a shove                     |
   +------+-----------+---------------------------------------------+
   | 0x08 | yes       | same direction, hard enough                 |
   +------+-----------+---------------------------------------------+
   | 0x10 | no        | head on, soft                               |
   +------+-----------+---------------------------------------------+
   | 0x20 | yes       | head on, hard                               |
   +------+-----------+---------------------------------------------+

   HEAD ON IS dot(nFast, nSlow) < 0 - the two travel directions, not
   the contact normal.

   CRASH is decided from the speed DIFFERENCE:

       |dv| > 25                                       or
       |dv| > 12.5 and either rider leans past 0.698132 (40 deg) or
       either rider is in a wheelie (wheelieAngle != 0)

   and is FORCED when either racer is airborne and either is a human
   player - landing on somebody is always a crash.

   *** AND THEN A COIN FLIP KILLS IT THREE TIMES IN FOUR. ***

       if (Game_RandNext(0x2b) & 3) crash = 0;

   A qualifying impact only actually crashes a quarter of the time;
   the other three are demoted to a shove.  A test that produces one
   hit and sees no crash has proved nothing.

4.2.  hard is NOT coin-flipped, and that is the important asymmetry

   The flip touches `crash` and nothing else.  `hard` is computed
   before it and survives, and `hard` is what the HEAD-ON branch
   reads.  So [verified]:

   +----------------+------------------+--------------------------+
   | Direction      | Decided by       | Random?                  |
   +================+==================+==========================+
   | same           | crash            | YES, one impact in four  |
   +----------------+------------------+--------------------------+
   | head on        | hard             | NO, deterministic        |
   +----------------+------------------+--------------------------+

   hard is (spFast > 22.222 || spSlow > 22.222 || either wheelie),
   forced when either is airborne and either is human.  So a HEAD-ON
   WITH EITHER BIKE OVER 80 km/h ALWAYS PUTS BOTH RIDERS DOWN.  That
   is the knock-down-from-speed case and there is no luck in it.

4.3.  Who actually falls off

   The two halves do not test the same thing, so a crash is not
   symmetric [verified]:

   +----------------+--------+------------------------------------+
   | Direction      | Rider  | Falls off when                     |
   +================+========+====================================+
   | same           | faster | crash                              |
   +----------------+--------+------------------------------------+
   | same           | slower | crash, BUT ONLY IF IT IS AN AI -   |
   |                |        | the human branch has no crash test |
   |                |        | at all and always takes bit 4      |
   +----------------+--------+------------------------------------+
   | head on        | both   | hard                               |
   +----------------+--------+------------------------------------+

   THE PLAYER CANNOT BE KNOCKED OFF BY BEING REAR-ENDED.  Run into
   the back of somebody and you can go down; be run into from behind
   and you are only shoved.  An AI in the same position can fall.

4.4.  What it does NOT look at

   THE BIKE'S HEADING IS NEVER READ.  matOrientation plays no part in
   any of the decisions - only the TRAVEL direction, which is the
   per-frame displacement normalised.

   So a T-BONE IS NOT A HEAD-ON.  headOn is dot(nFast, nSlow) < 0,
   and two bikes crossing at right angles give a dot near zero, which
   fails that test - a square side impact takes the SAME-DIRECTION
   path and is therefore coin-flipped like any other.  Only genuinely
   opposed travel gets the deterministic branch.

   It does read LEAN (+0x2e1d) and wheelieAngle (+0x307d), so how far
   the bike is banked over does matter even though which way it
   points does not.

4.1.  The speed each racer is left with

   The two halves of the pair are NOT treated symmetrically, and each
   half has a human branch and an AI branch.  In every case the speed
   a racer keeps is derived from the OTHER one's:

   +--------+--------+----------------------------------------------+
   | Racer  | Rider  | Same direction          | Head on            |
   +========+========+=========================+====================+
   | faster | human  | slower * 0.95           | 0, and collideDir  |
   |        |        |                         | = nSlow, push 5.56 |
   +--------+--------+-------------------------+--------------------+
   | faster | AI     | slower * 0.9            | 5.5556, moveDir    |
   |        |        |                         | = nSlow            |
   +--------+--------+-------------------------+--------------------+
   | slower | human  | collideDir = nFast, its | a combined         |
   |        |        | push speed from |n|*1.1 | direction, see     |
   |        |        | minus its own speed     | below              |
   +--------+--------+-------------------------+--------------------+
   | slower | AI     | a combined direction    | speed = fast -     |
   |        |        | and speed * 1.1         | slow               |
   +--------+--------+-------------------------+--------------------+

   THE SLOWER RACER GETS A COMBINED DIRECTION in three of the four
   cells:

       v = nFast * speedFast + nSlow * speedSlow

   normalised, with its length carried as the push magnitude.  That is
   the momentum of the pair pointed at the one that was hit.

   Both crash branches clamp a speed over 22.222221 (80 km/h) down to
   it before calling BeginCrash, and the slower racer's push speed is
   floored at 5.5555553 (20 km/h).

   THE HUMAN AND AI BRANCHES DIFFER IN WHERE THE SPEED LANDS.  The
   human path writes speed and then runs Racer_SetLongSpeed,
   Gearbox_SyncRoadSpeed and maybe_Gearbox_SpeedToEngine to resync the
   drivetrain; the AI path writes speed, engineSpeed and aiRoadSpeed
   directly.

5.  The helpers

   FUN_00411f00, SWEPT SPHERE AGAINST SWEPT SPHERE.  A textbook
   quadratic; the constants at 0x004d0390 and 0x004d0394 are its 2 and
   its 4 [verified]:

       d = (a1 - a0) - (b1 - b0)
       p = a0 - b0
       a = |d|^2                 |a| < 1e-05 -> no relative motion
       b = 2 * dot(d, p)
       c = |p|^2 - (rA + rB)^2

   Both roots must be non-negative - spelled as an unsigned compare of
   the float bits against 0x80000001 - and the SMALLER is returned.
   It does NOT test t <= 1; the caller does, with the same float-bits
   idiom against 0x3f800001.

   FUN_0046c9f0, the forward segment distance, three lines.

   FUN_00466220, two flag clears.

   *** THE RECEIVING END IS ALREADY WIRED. *** racer.c's integrate
   step already reads +0x174 as a displacement direction and +0x180 as
   its magnitude:

       disp[i] += *(float *)(r->unk_0174 + i * 4) * scale
       scale    = f_0180 * INT_MOVE_SCALE * dt

   so what is missing is only the function that SETS them.  That also
   settles what unk_0174 is and it should be renamed.

   racer_begin_crash, physics_set_long_speed, physics_sync_road_speed,
   physics_speed_to_engine and racer_update_lean were all already
   imported, so the collision needed no new callee.

6.1.  Divergences in the import

   o THE ORDER OF THE TWO HALVES.  The original moves BOTH racers and
     then runs both outcome blocks; the port does one racer end to end
     and then the other.

     CHECKED FIELD BY FIELD RATHER THAN ASSERTED.  The two outcome
     blocks write only their own racer's speed, engineSpeed,
     aiRoadSpeed, riderStateBits, collideDir, collideSpeed and
     moveDir, and read only their own plus the four values passed in
     - nFast, nSlow, spFast, spSlow - which are snapshotted from the
     positions BEFORE either racer moves.  The separation step writes
     only its own position, wheel contacts and flImpactBlend.  There
     is no field one half writes and the other reads, so the two
     orders cannot produce different numbers.

     Confirmed empirically: with both games on the same bike the
     impact lands within 0.4 units and 0.07 m/s - see section 7.2 -
     and the fifteen frames before it are identical to the decimal.

   o THE GEARBOX IS THE PLAYER'S.  racer_begin_crash drops a gearbox
     to first, and the port has exactly ONE MR_Gearbox because the
     original keeps the gear on the racer at +0x7c and the separate
     object has not been retired.  The import first handed that
     object to EVERY crash, so two opponents touching would have put
     the player into first gear.  race_gearbox_of passes it only for
     a human player and NULL otherwise; racer_begin_crash still sets
     r->gear, which is the field the original writes.

   o rand() FOR Game_RandNext.  Same one-in-four rate, different
     stream, so WHICH impacts crash differs from the original.

   o THE NETWORK BRANCHES ARE NOT REPRODUCED - the local-player test
     against the player id at DAT_0050dbdc + 0x400, and FUN_00456bf0.
     Solo picks its branch on humanPlayer, which is what the original
     does once g_nSoloMode is set.

   o [DEBUG] collideinpractice=1 lifts the practice-mode gate.  Off by
     default and it changes nothing when unset; it exists because
     without it the only way to reach this code is to drive a whole
     single race.

7.  Testing it

   tools/mr_place.py --pair puts a second bike a fixed distance from
   the first, both facing the same way, on both games:

       python tools/mr_place.py --to both --at 0 0 2100 --yaw 0 \
              --speed 0 --pair 2 --side ahead --gap 160

   Three things will make a test look like a failure when it is not:
   practice mode disables collision entirely, the four-segment gate is
   FORWARD only, and a qualifying impact crashes one time in four.

7.1.  How to set one up so the numbers mean something

   Three traps, all of them hit before the method below worked.

   DO NOT INJECT A SPEED AND COMPARE TRAJECTORIES.  Writing speed and
   gearboxRoadSpeed without engineSpeed leaves the drivetrain
   inconsistent, and it pulls the speed back down within one frame -
   differently on each side.  Measured with NO obstacle anywhere near:
   both set to 30, and one step later MOTO read 6.76 and the port
   5.05, having travelled 57.4 and 42.9 units.  That difference is the
   setup, not the collision.  Accelerate from rest with identical
   input instead.

   PIN THE OBSTACLE EVERY STEP.  An AI racer set to speed 0 does not
   stay there: it drives from aiRoadSpeed and its own target, and one
   step took it 258 units in MOTO and 469 in the port.  Writing
   position, positionPrev, speed, gearboxRoadSpeed, engineSpeed and
   aiRoadSpeed before each step makes it a static wall.

   YOU CANNOT FAKE MOTION BY WRITING positionPrev.  The racer tick
   runs racer_save_prev_frame first, which overwrites it with the
   current position - so an obstacle given a backward positionPrev to
   force a head-on is read as stationary by the time the collision
   runs, and the hit comes out same-direction.  Producing a real
   head-on needs a racer genuinely travelling the other way.

   MATCH THE FRAME COUNTS.  MOTO runs TWO frames per SEND_INPUT and
   the port runs what it is asked, so "SEND_INPUT 1 1" pairs with
   "SEND_INPUT keys=z frames=2" - confirmed against MOTO 0x004e981c
   and the port's s_race_ticks, 2 and 2.

7.2.  The result

   Racer 1 accelerated from rest at z 2058 into an obstacle pinned at
   z 3400, contact therefore due at z 3304:

   +------+---------------------+---------------------+
   | step | MOTO      z      sp | port      z      sp |
   +======+=====================+=====================+
   | 14   |      3284.9   17.82 |      3203.7   21.47 |
   +------+---------------------+---------------------+
   | 15   |      3309.6    1.44 |      3310.0    1.51 |
   +------+---------------------+---------------------+

   THE FIFTEEN FRAMES OF RUN-UP ARE IDENTICAL TO THE DECIMAL, dz
   +0.0 at every step, and both stop dead on the same step within a
   frame of the predicted contact.  The impact frame differs by 0.4
   units and 0.07 m/s.

   AN EARLIER RUN SHOWED 17.82 AGAINST 21.47 ON THE APPROACH and that
   was not a divergence at all - the two games were on different
   bikes.  Worth remembering before reading any run as a defect: the
   bike must be the same selection on both sides.

   riderStateBits reads 0 on both after the impact, because both
   clear it at the top of the next tick.  It is a one-frame report,
   so the SPEED COLLAPSE is the evidence, not the bits; crashLatch
   (+0x30c1) is the evidence for a knock-down.

   A second run with the obstacle further out hit at 23.38 m/s on
   both sides and neither rider went down, which is what the 25 m/s
   threshold predicts.  Free acceleration reaches 40 m/s in gear 3
   with both games agreeing to 0.02 m/s over forty steps, so the
   over-25 route is reachable in an ordinary race.

   Once a racer is INSIDE the contact distance the sweep stops firing:
   the first root goes negative and the test rejects it, so both
   games then drive on through.  Nothing pushes an overlap apart.

7.3.  The pipeline is complete - audited, not assumed

   Every input the crash decision reads is live in the port and every
   consequence is imported [verified]:

   +-------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | Crash decision reads    | Written by                          |
   +=========================+=====================================+
   | speed, positions        | the drivetrain                      |
   +-------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | lean +0x2e1d            | racer_ai_lean / racer_lean_response |
   +-------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | wheelieAngle +0x307d    | physics.c, the wheelie state machine|
   +-------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | onGround +0x2db8        | racer_update_ground_contact         |
   +-------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | humanPlayer +0x08       | the roster - reads 1/0 for player   |
   |                         | and AI on BOTH games, checked live  |
   +-------------------------+-------------------------------------+

   +-------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | Consequence             | Port                                |
   +=========================+=====================================+
   | throw the rider off     | racer_begin_crash                   |
   +-------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | play the crash out      | racer_update_crash, called on the   |
   |                         | player path AND in racer_tick_ai    |
   +-------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | get back up             | racer_reset_crash                   |
   +-------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | player stops driving    | ApplyDrive and ApplySteering gated  |
   |                         | on !crashLatch                      |
   +-------------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | AI stops driving        | racer_ai_follow_line, FIVE          |
   |                         | crashLatch                          |
   |                         | gates - the same count as           |
   |                         | Racer_AiFollowLine                  |
   +-------------------------+-------------------------------------+

   EVERY CALLER OF maybe_Racer_BeginCrash IS ACCOUNTED FOR.  There are
   four: this collision, maybe_Racer_UpdateGroundContact (the wall,
   imported), FUN_00455280 (a network message, not ported) and
   Race_TickAllRacers - which is inside the TIME ATTACK GHOST branch,
   replaying a recorded crash onto the ghost racer, and is not a
   collision path at all.

   Observed end to end: forcing the wheelie route, which makes crash
   true at any closing speed, put the rider down on 2 of 6 impacts -
   the one-in-four flip working as read.
