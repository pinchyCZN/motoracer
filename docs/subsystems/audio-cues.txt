MotoRacer PC Reverse Engineering                           Analysis Note


                      AUDIO CUES: THE TWO ENTRY POINTS


Abstract

   The game asks for sound in exactly two ways, through two functions
   that sit one after the other in the binary and take
   similar-looking pairs of small integers. One plays a SAMPLE by
   number; the other plays a RED BOOK CD TRACK. Confusing them cost
   several sessions, so this note names both, records what their
   arguments mean, and lists every call site read so far.

   THIS NOTE COVERS THE FIXED CUE TABLE ONLY. A track's own
   ambient sounds are not cues at all - they are a script with
   ids assigned at load, and they have their own note:
   object-sounds.txt.

1.  The two functions

   +------------+--------------------------+------------------------+
   | Address    | Name                     | What it plays          |
   +============+==========================+========================+
   | 0x004049f0 | Snd_PlayCue              | a SAMPLE, DirectSound  |
   +------------+--------------------------+------------------------+
   | 0x00404990 | CdAudio_PlayTrackCue     | a CD TRACK, via MCI    |
   +------------+--------------------------+------------------------+

   Both take two small integers. Neither takes a filename, a handle,
   or an asset name. That is what makes the call sites transcribable
   before any audio back end exists.

1.1.  Snd_PlayCue - samples, by number

       voice = Snd_PlayCue(soundId, flags)

   A table at 0x00519ff8 turns soundId into a handle, which goes to
   the player at 0x00423fd0 with flags, and then to a volume call at
   0x00424030. Returns -1 when nothing started.

   THE ID SPACE IS SPLIT IN TWO, and not where you would guess: ids
   0x2f..0x5c are one group and EVERYTHING ELSE is the other. Each
   group has its own enable flag, its own volume and its own
   last-played id [verified]:

   +-------------------+------------+------------+------------------+
   | Group             | enable     | volume     | last id played   |
   +===================+============+============+==================+
   | <0x2f or >0x5c    | 0x004db664 | 0x004db658 | 0x00502710       |
   +-------------------+------------+------------+------------------+
   | 0x2f..0x5c        | 0x004db668 | 0x004db654 | 0x00502898       |
   +-------------------+------------+------------+------------------+

   SETTLED IN SECTION 5.2: the 0x2f..0x5c group is SPEECH. Both
   tables whose entries land in that range are gated on 0x004db668
   and both load from a Voices directory, so 0x004db668 is
   g_nCfgVoicesEnabled with volume 0x004db654, and 0x004db664 /
   0x004db658 are the sfx pair.

   THE FLAGS ARE THREE BITS, DECODED AT THREE DIFFERENT DEPTHS,
   which is why reading Snd_PlayCue alone makes it look like one.
   It decodes bit 2 and passes the word on untouched [verified]:

   +------+---------------+--------------------------------------+
   | Bit  | Meaning       | Decoded in                           |
   +======+===============+======================================+
   | 0x01 | loop          | FUN_00440ec0, which passes it        |
   |      |               | straight to Play as DSBPLAY_LOOPING  |
   +------+---------------+--------------------------------------+
   | 0x02 | polyphony     | FUN_00441a40 - duplicate the buffer  |
   |      |               | rather than restart a busy one       |
   +------+---------------+--------------------------------------+
   | 0x04 | no retrigger  | Snd_PlayCue - drop the call when     |
   |      |               | soundId is the group's last id       |
   +------+---------------+--------------------------------------+

   AND THE PLAY IS ALWAYS PRECEDED BY A REWIND. FUN_00440ec0 calls
   SetCurrentPosition(0) before Play, so a cue fired again WITHOUT
   bit 1 restarts from the beginning rather than layering - which
   is the behaviour bit 2 exists to suppress for a cue asked for
   every frame.

   An id outside 0..0xff on the outer branch raises a MESSAGE BOX -
   "Invalid sound handle 0x%08x in p..." - rather than failing
   silently. The inner branch merely rejects -1.

1.2.  CdAudio_PlayTrackCue - music, by track

       CdAudio_PlayTrackCue(track, mode)

   Selects a track with CdAudio_SelectTrack (0x004b18e0), stores mode
   at g_cdAudio+0x1c, and plays with CdAudio_PlayTrack (0x004b19f0).
   Both callees end in mciSendCommandA and carry the error strings
   "MCI_STATUS error in selectTrack" and "MCI_SET/MCI_PLAY error in
   playTrack", so this is red book audio off the game disc and there
   is nothing to decode or mix.

   TRACK 0 MEANS RANDOM, not silence: CdAudio_SelectTrack picks
   rand() % trackCount + 1 for 0, and treats -1 as "leave whatever is
   playing alone". Anything else is clamped to 1..trackCount.

   MODE IS TWO INDEPENDENT BITS, read out of CdAudio_PlayTrack's own
   tests rather than guessed [verified]:

   +-----------+-------------------------------------------------+
   | bit 0 (1) | do not restart if this track is already playing |
   | bit 1 (2) | repeat when it ends                             |
   +-----------+-------------------------------------------------+

1.3.  Snd_QueueDelayedCues - a voice line, later

       Snd_QueueDelayedCues(count, delayMs, soundId, ...)

   0x00404e10, and it is VARIADIC: an int count followed by that many
   (delayMs, soundId) PAIRS. It walks eight slots of 100 bytes at
   0x00504244, takes the first free one, and appends up to eight
   pairs to it [verified].

   ALL FOUR CALLERS PASS A COUNT OF 1, so the group form is never
   used for what it can do:

   +----------------------------+-----------------+----------------+
   | Caller                     | Call            | Voice          |
   +============================+=================+================+
   | MenuPage03_MainMenu_Enter  | 1, 600, 0x57    | makurchc       |
   | MenuPage05_ModeSelect_Ent  | 1, 600, 0x54    | selectmd       |
   | MenuPage06_RaceSelect_Ente | 1, 600, 0x53    | selerace       |
   | MenuPage15_NameEntry_Enter | 1, 600, 0x36    | entrname       |
   +----------------------------+-----------------+----------------+

   Every one is a single line 600 ms out, which is about when that
   page's slide-in finishes.

   ONLY PAGE 6 GATES ITS LINE. MenuPage06_RaceSelect_Enter tests
   g_nSelectedMode (0x004dc094) against 2 at 0x00448086 and jumps
   the queue call when it matches, so CHAMPIONSHIP GETS NO
   "select race" VOICE. The other three queue unconditionally.

1.4.  The menu speech bank, all eleven

   Bank B at 0x004db780 is eleven {name, id} pairs loaded from
   Voices\Menu\ when voices are on. Read straight out of the table
   [verified]:

   +------+-----------+---------------------------------------+
   | Id   | File      | Where it is used                      |
   +======+===========+=======================================+
   | 0x36 | entrname  | page 15, name entry                   |
   | 0x52 | slctrans  |                                       |
   | 0x53 | selerace  | page 6, race select                   |
   | 0x54 | selectmd  | page 5, mode select                   |
   | 0x55 | playsolo  | page 3, SOLO                          |
   | 0x56 | playlink  | page 3, LINK                          |
   | 0x57 | makurchc  | page 3, on entry                      |
   | 0x58 | letsgo    |                                       |
   | 0x59 | cngrecor  |                                       |
   | 0x5a | cngratim  |                                       |
   | 0x5b | yutrygan  | the podium                            |
   +------+-----------+---------------------------------------+

   The ids are contiguous from 0x52 apart from entrname at 0x36.
   THAT IS STILL SPEECH: 0x36 falls inside the 0x2f..0x5c range
   section 1.1 gives, so it is gated on the voices flag and
   volume like the other ten. It is only the numbering that is
   discontiguous.

2.  Why they were confused

   MenuPage25_DsiLogo_Enter ends with CdAudio_PlayTrackCue(0x0d, 0).
   While the function was unnamed, that was recorded as "a sound or
   music trigger [guess] - the same shape as the 0x29 the menu hover
   plays", and page 25's missing SHATTER SOUND was assumed to be it.

   It is not. It is the intro music. The shatter is sample 0x2a and
   it is fired from Menu_Tick, in a different subsystem, three
   functions away.

   o TWO FUNCTIONS THAT TAKE (small int, small int) AND SIT NEXT TO
     EACH OTHER WILL BE CONFLATED unless something says otherwise.
     The tell was available without any deep reading: follow the
     callee once and mciSendCommandA is right there. "Same shape" is
     an observation about a call site, never about what it does.

3.  Cues identified

3.1.  Samples

   FIXED IDS ONLY. Anything at 0x5d or above on a track that
   ships a SOUND.BPT belongs to the object-sound script and is
   NOT a constant - see object-sounds.txt 3.

   +------+-------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | Id   | What                    | Call site                      |
   +======+=========================+================================+
   | 0x29 | menu hover click        | Menu_UpdateHover    0x00409a1c |
   +------+-------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | 0x2a | valide - the CONFIRM    | Menu_Tick           0x00409d65 |
   |      | cue, over the shatter   |                                |
   +------+-------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | 0x2d | bike races past, title  | Page14 Enter        0x00446b30 |
   +------+-------------------------+--------------------------------+
   | 0x5b | podium                  | Page24 Enter        0x00452e3b |
   +------+-------------------------+--------------------------------+

   All four pass flags 0, so none of them uses the retrigger guard.

   THE HOVER CUE IS EDGE-TRIGGERED and only on the entering edge
   [verified, 0x00409a0c..0x00409a1c]:

       if (newIdx != oldIdx) {
           oldIdx = newIdx;
           if (newIdx != -1) Snd_PlayCue(0x29, 0);
           needsRedraw = 1;
       }

   Moving OFF a button changes the index to -1 and makes no sound.

   THE SHATTER CUE FIRES WITH THE ARM, NOT THE REQUEST.
   Menu_BeginTransition (0x0040c4a0) makes no sound call at all.
   Menu_Tick plays 0x2a inside the branch that stamps transitionStart
   and transitionEnd, one instruction before it clears
   g_nTransitionRequested at 0x00409d6a. The request and the arm are
   a frame apart - see menu-pages.txt section 4.3.1 - so putting the
   cue on the request would play the glass a frame before the glass
   exists.

   17 other Snd_PlayCue sites are not yet attributed - 21 in all.
   Pages 3, 6 and 10 have two, two and four respectively; pages 9,
   15 and 24 one each; and six non-menu functions the rest.

   o CORRECTION: an earlier version of this line said Menu_Tick has
     a second cue. It does not - it has exactly one, the 0x2a above.

   THERE IS ONE EXPLICIT STOP IN THE WHOLE BINARY, and it is not a
   Snd_PlayCue at all: MenuPage25_DsiLogo_Leave calls CdAudio_StopCue
   (0x004049e0) at 0x00453bff, which issues MCI_STOP and clears the
   repeat flag [verified]. Every other page just asks for its own
   track and lets the request replace whatever was playing.

   o IT IS THERE BECAUSE THE INTRO CAN BE SKIPPED. Track 13 starts
     with mode 0, so it plays once and never loops; without the stop
     it would still be sounding under the title page when page 14
     asks for track 3.

4.  What the reconstruction wires

   src/audio.h mirrors the originals - audio_cue and
   audio_music_cue - and these are the call sites [src/menu.c,
   src/glass.c, src/hud.c, src/racer.c]:

   +--------------------------+---------------------------------------+
   | menu_init                | audio_load_bank(AUDIO_BANK_MENU)      |
   | page25_enter             | audio_music_cue(0x0d, once)           |
   | page25_leave             | audio_music_stop()                    |
   | page14_enter             | audio_music_cue(3, loop), then        |
   |                          | audio_cue(0x2d)                       |
   | page14 hover, on the     | audio_cue(0x29)                       |
   | entering edge            |                                       |
   | glass_update, at the arm | audio_cue(0x2a)                       |
   +--------------------------+---------------------------------------+
   | page10 START             | audio_cue(0x2b or 0x2c), then         |
   |                          | audio_cue(0x58), then HOLDS the page  |
   |                          | for the longer of the two - see 4.4   |
   +--------------------------+---------------------------------------+
   | race_load                | audio_load_bank by the bike's         |
   |                          | PersoAnim - see 5.5                   |
   +--------------------------+---------------------------------------+
   | hud_menu_open            | audio_cue(0x03) and audio_cue(0x29)   |
   +--------------------------+---------------------------------------+
   | hud_prompt_open          | audio_stop_all(), then the same pair  |
   +--------------------------+---------------------------------------+
   | hud_menu_on_key, both    | audio_cue(0x29)                       |
   | selection arms           |                                       |
   +--------------------------+---------------------------------------+
   | racer_audio_tick         | audio_voice_start(gear cue, LOOP |    |
   |                          | POLY) and a retune every frame        |
   +--------------------------+---------------------------------------+

   THE IN-RACE MENU MAKES TWO SOUNDS ON OPEN, not one [verified].
   InGameMenu_OpenEscape plays 0x03 at 0x00436446, and at its tail
   it reads the DEFAULT ROW's enter callback back out and calls it
   directly - that is InGameMenu_OnRowEnter (0x00436150), which
   plays 0x29. So the menu arrives with the open sound and the
   selection landing on CONTINUE.

   THE SELECTION IS WHAT SOUNDS, NOT THE KEY. The cue hangs off
   each row's widget rather than off the input, so it fires
   whether the row was reached by key or by pointer - and
   InGameMenu_OnRowLeave (0x004361c0) is silent, so there is no
   sound on the way out of a row.

   BOTH ARE ONE-SHOTS AND THAT IS WHY THEY SURVIVE THE PAUSE the
   same frame raises - 4.3. Nothing exempts the menu.

   THE POST-RACE QUESTION CUTS RATHER THAN PAUSES. FUN_00437b70
   calls Snd_StopAll (0x00424020, on to Csample_StopAll) before
   its 0x03, because there is no race to resume into.


   PAGES 25 AND 14 ARE NOW COMPLETE, checked by listing every callee
   of all six handlers rather than by grepping for what was expected
   [verified]:

   +----------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | MenuPage25_DsiLogo_Enter   | CdAudio_PlayTrackCue only          |
   | MenuPage25_DsiLogo_Tick    | none                               |
   | MenuPage25_DsiLogo_Leave   | CdAudio_StopCue                    |
   | MenuPage14_Title_Enter     | CdAudio_PlayTrackCue, Snd_PlayCue  |
   | MenuPage14_Title_Tick      | none                               |
   | MenuPage14_Title_Leave     | none                               |
   +----------------------------+------------------------------------+

   o THE INTRO MUSIC IS RED BOOK, NOT A SAMPLE. Page 25 has no
     Snd_PlayCue callee at all, in any of its three handlers, so
     there is no sample anywhere in the intro. Its only sound is
     CD track 13.

   o THE TITLE MUSIC AND THE DRIVE-BY START TOGETHER because they are
     literally consecutive calls - 0x00446b27 and 0x00446b30, the
     last two instructions of Enter before the epilogue.

   o THE SHATTER SOUND IS NOT PAGE 25's. Its Tick has no audio callee;
     0x2a comes from Menu_Tick, which every page goes through.

   A fifth call site is the bank load: Menu_LoadResources calls
   Snd_LoadMenuBank, mirrored as audio_load_bank(AUDIO_BANK_MENU) in
   menu_init. IT NOW LOADS - tables C and B are transcribed into
   audio.c and decoded through wave_load, filling the 256-entry
   handle table. The race banks still only set the bank, because
   nothing loads a level yet; see section 5.5.

   WIRING THE CALLS AHEAD OF THE BACK END IS THE POINT. A cue that
   was never wired is invisible; a cue that fires into a stub shows
   up in the log the moment somebody looks. It also means the sample
   ids sit in the source next to the code that triggers them, where
   they can be checked, rather than in a table nobody reads.

   THE MUSIC WILL BE SILENT EVEN WITH A BACK END, on a stock
   install - it was on the disc, not in DATA. src/audio.h records
   that as a real limit of CLAUDE.md rule 10 rather than a bug.

4.1.  The back end is XAudio2

   src/audio.c holds every decision - the sample store, the bank
   table, the voice pool, the cue dispatch, the volumes and the
   retrigger guard - and is C. src/xa2.cpp holds the COM calls and
   nothing else, behind the C surface in src/xa2.h.

   THE SPLIT IS FORCED BY THE SDK HEADER. xaudio2.h carries an
   #ifndef __cplusplus block of IXAudio2_* vtable macros, so the
   API is meant to be reachable from C, but the header will not
   compile as C - two lines see to that, and audio.c's banner has
   both and why neither can be worked around. It is therefore
   CLAUDE.md rule 11's COM exemption, pared to the COM alone.

   THE ORIGINAL'S BUFFER IS TWO THINGS IN XAUDIO2. An
   IDirectSoundBuffer fuses the samples with the playback state;
   XAudio2 splits them into a caller-owned PCM block and a source
   voice over it. Two consequences:

   o THE PCM IS OURS TO KEEP. Csample_Load GlobalFrees its copy
     after CreateSoundBuffer because DirectSound took ownership;
     XAudio2 does not copy, so the block must outlive playback.
     The port holds it for the bank's lifetime, which is what
     makes a cue cost no I/O and no decode.

   o THE DUPLICATE CHAIN BECOMES A VOICE POOL. FUN_00441a40's
     DuplicateSoundBuffer walk exists to get a second playback
     state over the same samples, which is a second source voice
     and needs no duplication step.

   A voice carries one format, and the MENU BANK ALONE SHIPS FOUR
   RATES - 5000, 16000 and 22050 Hz across Menu\, and 11025 Hz for
   every one of the eleven Voices\Menu\ samples. So the pool is
   keyed by format, and the mastering voice takes the device's own
   rate rather than picking a winner among four.

   FAILURE IS SILENT AND IS NOT AN ERROR. xa2_open returns 0 with
   a logged reason when there is no device, no xaudio2_9.dll, or
   the endpoint is held exclusively; audio_ready stays 0 and every
   cue is dropped, which is the path the stub already took.


4.2.  The handle layer, and why audio_cue is not enough

   audio_cue is fire and forget - it starts a sample and the caller
   never hears of it again.  That is the whole of the menu, and it
   is not enough for a race, which has to hold a looping voice open
   for as long as the bike runs and retune it every frame.

   So the port has the second interface the original has, the five
   methods of section 9.1:

   +--------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | Port                     | Original                           |
   +==========================+====================================+
   | audio_voice_start        | maybe_Snd_StartVoice    0x00423fd0 |
   +--------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | audio_voice_stop         | Snd_StopVoice           0x00424000 |
   +--------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | audio_voice_set_volume   | Snd_SetVolume           0x00424030 |
   +--------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | audio_voice_set_freq     | maybe_Snd_SetFrequency  0x00424050 |
   +--------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | audio_voice_set_pan      | maybe_Snd_SetPan        0x00424070 |
   +--------------------------+------------------------------------+

   A HANDED-OUT VOICE IS OWNED and the cue layer will not recycle
   it, so it stays valid until audio_voice_stop.  Without that a
   cue on the same sample would seize the engine's voice and the
   next retune would drive somebody else's sound.

   TWO THINGS DIFFER FROM DIRECTSOUND, both in the port's favour
   only because they are stated:

   o FREQUENCY IS ABSOLUTE HERTZ IN THE INTERFACE, as DirectSound's
     was, and becomes a RATIO inside - XAudio2 wants a multiple of
     the source's own rate.  The division happens where the rate is
     known, in audio.c, so xa2.cpp stays free of policy.

   o THE RATIO CEILING IS FIXED WHEN A VOICE IS CREATED and above
     it XAudio2 CLAMPS SILENTLY.  The default is 2.0 and the game
     reaches 1.81 - ROULEMAN.WAV at 11025 Hz driven to 20000 - so
     the port asks for 4.0.  A note that stops rising with no error
     is a bad way to find this out.

   PAN attenuates the far channel rather than splitting the signal,
   which is what DirectSound does: a 0.5/0.5 matrix would drop
   every centred sound by 6 dB, and the loaders centre everything.
   The curve is linear in amplitude rather than in decibels; the
   endpoints and the centre agree.


4.3.  Pausing, and the one rule that is not obvious

   FUN_00441e80 (0x00441e80) is the global pause, reached through
   FUN_00424090 on the sound manager.  InGameMenu_OpenEscape calls
   it at 0x00436446, one instruction before its own Snd_PlayCue(3).

   ONLY LOOPING SAMPLES ARE PAUSED [verified].  The loop tests
   GetStatus for PLAYING and the sample's flag word for BIT 0 - the
   loop bit - and skips everything else.  A one-shot already in the
   air runs to completion.

   That is what makes the pause cue work.  The escape menu pauses
   the game and THEN fires cue 3, and the cue is heard because it
   is not a loop.  Nothing special-cases the menu; the rule does it.

   THE RESUME RESTARTS EACH LOOP FROM THE BEGINNING.  It calls
   Csample_Play, which does SetCurrentPosition(0) before Play, so
   every engine note jumps back to the top of its sample rather
   than continuing.  On a short engine loop that is a seam rather
   than a glitch, but it is a seam.

   AND THAT IS WHY AMBIENCE SURVIVES A PAUSE.  Retail's gulls and
   surf keep going while the engine stops, which looks like a
   special case and is not: the ambient samples are not in any of
   the six cue tables and are fired as one-shots, so the loop rule
   simply never reaches them.  A port that paused the whole mixer
   instead - XAudio2's StopEngine would - would silence them too
   and be LESS faithful, not more.

   THE PORT KEEPS THE FIRST RULE AND BREAKS THE SECOND, on purpose.
   audio_set_paused pauses exactly the same set of voices, but
   XAudio2 can stop a source voice WITHOUT discarding its buffer -
   Stop with no FlushSourceBuffers - so Start resumes on the sample
   it stopped on.  Same voices, no seam.  race.c sets it from
   hud_menu_active OR tm_paused every frame - the escape menu is
   the original's pause and SPACE is ours, and both stop the race,
   so both have to stop the engine.  The call is idempotent.


5.  The handle table and what loads it

   g_nSndHandles at 0x00519ff8 is 256 ints, indexed by the cue id
   Snd_PlayCue takes. BSS; -1 means empty. Snd_ResetHandleTable
   (0x00403c70) fills all 0x100 entries with -1, and both loaders
   repeat that fill before they start. Snd_LoadSample returns -1 on
   failure and the player drops a -1 handle, so an id that never
   loaded is silently inaudible rather than a crash.

   TWO LOADERS FILL IT [verified]:

   +---------------------+------------+---------------------------+
   | Snd_LoadMenuBank    | 0x00403f90 | sole caller               |
   |                     |            | Menu_LoadResources        |
   +---------------------+------------+---------------------------+

5.1.  The path the loaders build

   Both build a filename by concatenation, five pieces:

       "data\sfx\"
       + (g_cfgWave16Bits ? "16_Bits\" : "8_Bits\")
       + <category>
       + <name from the table>
       + ".wav"

   The 8/16-bit choice is a whole parallel tree, not a format flag -
   SFX.BKF carries both, 125 members each.

   The categories are "Menu\", "Voices\Menu\", "Voices\", "Divers\",
   "Ktm\" and "Ninja\".

5.2.  The six pair tables

   Each is an array of {const char *name; int id;}, stride 8. The
   loops start on the ID field and read the name at [-1], which is
   why a naive dump lands four bytes out.

   +-----+------------+-------+---------------+---------------------+
   | Tbl | Address    | Count | Category      | Loaded by           |
   +=====+============+=======+===============+=====================+
   | A   | 0x004db680 |  30   | Voices\       | race, if voices on  |
   | B   | 0x004db780 |  11   | Voices\Menu\  | menu, if voices on  |
   | C   | 0x004db7d8 |   7   | Menu\         | menu                |
   | D   | 0x004db810 |  18   | Divers\       | race                |
   | E   | 0x004db8a0 |  16   | Ktm\          | race, arg != 0      |
   | F   | 0x004db920 |  11   | Ninja\        | race, arg == 0      |
   +-----+------------+-------+---------------+---------------------+

   o THE ID DECIDES THE GROUP, THE TABLE DECIDES THE FILE, and they
     are independent. This is what settles section 1.1's open
     question: ids 0x2f..0x5c are SPEECH. Both voices tables are
     gated on the same flag Snd_PlayCue tests for that range, and
     both load from a Voices directory. So 0x004db668 is
     g_nCfgVoicesEnabled and 0x004db654 its volume; 0x004db664 and
     0x004db658 are the sfx pair.

   o THE SAME ID IS REUSED ACROSS BANKS. 0x28 deplett and 0x29
     select load from "Menu\" in the menus and from "Divers\" in a
     race; 0x03 continue likewise. A cue id only means something
     together with whichever bank is resident, which is worth
     knowing before anybody builds a global id-to-file map.

5.3.  The cue names

   Menu\ (table C) - the menu bank, and where every cue this project
   has wired comes from:

       0x28  deplett     0x29  select      0x2a  valide
       0x2b  goktm       0x2c  goninja     0x2d  title
       0x03  continue

   So the three identified in section 3.1 are:

       0x29 = select.wav   the hover click
       0x2a = valide.wav   plays as the glass shatters
       0x2d = title.wav    the bike that races past on page 14

   o 0x2a IS NAMED "valide", NOT "glass". Menu_Tick plays it whenever
     a transition ARMS, and every page change arms the glass, so it
     is the CONFIRM sound and the shatter is what happens to be on
     screen while it plays. There is no glass-named sample anywhere
     in SFX.BKF - nothing matching verre, vitre, glass, casse, bris
     or break among 250 members. At 44162 bytes it is the largest
     menu cue bar title.wav, which is at least consistent with a
     shatter, but the name is the evidence and the name says
     confirm.

   Divers\ (table D) - race ambience and surfaces:

       0x01 bip1      0x02 bip2      0x06 chckpvox  0x03 continue
       0x28 deplett   0x29 select    0x2e pause     0x0a derap
       0x1a brake1    0x1b brake2    0x12 crash     0x11 rouleman
       0x14 wterdeb   0x15 wterpas   0x16 wterfin   0x17 gravier
       0x18 glace     0x19 herbe

   Ktm\ (E, 16) and Ninja\ (F, 11) - the bike itself. Ktm carries
   five gears and three landing samples; Ninja one landing and a
   suspension. The extra five are what makes motocross a different
   bank rather than the same bank retuned.

   Voices\ (A, 30) is race speech - the 3/2/1/go countdown,
   wrongway, finlap, newrecor and so on. Voices\Menu\ (B, 11) is
   menu speech - entrname, selerace, playsolo, letsgo.

5.4.  The two entries that look unloadable, and are not

   Between table A (Voices\, ends 0x004db770) and table B
   (Voices\Menu\, starts 0x004db780) sit two more pairs:

       0x004db770   podium1   id 0x04
       0x004db778   podium2   id 0x05

   READ BY EYE, table A looks 32 entries long and those two look
   like Voices\ entries naming files that exist only under Menu\ -
   a silent load failure shipping since 1997. THAT READING IS
   WRONG, and it is recorded here because it is the one a reader
   arrives at independently.

   A TABLE'S EXTENT IS ITS LOADER'S BOUND, NOT ITS APPARENT SIZE
   [verified]. Every loop starts on the ID field and compares
   against a hard address:

   +---------------------+------------+------------+-------------+
   | Loop                | First id   | Bound      | Entries     |
   +=====================+============+============+=============+
   | A  Voices\          | 0x004db684 | 0x004db774 | 30          |
   | B  Voices\Menu\     | 0x004db784 | 0x004db7dc | 11          |
   | C  Menu\            | 0x004db7dc | 0x004db814 | 7           |
   +---------------------+------------+------------+-------------+

   So the Voices\ loop stops at CMP EBX,0x4db774 (0x00404959),
   thirty entries ending on 0x51 wouaou, and never reaches
   podium1. The two pairs belong to Snd_LoadPodiumBank
   (0x00404310), which loads them from Menu\ - where both files
   exist - on entry to the podium page. Its sole caller is
   MenuPage24_Podium_Enter.

   Likewise table B ends on 0x5b yutrygan. The deplett/0x28 that
   appears to follow it is table C's FIRST entry, loaded from
   Menu\.

   o THERE IS NO SILENT LOAD FAILURE IN ANY OF THE FOUR MENU-SIDE
     LOADERS. Every entry every loop reaches names a file that is
     in SFX.BKF at the path the loader builds.


5.5.  The race bank seam, and what picks which one

   Everything the reconstruction plays today is the MENU bank. The
   race bank has never been asked for, and this section records
   where it would be, because that is the next thing gameplay
   needs.

   THE ARGUMENT IS THE BIKE, NOT THE TRACK [verified]. The smaller
   of Snd_LoadRaceBank's two callers is 0x00410d70, and it is four
   lines long:

       maybe_Loader_ReportStep("Init WAV samples ...")
       Snd_LoadRaceBank(g_bikeCarac[g_nSelectedBike].PersoAnim)

   PersoAnim is the same field page 10 uses to choose the engine
   start sample - (PersoAnim == 0) + 0x2b - so ONE field selects
   both the bike's engine cue and its whole sample bank. That is
   the road/dirt split: Ninja against Ktm, which section 5.1 lists
   as two of the six categories.

   o SO AUDIO_BANK_RACE_NINJA / _KTM ARE SELECTED BY THE BIKE,
     and race.c now does it: race_load reads the selected bike's
     persoAnim beside race_load_bike, which is the same seam the
     original uses.

   o AND IT IS A LOADING STEP. The call sits behind a caption
     report, so the race bank is loaded during the page 1 stall
     with the loading screen up - see menu-pages.txt 7.1. That is
     the seam: the same place that will drive the lodanim blink.

   THE RACE MUSIC TRACK COMES FROM THE LEVEL SCRIPT. The last key
   0x00427e80 reads out of "%sLevel.Spt" is AudioTrack, stored to
   0x004db67c [verified]. So a race's CD track is per level and
   read from data rather than chosen in code - which matters for
   the port, since section 4 already records that music cannot come
   off a stock install at all.

   THE RETURN IS ONE SEAM AND IT IS Menu_SetPage.  The original
   reloads the menu bank from Menu_LoadResources, which
   Menu_SetPage calls on EVERY page change, so entering any menu
   page restores it.  menu_set_page does the same.

   WITHOUT IT THE SYMPTOM IS WRONG, THEN SILENT - not a failure,
   and nothing is logged.  The menus run on the resident race
   bank, where the ids that happen to exist resolve to the wrong
   sample and the rest resolve to nothing:

       0x29   hover/activate   Divers\select, not Menu\select
                               - so the buttons still click
       glass shatter           absent - the page transition is
                               silent
       every menu voice        absent - the announcer is gone

   A MENU WHOSE BUTTONS CLICK AND WHOSE EVERYTHING ELSE IS QUIET
   IS THIS BUG, and the one-line check is audio_current_bank().
   It was reached by the continue menu's NO, which goes straight
   to page 6 rather than through page 9.

5.6.  Snd_SetFrequency has a WINDOW, and it skips outside it

   maybe_Snd_SetFrequency (0x00424050) forwards to
   Csample_SetFrequency (0x00441dc0), whose guard is four
   instructions at 0x00441de6 [verified]:

       [sample+0x14] = hz          stored whatever it is
       if (hz <= 1000)   return    no call
       if (hz >= 44100)  return    NO CALL
       IDirectSoundBuffer::SetFrequency(hz)

   IT IS A SKIP, NOT A CLAMP, and the difference is audible.
   Above 44100 the buffer KEEPS THE LAST FREQUENCY IT WAS GIVEN,
   so the note does not clamp to 44100 and does not jump - it
   stops climbing at whatever it last reached inside the window.

   44100 IS THE GAME'S OWN CEILING, not DirectSound's;
   DSBFREQUENCY_MAX is 200000.

   o IT IS WHY THE BOTTOM TWO GEARS SING WITHOUT IT. Road gears
     1 and 2 sweep to 48000 Hz on a 22050 Hz sample (racers.txt,
     the engine tables), so the top of each is OUTSIDE the window
     and the original stops tracking at ratio 2.0.  A port that
     just sets the rate reaches 2.36 and is a couple of semitones
     sharp at the redline.  Gears 3 to 6 peak near 40000 on a
     14000 Hz sample, inside the window, and match either way.

   o AND EVERY INPUT MATCHED WHILE IT WAS WRONG.  Both games
     paused at the gear 1 redline held tachoRpm 11994, engine
     6.9967 against 6.9965, the same gear and the same cue, and
     both computed the same 47980 Hz before the wheelie term.
     The divergence was entirely in what the back end did with
     that number, which no field-by-field comparison can see.

6.  The samples are WAV files with a cipher over them

   Every one of SFX.BKF's 250 members has the extension .WAV and none
   of them LOOKS like a RIFF file: all 250 begin with the same four
   bytes [verified]:

       72 6d 6e 72     "rmnr"

   DECODED - see formats/rmnr.txt, which is the home for the format.
   They are ordinary uncompressed PCM RIFF WAVE files with a
   positional cipher applied:

       plain[i] = (cipher[i] - 0x20 - 4*i) & 0xff

   There is no compression and no custom container.  "rmnr" is not a
   magic the game looks for - it is what "RIFF" becomes under that
   arithmetic, and the loader's actual test is the LETTER CASE of
   byte 0, so plain RIFF files pass through untouched.

   THIS MATTERS FOR THE PORT in one specific way: the loader unmasks
   THREE regions and restarts the counter at zero for each, so a
   decoder that treats the file as one stream produces garbage from
   byte 0x24 onward.  tools/rmnr.py is the oracle and reads every
   shipped file.

8.  THE ANNOUNCER, AND NONE OF IT IS IMPLEMENTED

   The race commentator is a QUEUE, not a set of cue calls, and
   that is the thing to know before reconstructing any of it.

8.1.  Snd_QueueVoice - the 8-slot ring

   Snd_QueueVoice(cueId, delayMs) @ 0x00404da0 stores into a ring
   of eight and returns; maybe_Snd_TickRiderVoice drains it and
   honours the delay (it reads the read index at 0x00407923).

       0x005028ec[8]   cue ids
       0x0050290c[8]   delays, ms
       0x005028d8      write index, (i + 1) & 7
       0x005028d4      read index

   THE DRAIN IS ON ANOTHER THREAD, and that is why it appears in
   no xref.  Nothing in the database reads 0x005028ec or
   0x005028cc; the enqueue clears the latter and something sets
   it back to 1 when the queue empties.  Proved live: a hardware
   watchpoint on the read index caught NOTHING while the value
   advanced under it, and hardware debug registers are
   per-thread.  It is the mixer thread.

   ONE ENTRY PER CONSUME.  Hand-feeding the ring advanced the
   read index by exactly one each time (2 -> 3 -> 4), with
   0x005028cc returning to 1 between.  [verified - live]

   BOTH BUILDS RUN THE SAME STATE MACHINE, checked side by side
   through one checkpoint with the two rings read every few
   frames:

       enqueued      MOTO rd 0 wr 2, flight 06,34
                     port hd 0 tl 2, flight 06,34
       blip gone     MOTO rd 1 wr 2, flight 34
                     port hd 1 tl 2, flight 34
       word gone     MOTO rd 2 wr 2, empty, 0x005028cc back to 1
                     port hd 2 tl 2, empty

   One consumed at a time, the second held behind the first, in
   the order queued.  0x005028cc reading 1 again at the end
   confirms it as the idle flag.  [verified - live, both
   builds]

   THE GAP LENGTH IS NOT COMPARABLE UNDER STEPPING, and that is
   not a defect: MOTO's drain is on the mixer thread and runs on
   REAL time, so it keeps going while the harness has the game
   paused, where ours is on tm_now and stops with it.  That
   divergence is deliberate - see the note in audio.c - and only
   shows under a debug pause.

   AND THE NEXT LINE ALSO WAITS FOR THE LAST TO FINISH.
   [observed]  The gap alone is not enough: a win is announced
   with a phrase well over half a second long and RACE COMPLETED
   follows it, and on retail the phrase is allowed to finish
   before the second speaks.  Both conditions hold - the gap has
   passed AND the previous line is quiet - so a short blip still
   clears in its 500 ms and only a long line extends the wait.

   THE 500 IS THE GAP AFTER A LINE, NOT A WAIT BEFORE IT.
   [inferred]  Read off behaviour rather than off the drain,
   which is not in the database to read: retail plays the
   checkpoint blip ON the geometry and its word well after, one
   then the other, which a lead-in delay cannot produce and
   spacing does.  A port that treats it as a lead-in gets both
   cues half a second late AND on top of each other.

   FULL MEANS DROPPED, SILENTLY - the enqueue compares the next
   write index against the read index and returns without
   storing.  Eight pending lines is the cap.

   THAT QUEUE IS WHY LINES NEVER OVERLAP and why each lands a
   beat after its event: every one of the ten callers passes a
   delay of 500 ms.

8.2.  Every announcer line in the game

   +------------+---------------------------+------------------+
   | Site       | Function                  | Cue              |
   +============+===========================+==================+
   | 0x0041321a | Hud_DrawInRace            | 6, an SFX        |
   +------------+---------------------------+------------------+
   | 0x00413299 | Hud_DrawInRace            | 0x34 ckckpnt     |
   +------------+---------------------------+------------------+

   THE WHOLE CHECKPOINT BLOCK NEEDS raceState == 1, tested at
   0x004131d0 and again at 0x004131e6.  THE FINISHING CROSSING
   IS ALSO A CHECKPOINT CROSSING, so without that gate the line
   is announced as a checkpoint at the same moment the result
   and RACE COMPLETED are queued and three voices arrive
   together.  Finishing moves the racer to state 2, which is
   what silences it.

   THE CHECKPOINT PAIR IS NOT QUEUED FROM THE CROSSING.  The
   crossing, in maybe_Race_UpdateProgress, only adds its time,
   opens a five-second window (0x00519fec = 5000) and bumps a
   per-racer CHECKPOINT COUNTER at racer+0x2f79.  Hud_DrawInRace
   then runs its whole block only while that window is open,
   compares the counter against a value it remembers on itself,
   and queues both cues when they differ - storing the new one so
   it fires once.

   THE ORDER MATTERS, AND IT IS EASY TO GET WRONG.  Because the
   HUD runs AFTER maybe_Race_UpdateProgress, lapCounter is
   already current when the cue is queued.  Queue it from inside
   the crossing sweep instead and the suppression reads the lap
   count from BEFORE the crossing that closed the lap - so on the
   lap that turns into the final one the checkpoint fires anyway
   and talks over FINAL LAP.

   THE MUTE IS ONE CHECKPOINT, AND IT FORKS ON DIRECTION.  Both
   arms need the counter to be 1 - so at most ONE checkpoint per
   lap is ever dropped - and then g_nRaceReverse (0x004dc144)
   picks which lap:

   +---------------+------------+---------------------------------+
   | g_nRaceReverse| Tested at  | Dropped when                    |
   +===============+============+=================================+
   | 0, forward    | 0x004131d0 | lapCounter == 0                 |
   +---------------+------------+---------------------------------+
   | non-zero      | 0x004131e6 | lapCounter - g_nRaceLaps == -1  |
   +---------------+------------+---------------------------------+

   so running FORWARD - which is every normal race - the dropped
   one is the first crossing of the FIRST lap.  THAT CROSSING IS
   THE START LINE, NOT A CHECKPOINT: the line carries a linkA like
   any checkpoint, the grid sits behind it, and so it is crossing
   number one.  Measured on MOTO mid-lap on ile01 with two visible
   checkpoints: counter 2, lapCounter 0, wrap limit 3 - line then
   cp1, with cp1 audible.  Only reversed does the final lap come
   into it.

   WIDENING EITHER ARM TO THE WHOLE LAP SILENCES EVERYTHING on a
   one-lap event, because lapCounter is then both 0 and
   raceLaps - 1 for the entire race - and LIST.BPT gives all eight
   tracks a lap count of 1, so that is every practice run and
   every single race.  The counter test is what keeps it to one
   crossing.

   THE WRAP LIMIT IS NOT nbCheckPoint, though on ile01 both are 3.
   maybe_Game_LoadRace builds [0x004e9844] at 0x0047b4ff by
   counting the segments whose linkA (+0xd8) is non-negative - the
   same predicate the crossing sweep uses - so it is the number of
   TIMING CROSSINGS on the track, the start line included.

   AND THE COUNTER IS NOT BUMPED PER SWEPT SEGMENT.  The sweep only
   RAISES A FLAG when it meets a timing segment; the counter is
   incremented ONCE for the frame afterwards, gated on that flag
   and raceState == 1, and the same block calls
   Race_RecordCheckpointSplit.  So a frame in which a fast bike
   crosses two timing segments advances the counter by one, not by
   two.  A port that increments inside the sweep counts a different
   sequence on exactly those frames.

   THE LAP BOUNDARY IS A SEPARATE MECHANISM and not a timing
   segment at all: it is detected by the SEGMENT INDEX WRAPPING
   past zero, which is the second flag the delta arithmetic raises,
   and that flag drives lapCounter and the FINAL LAP cue.  Reading
   the start line as one of the timing segments is the trap here -
   the two sets are disjoint.
   | 0x0047decc | maybe_Race_TickClock      | 0x39 gameover    |
   +------------+---------------------------+------------------+
   | 0x0047df2f | maybe_Race_TickClock      | 0x42 timeout     |
   +------------+---------------------------+------------------+
   | 0x0047e72f | maybe_Race_UpdateProgress | 0x46 wrongway    |
   +------------+---------------------------+------------------+
   | 0x0047e9fb | maybe_Race_UpdateProgress | 0x37 finlap      |
   +------------+---------------------------+------------------+
   | 0x0047ebf8 | maybe_Race_UpdateProgress | 0x47 / 0x48      |
   +------------+---------------------------+------------------+
   | 0x0047d9e3 | maybe_Game_Tick           | 0x47 / 0x48      |
   +------------+---------------------------+------------------+
   | 0x00412b3e | FUN_00412a50              | 0x3e racecmpl    |
   +------------+---------------------------+------------------+
   | 0x004583bb | FUN_00458310              | 0x3b newrecor    |
   +------------+---------------------------+------------------+

   0x47 AGAINST 0x48 IS CMP EAX,2 / JLE - third or better gets
   yuralosr, worse gets youlost.

   THE WRONG-WAY TEST IS A PLANE SIDE, NOT AN ANGLE:

       dot(segment.plane0.normal,
           raceDir * forward + position)  <  0

   forward is matOrientation row 2, position its translation row,
   so the point tested is one unit in FRONT of the bike - ahead
   in a normal race and behind in a reversed one, which is the
   whole job raceDir does.  The threshold at 0x004d1900 is 0.0f.
   It is asked only when the segment step came out below 1, so
   the moment progress stops going forward rather than on a
   timer, and only for the racer on screen.

   THE LATCH IS ON THE VOICE, NOT ON THE WARNING.  The caption
   goes up BEFORE the latch is tested - a comma expression at
   0x0047e727 - so the text is raised every frame while the line
   is spoken once.  Reading that the other way round gives either
   a warning that flashes for one frame or an announcer that will
   not stop talking.

   WRONGWAY IS LATCHED so it is said ONCE per episode:
   0x004f0c58 is cleared at 0x0047e568 whenever the bike is
   pointing the right way and set at 0x0047e734 after the line is
   queued, so turning back round re-arms it.

   TIMEOUT IS NOT LATCHED, IT IS RATE-LIMITED - 0x004f0c54 holds
   the time it last spoke rather than a flag - AND IT DOES NOT
   FIRE WHILE THE CLOCK IS MERELY LOW.  Both halves live inside
   one arm:

       if (timer < 0) {
           if (timer + 500 < 0)  GAME OVER, 0x39, raceState 3
           else                  TIMEOUT, 0x42
       }

   So the nag occupies the 500 ms BETWEEN the clock passing
   zero and game over.  Its own 2000 ms rate limit is wider
   than that window, so it speaks ONCE.

   Reading it as 'nags while the clock is low' is the mistake
   the call site invites, and it is wrong: at any positive
   clock the arm is not reached at all.  Confirmed by setting
   MOTO's clock to -200 and stepping - the arm ran and queued
   0x42; at 5000, 1816 and 536 it never did.  [verified -
   live]

   PRACTICE IS UNTIMED, and the gate is on the ARM rather than
   on the clock: g_nSelectedMode 0 without time attack never
   runs any of it and the timer counts on into deep negatives
   with nothing reading it.

8.3.  The countdown does not use the queue

   Hud_DrawCountdown (0x00413030) calls Hud_CountdownSound
   (0x004082f0) directly, which is why the digits land on the
   beat rather than 500 ms behind it.

   TWO SOUNDS PER TICK: a beep, always, and the spoken digit only
   while n < 4.  n is g_nTimeSeconds / 2 + 1, and it is
   edge-gated TWICE - the drawer on DAT_00542854 and the sound on
   DAT_00504564.

       n=3 beep 1 + 0x30    n=1 beep 1 + 0x32
       n=2 beep 1 + 0x31    n=0 beep 2 + 0x33 go

   CASE 0 IS UNREACHABLE FROM THE COUNTDOWN.  n is
   seconds / 2 + 1, which is one at its lowest, so the drawer
   can never ask for GO.  The GO arm is entered from the OTHER
   caller: Hud_DrawInRace runs Hud_CountdownSound(0) at
   0x004133e6, between drawing the GO widget and clearing the
   arm flag.

   So GO's voice and its bip2 - the higher beep, the ding that
   ends the sequence - belong to the first RACING frame, not to
   the countdown.  Looking for them in the countdown drawer
   finds nothing, because the switch arm is dead code from
   there.

   THE SECOND EDGE GATE EARNS ITS KEEP HERE.  hud_draw runs its
   body twice, once per overlay pass, so both callers are
   reached twice a frame.  GO is saved by clearing the arm flag
   immediately after; the digits are saved by nothing but that
   gate on the last value played.

8.4.  The cue id decides voice or sfx

   Not the caller.  Every play site applies the same test:

       id in 0x2f..0x5c  ->  g_nCfgVoicesEnabled, voices volume
       otherwise         ->  g_nCfgSfxEnabled,   sfx volume

   So the speech setting silences the spoken digits and leaves
   the countdown beeps, which is what it is for.  A port that
   routes by call site instead will get that wrong.

8.5.  Remarks about your place - Snd_TickPositionVoice

   0x00407420, and it is where runlast, running4, stillrun and
   yurdalst come from.  None of them appears at any of 8.2's ten
   sites, which makes them look dead; they are behind two tables
   here instead.

   ONCE A MINUTE AT MOST - the whole function is gated on
   DAT_0054299c + 60000 < g_framePrevMs, on voices being on, on
   not being paused, and on the local racer being the one on
   screen.  Racer count must be above 1.

   +-----------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | Standing              | Cue                                 |
   +=======================+=====================================+
   | leading               | 0x41 stillrun or 0x40 running4      |
   +-----------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | last, gap of 1        | 0x49 yurdalst, on a coin toss       |
   +-----------------------+-------------------------------------+
   | mid-pack              | 0x38 fuel, 0x3c onegear or          |
   |                       | 0x3f runlast                        |
   +-----------------------+-------------------------------------+

   Mid-pack needs count >= 3 with a gap of 2, or count >= 4 with
   a gap of 2 or 3; anything else says nothing.

   THE LAST-PLACE LINE IS THE ODD ONE OUT.  The other two arms
   queue with the usual 500 ms; this one plays DIRECT and carries
   its own no-repeat guard.  So being last is remarked on at once
   while the other observations arrive a beat late.

8.6.  What the port has

   o THE QUEUE AND THE COUNTDOWN ARE IN.  audio_cue_delayed is
     the ring, drained in order from one head by audio_update,
     and race_audio_stop clears it so a race cannot open with the
     last one's commentary.  hud_countdown_sound plays the beep
     and the digit, and hud_draw_go plays GO with its
     bip2 on the first racing frame.

   o THE TWO GROUP VOLUMES ARE IN AND ARE SEPARATE, which is
     8.4's rule made real: audio_group_gain scales by
     AUDIO_GROUP_SPEECH or AUDIO_GROUP_SFX on the cue id alone.
     Both default to full and nothing sets them yet - the
     original never exposed them either - but the split is there
     to be wired to an ini setting without revisiting call
     sites.

   o THE FINISH LINE IS IN.  Solo, viewed racer only:

       won        WellDone or yeaah, a coin toss on
                  Rand_Next(1) under 0x4000
       last       yuralosr
       2nd, 3rd   NOTHING - the silent arm is deliberate
       4th+       youlost

     THE LAST TEST COMES BEFORE THE PODIUM TEST, so last of
     three is remarked on where third of four is not.  And it
     draws on STREAM 1, not the stream the rest of the race
     audio uses - maybe_Rand is a wrapper on Rand_Next(1).

   o WRONG WAY IS IN - race_wrong_way_tick, with the plane
     test, the latch and the re-arm on the forward arm.

   o CHECKPOINT AND FINAL LAP ARE IN.  Final lap fires on the
     crossing that leaves one to go.  The checkpoint pair is
     noted in the sweep and spoken AFTER the racer loop, so it
     sees the same lapCounter the HUD would.

     The counter now has the original's shape - flagged in the
     sweep, incremented once a frame before the lap close-out,
     wrapped at the timing-segment count the loader counts with
     the sweep's own predicate.

     THE COUNTER STARTS AT ONE, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE OF IT.
     Hooked at 0x0047e83a - the store itself - over a one-lap time
     attack on ile01, the local racer's stores were 2, 3, 1, and
     [0x004e9844] is 3 so the 3 is immediately overwritten with 0
     at 0x0047e849.  Reading them back against a 4-crossing lap:

   +----------------+---------+-------------------------------------+
   | Crossing       | Counter | Mute: counter 1 AND lapCounter 0?   |
   +================+=========+=====================================+
   | grid -> line   | no store| skipped, raceState still 0          |
   +----------------+---------+-------------------------------------+
   | checkpoint 1   |       2 | no - speaks                         |
   +----------------+---------+-------------------------------------+
   | checkpoint 2   |  3 -> 0 | no - speaks                         |
   +----------------+---------+-------------------------------------+
   | finish line    |       1 | lapCounter is now 1 - speaks        |
   +----------------+---------+-------------------------------------+

     so the first store of 2 means the field held ONE before any
     crossing, and the forward mute NEVER FIRES over a drivable
     race.  Confirmed after the run: counter 1, lapCounter 1 - and
     lapCounter 1 after exactly one lap is also what proves it
     0-based, which is what rules out "both fields start at 1".

     SEEDING IT TO ZERO IS THE BUG.  The counter then reaches 1 at
     the first checkpoint and the mute eats it, which is what a
     faithful transcription of 0x004131d0 produced here. The port
     seeds 1 in racer_reset_nonzero.

     THE WRITER IS maybe_Race_StartPending, 0x004112ac [verified
     by watchpoint].  It is the only writer of that 1 in the image
     and it sets the pair together, one racer per iteration:

         004112ac  MOV ECX,0x58739a             racer 1's +0x2f79
         004112b2  MOV word ptr [ECX],0x1       checkpointIndex = 1
         004112b7  ADD ECX,0x3209               next racer
         004112bd  MOV word ptr [ECX-0x3207],DX lapCounter = 0

     ECX advances BEFORE the second store, so [ECX-0x3207] is
     +0x2f7b of the racer just handled - both stores belong to the
     same racer, and DX is 0 throughout.  The address is
     HARDCODED, g_racers + 0x3209 + 0x2f79, so the loop starts at
     racer 1 and never touches racer 0.

     maybe_Game_InitRace is NOT the seeder, which is what sent the
     search wrong: both its sites store BX, the register they also
     store into lapCounter, so they store 0.  FUN_0045bdf0 at
     0x0045bdfc stores 0 as well.  This loop runs after both.

     The port seeds 1 in racer_reset_nonzero.

     THE WORD IS GATED ON THE MODE, 0x0041323f: the blip goes out
     on every counted crossing but Snd_QueueVoice(0x34) only when
     g_nSelectedMode or g_nTimeAttack is set.  So plain practice
     gets the ding and no announcer, which is why a one-lap time
     attack is the only solo run that talks.

     [open] THE REST OF maybe_Race_UpdateProgress is still a
     skeleton - rule 15 wants it whole.  What that would bring:
     the wrong-way cue's geometry test, "new record" through
     FUN_00458310, "well done" / 0x47 / 0x48 at the finish, and
     the anti-cheat condition that the sweep runs only while

   o THE DRAIN NOW RELEASES ONE LINE AT A TIME and treats the
     500 as the gap that follows it, so the first cue goes out
     at once.  Before this it fired everything due in one pass,
     which spoke the checkpoint blip and its word simultaneously

   o THE CROSSING ITSELF IS IN THE RIGHT PLACE.  Creeping up to

     [observed]

   o NONE OF THE CAPTIONS ARE IN - WRONG WAY, the FINAL LAP

   o GAMEOVER IS IN.  The clock going 500 ms PAST zero - not

   o TIMEOUT IS IN TOO, with the 500 ms window, the 2000 ms

     obj+0x0c still gates the nag in the original and is

   o TRY AGAIN IS IN, with its 3000 ms countdown - a SECOND

8.6a.  The end-of-time sequence

   The clock running out is a chain, not a cue:

     1. clock low            TIMEOUT banner (FUN_00481af0) and
                             0x42 every 2 s
     2. clock + 500 < 0      0x0052c238 = 1, GAME OVER banner
                             (FUN_00482120), queue 0x39
     3. 3000 ms on DAT_004e9828, in FUN_00412a50
     4. solo only            FUN_004358b0 - Snd_StopAll, then
                             Snd_PlayCue(0x44) DIRECT, then the
                             CONTINUE menu

   THE CONTINUE MENU IS A THIRD IN-GAME MENU MODE - it builds a
   fresh InGameMenu with *g_pEscapeMenu = 5, beside the escape
   menu and the post-race prompt.  Yes restarts the race, no
   returns to the race select.

   tryagain IS PLAYED DIRECT, like the countdown digits and
   unlike everything else in 8.2, and it follows Snd_StopAll -
   so whatever was still queued is discarded rather than
   spoken over it.

8.7.  The voice pool must be able to rebind

   A slot is created for one sample and, unless something says
   otherwise, stays bound to it for the rest of the run.  Once
   all 48 exist, a sound with no slot of its own is refused no
   matter how many slots have finished - and the pool never
   recovers, because nothing ever rebinds one.

   SEEN AS: 48 allocated, NONE owned, none held, twenty of them
   still nailed to one throttle blip, and every later cue
   refused.  The debug fast-forward fills it in seconds - the
   game legitimately triggers far more sounds than can play in
   real time - and dropping back to normal speed never gets it
   back.  The race simply goes quiet and stays quiet.

   EXHAUSTING IT UNDER FAST-FORWARD IS FINE.  Recovering
   afterwards is not optional, and recovery means taking a
   FINISHED slot off another sample: not owned, not looping,
   not held by the pause, not still sounding.  The voice is
   destroyed and remade because its format is fixed at
   creation.

   THERE ARE TWO ALLOCATORS AND BOTH NEED IT.  audio_voice_for
   serves the cue layer and says "cue dropped"; the tail of
   audio_voice_start serves everything else and says "cue 0x...
   not started".  They allocate independently, so fixing one
   leaves the other failing with a nearly identical message -
   read which wording the log carries before deciding the bug
   is still there.

9.  MASTER VOLUME IS THE WINDOWS MIXER

   The three volume options on the sound page are not software
   gains. Settings_LoadAll (0x0042b2a0) reads three registry
   values and passes each to Mixer_SetVolume (0x00470cf0), whose
   body ends in mixerSetControlDetails - the Win32 mixer API.
   [verified]

   +------+----------------+------------+----------------------+
   | line | registry key   | string at  | index pushed at      |
   +======+================+============+======================+
   | 0    | "MasterVolume" | 0x004e7ee4 | 0x0042b2f0           |
   +------+----------------+------------+----------------------+
   | 1    | "CDVolume"     | 0x004e7ed8 | 0x0042b322           |
   +------+----------------+------------+----------------------+
   | 2    | "WaveVolume"   | 0x004e7ecc | 0x0042b354           |
   +------+----------------+------------+----------------------+

   THE GAME THEREFORE CHANGES THE OPERATING SYSTEM'S VOLUME on
   startup, restoring whatever was saved. That is why it can be
   startlingly loud on a machine it has not run on before.

   o THE MIXER OBJECT IS 0x005d2958. mixer[0] is non-zero once
     initialised, mixer[1] is the HMIXEROBJ, and the per-line
     range is mixer[line + 0x119], which reads 32.

   o THE VOLUMES LIVE IN AN XOR-ENCRYPTED SETTINGS FILE, not the
     registry and not an ini. Settings_ReadInt (0x00425bb0) is
     the reader; it goes through FUN_00425080, which calls
     Crypt_Xor (0x0045a4a0) and parses with sscanf, and
     FUN_0042f310 writes it back with fwrite. [verified from the
     call graph]

     The registry holds only install information - DataPath,
     SavingPath, Language, Version, InstallType. Two separate
     stores; do not assume a setting is in one because another
     is. See subsystems/config-file.txt.

   o THERE IS NO COMMAND-LINE FLAG for volume. The flag block at
     0x004dc664 ends at -FrameRateMax and -NoSound is the only
     audio switch, disabling DirectSound entirely.

   FOR THE PORT: a reimplementation should NOT move the system
   mixer. Applying gain to its own DirectSound or XAudio2 buffers
   is the modern equivalent and does not surprise the user by
   changing a machine-wide setting. tools/mrhook can set it for
   testing - see tools/mrhook/README.txt section 12.


10.  maybe_Snd_TickRacer - the per-racer engine and effects mixer

   0x00406290, renamed from FUN_00406290. Sections 1 to 7 cover the
   CUE layer - fire sample N, play track N. This is the layer above
   it: one call per racer per frame that holds looping voices open
   and retunes them, and it is where the engine note actually comes
   from.

       __thiscall(int *snd, MR_Racer *racer, int master, int pan,
                  int allowShiftCue)

9.1.  The voice API, five methods on the sound manager

   All five read [ECX+4] and bail if it is zero, and all five ignore
   a voice id of -1, so they are methods on the manager object with
   the free slot marked -1 [verified from RET n and the prologues]:

   +--------------------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | Name                     | Address      | Signature, this omitted              |
   +==========================+==============+======================================+
   | maybe_Snd_StartVoice     | 0x00423fd0   | (int cue, int mode) returns a voice  |
   |                          |              | id, or -1                            |
   +--------------------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | Snd_StopVoice            | 0x00424000   | (int voice)                          |
   +--------------------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | Snd_SetVolume            | 0x00424030   | (int voice, int vol)                 |
   +--------------------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | maybe_Snd_SetFrequency   | 0x00424050   | (int voice, int hz)                  |
   +--------------------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+
   | maybe_Snd_SetPan         | 0x00424070   | (int voice, int pan)                 |
   +--------------------------+--------------+--------------------------------------+

   o WHICH SPLITS THE AUDIO CODE IN TWO. Snd_PlayCue in section 1.1
     is fire-and-forget; these five are the handle-based path, and
     nothing in sections 1 to 7 used them. A reconstruction needs
     both.

   o maybe_Snd_SetFrequency AND maybe_Snd_SetPan ARE TOLD APART BY
     THEIR ARGUMENTS, not by their bodies. 0x00424050 is always
     given __ftol of an expression in rpm or speed; 0x00424070 is
     always given the caller's fourth argument unchanged, identical
     for every voice of one racer. That is a pitch and a position.
     [inferred, and the reason both keep maybe_]

9.2.  The per-racer audio record

   THE RECORD IS EXACTLY 0x78 BYTES [verified]. It is a state
   block of ints that nothing else in this project has touched,
   and it is NOT the racer and NOT the manager - see 9.2.1 for
   why that is easy to get backwards.

   Two proofs, both out of FUN_004079e0, and they agree:

   o ITS RESET LOOP SUBSCRIPTS A DWORD POINTER BY iVar21 * 0x1e,
     and 0x1e dwords is 0x78 bytes. The stride of an array IS
     the size of its element, so this alone settles it.
   o AND THE HIGHEST INDEX TOUCHED ANYWHERE IS [0x1d], at +0x74
     - the last dword that fits inside 0x78. The record is
     fully used, with no room left over.

   Where they live:

   +--------------+------------+----------------------------------------------+
   | Address      | Count      | Which racers                                 |
   +==============+============+==============================================+
   | 0x005032c0   | 4          | audible OTHER racers, stride 0x78. The count |
   |              |            | comes from the slot table at                 |
   |              |            | 0x00504210..0x0050423f walked 12 bytes at a  |
   |              |            | time                                         |
   +--------------+------------+----------------------------------------------+
   | 0x005034b0   | 1          | the VIEWED racer, standalone. Identical      |
   |              |            | field offsets, reset field by field rather   |
   |              |            | than in a loop                               |
   +--------------+------------+----------------------------------------------+

   +------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
   | Index      | Meaning                                                        |
   +============+================================================================+
   | [0]        | state: 0 idle, 1 engine running, 2 stalled or below the low    |
   |            | threshold                                                      |
   +------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
   | [0xd]      | THE PER-RACER MASTER. Every volume in the function is (v *     |
   |            | [0xd]) >> 8, so this is the distance attenuation the caller    |
   |            | computed.                                                      |
   +------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
   | [4] [5]    | nine voice slots, each -1 when free. Stopped with              |
   | [6] [7]    | Snd_StopVoice before being reused.                             |
   | [8] [9]    |                                                                |
   | [10] [0xb] |                                                                |
   | [0xc]      |                                                                |
   +------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
   | [0x11]     | a rising volume ramp, 0x50 to 200 in steps of 0x1e.            |
   | [0x10]     | [0x10] is the SKID voice's own ramp - see 9.7.                 |
   +------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
   | [0x14]     | the roll loop frequency: 5000, 8000, 18000 or 20000 by surface |
   |            | class.                                                         |
   +------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
   | [0x1b]     | a PEAK HOLD of wheelieAngle, clamped at 0x3fc90fdb = PI/2,     |
   |            | used to size the WHEEL thud and then zeroed.  See 9.14.        |
   +------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
   | [0x12]     | previous-frame copies of surface class, tickInputWord, rpm,    |
   | [0x16]     | gear, wheelieAngle and crashLatch - the edges everything       |
   | [0x17]     | triggers on.                                                   |
   | [0x19]     |                                                                |
   | [0x1a]     |                                                                |
   | [0x1d]     |                                                                |
   +------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+

   o A RISING EDGE ON crashLatch RESETS THE WHOLE RECORD - nine
     voice slots to -1 and a dozen counters to 0, via FUN_00406190.
     So a crash silences the bike completely and it restarts from
     idle.

9.3.  riderStateBits is an event word, and this is its decoder

   racers.txt section 3 records racer+0x2dac as "flags; collision
   ORs 4, 8, 0x10, 0x20" from the writer's side. This is the reader,
   and it maps bits to cues [verified]:

   +------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
   | riderStateBits   | Cue fired                                                |
   +==================+==========================================================+
   | 0x40             | cue 0x11                                                 |
   +------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
   | 0x02 | 0x08 |    | cue 0x12, at 0xff when 0x02 is among them and 200        |
   | 0x20             | otherwise                                                |
   +------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
   | 0x04 | 0x10      | a voice line, cue 0x3d - LOCAL RACER ONLY, one time in   |
   |                  | four, and only if g_nCfgVoicesEnabled                    |
   +------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
   | 0x02             | a voice line, cue 0x4b, on the same terms                |
   +------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
   | 0x01 | 0x04 |    | cue 0x0e or 0x0f, Rand_Next picks between them           |
   | 0x10             |                                                          |
   +------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+

   o TWO OF THE FIVE ARE VOICE LINES AND ARE LOCAL-RACER ONLY,
     gated on the racer being g_racers[g_localRacerIndex] and on a
     one-in-four Rand_Next draw. So the rider only comments on his
     own crashes, and only sometimes.

9.4.  Two engine banks, chosen by the bike

   local_10 points at 0x004db638 when g_bikeCarac[bike].PersoAnim is
   set and 0x004db618 when it is clear - two tables of eight ints,
   indexed by racer->gear, giving the cue for the current gear.

   o SO THE DIRT AND ROAD BIKES HAVE SEPARATE ENGINE BANKS, selected
     by the same PersoAnim field that maybe_Racer_UpdateSkeleton uses
     to pick an animation set. One flag, two subsystems.

   o AND THE GEAR CUE CHANGES ONLY WHEN THE TABLE ENTRY CHANGES, not
     on every shift: the code compares
     table[prevGear] against table[gear] before restarting the loop,
     so two gears sharing a sample do not retrigger it.

9.5.  The surface class

   FUN_00465d20(rearWheelContact, 1) returns a small integer that is
   then remapped 1->2, 9->6, 0xb->5, 0xc->4 and forced to 0 when the
   racer is not onGround. It selects the roll loop, its frequency
   from [0x14] and its volume ceiling.

   o THE REMAP MEANS THE AUDIO CLASSES ARE COARSER THAN THE SURFACE
     IDS. Several track surfaces share one sound, which is worth
     knowing before treating the returned number as a material id.
     formats/bpt-carac.txt section 5 lists nine named surfaces in the
     grip table; this collapses them to about six. [inferred]

9.6.  Rand stream 4, and why that matters

   Every draw here is Rand_Next(4). The FX spray and the finish
   jingles use maybe_Rand, which is Rand_Next(1).

   o SEPARATE STREAMS MEAN AUDIO RANDOMNESS CANNOT PERTURB THE
     SIMULATION. replay.txt section 6 proved a race replays bit for
     bit; a shared RNG between the mixer and the physics would have
     broken that the moment a sample was skipped for being out of
     earshot. The per-stream generator in formats/bpt.txt is what
     makes it safe. [inferred - the determinism is measured, the
     causal link is not]

   o THE SEEDS ARE BAKED INTO THE IMAGE, and the mixer depends
     on it. g_randState at 0x004f2590 is seven dwords of
     INITIALISED data, not bss, and stream 4's seed is
     0x1a3d5e7f. Nothing seeds stream 4 at run time - the only
     Rand_Seed call in the image is the replay file's, on
     stream 6 - so the initialiser is the only thing keeping
     the stream alive. Zero is the generator's fixed point, so
     a zero-filled array would make every draw here 0 and
     leave the brake cue stuck on brake1, the throttle blip
     firing on every edge, and the volume wander frozen at
     240. [verified - read out of the image, and confirmed
     against the running retail game, where streams 1, 4 and
     6 had advanced and 0, 2, 3 and 5 had not]

   o AND Rand_Next / Rand_Seed WERE NAMED IN docs/formats/bpt.txt
     AND NOT IN THE DB. Both are now named in Ghidra too. That is
     the exact divergence CLAUDE.md rule 5b exists to prevent, found
     by reading a function that used them.

9.2.1.  It is __thiscall, and the decompiler says otherwise

   ECX IS THE AUDIO RECORD AND THE RACER IS A STACK ARGUMENT:

       00406297  MOV EDI,ECX            EDI = the audio record
       00406299  MOV EBP,[ESP+0x48]     EBP = the racer
       0040629d  MOV ECX,[EBP+0x78]     racer+0x78 = bikeIndex

   o UNTIL THE PROTOTYPE WAS SET, Ghidra modelled the function as
     __cdecl and rendered the call in FUN_004079e0 as
     maybe_Snd_TickRacer(&g_racers + i*0x3209, vol, ...). That
     reads as "the first parameter is the racer", which is
     exactly wrong: it is the stack argument promoted to first
     place with ECX dropped. THIS NOTE WAS ALMOST REWRITTEN ON
     THE STRENGTH OF IT - the section above was correct all
     along and nearly got "corrected" into an error.
   o SAME TRAP AS FUN_0040c8b0, where an "argument type
     contradiction" turned out to be a hidden this. A decompile
     whose calling convention has not been set is evidence about
     Ghidra, not about the program.
   o The prototype is now set in the DB, so the call sites
     render correctly.

9.7.  The skid voice

   Record[0xc] at +0x30, and IT IS THE BRAKE SOUND. That is why
   nothing called 'brake' appears anywhere in the tick: cues
   0x1a and 0x1b never reach Snd_PlayCue as constants. They are
   computed, (Rand_Next(4) & 1) + 0x1a, inside a surface switch.

   THE GATE IS THE BRAKE INPUT, not a deceleration: bit 1 of
   tickInputWord, plus onGround, plus a free slot, plus speed
   above 8.3333 (g_sndSkidMinSpeed, 0x004d01e8).

   o THE onGround TEST GUARDS THE START AND THE PER-FRAME
     UPDATE, NOT THE RELEASE. The release at 0x00406f59 reads
     the brake bit alone, so braking in mid-air neither starts
     a skid nor fades a running one. [verified]

   The switch, through the jump table at 0x004073c0:

   +---------------+------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | Surface class | Cue        | Sample                                  |
   +===============+============+=========================================+
   | 3, 5, 8, 10   | 0x17       | gravier                                 |
   +---------------+------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | 4             | 0x19       | herbe                                   |
   +---------------+------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | 6, 7          | 0x1a 0x1b  | brake1 or brake2, one Rand_Next(4) draw |
   +---------------+------------+-----------------------------------------+
   | 0xd           | 0x18       | glace                                   |
   +---------------+------------+-----------------------------------------+

   o ONLY FIVE OF THESE CLASSES CAN ARRIVE HERE - 4, 5, 6, 10
     and 0xd. SurfaceUnder's +9 and the remap in 9.5 never
     produce 3, 7 or 8; those belong to the other, unflagged
     caller. And class 6 is plain ROAD, so the sound heard
     under braking through most of a race is the brake squeal
     rather than a scrape. [verified]

   Frequency, recomputed every frame at 0x00406eea:

       hz = (int)(base + speed * 4000.0 / RealSpeedMax)
       hz = (int)(hz * velocityPitch)

   The base is 22050, or 12050 on classes 6 and 7 where the cue
   is the brake - the lower base is what makes a brake squeal
   read lower than gravel. The span is a full 4000 Hz
   (g_sndPitchSpan4000, 0x004d0228) between a standstill and
   the BIKE'S OWN top speed - carac index 18, not a shared
   constant, so a slow bike and a fast one sound alike at their
   respective limits. Volume is record[0x10] * record[0xd] >> 8,
   starting at 0xe6.

   THE RELEASE HAS TWO TRIGGERS, not one: letting the brake go,
   or the rpm fraction falling under 0.2 (g_sndSkidRpmFloor,
   0x004d0230). The second is what stops the squeal carrying on
   into a standstill. record[0x10] then falls by 0x32 a frame
   and the voice is stopped when it goes NEGATIVE.

   o THE RAMP IS NEVER FLOORED AT ZERO, so with no voice up and
     the engine idling it keeps counting down for as long as
     the race lasts. At 50 a frame that needs days to matter,
     and it is the original's arithmetic, so the
     reconstruction keeps it rather than quietly diverging.

9.8.  The five arguments, and the two the caller computes

   Settled from the prologue and the call site at
   0x00407d48..0x00407d5e. Push order puts the LAST push first,
   so the list reads backwards from the assembly.

   +---------+----------------+--------------------------------------+
   | Slot    | Argument       | What it is                           |
   +=========+================+======================================+
   | +0x48   | racer          | MR_Racer *                           |
   +---------+----------------+--------------------------------------+
   | +0x4c   | master         | 0..255, clamped by the caller        |
   +---------+----------------+--------------------------------------+
   | +0x50   | pan            | handed to maybe_Snd_SetPan           |
   +---------+----------------+--------------------------------------+
   | +0x54   | bPlayerVoices  | 1 from the caller, but CLEARED at    |
   +---------+----------------+--------------------------------------+
   |         |                | 0x004063f2 when humanPlayer is 0     |
   +---------+----------------+--------------------------------------+
   | +0x58   | velocityPitch  | float; MULTIPLIES EVERY FREQUENCY    |
   +---------+----------------+--------------------------------------+

   o velocityPitch IS ONE FACTOR OVER THE WHOLE BIKE, not a
     per-voice tweak: the same slot scales the engine note at
     0x00406dda and the skid at 0x00406f0d. The caller builds
     it at 0x00407ce3..0x00407d1b from two dot products scaled
     by -1/46080 and +1/46080, each offset by 1.0 - a
     Doppler term. [verified]

   o THE master ARGUMENT IS A LINEAR DISTANCE RAMP, computed
     beside it: (10240.0 - dist) * 0.033203125, clamped 0..255.
     So a bike is at full volume inside 2560 units and silent
     past 10240 (g_sndDistMax, 0x004d0280). [verified]

   FOR THE VIEWED RACER ALL THREE ARE ALREADY RIGHT, and that
   was not obvious. The driver passes pan 0 and bPlayerVoices
   1 for it literally, and velocityPitch is set to 1.0 and
   overwritten ONLY when g_dwReplayMode is set - so outside a
   replay the viewed bike has no Doppler at all. race.c's
   master of 255 is the one approximation left, and it is
   right whenever the camera is on the bike.

   THE DOPPLER IS FOR THE OTHER RACERS. It is computed per
   slot in the loop at the foot of the driver, which is what
   has to exist before any of it matters. See 9.11.

9.9.  The engine note

   Recomputed every frame while record[0] is 1 and the gear is
   above neutral. Seven terms, in this order
   [0x00407112..0x00407212]:

       hz  = lo + (hi - lo) * rpmFraction
       hz += 2000 + rand % 3000      one frame in four, on the
                                     throttle's RISING edge
       hz -= revDrift
       hz  = hz + wheelieAngle * 4000.0
       hz -= (frontY - rearY) / (AxeDistance * 48.0) * 1800.0
       hz += record[0x1c]
       hz  = hz * velocityPitch

   lo and hi come from the per-gear table the bank flag picks -
   see 9.4 - indexed by gear with a stride of 8 bytes. frontY
   and rearY are racer+0x1b8 and racer+0x33c, the Y components
   of the two wheel contact points.

   o THE DRIFT IS SUBTRACTED. The decompiler renders the whole
     chain as additions because revDrift is always zero (9.5),
     so the sign shows only in the SUB at 0x004071b1. Nothing
     can hear the difference, which is exactly why it is worth
     writing down. [verified]

   o THE WHEELIE TERM IS 4000 Hz PER RADIAN, not a 4000 Hz
     ceiling. The tick clamps its own peak hold at PI/2, so a
     full wheelie is worth about 6280 Hz - much the largest of
     the three corrections. [verified]

   o THE SLOPE TERM IS THE BIKE'S PITCH, NOT THE GROUND'S. It
     is the height difference between the two wheel contact
     points over the wheelbase, and the 48.0 is the same
     constant that PLACES those points - BIKE_AXLE_K in
     bikes.h. Nose-up lowers the note. [verified]

   o record[0x1c] IS VESTIGIAL. The only instruction in the
     image that writes +0x70 is FUN_004073f0, and it stores
     zero, so the term contributes nothing. It is not
     reconstructed. [verified]

   o TRUNCATION HAPPENS FOUR TIMES, not once - after the gear
     ramp, after the wheelie term, on the slope term by
     itself, and after velocityPitch. Each is its own __ftol,
     and folding them into one expression drifts by a few
     hertz.

   The volume is separate: 250 for an AI, 255 for a human on
   the throttle or up on the back wheel, and otherwise a slow
   wander of 240 less a draw modulo 50, re-rolled no oftener
   than every 50 ms on the road bike and 100 ms on the dirt
   one. Under 20 km/h the result is then scaled linearly to
   nothing at 1 km/h [0x004072ac] - which is what keeps a bike
   sitting on the grid quiet, and was missing from the port
   until the expression was read whole.

9.10.  The idle loop, and why a stopped bike is not silent

   Cue 0x21, 'motostp', record[8] at +0x20 [0x00406c10]. It is a
   SEPARATE SAMPLE, not a gear loop slowed down, and it is already
   in both engine bank tables.

   Two thresholds, and between them BOTH loops play:

   +-------------+------------------+----------------------------+
   | Voice       | Full volume      | Silent / stopped           |
   +=============+==================+============================+
   | gear loop   | above 20 km/h    | fades to nothing at 1 km/h |
   |             |                  | and STOPS under 5 km/h     |
   +-------------+------------------+----------------------------+
   | motostp     | below 10 km/h    | fades out by 29 km/h       |
   +-------------+------------------+----------------------------+

   o BOTH SLOPES ARE 19/3.6 WIDE. The gear loop's fade rises over
     1..20 km/h and the idle loop's falls over 10..29; they are
     the same curve twice, offset. That is the tell that they are
     meant as a crossfade rather than two independent gates.

   o UNDER 5 km/h THE GEAR LOOP IS STOPPED OUTRIGHT and record[0]
     goes to 2 - which is the state that keeps it from restarting.
     So below walking pace the idle loop is the ONLY engine voice,
     and a port without it is completely silent at a standstill.
     [verified]

   Frequency is rpmFraction * 8000 + 16050, so blipping the
   throttle at rest is still heard. Volume is (8.0555 - speed) *
   918/19, clamped 0..255 - the product reaches 389 standing
   still, so the clamp is doing real work.

   o record[0xa] at +0x28 IS VESTIGIAL, like record[0x1c]. It is
     cleared to -1 beside the engine voice in three places and no
     code anywhere assigns it a voice. [verified]

9.11.  Snd_TickRace - the driver, and how AI racers get heard

   FUN_004079e0, called once a frame. It is the whole reason the
   per-racer mixer takes master, pan and velocityPitch as
   arguments: this is what computes them.

   Gated on g_nCfgSfxEnabled. In order:

   1. Distance pass. For every active racer, the straight-line
      distance from the CAMERA EYE to the bike, cached at
      racer+0x2fe1.

   2. The viewed racer, ticked from the standalone record at
      0x005034b0 with pan 0, bPlayerVoices 1 and velocityPitch
      1.0 - see 9.8. Its record is wiped whenever the viewed
      index changes.

   3. FUN_00407600 for the LOCAL racer only, outside replay -
      the rider commentary scheduler, which is a separate thing
      from the per-racer mixer. Wheelie-held lines, a dirt-bike
      random table at 0x004db978, and a road-bike delayed cue.

   4. Every OTHER active racer is collected as {index, distance},
      qsorted by distance (FUN_004079a0), and cut at 10240 -
      g_sndDistMax again, the same range the volume ramp dies at.

   5. The survivors are fitted into FOUR slots at 0x00504210,
      stride 12. A racer already holding a slot KEEPS it, and
      only then are free slots handed out - so a bike that stays
      in earshot keeps its record, and with it its engine voice
      and gear state, instead of restarting every frame.

   6. Each occupied slot is ticked from its own 0x78 record in
      the array at 0x005032c0, with its own volume, its own pan,
      and a real velocityPitch built from the camera's movement
      and the bike's.

   o FOUR IS THE AUDIBILITY BUDGET, not the racer count. The
     table is four entries and the code walks it to 0x0050423f.
     [verified]

   o bPlayerVoices IS 0 FOR EVERY AI RACER, so they never get
     the depart voices - those are the player's alone.
     [verified]

   o THE SLOT-KEEPING IN STEP 5 IS THE PART TO GET RIGHT. Clear
     the table and reassign from scratch each frame and every
     audible bike restarts its engine loop every frame.

9.12.  The throttle blip, and why it is not the rev bump

   Cue 0x1d..0x1f - the three DEPART samples - fired as a
   ONE-SHOT on the throttle's rising edge [0x00406fcd]. record
   [0xb] at +0x2c.

   o IT IS A SEPARATE SAMPLE, NOT THE PITCH BUMP AT 0x0040714b.
     The two are easy to conflate because both are throttle
     edges, but the bump only shifts a RUNNING gear loop's
     frequency. This starts a recording. [verified]

   o WHICH IS WHY TAPPING THE GAS AT A STANDSTILL MAKES A NOISE.
     Below 5 km/h the gear loop is stopped outright and record[0]
     is 2 (see 9.10), so there is no loop left for a pitch bump
     to act on - the blip is the only thing that can be heard
     down there, and a port without it is silent on a throttle
     tap no matter how the engine note is wired. [verified]

   o 'DEPART' IS THE RACE START, which is why it sounds like the
     rev under the menu's 'let's go' - the same family of
     recording, and the reason the sample name looks unrelated to
     a throttle blip when scanning the bank.

   The choice differs by bike:

   +--------+------------------------------------------------+
   | Bike   | Sample                                         |
   +========+================================================+
   | dirt   | Rand_Next(4) % 3 + 0x1d - any of the three     |
   +--------+------------------------------------------------+
   | road   | 0x1f - min((int)(speed/RealSpeedMax * 5), 2):  |
   |        | DEPART3 standing still, DEPART2 from a fifth   |
   |        | of top speed, DEPART1 from two fifths          |
   +--------+------------------------------------------------+

   Mode 2 - polyphonic, not looping - at volume master * 200 >> 8.
   record[0xb] carries the CUE on the way in and is overwritten
   with the VOICE handle on the way out, which is worth knowing
   before reading the field's type as one or the other.

   o THE PLAYER'S ALONE. bPlayerVoices is 1 only for the viewed
     racer and is cleared again for anyone whose humanPlayer is
     0, so AI bikes never blip. [verified]

9.13.  Open

   x The record at param_1 is EXACTLY 0x78 bytes. CLOSED - see
     9.2. The array stride in FUN_004079e0 settles it without
     needing an allocation site at all, which is worth
     remembering: for a statically allocated array the stride is
     as good as a malloc size, and often easier to find.
   x [0xf], the pitch base.  CLOSED.  It is the skid voice's
     base frequency, written the instant that voice starts -
     see 9.7.
   x maybe_Snd_TickRacer calls maybe_Racer_SlipSpeed and DISCARDS
     the result. CLOSED, AND IT IS DEAD CODE. The call is at
     0x0040672e and the next instruction is FSTP ST0 - pop and
     throw away. maybe_Racer_SlipSpeed reads its receiver and
     never writes it, and its one call is handed a stack local,
     so there is no side effect for the call to be there for.
     [verified]
     A LEFTOVER FROM A REMOVED FEATURE is the likeliest reading
     [guess]. racers.txt 9.19.4 briefly guessed the opposite -
     that audio read slip to drive tyre skid - on the strength
     of the call existing. It does not; that is retracted there.
   x FUN_0048cf20, called once on the landing frame, is unread.
     Still open - moved to 9.15's list, where the landing cue
     it sits beside is recorded.

9.14.  The wheelie landing thud, cue 0x13

   The one-shot that fires when a wheelie comes back down.  It is
   cue 0x13, named WHEEL in BOTH bike tables - Ktm at 0x004db8a0
   and Ninja at 0x004db920 - so the sample differs per bike and
   the id does not [verified].

   ORIGIN  maybe_Snd_TickRacer @ 0x00406290, at 0x00406b72.

   Three conditions, every one a sign-masked test:

       00406b75  TEST [rec+0x68], 0x7fffffff    prev angle != 0
       00406b86  TEST racer+0x307d, 0x7fffffff  angle now == 0
       00406b93  TEST racer+0x2db8              onGround

   So it fires on the frame the angle reaches zero, and ONLY with
   the wheels down: bring the nose down in mid-air off a jump and
   there is no thud.

   THE VOLUME COMES FROM THE PEAK, NOT THE CURRENT ANGLE, which is
   the whole reason the peak hold at [0x1b] exists.  By the time
   the test passes the angle IS zero, so a reading taken at the
   trigger would be silent every time.

       peak = min(peak, PI/2)                   004d0158
       vol  = (int)(peak * 121.59437652220804)  004d0200
              + 0x40                            00406bd1
       Snd_SetVolume(v, master * vol >> 8)

   THE MULTIPLIER IS NOT ROUND AND THAT IS DELIBERATE.  PI/2 times
   121.59437652220804 is 191.0 exactly, and 191 + 64 is 255 - so
   the constant was chosen to land a FULL wheelie at full volume,
   with 64 the floor a flick of the nose gets.  Range 64..255.

   The peak is zeroed at 00406bfb, by this block alone.  It is
   raised in the tail at 004072f4 and nowhere else, so a wheelie
   that ends airborne carries its peak into the next one.

9.15.  What the port's racer_audio_tick still does NOT play

   racer_audio_tick is a skeleton import (rule 15.1) and the gaps
   are all in one place: the impact and rider-event group between
   0x004064f0 and 0x00406b72.  The engine, idle, skid and depart
   voices are live; these are not.

   +-------+--------------+----------------------------------------+
   | Cue   | Sample       | Trigger                                |
   +=======+==============+========================================+
   | 0x13  | WHEEL        | wheelie landing.  DONE - see 9.14      |
   +-------+--------------+----------------------------------------+
   | 0x11  | rouleman     | riderStateBits & 0x40                  |
   +-------+--------------+----------------------------------------+
   | 0x12  | crash        | riderStateBits & 0x2a; 0xff if bit 1,  |
   |       |              | else 200                               |
   +-------+--------------+----------------------------------------+
   | 0x0e  | choc1        | riderStateBits & 0x15, Rand_Next(4)&1  |
   | 0x0f  | choc2        | picks; volume capped at 0xe1           |
   +-------+--------------+----------------------------------------+
   | 0x0b  | land1        | onGround rising edge - the JUMP        |
   | 0x0c  | land2        | landing.  Road takes 0x0b alone; dirt  |
   | 0x0d  | land3        | picks Rand_Next(4) % 3 + 0x0b          |
   +-------+--------------+----------------------------------------+
   | 0x3d  | a voice line | riderStateBits & 0x14, local racer,    |
   |       |              | 1 in 4, gated on VoicesState           |
   +-------+--------------+----------------------------------------+
   | 0x4b  | a voice line | riderStateBits & 2, otherwise as above |
   +-------+--------------+----------------------------------------+

   o THE JUMP LANDING IS THE AUDIBLE ONE OF THESE.  0x0b..0x0d
     fire on every landing off every jump, which on the dirt
     tracks is constant.

   o riderStateBits IS racer+0x2dac and its decoder is 9.3.  The
     bits are already written by the port's collision code, so
     these are trigger-side gaps only - nothing upstream is
     missing.

   o FUN_0048cf20 is called once on the landing frame, before the
     0x0b cue, and is still unread.


11.  THE CD AUDIO MAP - every track call in the game

   CdAudio_PlayTrackCue (0x00404990) is called from ELEVEN sites
   and CdAudio_StopCue (0x004049e0) from ONE.  That is the whole
   of the game's CD audio.  Every row below is read off its own
   pushes [verified].

   The mode bits are section 1's: bit 0 do not restart if this
   track is already playing, bit 1 repeat at the end.

11.1.  The eleven starts

   +------------------------------+-------+------+------------+
   | Caller                       | Track | Mode | Call site  |
   +==============================+=======+======+============+
   | MenuPage25_DsiLogo_Enter     |  13   | ESI  | 0x00453b61 |
   +------------------------------+-------+------+------------+
   | MenuPage14_Title_Enter       |   3   |  2   | 0x00446b27 |
   +------------------------------+-------+------+------------+
   | MenuPage03_MainMenu_Enter    |   3   |  3   | 0x004426f5 |
   +------------------------------+-------+------+------------+
   | MenuPage05_ModeSelect_Enter  |   3   |  3   | 0x004476ea |
   +------------------------------+-------+------+------------+
   | MenuPage06_RaceSelect_Enter  |   3   |  3   | 0x0044807c |
   +------------------------------+-------+------+------------+
   | MenuPage07_NetSessionList    |   3   |  3   | 0x0044954d |
   +------------------------------+-------+------+------------+
   | MenuPage08_NetRaceLobby      |   3   |  3   | 0x0044ae99 |
   +------------------------------+-------+------+------------+
   | maybe_Game_InitRace          | level |  0   | 0x0047bf95 |
   +------------------------------+-------+------+------------+
   | MenuPage24_Podium_Enter      |  11   |  0   | 0x00452e32 |
   +------------------------------+-------+------+------------+
   | MenuPage24_Podium_Enter      |  12   |  0   | 0x00452e44 |
   +------------------------------+-------+------+------------+
   | FUN_0048aca0, the credits    |  12   |  0   | 0x0048acc1 |
   +------------------------------+-------+------+------------+

   TRACK 3 IS THE MENU TRACK and six pages ask for it.  Five of
   them pass mode 3 - loop, and do not restart if it is already
   playing - which is what carries one continuous piece of music
   across the whole menu chain.  ONLY THE TITLE PAGE PASSES 2, so
   returning to the title restarts it and moving between the
   other five does not.

   THE RACE TRACK IS THE LEVEL'S OWN, and this is the entry the
   reconstruction was missing:

       CALL Snd_StopAll              0x0047bf84
       PUSH 0                        mode - play ONCE
       MOV  EAX, [0x004db67c]        AudioTrack, from Level.Spt
       PUSH EAX
       CALL CdAudio_PlayTrackCue     0x0047bf95

   o MODE 0 MEANS RACE MUSIC DOES NOT LOOP.  It plays once and
     stops, and nothing restarts it - so a race longer than its
     track finishes in silence.  That is the original's
     behaviour, not a gap to fill.

   o 0x004db67c IS THE LEVEL SCRIPT'S AudioTrack, the last key
     0x00427e80 reads out of %sLevel.Spt (5.5).  The track is per
     level and read from data, which is why no table of race
     tracks appears anywhere in the code.

   o THE Snd_StopAll IN FRONT OF IT IS THE SAMPLES, NOT THE CD.
     The menu music is still playing at this point and is
     REPLACED by the new track rather than stopped first.

   THE PODIUM PICKS ONE OF TWO BY POSITION.  EDI counts up a list
   ending at 0x53f848 and then:

       EDI >  2   track 11, and Snd cue 0x5b beside it
       EDI <= 2   track 12

   so track 12 is the good result and track 11 the poor one, the
   latter with the consolation line - 0x5b, yutrygan - over it.
   The credits use track 12 as well.

   [open] MenuPage25's mode is the register ESI rather than a
   literal.  The reconstruction reads it as 0 and the observed
   behaviour agrees - the intro plays once - but it is the one
   mode in this table not read off a push.

11.2.  There is exactly ONE stop

   MenuPage25_DsiLogo_Leave, at 0x00453bff.  Nothing else in the
   game ever stops the CD.

   THAT IS THE DESIGN AND NOT AN OVERSIGHT.  A page that wants
   music asks for its own track, and the request REPLACES
   whatever was playing; a page that does not care says nothing
   and INHERITS what is already going.  The intro is the
   exception because it is the one place where the next thing to
   happen - the title page - would otherwise start track 3 over
   the top of track 13 still running.

   SO MUSIC PLAYS ON EVERY MENU PAGE and only six of them start
   it.  Bike select, options, the controller config, TOP 5, name
   entry, the championship load and save pages and the loading
   page have NO CD call at all - they keep whatever the page
   before them left running.

11.3.  The track a race asks for comes out of the level

   maybe_Game_InitRace reads AudioTrack from the level manifest and
   passes it to CdAudio_PlayTrackCue with MODE 0 - play ONCE - at
   0x0047bf95.  So there is no table of race tracks anywhere in the
   code, and a race longer than its track finishes in silence
   because nothing restarts it.

   Decoded from the ten shipped manifests, AudioTrack takes the
   values 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10.  Never 3, which is the menu
   track, and never 11 to 13, which are the podium and the intro.

   o A ZERO WOULD MEAN RANDOM, and the reconstruction passes it
     straight through as the original does.  No shipped level
     carries one.

   Reconstructed in race_build_grid, beside the sample bank,
   because the original does both behind the loading caption.
