MotoRacer PC Reverse Engineering                           Analysis Note


                         THE .TRK TRACK FORMAT


Abstract

   A versioned scene graph holding the whole circuit: track segments,
   the meshes attached to them, named prop templates, placed instances,
   a per-segment link table, and an optional animated-decor block.

   Verified: all ten shipped tracks parse with every byte consumed and
   zero trailing data [verified]. Reference reader at tools/trk.py.

   +----------------+------------+-----------------------------+
   | Function       | Address    | Role                        |
   +================+============+=============================+
   | Trk_Load       | 0x0040d650 | the whole loader            |
   +----------------+------------+-----------------------------+
   | Trk_ReadVec3   | 0x004902f0 | 12 bytes                    |
   +----------------+------------+-----------------------------+
   | Trk_ReadMatrix | 0x00490320 | 48 bytes, a 4x3 matrix      |
   +----------------+------------+-----------------------------+
   | Trk_ReadPlane  | 0x004b1120 | 16 bytes, vec3 plus scalar  |
   +----------------+------------+-----------------------------+
   | Trk_ReadMesh   | 0x00422a20 | vertex and face arrays      |
   +----------------+------------+-----------------------------+
   | Trk_ReadFace   | 0x00423240 | 58 bytes, version < 6 only  |
   +----------------+------------+-----------------------------+
   | AnimDecor_Load | 0x0048b7e0 | the optional trailing block |
   +----------------+------------+-----------------------------+

1.  Overall shape

   "TRK\0"              4
   version              4      signed; all shipped tracks are 5
   segmentCount         4

   segment[segmentCount]
   meshCount            4
   mesh[meshCount]

   if version > 0:
       namedCount       4
       named[namedCount]
       instanceCount    4
       instance[instanceCount]

   if version > 1:
       linkCount        4
       link[linkCount]

   if version > 2:
       two dwords
       optional AnimDecor block

   The loader is written against versions 0 to 5 with separate guards at
   each step, so it can still read older tracks. Only version 5 ships.

2.  Segment

   Fixed 132 bytes, plus a variable index array from version 1 on.

   12   vec3
   48   4x3 matrix; the first segment's is the identity
    4   dword
   64   four planes, 16 bytes each
    4   dword
   if version > 0:
        4   index count n
      n*2   n u16 indices
   if version > 3:
        8   two dwords

   In memory a segment occupies 0xfc, 252 bytes.

2.1.  The first decoded segment fields

   Section 10 has carried "the field meanings inside a segment are
   not decoded" since this document was written. Trk_SurfaceAtPoint
   (0x0040ec60, was FUN_0040ec60) decodes seven of them, and it is
   nineteen lines long [verified]:

       seg = g_pTrkSegments + segment * 0xfc;
       n   = (float *)(seg + 0x0c);          matrix row 0
       t   = n + 9;                          matrix row 3

       d = n[0]*(x - seg[0x00]) + n[1]*(y - seg[0x04])
         + n[2]*(z - seg[0x08]) - dot(n, t);

       return (seg->f0 < d && d < seg->f4) ? seg->e4 : seg->e8;

   +------------+--------------+------------------------------------------------+
   | Offset     | Type         | Role                                           |
   +============+==============+================================================+
   | +0x00      | float[3]     | the segment origin - the leading vec3 of       |
   |            |              | section 2                                      |
   +------------+--------------+------------------------------------------------+
   | +0x0c      | float[3]     | ROW 0 of the 4x3 matrix, used as a DIRECTION   |
   +------------+--------------+------------------------------------------------+
   | +0x30      | float[3]     | ROW 3 of the 4x3 matrix, used as a TRANSLATION |
   +------------+--------------+------------------------------------------------+
   | +0xe4      | int          | surface id INSIDE the band                     |
   +------------+--------------+------------------------------------------------+
   | +0xe8      | int          | surface id OUTSIDE the band                    |
   +------------+--------------+------------------------------------------------+
   | +0xf0      | float        | low bound of the band                          |
   +------------+--------------+------------------------------------------------+
   | +0xf4      | float        | high bound of the band                         |
   +------------+--------------+------------------------------------------------+

   o d IS A SIGNED DISTANCE ALONG ROW 0, from the segment origin and
     offset by the matrix translation, tested against a low and a
     high bound. Two surface ids either side of that band reads as
     ROAD versus VERGE. The arithmetic is verified; the road/verge
     reading is [inferred].

   o THE PER-SEGMENT 4x3 MATRIX IS A REAL FRAME. Section 10's last
     open question asks "whether the per-segment 4x3 matrix is a
     placement transform, which would affect prop positions". This
     does not answer that in full, but it settles that the matrix is
     LIVE GEOMETRY rather than inert data: row 0 is consumed as a
     direction and row 3 as a translation, by the physics, every
     frame, for every racer.

   o THE FOUR PLANES ARE NOT READ BY THIS FUNCTION. An earlier draft
     of this bullet concluded from that that the planes are not the
     road surface. THEY ARE - see 2.3. Trk_SurfaceAtPoint returns a
     surface ID and has no reason to touch them; Trk_GroundHeightAt
     is the reader, and it uses three of the four.

   o THE FIELDS ABOVE +0x84 ARE NOT IN THE 132-BYTE FIXED PART.
     +0xe4 and +0xe8 come from the second per-segment section - see
     5 - and are read from the file, not built.

   o +0xf0 AND +0xf4 ARE THE LAST TWO WORDS OF THE SEGMENT RECORD -
     ANSWERED, and the loader was already reading them.  They are the
     version > 3 pair this document and src/trk.c both carried as an
     unnamed `tail`; nothing had looked at the values.

     THEY ARE FLOATS, not words.  Read live on ile01 they are
     -1024.0 and +1024.0 on every segment.

     HOW IT WAS SETTLED, because a fixed-stride assumption is what
     hid it: the per-segment geometry records are NOT a fixed length.
     Correlating each segment's live origin against ile01.trk gives
     record starts 12, 158, 304, 450, 596, 740, ... - strides that
     alternate 146 and 144, because the record carries a
     count-prefixed index list.  The band bounds are the LAST EIGHT
     BYTES of each record whatever its length, confirmed across 23
     consecutive segments.  Testing at a FIXED offset of 138 matches
     the first four segments and then silently drifts.

     NO INSTRUCTION WRITES THEM.  The immediate -1024.0f occurs ONCE
     in the whole image, at 0x004d0318 in .rdata, and its only reader
     is a mesh builder.  That is what rules out a load-time default
     and points at the file.

2.2.  The reader, and the two surface banks

   maybe_Racer_SurfaceUnder (0x00465d20, was FUN_00465d20) is the
   racer-side wrapper, and the whole of it is:

       int r = 0;
       if (g_nTrkSegmentCount != 0)
           r = Trk_SurfaceAtPoint(this->segmentStart, p[0], p[1], p[2]);
       if (bAlt) r += 9;
       return r;

   o THE FLAG ADDS NINE, which selects a parallel id range.
     formats/bpt-carac.txt section 5 records the grip table as NINE
     named surfaces - neutral, deep water, water, sand, grass, earth,
     tarmac, oil and roadside - so a bank of nine and an offset of
     nine fit each other exactly. [inferred]

   o AND WITH NO TRACK LOADED IT RETURNS 0, or 9 with the flag. So
     "neutral" is what the code falls back to, not an error value.

   o maybe_Snd_TickRacer CALLS IT WITH THE FLAG SET and then folds
     three of the results together - 9 to 6, 0xb to 5, 0xc to 4 -
     before choosing a roll loop. The audio classes are therefore
     coarser than the surface ids. See subsystems/audio-cues.txt 9.5.

   o g_pTrkSegments AND g_nTrkSegmentCount WERE IDENTIFIED IN
     subsystems/game-object.txt AND NOT NAMED IN THE DB. Both are
     named in Ghidra now.

2.3.  The four planes ARE the road surface

   Section 10 guessed that the four planes are "probably the
   segment's bounding volume or its road surface". Section 2.1 of
   this document then said the road-surface half of that guess had
   no support. THAT WAS WRONG, and it was wrong because 2.1 was
   written from Trk_SurfaceAtPoint alone - the function that returns
   a surface ID, which genuinely does not read the planes.

   Trk_GroundHeightAt (0x0040c8b0, was FUN_0040c8b0) does, and it is
   the segment's ground-height solver [verified]:

       if (trk->segCount <= 0) return 0.0f;
       seg = trk->segments + segIndex * 0xfc;
       if (Plane_Distance(seg + 0xa0, p) >= 0.0f)
            { pl = seg + 0x80; d = *(float *)(seg + 0x8c); }
       else { pl = seg + 0x90; d = *(float *)(seg + 0x9c); }
       if ((bits(pl->y) & 0x7fffffff) == 0) return 0.0f;
       return -((pl->x * p->x + pl->z * p->z) + d) / pl->y;

   That is the standard plane-height solve: for
   a*x + b*y + c*z + d = 0, y = -(a*x + c*z + d) / b.

   +--------------+------------------------------------------------------------+
   | Memory       | Role                                                       |
   +==============+============================================================+
   | +0x80        | GROUND PLANE A - used when the point is on the positive    |
   |              | side of the selector                                       |
   +--------------+------------------------------------------------------------+
   | +0x90        | GROUND PLANE B - used on the negative side                 |
   +--------------+------------------------------------------------------------+
   | +0xa0        | THE SELECTOR - Plane_Distance against this picks A or B    |
   +--------------+------------------------------------------------------------+
   | +0xb0        | NOT A PLANE - the racing line offset, section 10           |
   +--------------+------------------------------------------------------------+

   o SO A SEGMENT'S ROAD SURFACE IS TWO PLANES SPLIT BY A THIRD,
     which is what a quad divided into two triangles looks like. The
     selector is the diagonal.

   o THREE BUILT PLANES AT +0x80..+0xaf, AND THE FILE'S FOUR AT
     +0x40..+0x7f. Section 2 gives the file's at offset 64 of a
     132-byte record, and 64 bytes of header precede them in memory
     too, so those do NOT move. What is new in memory is the ground
     pair and the selector, which the loader computes.

     +0xb0 IS NOT A FOURTH PLANE. It is the record's last dword, and
     it is the racing line - section 10. An earlier version of this
     note ran the plane block to +0xbf and that was wrong.

   o Plane_Distance IS AT 0x004b10c0 - thirteen instructions,
     dot(plane.xyz, p) + plane.d, seven callers, and ANOTHER GAME
     FUNCTION ABOVE THE 0x004b0000 CLIFF. See
     subsystems/copy-protection.txt 5.3.2.

2.4.  The track object, and a pair that is really one thing

   Trk_GroundHeightAt is __thiscall and its ECX is the TRACK OBJECT.
   Its first two fields are

       +0x00   the segment array base
       +0x04   the segment count

   which are exactly g_pTrkSegments (0x0052c108) and
   g_nTrkSegmentCount (0x0052c10c).

   o SO THOSE TWO ARE NOT INDEPENDENT GLOBALS. They are fields 0 and
     1 of one object at 0x0052c108, which is why every function that
     touches one touches the other. subsystems/game-object.txt
     identified them as a pair from the outside; this says why.

   o AND IT SETTLES AN ARGUMENT-TYPE QUESTION THAT WAS NEVER REAL.
     subsystems/racers.txt 9.16.2 flagged a contradiction: two
     callers appeared to pass different kinds of first argument, and
     the function seemed to dereference it. There was no
     contradiction - ECX is the track, both stack arguments are
     exactly what the callers pass, and the [ECX+4] that looked like
     a pointer dereference was the segment count. racer->segmentStart
     is an index, and so is Trk_FindSegmentForPoint's return.

     THE LESSON IS THE SAME ONE AS 5.3.2's: a hidden this makes a
     decompiled argument list shift by one, and an argument list
     that does not add up is more often a missing prototype than a
     real inconsistency.

3.  Mesh

   4          vertex count
   n * 16     per vertex: vec3 plus one dword
   4          face count
   m * 58     per face

   A face is 58 bytes read as 2, 2, 2, 2, 10, 40.

3.1.  Face colour uses the engine's 16-bit layout

   After reading a face the loader expands the u16 at face+2 into a
   packed RGB888 value:

   c = *(u16 *)(face + 2);
   face[0x3c] = (((c & 0x7c0) >> 6) << 8
               | (c >> 11) << 16
               | (c & 0x1f)) << 3;

   Five bits per channel, shifted left three to fill eight.

   An earlier version of this note called that RGB555. The formula above
   was right but the label was not, and misleadingly so: true RGB555
   puts red at bit 10, whereas this reads red from bit 11 and green from
   bit 6. It is the engine's own 16-bit layout, which is 5-5-5
   positioned to sit on a 565 surface with bit 5 unused. See
   subsystems/pixel-format.txt.

   It is still a genuine hardcoded colour format, unlike the .lez
   palette path and the TGA path, which both derive their shifts at run
   time from the DirectDraw surface. formats/lez.txt records that colour
   handling is parameterised; that is true of the image loaders but not
   of track face colours.

   Verified at instruction level in FUN_00423240: AND CX,0x7c0 for
   green, SHR DX,0xb with AND EAX,0x1f for red, AND 0x1f for blue.

4.  Named templates and instances

   A named entry is a length-prefixed name followed by three meshes. The
   three parallel arrays the loader builds are 0x79, 121 bytes, each.

   An instance is a length-prefixed name, a vec3, three dwords, an index
   selecting which named entry it refers to, and from version 5 one more
   dword. In memory an instance is 0x65, 101 bytes, and its name is
   copied with a 15-character limit.

   So the file separates prop templates from their placements, which is
   why a track with 63 named entries can carry 257 instances.

   Instances named CAMERA are not props. maybe_Trk_CountCameras at
   0x00473240 walks the instance array after loading, compares the first
   six characters of each one's TEMPLATE name - +0x5c, not the instance
   name at +0x04 - against "CAMERA", and counts the matches into
   maybe_g_trkCameraCount, clearing each one's +0x2c as it goes
   [verified].

   So the track's camera positions are authored as ordinary named
   instances and picked out by name at load time. The limit is 256,
   enforced with a message box that names the offending instance.

   CLEARING +0x2c IS WHAT MAKES THEM INVISIBLE - it is the decor draw's
   own enable, tested on the first line of FUN_004022f0. See
   subsystems/decor.txt section 4.1.

   Instance fields that follow from this: +0x38 must be 1 for an
   instance to be considered at all, and +0x5c holds the name copied
   from its template.


5.  Link table - a SECOND PER-SEGMENT SECTION

   One record per entry: three u16, plus four dwords from version 4 on.

   IT IS NOT A LINK TABLE. Trk_Load's loop at 0x0040df49 indexes
   g_pTrkSegments by the SEGMENT stride - i*0xfc, built as ((i<<6)-i)<<2
   - and writes every field straight into the segment [verified]. There
   is no link array. The name is kept here because it is where the file
   format puts the section, not because it describes the contents.

   +--------------+------------+-----------------------------------+
   | File order   | Lands at   | Meaning                           |
   +==============+============+===================================+
   ROW 0 IS worldUp CROSS (row3 + row2), and that is verified rather
   than assumed: across all 788 segments of ile01 the file's row 0
   points the same way as normalise((0,1,0) x (row3 + row2)) - 788
   agreeing, none opposite.

   IT MATTERS FOR REVERSE MODE.  row3 + row2 is the heading a racer
   builds for itself (maybe_Racer_ApplyDrive), and
   maybe_Game_InitRace's reverse arm constructs row0 the same way
   from a NEGATED forward.  So both directions lay row 0 out
   right-handed about their own travel, and the bike's lateral axis
   is the segment's going forwards and MINUS the segment's going
   back.  Anything mixing a bike-frame quantity with a
   segment-frame one has to account for that - see
   subsystems/autopilot.txt 3.1.5.

   | u16          | seg +0x0d8 | gridShort                         |
   +--------------+------------+-----------------------------------+
   | u16          | seg +0x0da | stored as a DWORD, then           |
   |              |            | overwritten from a walked array   |
   |              |            | when +0x0d8 is not -1             |
   +--------------+------------+-----------------------------------+
   | u16          | seg +0x0de |                                   |
   +--------------+------------+-----------------------------------+
   | dword        | seg +0x0ec | unknown                           |
   +--------------+------------+-----------------------------------+
   | dword        | seg +0x0e8 | surfaceOut - outside the band     |
   +--------------+------------+-----------------------------------+
   | dword        | seg +0x0e4 | surfaceIn - inside the band       |
   +--------------+------------+-----------------------------------+
   | dword        | seg +0x0e0 | SHADED - see below                |
   +--------------+------------+-----------------------------------+

   THE FOUR DWORDS ARE READ IN DESCENDING OFFSET ORDER, +0xec first
   and +0xe0 last, at 0x0040dff8..0x0040e08b. Reading them in offset
   order silently swaps the two surface ids with the shade flag.

   +0x0e0 IS THE SHADE FLAG. maybe_Racer_ShadeLevel (0x00465d60) is
   its whole consumer and returns 0x10 when it is set and 0x1f when it
   is clear, so a bike darkens under a bridge. It is strictly 0 or 1
   in every shipped track [verified]:

   +-------------+----------+----------+-------------+
   | Track       | Segments | Shaded   | Links       |
   +=============+==========+==========+=============+
   | ile01       |      788 |       32 | 788         |
   | ile02       |      648 |      154 | 648         |
   | Track03     |      622 |       20 | 622         |
   | ile04       |      782 |      155 | 782         |
   | ile05       |      668 |       40 | 668         |
   | Track06     |      435 |       18 | 435         |
   | ile08       |      919 |      263 | 919         |
   | bercy       |      716 |        3 | 716         |
   | ile10       |     1048 |        8 | 1048        |
   | ile11       |      848 |       15 | 848         |
   +-------------+----------+----------+-------------+

   The link count equals the segment count in all ten tracks [verified].
   That invariant is a useful integrity check: if any earlier field were
   mis-sized the count read here would be nonsense. It is also WHY the
   loop can index segments with its own counter - and the original does
   so with no bounds test, which a malformed file would walk off.

6.  AnimDecor

   Introduced by a 10-byte tag. If the tag does not read "AnimDecor" the
   loader seeks back 10 bytes and the section is simply absent, so it is
   genuinely optional.

   10   tag, "AnimDecor"
    4   subversion
    4   object count
   per object:
       0x20 bytes when subversion == 1, otherwise 0x40
       if subversion > 2 and the object kind is 7:
           4        path count
           4        frames per path
           paths * frames * 12   vec3 per frame

   The loader enforces two limits and aborts the game if either is
   exceeded, with messages in French: at most 63 animated objects,
   and at most 79 PATHS. The second message says frames, but 0x4f is
   tested against the path count read above it; frames per path are
   not bounded. The object kind is bits 16 to 23 of the record's
   first dword.

   grouping settles near four [verified]. And ile04 has an AnimDecor block with

   bytes were 26 before that section was implemented.

   ile10 is the largest circuit on every measure and sits at exactly the
   63-object ceiling for named entries, which suggests the limit was
   reached in production rather than chosen with headroom [guess].

8.  Geometry export and validation

   The structure above was established by walking the loader, which
   proves the file is being *consumed* correctly. It does not prove the
   geometry is being *interpreted* correctly. Exporting a track and
   comparing it against an independent source does.

   Recovering the face layout was the missing step. Trk_ReadFace
   (0x00423240) reads the 58 bytes out of order, so the file layout is
   not the struct layout, and src/prim.h names the struct fields.
   They were pinned by cross-referencing the struct
   offsets against FUN_00420a50, the .map loader, which passes +0x2c,
   +0x2e, +0x30 and +0x32 as the four vertex indices to its triangle and
   quad setup routines and tests +0x34 against -1 to choose between
   them. See formats/mesh-3df-map.txt.

   +-------------+---------------+---------------------------------+
   | File offset | Struct offset | Meaning                         |
   +=============+===============+=================================+
   | 0           | +0x00         | kind, the authored kind         |
   +-------------+---------------+---------------------------------+
   | 2           | +0x38         | flags, five bits used           |
   +-------------+---------------+---------------------------------+
   | 4           | +0x02         | colour, engine 16-bit layout    |
   +-------------+---------------+---------------------------------+
   | 6           | +0x36         | bankIndex, rebased on load      |
   +-------------+---------------+---------------------------------+
   | 8           | +0x2c         | vertex index 0                  |
   +-------------+---------------+---------------------------------+
   | 10          | +0x2e         | vertex index 1                  |
   +-------------+---------------+---------------------------------+
   | 12          | +0x30         | vertex index 2                  |
   +-------------+---------------+---------------------------------+
   | 14          | +0x32         | vertex index 3                  |
   +-------------+---------------+---------------------------------+
   | 16          | +0x34         | quad marker; -1 means triangle  |
   +-------------+---------------+---------------------------------+
   | 18          | +0x04         | 40 bytes of uv and shading data |
   +-------------+---------------+---------------------------------+

   With that, ile01.trk exports to 197 meshes, 8738 vertices and 11408
   faces, with no face skipped: every index resolves inside its own
   mesh's vertex array [verified].

   The bounds corroborate the axis convention. X and Z each span about
   117000 units while Y spans about 24000, so the track is broad and
   shallow and Y is vertical.

8.1.  Cross-validation against the minimap

   The decisive check is that MAP.LEZ, the in-game minimap for the same
   circuit, decodes through a completely separate path: an LZ77 stream
   wrapping a TGA, described in formats/lez.txt, with no code or
   assumptions shared with the .trk reader.

   Rendered side by side, the exported geometry and the minimap show the
   same circuit [verified]: a serpentine layout with three hairpins, a
   narrower left loop and a wider right loop joined at the top, and the
   start line on the bottom straight where the minimap draws its arrow.

   Two independent decoders agreeing on the same shape is strong
   evidence that both are right. It is the same technique that validated
   the .bkf reader against the pre-extracted tree, and it is worth
   reaching for whenever a second representation of the same data
   exists.

   The export also carries detail the minimap does not: banked corners
   built from radial fans at the hairpins, rectangular grandstand or
   barrier blocks along the straights, and a large flat paddock area off
   the top left of the circuit.

8.2.  What the export does not do

   Meshes are written as stored, with no segment transform applied.
   Each of the 197 meshes keeps its own vertex array in the file and
   indices are rebased per mesh on output.

   That the result forms a coherent circuit implies the meshes are
   already in world space rather than in a per-segment local space. That
   is an inference from the picture, not something read out of the code
   [inferred]. Each segment record does carry a 4x3 matrix and it
   clearly means something; if it turns out to be a placement transform
   then some props may be positioned wrongly even though the track
   surface reads correctly. Treat prop placement as unverified.

8.3.  The reconstruction

   src/trk.h has the structures and src/trk.c the loader, one
   section per heading above. page01_enter calls it for the
   selected track, taking the file name from the level manifest's
   FileLand rather than building it from the island number - three
   of the eight are not ileNN.trk.

   THE PARSE IS BYTE-EXACT OR IT FAILS, the same rule tools/trk.py
   applies. That matters more for a binary format than for a text
   one: a wrong field width still parses and yields plausible
   garbage geometry, so it is nearly unfalsifiable on its own - but
   a wrong width that must ALSO land on the final byte very rarely
   survives. The loader logs consumed-of-total when it does not.

   AND SECTION 7 IS A KNOWN-ANSWER TEST. The table above was
   produced by the reference reader for all ten tracks, so the
   counts trk_log prints can be read straight against it with no
   new tooling. ile01 must say 788 segments, 197 meshes, 8738
   vertices, 11408 faces, 49 named, 112 instances and 42 anim.

   IT DOES, MEASURED. All seven counts match, the parse landed
   exactly on the last byte of 1.3 MB, and links came back 788 -
   equal to the segment count, which is section 5's invariant and
   an INDEPENDENT check, because the link table is read after the
   meshes, the named templates and the instances. A wrong field
   width anywhere earlier would have made that count nonsense.

   The assets line on the same run read "110 from archives, 3
   loose, 0 from the pre-extracted tree, 0 missing", so both the
   manifest and the track resolve the way a stock install would:
   LEVEL.BPT out of ILE01.BKF, ile01.trk from the loose
   DATA\ile01\ path. CLAUDE.md rule 10.

   o GETTING THERE TOOK ONE WRONG TURN WORTH RECORDING. The first
     run failed with "cannot find ILE01\LEVEL.SPT". bpt_open does
     NOT rewrite the extension - bpt.h says so in capitals and
     tells callers to name the file they want - but level.h had
     claimed it did, copied from tools/bpt.py's docstring, which
     describes the ORIGINAL's Script_Open. A true statement about
     the game asserted about the port. Callers pass Level.Bpt.

   Deliberate differences from the original:

   o COUNTS ARE BOUNDED. The original trusts the file; a corrupt
     count there allocates whatever it says. Ours refuses anything
     over 2^22, and keeps the loader's own AnimDecor limits of 63
     objects and 79 paths - but fails the load where the original
     puts up a French message box and aborts the game.

   o VERSION 6 IS REFUSED RATHER THAN GUESSED. Trk_ReadFace is
     gated on version < 6 and nothing establishes what 6 changes,
     so reading 58 bytes that may not be 58 bytes is not attempted.
     No shipped track is affected; all ten are version 5.

   o ANIMDECOR IS LOCATED AND SKIPPED, not parsed into a structure.
     It is skipped EXACTLY, so the byte-exact check still covers
     it, and animDecorObjects is recorded. Nothing needs the
     animation data until something draws scenery.

   NOT REPRODUCED: nothing consumes a segment or a mesh yet. The
   loader is inert on purpose, the same way level.c is - the point
   of the byte-exact check is that it can be trusted before there
   is any geometry on screen to judge it by.


8.4.  THE TRACK DOES NOT FIT IN THE QUEUE, and it never will

   ile01 alone holds 11408 world faces across 197 meshes, plus 112
   prop instances drawn from 49 named templates, plus whatever the
   racers cost. QUEUE_NODE_MAX is 7499 - the ORIGINAL's constant,
   not ours. So under 60 per cent of one track's static geometry can
   be queued at once even with nothing else in the scene.

   THAT IS NOT A DEFECT, it is the absence of a subsystem - but not
   the subsystem this section used to name. It said the visibility
   system was "LenBPlane, MediumZone, HightZone and the per-segment
   index arrays". IT IS NONE OF THOSE. The per-segment index arrays
   at +0xb4 with their count at +0xd4 are the DECOR lists, and
   MediumZone and HightZone are per-detail-level distances at
   0x0051d1c8 and 0x0051d1cc, not a visibility set.

   THE REAL SYSTEM IS A ONE-DIMENSIONAL RING WINDOW, and it is much
   simpler than any of that. maybe_Trk_DrawZonesAndListSegments
   (0x0040ce60) draws the mesh covering the camera segment plus a
   fixed number of meshes each way along the track:

       forward + backward + 1  meshes, at most 12 of ile01 197

   with the split chosen by one dot product and the span a hard-coded
   10 from the Track constructor at 0x0040c5b4. There is no bounding
   volume, no tree, no precomputed visibility - just the assumption
   that a closed circuit is a loop and you can only see so far along
   it. Section 9.1 has the whole algorithm.

   SO THE QUEUE PRESSURE THIS SECTION DESCRIBES IS AN ARTEFACT OF
   OUR OWN SUBSTITUTE. Twelve meshes is roughly 700 faces against
   QUEUE_NODE_MAX 7499; the problem disappears with the real
   selection rather than needing a budget. src/trk.c still uses
   "nearest first until the queue is full" for the geometry, and
   that is now the only part left to replace - the segment LIST half
   is reconstructed as trk_list_visible_segments.

   WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE FROM THE FRONT. Nearest-first is exactly the
   wrong order for large distant flat things, so the far ocean is
   among the first casualties [observed, the start grid on ile01].
   With the horizon in place the hole is now obvious: what shows
   through where the sea should be is the horizon's GROUND FILL, so
   it reads as the sky bleeding down over the water rather than as
   missing geometry. Worth knowing before someone goes looking for
   a bug in horizon.c.

   o A COUNTING TRAP THAT HID THIS. trk_draw returns props PLUS
     world meshes as one number, and page 1 logged it against
     meshCount alone. 76 of 197 world meshes drawn therefore printed
     as "188 of 197" once the 112 props were folded in - 39 per cent
     reading as 95. The two are now counted and logged separately.

8.5.  DRAW DISTANCE IS A SETTING

   The manifest's LenBPlane is 18000 on ile01 and the original clips
   there, which is why scenery pops in as you approach.

8.5.0.  MEASURED AGAINST MOTO ON THE DIRT ARENA

   Read live, both sides on the same track:

       MOTO  near 64      DAT_00625788 10000   DAT_0062e438 2000
             DAT_006d1f70 8000      <- the live LenBPlane
       ours  near 64      g_drawSceneZFar 40000
             s_race_backPlane 7000

   THREE SEPARATE THINGS MAKE OURS SHORTER, and only one of them is
   the back plane.

   o THE DETAIL LEVEL PICKS THE ZONE RECORD AND WE IGNORE IT.
     Race_SetupRenderState (0x00410e10) does

         DAT_006d1f70 = DAT_0051d1c0[g_cfgDetailLevel * 9]

     - a table of five nine-key records, selected by the config's
     detail level.  MOTO is running at level 4 and gets 8000; the
     port hardcodes index 0 and gets 7000.  The port already HAS
     g_cfgDetailLevel (fx_draw_all_particles reads it); it simply is
     not used for these keys.

   o AND THE KEY ORDER DOES NOT AGREE. Column 0 of MOTO's table runs
     4000, 3000, 4000, 4000, 8000 across the five levels, and that is
     exactly what OUR reader calls flatZ; our lenBPlane reads 7000 at
     all five.  One of the two labellings is wrong and it has to be
     settled from the .spt text before anything is wired to it.
     [open]

   o THE REAL DIFFERENCE IS NOT A DISTANCE AT ALL. Section 8.4: the
     original's visibility is a RING WINDOW - the camera segment plus
     ten meshes each way - with no bounding volume and no budget.
     trk_draw still uses "nearest first until the queue is full",
     and nearest-first is exactly the wrong order for large distant
     flat things, so they are the first casualties and they come and
     go as the near count changes.  THAT is what reads as harsh
     popping rather than as a clean far clip.

     trk_list_visible_segments was already reconstructed and already
     fed race_draw_racers; trk_draw is converted now.

8.5.1.  The ring window, in trk_draw

   trk_draw takes camSegment and camForward and selects world meshes
   the way 0x0040ce60 does: the camera's mesh, plus a span each way
   along the track, and nothing else.  trk_zone_span holds the split
   rule - one dot product against the camera segment's first plane
   normal gives 10 ahead and 1 behind, or 5 and 5, or 1 and 10.

   A MESH COVERS FOUR SEGMENTS.  716 segments to 179 meshes on the
   dirt arena and 788 to 197 on ile01, exact on both - which is what
   the *4 in trk_list_visible_segments was already encoding.  The
   mesh index is camSegment / 4.

   THE DISTANCE SORT IS STILL THERE, as the fallback for a caller
   with no track position - page 10's preview - and the window is
   still sorted nearest-first inside itself, which costs nothing and
   suits the queue.

   THE SETTING IS REDEFINED and the three values are now genuinely
   different:

   +----------------------+--------------------------------------+
   | [RENDER] drawdistance | Effect                              |
   +======================+======================================+
   | 0, or absent         | the ring window alone - retail       |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------+
   | -1                   | NO window: every mesh, nearest       |
   |                      | first, no clip. A debug view for a   |
   |                      | flyover, NOT what retail does        |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------+
   | N > 0                | the window AND a clip at N           |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------+

   THE BACK PLANE IS NO LONGER THE SELECTION.  It was standing in for
   a rule it was never the whole of, and the default is now no
   distance test at all - which is what the original does.

   +----------------------+--------------------------------------+
   | [RENDER] drawdistance | Effect                              |
   +======================+======================================+
   | 0, or absent         | the manifest's LenBPlane - the       |
   |                      | DEFAULT, and what the original does  |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------+
   | -1                   | unlimited, no far clip               |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------+
   | N > 0                | N world units                        |
   +----------------------+--------------------------------------+

   Drawing further than the original is a deliberate deviation and
   the user's decision rather than a reconstruction one, which is
   why it is opt-in and the retail value is the default.

   o UNLIMITED DOES NOT CURRENTLY WORK, and the reason is worth
     knowing before anyone tries it again. queue.c buckets a face by
     its INTEGER camera depth and clamps at 7998, so everything
     beyond 7998 units shares one bucket with no ordering between
     its members. Retail never notices because it clips at 18000.
     Remove the clip and the enormous far meshes - 106 to 117 are 54
     faces each with extents up to 43771 units at 51000 to 58000
     out - land unordered in that bucket and paint over the frame.
     Scaling the buckets with the draw distance was tried and did
     not fix it; the next suspect is drawscene.c's rule that turns
     depth WRITES off for blended faces.

   o AND THE CLIP WAS ADDED AS A REGRESSION IN THE FIRST PLACE. It
     did not exist, was introduced on the reasoning that the
     original clips there, and took the scene from 76 world meshes
     to 29 - removing the distant palms and the offshore boats -
     while the primitive budget sat at 596 of 6800. Several rebuild
     cycles were then spent looking for a rendering fault that was
     this clip. Check whether a limit is doing anything before
     adding one.

   o THE MEANING OF -1 CHANGED TWICE while this was being settled,
     at one point to a value where -1 and ABSENT were both
     unlimited - so the two settings anyone would compare first were
     the same setting. The table above is final.


9.  Reference reader

   tools/trk.py.

   python tools/trk.py --check PATH [PATH ...]
   python tools/trk.py --info  PATH

   --check walks the file exactly as the loader does and reports the

10.  Open questions

   o PARTLY RESOLVED. Seven segment fields are decoded in section
     2.1 - the origin, two rows of the 4x3 matrix, two surface ids
     and a low/high band - by Trk_SurfaceAtPoint. THREE OF THE FOUR
     PLANES are decoded in 2.3 by Trk_GroundHeightAt: two ground
     planes and a selector between them, so the "road surface" half
     of the guess is CORRECT. The fourth plane at +0xb0 and the u16
     index array are still unconfirmed.
   o PARTLY RESOLVED. The four vertex indices and the triangle/quad
     marker are now identified, at file offsets 8 to 16. The 40-byte
     block at struct +0x04 is still undecoded and is presumably uv
     coordinates and shading data.
   o THE BIKE DRAWS SUNK INTO THE GROUND OFF THE SIDE OF THE ROAD,
     at one spot on SPEED_BAY, and the BIKE IS NOT THE CAUSE.
     Driving the same lap on both games and sampling every frame,
     the racer's position.y, the matBikeRoot translation, both wheel
     contact heights and riderPosition.y all agree with MOTO to
     0.02 units, with a single 0.76 transient - so the collision
     surface and everything hung off it are right, and what differs
     is the DRAWN ground beside the road.  A placement sweep across
     the rail line at z = 2058 also came back identical, so the
     stretch to look at is not that one.  The suspects are the
     apron geometry in the segment mesh and the Y placement of the
     MR_TrkInstance props that carry it (section 4).  [open]
   o RESOLVED, AND IT IS NOT A PLANE.  +0xb0 is the THE RACING LINE:
     a per-segment lateral offset along matrix row 0, in world
     units.  Racer_AiFollowLine adds the rider's chargeMeter to it
     and aims there; Trk_LaneOfRacer centres its three 240-wide lane
     bands on it.

     IT IS THE DWORD THE FILE STORES AFTER THE FOUR PLANES, read as
     a float - the last field of the 132-byte record, section 2.
     ile01 spans -432 to +128 over 367 distinct values and varies
     smoothly segment to segment; ile04 spans -368 to +112 with
     plateaus through its constant-radius corners.  Both sit well
     inside the +-1024 band, which is what a racing line should do.

     THIS ENTRY USED TO SAY "the fourth plane's FIRST float", and
     that reading cost two functions in the port.  The error is in
     the sentence below about +0x80..+0xbf: the FILE's four planes
     land at +0x40..+0x7f, the three at +0x80..+0xaf are BUILT by
     the loader, and +0xb0 is this dword - not a fourth built plane.
     Reading planes[3][0] instead gives wallPlane1's normal x, a
     flat -1.0 on ile01 with the wall distance 1280 beside it, so
     the "line" it produced was the centreline give or take a unit.
   o The three meshes per named entry are presumably levels of detail
     [guess].
   o The two dwords before AnimDecor are unread by this parser and their
     meaning is unknown.
   o The 0x20 versus 0x40 animated-object record is not decoded beyond
     the kind field.
   o RESOLVED. ile01 exports cleanly and matches its own minimap; see
     section 8. Still open is whether the per-segment 4x3 matrix is a
     placement transform, which would affect prop positions.
