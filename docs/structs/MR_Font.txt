MotoRacer PC Reverse Engineering                           Analysis Note


                                MR_FONT


Abstract

   Size: 0x54a6 [verified] - PUSH 0x54a6 into operator new at six sites
   in maybe_Font_LoadAll, each followed by Font_Ctor.

   Allocated at: 0x0042698c and five sibling sites, all in
   maybe_Font_LoadAll (0x00426460). Freed in FUN_00473540, which then
   clears g_fontTable. Ghidra type applied: yes. Belongs to: the font
   module at 0x00490000-0x00497000.

   A bitmap font. It owns up to four source faces, a texture atlas built
   from their glyphs, and a prebuilt drawing descriptor per character.
   Fonts live in g_fontTable at 0x004f1810 and are reached by id through
   Font_Get.

   There are eight configured font types across six classes. See section
   4.

2.  The three per-character tables

   Three 256-entry arrays are indexed by character code, and
   Font_Rebuild writes all three in one pass [verified]:

   +--------+-------------+--------------------------------------------------+
   | Offset | Name        | Holds                                            |
   +========+=============+==================================================+
   | 0x0018 | glyphPixels | pointer to the glyph's pixels in the face bitmap |
   +--------+-------------+--------------------------------------------------+
   | 0x0618 | glyphWidth  | advance width; zero means absent                 |
   +--------+-------------+--------------------------------------------------+
   | 0x0a40 | glyphAtlasX | x position within the atlas                      |
   +--------+-------------+--------------------------------------------------+
   | 0x0e40 | glyphAtlasY | y position, (page - 1) * glyphHeight             |
   +--------+-------------+--------------------------------------------------+

   Font_Rebuild reaches them through one moving pointer. It sets piVar7
   to glyphWidth + character, then addresses the others as
   piVar7[-0x180], piVar7[0x10a] and piVar7[0x20a] - offsets of -0x600,
   +0x428 and +0x828 bytes, which land exactly on the other three
   tables. That is what makes the four array bases certain.

   A fourth 0x200-byte region at 0x0418 sits between glyphPixels and
   glyphWidth and is never touched by anything read so far.

2.1.  colorLut, and it is the whole design

   The 0x200 bytes at 0x0418 sat between two per-character tables and
   this note had them as "never touched by anything read so far", with
   section 7 guessing a third per-character table.

   IT IS NOT PER CHARACTER. It is 256 RGB565 entries INDEXED BY THE
   GLYPH'S PIXEL BYTE, which every slot-6 blitter reads as
   lut[*glyphPixel] [verified from two of them independently].

   Font_BuildColorLut (0x00490b90, slot 1, was Font_BuildCharRange)
   fills it. It walks a 32-entry gradient from backColor to foreColor
   in 16.16 fixed point,

       step = ((fore - back) << 16) >> 5      per channel

   packs each through Color_Pack888ToSurface, and then writes
   lut[i] = ramp32[i & 0x1f] over the requested index range.

   o SO SLOT 1'S RANGE ARGUMENT IS A LUT INDEX RANGE, NOT A CHARACTER
     RANGE, even though slot 0 passes (1, 0xff) and the obvious
     reading is "every character". Nothing about it is per character -
     the same 32 colours repeat eight times across the 256 entries.
     The old name Font_BuildCharRange encoded that misreading.

   THE PIXEL BYTE THEREFORE SPLITS IN TWO:

       bits 0..4   which of 32 gradient steps       b & 0x1f
       bits 5..7   the blend level, 0 = opaque      b >> 5

   which is what makes AntiAlias8 work with one byte per pixel, and
   what the shipped faces show: HIRES\MSG.FNT uses exactly
   0, 31, 63, ... 255, that is 31 | (level << 5).

   The face supplies pixels and the FONT supplies colour. That is why
   one face can be drawn in several colours without touching it, and
   why slot 0 has to call slot 1 when a colour changes.


2.2.  The eight blitters, all read

   Slot 6 has eight distinct implementations and every one has now
   been read. They fall into four behaviours, not eight [verified]:

   +----------------------+------------+------------------------------+
   | Class                | Address    | Slot 6 does                  |
   +======================+============+==============================+
   | Direct               | 0x00493100 | lut[b], opaque               |
   +----------------------+------------+------------------------------+
   | Pal                  | 0x00495840 | THE SAME, byte for byte in   |
   |                      |            | behaviour                    |
   +----------------------+------------+------------------------------+
   | Moto                 | 0x00495d50 | THE SAME again               |
   +----------------------+------------+------------------------------+
   | DirectTrame          | 0x00492d20 | lut[b] through a halftone    |
   +----------------------+------------+------------------------------+
   | CustomColor          | 0x00494170 | gradient computed per pixel  |
   +----------------------+------------+------------------------------+
   | CustomColorTrame     | 0x00494590 | the same, through a halftone |
   +----------------------+------------+------------------------------+
   | AntiAlias8           | 0x00494bb0 | lut[b] blended, /8 tables    |
   +----------------------+------------+------------------------------+
   | AntiAlias8Transp     | 0x00495280 | lut[b] blended, /7 explicit  |
   +----------------------+------------+------------------------------+

   o THREE OF THEM ARE ONE ROUTINE COMPILED THREE TIMES. Direct, Pal
     and Moto are all 898 bytes; 113 and 251 bytes differ from the
     base respectively and EVERY difference is register allocation.
     Pal and Moto specialise entirely in slot 1, so their blitters had
     nothing to change. A byte-level difference between two of these
     does not imply a behavioural one.

   o CustomColor DOES NOT USE THE LUT. It computes
     (fore*step + back*(31-step))/31 per pixel from b & 0x1f. THAT IS
     WHY ITS SLOT 0 AND SLOT 1 ARE BARE RETURNS - there is no table to
     maintain, so a colour change takes effect on the next glyph with
     no rebuild. The three-byte RET 0x8 overrides are the point of the
     class, not stubs.

   o THE TWO SHADED CLASSES GENUINELY DIFFER. AntiAlias8 blends
     through the seven (i*n)/8 tables; AntiAlias8Transp does the same
     blend with explicit arithmetic and an alpha of level/7:

         alpha = bias + (b > 0x1f ? (b & 0xe0) : 0);
         if (alpha < 0xe1) {
             alpha = (alpha << 8) / 0xe0;
             out   = src - ((src - dst) * alpha >> 8);
         }

     So level 0 is opaque and level 7 is FULLY TRANSPARENT. The /8
     tables can never reach zero ink and this can - which is what
     "Transp" means, and it also confirms independently that rising
     level means rising transparency.

2.3.  The five LUT builders

   Slot 1 has five implementations and they are five ways of filling
   the same table [verified]:

   +-------------+------------+-------------------------------------+
   | Class       | Address    | Fills                               |
   +=============+============+=====================================+
   | base        | 0x00490b90 | lut[i] = ramp32[i & 0x1f], a        |
   |             |            | back->fore gradient                 |
   +-------------+------------+-------------------------------------+
   | AntiAlias8  | 0x00494b90 | forwards to the base, 18 bytes      |
   +-------------+------------+-------------------------------------+
   | Pal         | 0x00495bd0 | lut[i] = palette[i % paletteCount]  |
   +-------------+------------+-------------------------------------+
   | Moto        | 0x004960e0 | TWO ZONES - see below               |
   +-------------+------------+-------------------------------------+
   | CustomColor | 0x00494150 | nothing, a bare RET 0x8             |
   +-------------+------------+-------------------------------------+

   MOTO IS THE ONLY HYBRID. It fills lut[1..14] with a FOURTEEN-step
   gradient, (fore*k + back*(14-k))/14, and lut[32..62] from the face
   palette, leaving everything else alone. It also IGNORES ITS RANGE
   ARGUMENTS entirely - the zones are hardcoded - so slot 0 calling it
   with (1, 0xff) rebuilds exactly those two zones regardless.

   Fourteen steps matches the data: the MOTO faces hold pixel values
   0..15, which land in the gradient zone.

   o EVERY BUILDER SHARES A STORE IDIOM worth recognising: the index
     is incremented BEFORE the store and the base is written as
     0x416, so 0x416 + (i+1)*2 lands on lut[i]. Reading the store
     address literally puts every table one entry out.


3.  Faces and the atlas

   A font is assembled from up to four source files. The face path
   records are 0x81 bytes each and the array is exactly 0x204 bytes
   wide, which is four of them - the count and the space agree
   [verified].

   Font_Rebuild picks a loader by file extension, comparing the first
   three characters against two strings and dispatching through vtable
   slot +0xc or +0x14. An unrecognised extension throws a C++ exception
   rather than failing quietly.

   Glyphs are then packed left to right into the atlas. When the next
   glyph would exceed atlasWidth the row is closed, atlasPages is
   incremented and packing continues at x = 0. Afterwards each
   character's MR_FontGlyph is filled by dividing its pixel coordinates
   by the atlas dimensions, giving normalised texture coordinates - so
   the descriptors are texture-ready and the draw path does no per-glyph
   arithmetic.

4.  The class family

   Fonts are declared in configuration, not in code. Font_LoadAll reads
   the keys FontNumber, FontTypes, FontSpacings, FontForeColors,
   FontBackColors and FontData, and for each declared font compares its
   type name against eight known names [verified]:

   +------------------+--------+---------------------------+-------------+------------+
   | Type name        | Size   | Constructor               | kind chain  | vtable     |
   +==================+========+===========================+=============+============+
   | Direct           | 0x54a6 | Font_Ctor                 | 1           | 0x004d1e68 |
   +------------------+--------+---------------------------+-------------+------------+
   | DirectTrame      | 0x54a6 | Font_Ctor, vtable swapped | 1           | 0x004d0710 |
   +------------------+--------+---------------------------+-------------+------------+
   | CustomColor      | 0x54a6 | FontCustomColor_Ctor      | 1 -> 3      | 0x004d1e88 |
   +------------------+--------+---------------------------+-------------+------------+
   | CustomColorTrame | 0x54a6 | the same, vtable swapped  | 1 -> 3      | 0x004d06f0 |
   +------------------+--------+---------------------------+-------------+------------+
   | AntiAlias8       | 0x70a6 | FontShaded_Ctor           | 1 -> 5      | 0x004d1ea8 |
   +------------------+--------+---------------------------+-------------+------------+
   | AntiAlias8Transp | 0x70a6 | FontShadedTransp_Ctor     | 1 -> 5 -> 6 | 0x004d1ec8 |
   +------------------+--------+---------------------------+-------------+------------+
   | Pal              | 0x54a6 | FontPal_Ctor              | 1 -> 7      | 0x004d1ee8 |
   +------------------+--------+---------------------------+-------------+------------+
   | Moto             | 0x54a6 | FontMoto_Ctor             | 1 -> 7 -> 8 | 0x004d1f08 |
   +------------------+--------+---------------------------+-------------+------------+

   The kind chain is read straight out of each constructor. Every one of
   them begins by running the base constructor body, which installs
   vtable 0x004d1e68 and sets kind to 1, and then overwrites both. Where
   a constructor does that twice, as FontShadedTransp_Ctor and
   FontMoto_Ctor do, the class is two levels deep. That is the ordinary
   C++ derived-constructor pattern and it is what makes the hierarchy
   legible:

       MR_Font (1)
         +-- CustomColor (3)
         +-- AntiAlias8 (5)
         |     +-- AntiAlias8Transp (6)
         +-- Pal (7)
               +-- Moto (8)

   The two Trame variants are not classes at all. Font_LoadAll
   constructs the plain object and then assigns a different vtable over
   the top, in the caller rather than in a constructor. Trame is French
   for a halftone screen, so these are presumably the screened or
   dithered renderings of the same font data [inferred].

   Only two sizes exist. The six classes that fit 0x54a6 differ from
   each other only in vtable and kind; the two AntiAlias8 classes add
   one array at offset 0x54a6, the first byte past the base: seven
   256-entry tables of (i * level) / 8 for level 7 down to 1. Seven
   times 0x400 is 0x1c00, exactly 0x70a6 minus 0x54a6, so they add that
   and nothing else [verified]. Those are precomputed intensity ramps,
   matching Font_DrawTextInternal's special handling of kinds 5 and 6.

   A second pass then assigns each font a usage condition drawn from
   twelve names: GameMsgLowRes, GameMsgHiRes, InGameLowRes, InGameHiRes,
   InGameMenu, InGameMenuLowRes, InGameMenuHiRes, InGameGeneric,
   InGameHelpMenu, OutGame, Obsolete and NoCondition. Several are
   explicitly low-resolution or high-resolution variants of the same
   role, which is the same pairing Font_Get switches between [inferred].

   An unrecognised type name throws a C++ exception rather than falling
   back.

4.1.  The vtable, all seven slots

   All eight vtables have now been read, and the shape README section
   24.3 predicted is exactly right [verified]:

   +------+-------+---------------------------+----------+------------+
   | Slot | Off   | Role                      | Distinct | Base       |
   +======+=======+===========================+==========+============+
   | 0    | +0x00 | set fore/back colour      | 2        | 0x00490b00 |
   +------+-------+---------------------------+----------+------------+
   | 1    | +0x04 | rebuild a character range | 5        | 0x00490b90 |
   +------+-------+---------------------------+----------+------------+
   | 2    | +0x08 | load .FNT face, range     | 1        | 0x00492100 |
   +------+-------+---------------------------+----------+------------+
   | 3    | +0x0c | load .FNT face            | 1        | 0x00491380 |
   +------+-------+---------------------------+----------+------------+
   | 4    | +0x10 | load other face, range    | 1        | 0x00492140 |
   +------+-------+---------------------------+----------+------------+
   | 5    | +0x14 | load other face           | 1        | 0x00492130 |
   +------+-------+---------------------------+----------+------------+
   | 6    | +0x18 | draw one glyph            | 8        | 0x00493100 |
   +------+-------+---------------------------+----------+------------+

   THERE IS NO SLOT 7. +0x1c is already the next object; the value
   there differs between vtables and is not a code address in two of
   them.

   o SLOT 5 IS A STUB IN EVERY CLASS - five bytes, XOR EAX,EAX /
     RET 0x10. It is the loader for a second face extension, and since
     all 33 shipped faces are .FNT, nothing ever called it. Slot 4
     wraps it and is equally dead. A second face format was designed
     and never used; see formats/fnt.txt section 5.

   o SLOTS 2 AND 4 ARE WRAPPERS, identical but for which loader they
     tail-call. Each converts an inclusive (firstChar, lastChar) pair
     into (firstChar, count) and forwards - slot 2 to slot 3, slot 4
     to slot 5.

   SLOT 0 IS WHAT PINNED DOWN foreColor AND backColor. It compares
   three bytes at +0x5494 against its first argument and three at
   +0x5497 against its second, copies each only if it differs, and
   calls slot 1 over (1, 0xff) if EITHER changed - once, not twice.
   Those are the FontForeColors and FontBackColors the configuration
   supplies, and the early-out matters because menu code sets the same
   colour every frame.

   CustomColor and CustomColorTrame override BOTH slot 0 and slot 1
   with three-byte bare returns. They ignore colour changes and never
   rebuild, which is self-consistent: nothing they depend on can
   change.

   o SO IN C THE FAMILY IS THREE FUNCTION POINTERS AND FOUR ORDINARY
     FUNCTIONS. src/font.c implements it, and the four shared slots
     are plain functions there because they are plain functions here -
     virtual only because the base declared them so.

   g_fontTable[i] where i is its index in the configuration list, so the

   The direction is uniform once the ids are named. The branch taken
   when g_cfgSmallFonts is clear and g_cfgScreenMode is 2 maps LowRes to
   HiRes in all twelve cases; the other branch maps HiRes to LowRes.
   Screen mode 2 is 640x480, the highest of the three supported modes,
   so the rule reads plainly: at the highest resolution with small fonts
   turned off, a request for a low-resolution font is answered with its
   high-resolution twin, and otherwise the reverse [verified].

   Three ids the code hardcodes now have names. Font 8, the last-resort
   fallback, is InfosDebug. Font 0x1b, substituted when a kind 5 or 6
   font is unavailable, is 27, Generic - a plain Direct font with the
   InGameGeneric condition, which is a sensible thing to fall back to.

   Id 18, Symbol, carries the condition Obsolete, and it is the only
   CustomColor font in the table. Neither of the two CustomColorTrame or
   DirectTrame types is used by any font, so four of the eight type
   branches in Font_LoadAll are dead in the shipped data [verified].

   The file also carries per-font spacing, foreground and background
   colours, and the face list. The face records confirm the multi-face
   design: font 9, MenuLink, declares two faces,
   Data\Fontes\HiRes\MenuLink.Raw for characters 32 to 95 and
   MenuLin2.Raw for 192 to 211, which is why MR_Font carries four face
   slots with a first character and a count each.

5.1.  The read schema, and why a parser cannot guess it

   A .bpt carries NO TYPE TAGS.  tools/bpt.py guesses - it takes a
   plausible length, trial-decodes, and accepts the string only if
   the bytes come out printable - and its own docstring says why
   that is a dump tool's trick and not the game's: the real reader
   knows only because it calls ReadValue and ReadString in a FIXED
   ORDER.

   So a port reads Fonts by transcribing that order.  Guessing
   desynchronises the moment a small integer looks like a length.

   Font_LoadAll (0x00426460) reads SIX KEYED SECTIONS, rewinding
   before each one.  Every section after the first is a list of
   records that begins with an id and ENDS ON AN ID OF -1.

   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | Key              | Record                                     |
   +==================+============================================+
   | FontNumber       | one int, the count.  Rejected outside      |
   |                  | 0..0x30, and the table is allocated to it  |
   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | FontTypes        | int id, str name, str type, str condition  |
   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | FontSpacings     | int id, int a, int b                       |
   |                  | stored at font+0xa20 and font+0xa1c        |
   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | FontForeColors   | int id, int r, int g, int b                |
   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | FontBackColors   | int id, int r, int g, int b                |
   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | FontData         | int id, int firstChar, int lastChar,       |
   |                  | int kind, str path                         |
   +------------------+--------------------------------------------+

   o THE FACE COUNT IS DERIVED, NOT STORED.  The loader passes
     (lastChar - firstChar) + 1 to the face adder at 0x004929f0,
     which is what settles the second and third ints as a
     CHARACTER RANGE rather than a start and a count.  MenuLink
     reads 32, 95 and then 192, 211 - the two ranges section 5
     already describes.

   o AN ID OUTSIDE 0..count THROWS, in every section.  So does an
     unrecognised type or condition name.  The file is trusted to
     be consistent with its own FontNumber.

   o A STRAY INT SITS BETWEEN THE COUNT AND THE FIRST ID, and
     both read 35 in the shipped file.  Font_LoadAll cannot
     settle which is which: Settings_ReadInt collapses to a
     single read on a compiled .bpt - the key-matching loop is
     skipped entirely and the key name is never compared - so
     the two reads look identical in the disassembly.

     THE FILE SETTLES IT.  Skipping the second value parses all
     4347 bytes to the last one, with every section ending on
     its -1 and every id inside 0..34; not skipping it makes the
     first id 35, which Font_LoadAll would throw on.  src/fonts.c
     detects rather than assumes - an id outside the range is
     consumed and logged.

   o THE SECTIONS ARE SPARSE.  35 fonts, but only 28 spacings, 17
     foreground colours and 20 background ones, and 38 faces over
     35 fonts because some declare two.  A reader must key on the
     id in each record and not assume one record per font.

   o Font_Rebuild RUNS ON EVERY FONT AT THE END, after all six
     sections.  Colours and spacing are therefore set before any
     LUT is built, which is why nothing rebuilds per key.

5.2.  The face paths name a file that is not shipped

   FontData paths read Data\\Fontes\\HiRes\\MenuLink.Raw,
   and FONTES.BKF holds HIRES\\MENULINK.FNT.  The extension in the
   manifest is not the extension on disk for any of the HiRes or
   LowRes faces; only SYMBOL.RAW and DELPHINE\\DEBUG.RAW are
   genuinely .RAW.

   A reader therefore has to strip the Data prefix - rule 10 wants
   a logical name anyway - and swap the extension.  Script_Open
   does the same trick for scripts, uppercasing its argument and
   replacing whatever extension it finds with BPT, so the pattern
   is the file system's convention rather than a special case.
   [inferred - the swap is forced by the data; the instruction
   that performs it has not been read.]

5.3.  Nothing here is localised

   There is no language in any of it.  The paths carry LowRes and
   HiRes and no language directory, and no section is read twice.
   What varies an id is RESOLUTION: Font_Get substitutes between
   the twelve LowRes/HiRes pairs in section 5 on g_cfgSmallFonts
   and the screen mode.  Menu SPRITES are localised; fonts are
   not.

   Six further methods in the 0x00492000-0x00494000 range take a font as
   this and are in the MR_Font class namespace in the database, but have
   not been read: 0x00492b10, 0x00492c20, 0x00492d20, 0x00493100,

6.1.  The blit target block, and a shipped Beep

   Font_DrawTextInternal at 0x00493820 does not blit through DirectDraw.
   It writes pixels directly, through a four-field descriptor in a
   contiguous global block [verified]:

   +------------+------------------+----------------------------+
   | Address    | Holds            | Written by                 |
   +============+==================+============================+
   | 0x004e704c | width            | maybe_Cdd_CreateOffScreen  |
   +------------+------------------+----------------------------+
   | 0x004e7050 | height           | maybe_Cdd_CreateOffScreen  |
   +------------+------------------+----------------------------+
   | 0x004e7054 | stride in pixels | FUN_004236b0               |
   +------------+------------------+----------------------------+
   | 0x004e7058 | the bits pointer | FUN_00423590, FUN_004236b0 |
   +------------+------------------+----------------------------+

   All four are read only by this function. The address arithmetic is:

       addr = bits + (stride * y + x) * 2;

   another hardcoded two bytes per pixel, now recorded in
   subsystems/pixel-format.txt section 1.1.3. It matters because it sits
   outside screen3d\ - a port that converts the rasterizer and forgets
   the font code gets a correct 3D scene with garbled text.

   The same block continues with three dead fields at 0x004e705c,
   0x004e7060 and 0x004e7064 - the tile grid of subsystems/dd-
   present.txt section 6, written once and read by nothing.

6.1.1.  A one-shot Beep on a null target

   If the computed address comes out zero - which happens when the bits
   pointer is null, so nothing has locked the target - the function does
   this [verified]:

       if (addr == 0) {
           if (DAT_004f1830 == 0) {
               DAT_004f1830 = 1;
               Beep(0, 0);
           }
       }

   A developer diagnostic that shipped. The latch at 0x004f1830 is read
   and written only here and never reset, so the beep sounds at most
   once per run. Beep(0, 0) with both arguments zero is the default
   system beep on Windows 95.

   o It is a useful marker rather than a curiosity: if a port ever hears
     it, text is being drawn with no surface locked, and the original
     would have silently skipped that draw after the first warning.

   o The function also has its own surface-loss recovery - on
     DDERR_SURFACELOST it frees the font's texture through
     D3D_FreeBankTextures, clears its handle and calls Font_Rebuild. So
     fonts restore themselves independently of the bank machinery in dd-
     statics.txt.


6.2.  The antialias fallback

   Font_Get carries a substitution that is easy to read past
   [verified, 0x00490549]:

       if (DAT_004f1814 && !DAT_004f1828 && (kind == 5 || kind == 6))
               font = g_fontTable[0x1b];        /* 27, Generic */

   so while that flag is set EVERY AntiAlias8 and AntiAlias8Transp
   font is replaced by Generic - HiRes\Generic.raw.
   Font_DrawTextInternal (0x00493820) carries the matching gate and
   draws NOTHING at all for a kind 5 or 6 font under the same
   condition.

   o DAT_004f1828 IS ONLY EVER WRITTEN AS 0 (0x004961d0), so it
     never rescues anything and the condition is effectively the one
     flag.

   o DAT_004f1814 IS A REBUILD LATCH, NOT A CAPABILITY BIT.  App_Init
     sets it around its font-construction loop and clears it after
     (0x00473042, 0x00473069); Font_Rebuild and Font_ReloadAll set it
     the same way, and FUN_00496940 - which frees every font's D3D
     bank textures - clears it. [inferred: every writer is
     rebuild-scoped]

   o SO A RUNNING BUILD WITH IT STUCK AT 1 IS SHOWING THE FALLBACK,
     not the intended fonts.  Read live as 1 in a paused mid-race
     MOTO whose display mode had failed, which made the grid
     countdown draw in Generic instead of GameMsg and Messages - one
     typeface where there should be two, at one size where there
     should be 17 and 21 pixels.

     THAT IS A TRAP FOR VISUAL COMPARISON.  A capture from such a
     build is evidence about the fallback path and nothing else, and
     matching the reconstruction to it would bake the degraded look
     in.


7.  Intensity is scaled by font kind

   Font_DrawTextInternal (0x00493820) does NOT hand its intensity
   argument to the blitter untouched.  At 0x00493d47 it scales the
   caller's 0..100 value, and the three scales have nothing in
   common [verified]:

   +---------------------------+---------------------------+-------+
   | Case                      | Scale                     | Range |
   +===========================+===========================+=======+
   | kind 5, AntiAlias8        | (a << 11)    / 100 >> 8   | 0..8  |
   +---------------------------+---------------------------+-------+
   | kind 6, AntiAlias8Transp  | (a * 0xe000) / 100 >> 8   | 0..224|
   +---------------------------+---------------------------+-------+
   | hardware renderer         | (a * 0x3f00) / 100 >> 8   | 0..63 |
   +---------------------------+---------------------------+-------+
   | anything else             | unchanged                 | 0..100|
   +---------------------------+---------------------------+-------+

   The hardware arm is chosen by g_rendererReady and g_rendererMode
   and wins over the kind test.

   o THE RANGES ARE THE BLITTERS', NOT ARBITRARY.  The kind 6 glyph
     blitter adds the bias to the glyph byte's top three bits and
     drops the pixel at 0xe1, so 224 is exactly "fully transparent"
     for that kind.  The kind 5 tables stop at 8.

   o SO THE SAME ALPHA MEANS DIFFERENT THINGS TO DIFFERENT FONTS,
     and a caller that passes a value scaled for one kind to a font
     of another gets a fade that finishes in the first few per cent
     of its duration.  The widget system relies on this: every
     message widget hands over a plain 0..100 and lets the font
     decide.
