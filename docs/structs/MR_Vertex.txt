MotoRacer PC Reverse Engineering                           Analysis Note


                               MR_VERTEX


Abstract

   Size: 0x1c = 28 bytes [verified] - the stride every setup function
   uses to index the vertex array, verts + i * 0x1c.

   Ghidra type applied: created, not force-applied to signatures. The
   record is passed as a bare int in most decompiled prototypes and
   retyping fifteen call sites was judged more churn than benefit.

   The vertex record of the software rasterizer. Every field was
   established from use rather than from a loader, and the last two only
   fell when maybe_Screen3D_ClipEdgeNear was read - it is the one
   function that writes all seven.

2.  How each field was established

   +-----------+------------------------------------------------------------+
   | Field     | Evidence                                                   |
   +===========+============================================================+
   | camZ      | compared against maybe_g_nearPlaneZ by all 5 setups        |
   +-----------+------------------------------------------------------------+
   | shade     | averaged by flat setups, passed per vertex by Gouraud ones |
   +-----------+------------------------------------------------------------+
   | screenX/Y | passed as the six coordinates every emitter sorts by Y     |
   +-----------+------------------------------------------------------------+
   | invZ      | UVs multiplied by it, and divided by it to recover true UV |
   +-----------+------------------------------------------------------------+
   | camX/camY | linearly interpolated to the near plane by ClipEdgeNear    |
   +-----------+------------------------------------------------------------+

   The shade identification took three attempts and is written up in
   subsystems/rasterizer-pipeline.txt sections 18 and 22. It was twice
   called a depth on the strength of being averaged into a per-primitive
   value, and only settled when a Gouraud setup was seen forwarding it
   per vertex and a Gouraud emitter seen interpolating it per scanline.

   camX and camY were the last two. maybe_Screen3D_ClipEdgeNear computes
   them as the linear interpolation of the two input vertices toward the
   near plane, which both identifies them and confirms the guess
   recorded in section 13.2 that +0x00, +0x04 and +0x08 form a float x,
   y, z triple [verified].

3.  The projection

   maybe_Screen3D_ClipEdgeNear is the only place the whole record is
   built from scratch, so it is also the clearest statement of how a
   vertex is projected [verified]:

       t       = (nearZ - a->camZ) / (b->camZ - a->camZ);
       out->camX = a->camX + t * (b->camX - a->camX);
       out->camY = a->camY + t * (b->camY - a->camY);
       out->camZ = nearZ;
       out->invZ = 1.0f / nearZ;
       out->screenX = g_viewCentreX + (int)project(camX);
       out->screenY = g_viewCentreY - (int)project(camY);

   Two details worth keeping. The clipped vertex lands exactly on the
   near plane, so camZ and invZ are both exact rather than interpolated.
   And screen Y is viewCentreY MINUS the projected value, so camera Y
   grows upward while screen Y grows downward.

   The 1.0 numerator is maybe_g_minDenom, the same constant the emitters
   use as a minimum triangle area. That is the compiler sharing one
   float, not a connection between the two uses.

4.  What is NOT in the record

   o NO texture coordinates. UVs are per FACE, four u,v pairs at face
     +0x04, +0x0c, +0x14 and +0x1c, which is why a quad carries four of
     them and a triangle ignores the last. See rasterizer-pipeline.txt
     section 19.2.

   o NO colour. Flat primitives take a packed 555 colour from face
     +0x02; Gouraud primitives interpolate the per-vertex shade at +0x0c
     instead. Whether that shade is a scalar intensity or a packed
     colour is not settled - see section 18.2.

   o NO normal. Lighting is resolved before rasterization; the vertex
     carries only its result.

   So the record is purely post-transform: everything in it is the
   output of the geometry stage, and the rasterizer never sees object
   space.

5.  Open questions

   o CLOSED - it is BOTH, selected by a flag bit. See section 6. The
     question had been posed as either/or three times; the answer is
     that bit 31 switches the meaning of the low 24 bits.

   o CLOSED - see section 7. The array is the pool at 0x006d20d8 and
     the two functions that fill it are Screen3D_TransformVertsFlat
     and Screen3D_TransformVertsShaded.

   o Whether screenX and screenY are plain integers or fixed point.
     CLOSED - plain integers [verified]. Screen3D_TriK9 multiplies the x
     arguments by 0x2000 and uses the y arguments raw as subscripts into
     g_scanlineOffset and g_spanScanlineHead, which a value carrying
     fraction could not do. The scaling exists to give the edge DDA sub-
     pixel precision the vertex does not carry. See
     subsystems/rasterizer-pipeline.txt section 42.4.


6.  The shade word is packed

   +0x0c is not a plain intensity. D3D_WriteVertex (0x004af420) reads it
   once and branches on two high bits, which settles a question this
   note carried open through three attempts [verified]:

       uVar3 = *(uint *)(vertex + 0x0c);
       if (uVar3 & 0x80000000)                    /* section 6.1 */
           diffuse  = uVar3 & 0xffffff;
           specular = 0xffffffff;
       else if (uVar3 & 0x40000000)               /* section 6.2 */
           diffuse  = 0x00ffffff;                 /* or 0xffffffff */
       else {                                     /* section 6.3 */
           g = (uVar3 & 0x3fc00) >> 10;
           if (DAT_0053bb38) g /= 2;
           diffuse  = g << 16 | g << 8 | g;
           specular = diffuse | 0xff000000;
       }

   Layout, high to low:

   +---------+------------------------------------------------------+
   | Bits    | Meaning                                              |
   +=========+======================================================+
   | 31      | the low 24 bits are a literal RGB colour             |
   +---------+------------------------------------------------------+
   | 30      | full bright - draw unshaded white                    |
   +---------+------------------------------------------------------+
   | 18..29  | not read by anything found                           |
   +---------+------------------------------------------------------+
   | 10..17  | the intensity, 8 bits                                |
   +---------+------------------------------------------------------+
   | 0..9    | low fraction, not read                               |
   +---------+------------------------------------------------------+

6.1.  Bit 31 - the low 24 bits are a literal colour

   With bit 31 set the field stops being an intensity: the diffuse
   colour is taken as shade & 0xffffff directly and the specular is
   forced to opaque white. So a vertex can carry either a light level or
   an explicit colour, and the flag says which.

   That is the honest answer to section 5's old question. It was posed
   as "intensity or packed colour"; it is intensity by default and a
   packed colour when bit 31 says so.

6.2.  Bit 30 - full bright

   With bit 30 set the diffuse becomes 0x00ffffff, or 0xffffffff when
   -Alpha (DAT_004dc1c0) or DAT_004dc1bc is on - white, with the alpha
   byte chosen by the texture-format configuration. No shade lookup
   happens at all.

   D3D_DrawScene reads the same bit for a second purpose: it passes
   (shade & 0x40000000) != 0 to D3D_SetDither as a suppression flag and
   forwards it in the high byte of the first argument to
   D3D_EmitTriangle and D3D_EmitLine. That is consistent - an unshaded
   white vertex has no gradient to dither.

   o This closes the open question in subsystems/d3d-render-state.txt
     section 6, which recorded the 0x40000000 vertex bit as suppressing
     dithering without knowing what it MEANT. It means full bright.

   o Nothing in the image sets either flag with an OR. The only writer
     found is a store of the whole word - FUN_00485e50 writes
     0x40000000 exactly, which is bit 30 with an intensity of zero, an
     unshaded vertex. So the flags are composed when the word is built,
     not toggled afterwards.

6.3.  The intensity window, and why two readers disagree

   The two back ends read different windows of the same field, and both
   end at bit 17 [verified]:

   +------------------+-----------------+-------+----------------------+
   | Reader           | Mask and shift  | Range | Use                  |
   +==================+=================+=======+======================+
   | D3D_WriteVertex  | & 0x3fc00 >> 10 | 0..255| grey diffuse, the    |
   |                  |                 |       | same value in R, G, B|
   +------------------+-----------------+-------+----------------------+
   | D3D_DrawScene    | & 0x3e000 >> 5  | 0..31 | row index into the   |
   |                  |                 | x 256 | 8 KB shade ramp at   |
   |                  |                 |       | DAT_005d43b0         |
   +------------------+-----------------+-------+----------------------+

   Bits 13..17 are the top five of bits 10..17, so D3D_DrawScene simply
   takes a coarser five-bit version of the same intensity. Read as the
   19.13 fixed point this note already recorded, bits 13..17 are integer
   bits 0..4 - the range 0 to 31 - and bits 10..12 are the top three
   fractional bits. The two windows are the same number at two
   precisions, not a contradiction.

   o REPLICATION IS THE PROOF. D3D_WriteVertex expands the eight bits
     into R, G and B with the identical value, and D3D_DrawScene uses
     its five bits as ONE row selector applied to all three channel
     bytes in turn. Neither is something a packed colour could survive.
     That is what finally settles the intensity reading, and it is two
     independent sightings rather than one.

   o The 0..31 range is corroborated a third way.
     Screen3D_DispatchPrimitive picks the Full or Half variant of kinds
     1 and 2 on 0x3dfff < shade, and 0x3dfff is 31.0 minus one unit in
     19.13. So subsystems/rasterizer-pipeline.txt section 27.2's "full
     texel above 31.0, halved below" is literally this comparison.

   DAT_0053bb38 halves the intensity when set. D3D_DrawScene sets it for
   primitive kinds 0x15, 0x27 and 0x20 and clears it otherwise, so those
   three kinds draw at half brightness - the hardware counterpart of the
   Full and Half split above.

6.4.  The ramp itself, decoded

   The 8 KB table at 0x005d43b0 - now labelled g_d3dShadeRamp - is
   EMPTY IN THE IMAGE and built at run time. Its only writer is a nine
   instruction loop at the end of Cdd_ctor2 (0x00473950), reached from
   Cd3d_Ctor, and the whole table is [verified]:

       table[level][channel] = channel * level / 32

   32 levels by 256 channel values. The loop does it by repeated
   addition rather than multiplication - an accumulator advanced by the
   level each column - which is why it took reading the assembly to see
   what it was. A plain linear ramp with no gamma of any kind; in 1997
   this bought a table lookup instead of a multiply and a shift per
   channel per vertex.

   o FULL BRIGHTNESS IS 247, NOT 255. The largest entry is
     255 * 31 / 32 = 247, so a lit surface never quite reaches the
     primitive's own flat colour - it tops out about 3 per cent short.
     A port that scales by level/31 instead of level/32 will be
     slightly brighter than the original everywhere.

   D3D_DrawScene applies it at 0x004af8eb, computing the row offset in
   one step rather than forming the level first:

       row = (vertex->shade & 0x3e000) >> 5;      /* level * 256 */
       r = table[row + ((flatColor >> 16) & 0xff)];
       g = table[row + ((flatColor >>  8) & 0xff)];
       b = table[row + ( flatColor        & 0xff)];
       vertex->specular = rgb | 0xff000000;
       vertex->color    = (flatColor & 0xff000000) | rgb;

   Two things fall out of those last two lines:

   o THE PRIMITIVE'S ALPHA BYTE SURVIVES into the diffuse. That is what
     carries the 0x80 the half blend of d3d-render-state.txt section
     3.1 depends on - if the shading returned an opaque colour the
     blend would stop being 50 per cent.

   o THE SPECULAR STORE IS INERT. The same RGB goes into the specular
     with alpha forced to 0xff, and SPECULARENABLE is emitted from
     DAT_0053bb34, which Cd3d_Ctor zeroes in a run of stores right
     after XOR EAX,EAX. The only two references to that address in the
     image are that write and the read in D3D_EmitRenderStates, so the
     specular component is computed for every vertex and never used
     [verified]. Worth knowing rather than merely skipping: a port that
     wrote it faithfully on a device where specular IS enabled would
     add the colour to itself and roughly double every lit surface.

   NOT the same table as g_shadeLut (0x004f62f0), which is 32 x 32
   dwords, belongs to the software rasterizer, and still has no writer -
   subsystems/rasterizer-pipeline.txt section 31.2. The names are
   similar and the tables are not.

6.5.  There are TWO colour routes, and the selector is texture presence

   D3D_WriteVertex (0x004af420) has exactly one caller, and it is
   D3D_DrawScene. So that function carries both the inline shade-ramp
   computation of section 6.4 AND a call to the routine of section 6,
   which branches on bits 31 and 30 instead.

   WHICH ROUTE SERVES WHICH PRIMITIVE IS NOW ESTABLISHED. It is one
   boolean computed per primitive at the top of the body [verified]:

       untextured = kind == 0x0c || kind == 5 || kind == 0
                 || bankIndex > 0x2d
                 || bank[bankIndex].pTexSlot[slot] == -1

       UNTEXTURED  ->  the inline shade-ramp loop
       TEXTURED    ->  D3D_WriteVertex

   where slot is the queue node's texture slot, or 0 when that is -1.

   The sense reads backwards for a moment and then makes sense. An
   untextured face has no texel to take its colour from, so its flat
   colour goes through the intensity ramp. A textured face wants a GREY
   modulator, so that the MODULATE texture stage yields texel times
   light - and a grey is exactly what section 6 builds. Two different
   jobs, two routines.

6.5.1.  Three things that follow

   o THE HALF-BRIGHTNESS KINDS ARE TEXTURED-ROUTE ONLY. DAT_0053bb38 is
     set for kinds 0x15, 0x27 and 0x20 and cleared otherwise, and
     D3D_WriteVertex is its only reader. The ramp route never halves,
     so an untextured face of one of those kinds draws at full
     intensity.

   o THE BIT-31 AND BIT-30 CASES ARE TEXTURED-ROUTE ONLY as well. The
     ramp route reads bits 13..17 and nothing else, so a vertex
     carrying a literal colour or the full-bright flag is shaded as an
     intensity if its primitive happens to be untextured.

   o THE STATE MASK'S BIT 0 IS A DIFFERENT TEST. D3D_DrawScene builds
     that from the KIND alone - set for anything not 0, 5 or 0x0c -
     without asking whether a texture is bound. So a primitive can
     carry mask bit 0 and still take the untextured colour route, when
     its bank has no slot. The two tests overlap and are not the same,
     and subsystems/d3d-render-state.txt section 3's "textured" is the
     mask one.

6.5.2.  The hardware path IS Gouraud shaded, and a correction

   D3D_WriteVertex takes the corner index as its third argument and
   computes a colour only when that is 0, or when g_cfgGouraud
   (DAT_004dc1d8) is 1. D3D_EmitRenderStates reads the same global for
   SHADEMODE:

       SHADEMODE = (g_cfgGouraud == 0) ? 1                  /* FLAT */
                                       : 2 - (DAT_0053bdfc == 0);

   g_cfgGouraud IS INITIALISED TO 1 IN .data, and Cd3d_Ctor sets
   DAT_0053bdfc to 1, so the default resolves to SHADEMODE 2,
   D3DSHADE_GOURAUD, and every corner gets its own colour [verified].

   The single write to g_cfgGouraud anywhere in the image stores ZERO,
   at 0x004146c1 - and that is the HANDLER for the string "-NoGouraud"
   at 0x004dc558, which the flag block at 0x004dc664 documents as
   "Disable gouraud shading". It is a DISABLE switch.

   The address appears nowhere in .data or .rdata, so no table-driven
   parser reaches it indirectly either. Three instruction operands are
   the complete set of references [verified].

6.5.3.  How the previous version of this section got it backwards

   An earlier reading concluded the opposite - that the shipped
   hardware path never Gouraud shaded - and the reasoning is worth
   keeping because the shape of the mistake will recur.

   It searched for WRITERS of the global, found exactly one, saw that
   it stored 0, and took that for the initialisation. It even flagged
   the right residual risk, that a table-driven parser might write the
   flag indirectly, and checked for it.

   THE DEFAULT WAS NEVER A WRITE AT ALL. It was the initialised value
   in .data, which an instruction search cannot see by construction.

   Two things would each have caught it:

   o READ THE GLOBAL'S INITIAL VALUE before concluding anything about
     its default. For a .data global that value IS the default, and the
     writers are the exceptions to it.

   o TAKE THE FLAG'S NAME SERIOUSLY. A switch named as a negation -
     -NoGouraud, -NoD3D, -NoDither, -NoPal256, and this binary has many
     - implies the positive is the default. A "disable" handler storing
     0 is the exception path, not the initialisation.

   The generalisation for this project: "the only writer sets X" is a
   claim about the writers, not about the value. For anything in .data,
   pair it with the initialised value before drawing a conclusion.

7.  Where the array is built

   The pool is a bump-allocated array of MR_Vertex at 0x006d20d8, with
   the cursor in g_vertexPoolCount (0x0068b014), and two functions fill
   it [verified]:

   +-----------------------------+------------+----------------------+
   | Function                    | Address    | Shade source         |
   +=============================+============+======================+
   | Screen3D_TransformVertsFlat | 0x0049b2c0 | ONE intensity        |
   |                             |            | argument, reused for |
   |                             |            | every vertex         |
   +-----------------------------+------------+----------------------+
   | Screen3D_TransformVertsShaded| 0x0049b490| an int ARRAY,        |
   |                             |            | advanced per vertex, |
   |                             |            | and OPTIONAL - see   |
   |                             |            | section 7.2          |
   +-----------------------------+------------+----------------------+

   Both return the base of the slice they just filled, and the caller
   hands that straight to Screen3D_CullAndQueueFaces. The flat one is
   the whole transform in about forty lines:

       camX  = (x*m0 + y*m3 + z*m6 + m9 ) * g_xScale
       camY  = (x*m1 + y*m4 + z*m7 + m10) * g_yScale
       camZ  =  x*m2 + y*m5 + z*m8 + m11
       shade = intensity << 13
       invZ  = 1.0f / camZ
       if (camZ >= nearPlaneZ) {
           screenX = round(g_viewCentreX + invZ * camX)
           screenY = round(g_viewCentreY - invZ * camY)
       }

   The matrix is 3x4, twelve floats, column major. The viewport x and y
   scales are folded into the matrix rows once per call rather than
   applied per vertex.

7.2.  The shaded transform's interface, which is not what it looks like

   Read from the disassembly of 0x0049b490 [verified]. Three things
   about it matter to anything that calls or ports it.

   THE Y COORDINATE COMES FROM A SEPARATE ARRAY. The position source is
   stride 12 and only elements [0] and [2] are ever read; y comes from
   a second pointer of stride 4:

       FLD [ESI]     x       ESI advances by 0xc
       FLD [ESI+8]   z
       FLD [EAX]     y       EAX advances by 4

   That y pointer is also what the entry test rejects on. Pass NULL and
   the function returns the pool base having done nothing - and WITHOUT
   advancing g_vertexPoolCount, because the advance happens after the
   test.

   A heightfield-shaped interface: a base mesh supplying x and z, a
   parallel stream supplying y. animDecor.cpp is the neighbourhood, so
   deforming decorative geometry with per-vertex lighting is the
   obvious reading [inferred].

   THE INTENSITY ARRAY IS OPTIONAL, AND WHEN NULL THE SHADE FIELD IS
   NOT WRITTEN AT ALL. The slot keeps whatever the previous user of it
   left there - the same stale-slot hazard section 7.2's neighbour
   records for screenX and screenY, one field over. A consumer cannot
   tell the difference between an intensity and a leftover.

   THERE IS A SECOND MATRIX AND AN OVERRIDE LIST. The function builds
   two matrices: the argument one with the projection scales folded in
   exactly as the flat transform folds them, and a second from
   FUN_00403a80 combining two globals with the object's matrix at +0x68
   and its negated translation at +0x98..+0xa0.

   The override list is an array of TWELVE-BYTE records

       { float x; int vertexIndex; float z; }

   and the match is an INTEGER compare against the loop counter, not
   the float compare the decompiler shows. A matching vertex is
   transformed by the SECOND matrix from the record's own x and z, and
   the base stream still advances either way. What drives it has not
   been read.

   THE CONTROL WORD BRACKET IS IDENTICAL to the flat transform, so
   FISTP rounds here too. Two functions agreeing on that is worth more
   than either alone.

7.1.  It states the 19.13 format outright

   shade = intensity << 13 is the producer saying what section 6.3 had
   to infer from two readers [verified]. Bits 0..12 are therefore always
   zero in a transformed vertex, which is why the eight bits
   D3D_WriteVertex takes at 10..17 come out as the intensity times
   eight, and the five D3D_DrawScene takes at 13..17 come out as the
   intensity itself. Same number, two precisions, now explained from
   both ends.

   It also locates the flags. Bit 30 of the shade word is bit 17 of the
   intensity argument and bit 31 is bit 18, so a caller sets full
   bright or literal-colour by passing a large intensity rather than by
   OR-ing anything afterwards. That is consistent with section 6.2's
   finding that nothing in the image ORs those bits in.

7.2.  Two consequences worth carrying into a port

   o SCREEN COORDINATES ARE CONDITIONAL. screenX and screenY are
     written only when camZ passes the near plane. A vertex behind the
     camera keeps whatever the previous user of that pool slot left
     there. Every consumer must test camZ before reading a screen
     coordinate, and Screen3D_CullAndQueueFaces does exactly that.
     A port that always projects, or that zeroes the pool, will not
     reproduce the original's behaviour on near-plane-crossing faces -
     though it will be tidier, and the difference is only visible
     through the clipper.

   o THE POOL HAS NO BOUND CHECK in either transform. The cursor is
     bumped by the vertex count and the write proceeds.

     THE RESET IS Screen3D_BeginFrame [verified]. It zeroes
     g_vertexPoolCount once per frame, in the same guarded block that
     seeds the span free list, alongside the queue-node count and the
     split-primitive pool index. So the only thing bounding the pool is
     how much geometry one frame submits, and nothing checks it - the
     queue node count is capped at 0x1d4b by its consumer, but the
     vertex pool is not capped anywhere.

     The reset is SKIPPED when BeginFrame's first argument is non-zero,
     which is the resume path used around a mid-frame error dialog. A
     port must keep that conditional: resuming an interrupted frame and
     resetting the pool underneath it would strand every queued
     primitive's vertex pointers. See
     subsystems/rasterizer-pipeline.txt section 38.0.

   The flat and Gouraud split here is the same axis as the software
   pipeline's slots 4/6 versus 5/7 - see
   subsystems/rasterizer-pipeline.txt section 5 - so the decision is
   made once, at transform time, and the primitive kind carries it
   downstream.


7.3.  The loop runs under a modified x87 control word

   Neither of the two things that matter most about this function is
   visible in the decompiled C. Both are in three instructions before
   the loop [verified from the disassembly]:

       FSTCW  [ESP+0x10]        save the control word
       AND    EAX, 0xfcff       clear bits 8,9
       OR     EAX, 0x000c       set bits 2,3
       FLDCW  [ESP+0x12]
       ... the whole vertex loop ...
       FLDCW  [ESP+0x10]        restore

   Bits 8 and 9 are PRECISION CONTROL. Clearing them selects 00, single
   precision, 24-bit mantissa. Bits 2 and 3 are the ZM and OM exception
   masks - zero divide and overflow.

   THE TRANSFORM IS COMPUTED AT SINGLE PRECISION ON PURPOSE. No 80-bit
   intermediate survives inside the loop; every partial product is
   rounded to float32 as it goes. A port that computes in double will
   produce slightly different screen coordinates, and the difference
   will land on rounding boundaries.

   MASKING THE ZERO DIVIDE IS WHAT MAKES 1.0f / camZ SAFE. A vertex
   exactly on the camera plane yields infinity rather than raising, and
   the near-plane test afterwards discards it.

7.3.1.  It rounds. It does not truncate.

   ROUNDING CONTROL IS BITS 10 AND 11, AND THEY ARE NOT TOUCHED. They
   stay at 00, round-to-nearest-even, so the two FISTP instructions
   that write screenX and screenY ROUND.

   This is worth stating loudly because the usual MSVC truncation
   idiom looks almost identical and does the opposite:

       truncate (__ftol)   OR 0x0c00     bits 10,11  RC = toward zero
       this function       OR 0x000c     bits 2,3    exception masks

   0x0c00 against 0x000c. Same digits, different shift. Reading one for
   the other gives a transform that is wrong by up to a pixel
   everywhere and by a consistent whole pixel on negative coordinates,
   which is exactly the kind of error that looks like a viewport
   offset rather than a rounding bug.

   In C the equivalent is lrintf, which honours the current rounding
   mode. A cast is not: a cast truncates toward zero.

7.3.2.  A note on where this came from

   docs/subsystems/rasterizer-pipeline.txt section 50.3 wrote the
   projection as round(...) from Screen3D_ProjectVertex, and was right,
   but the reason was not established there - it was taken from
