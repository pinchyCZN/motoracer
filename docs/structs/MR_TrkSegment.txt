MotoRacer PC Reverse Engineering                           Analysis Note


                            MR_TRKSEGMENT


Abstract

   Size: 0xfc, 252 bytes [verified] - the stride every walker of
   g_pTrkSegments uses, and the figure formats/trk.txt section 2
   already recorded. Ghidra type applied: yes, 17 named fields.

   One track segment as it exists IN MEMORY. A track is an array of
   these, reached through the track object at 0x0052c108, whose first
   two fields are the array pointer and the count.

   THE FILE RECORD IS 132 BYTES AND THIS IS 252. The two are not the
   same layout and this document describes only the memory one. See
   section 3.

   | maybe_Trk_ClipMoveAgainstWal | 0x0040ca80   | wallPlane0, wallPlane1 -         |
   | ls                           |              | MR_Racer.txt 10                  |

   | maybe_Trk_DrawZonesAndListSe | 0x0040ce60   | decorIndex, decorCount           |

   o SEVEN PLANES IS THE STRIKING NUMBER. A segment carries two wall
     planes, two ground planes, a selector between the ground planes,
     and two more nobody has read. That is a lot of geometry per
     segment, and it is why the memory record is nearly twice the
     file record.

   o THE GROUND IS TWO PLANES SPLIT BY A THIRD, which is a quad
     divided into two triangles with the selector as the diagonal.
     formats/trk.txt 2.3 has the arithmetic.

3.  The file record is not this record

   formats/trk.txt section 2 gives the on-disk segment as 132 bytes:
   12 for a vec3, 48 for a 4x3 matrix, 4, then 64 for "four planes,
   16 bytes each", then 4.

   The first 60 bytes DO correspond - the origin at +0x00 and the
   matrix at +0x0c are read at those offsets in memory.

3.1.  FOUR SLOTS ARE BUILT, TWO ARE READ - and this took three
      readings to get right

   maybe_Trk_BuildSegmentPlanes (0x0040e5c0) is called once from
   Trk_Load and loops every segment.

   WRITTEN, all through 0x004b0f20 (plane from a normal and a point):

       0x0040e62e   +0x40   plane0, OVERWRITTEN
       0x0040ea21   +0xa0   groundSelector
       0x0040eb0d   +0x80   groundPlaneA
       0x0040ec05   +0x90   groundPlaneB

   READ, NOT WRITTEN:

       0x0040e7ef   +0x60   wallPlane0
       0x0040e8be   +0x70   wallPlane1

   both as the ECX argument to Plane_IntersectSegment (0x004b0f60),
   which is a RAY/PLANE INTERSECTION, not a constructor.

   o SO THE WALLS COME FROM THE FILE, and section 3's ORIGINAL
     reading - the file's four planes landing at +0x40..+0x7f as
     plane0, plane1 and the two walls - WAS RIGHT ALL ALONG.

   o THIS SECTION WAS WRONG TWICE BEFORE THIS. Reading the LEA sites
     alone gave "five planes built". Adding the CALL targets gave
     "six slots, two constructors". Reading the CALLEE gave this.
     AN OFFSET SAYS WHERE A POINTER POINTS, NOT WHETHER THE CALLEE
     READS OR WRITES IT - and that is the third time in one session
     the same shortcut has produced a wrong write-up.

3.2.  How the road surface is made

       corner = segment->pos
                + transform((+-2048, 0, 0), segment matrix)

   on THIS segment and the next, wrapping with (i+1) % segCount, for
   four corners of a quad. Each is then CLIPPED against a wall plane
   with Plane_IntersectSegment.

   o SO +-2048 IS NOMINAL, NOT ACTUAL. 4096 units is the widest a
     segment can be; the walls decide what it really is. An earlier
     draft of this section called 4096 "the track width" and used it
     to reason about the world scale - that inference is withdrawn.

   o plane0 IS THE SEGMENT'S ENTRY PLANE: the matrix's forward axis
     (0,0,1) transformed and normalised, through segment->pos.

   o THE SELECTOR IS VERTICAL. Its normal's Y is stored as a LITERAL
     ZERO at 0x0040e9c0 and its x and z are a difference of two
     clipped points with the components swapped and one negated - a
     90-degree turn in XZ. It is the vertical plane through the
     quad's DIAGONAL, and groundPlaneA and groundPlaneB are
     normalised cross products of clipped-point differences: the two
     triangles either side of it.

   o A ROAD SURFACE IS TWO TRIANGLES AND A CHOOSER, and the chooser
     is vertical because the choice is a question about x and z only.

3.3.  The pairings, pinned against the running game

   The decompile shows the argument flow only indirectly: the Vec3_Add
   and Plane_IntersectSegment returns come back as extraout_EAX
   phantoms, and the pairings have to be followed through the LOCALS
   each result is copied into.  Done that way it gives A/C/D/B in
   build order, each next-segment corner against the next segment's
   wall.

   IT WAS ALSO MEASURED, independently and first: a live track was
   read out of memory and every corner tested against every built
   plane.  The two agree, which is why both are kept - the static
   read gives the order, the measurement gives the geometry.

   Segment 300 of a 716-segment track, chosen because it CURVES AND
   SLOPES - a flat segment cannot tell the two triangles apart:

   +------------------------+--------------+--------------+--------------+
   | Corner, segment 300    | selector     | groundA      | groundB      |
   +========================+==============+==============+==============+
   | seg300 LEFT   A        | +288.098     | +0.001       | -84.643      |
   +------------------------+--------------+--------------+--------------+
   | seg300 RIGHT  B        | -0.001       | +0.001       | +0.000       |
   +------------------------+--------------+--------------+--------------+
   | seg301 LEFT   C        | -0.001       | +0.001       | -0.000       |
   +------------------------+--------------+--------------+--------------+
   | seg301 RIGHT  D        | -678.137     | +181.591     | +0.000       |
   +------------------------+--------------+--------------+--------------+

   o THE DIAGONAL IS B->C - this segment's RIGHT corner to the next
     one's LEFT. Only those two lie on the selector, and they do on
     segment 0 as well.

   o ground[0] IS THE TRIANGLE A, B, C and ground[1] IS B, C, D.
     Each plane contains exactly its own three corners to within
     0.001 units and misses the fourth by 85 to 182.

   o AND THE SIDE TEST MATCHES: A is on the positive side of the
     selector and lies on ground[0]; D is on the negative side and
     lies on ground[1]. So Trk_GroundHeightAt's `>= 0 picks
     groundPlaneA` is the triangle containing THIS segment's left
     corner.

3.4.  The normals reproduce to five decimal places

   +------------------+------------------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | Plane            | Live, read from memory             | Computed from the corners          |
   +==================+====================================+====================================+
   | groundA          | ( 0.25250, 0.89075, 0.37790)       | (C-A)x(B-A) -> ( 0.25247, 0.89078, |
   |                  |                                    | 0.37786)                           |
   +------------------+------------------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | groundB          | (-0.08107,-0.97730,-0.19572)       | (C-D)x(B-D) -> (-0.08107,          |
   |                  |                                    | -0.97730,-0.19572)                 |
   +------------------+------------------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | selector         | (-0.66701, 0.00000,-0.74505)       | (Bz-Cz,0,Cx-Bx) -> (-0.66702,      |
   |                  |                                    | 0.00000,-0.74504)                  |
   +------------------+------------------------------------+------------------------------------+

   o SO THE CROSS-PRODUCT ORDER IS SETTLED, including the winding.
     ground[1] comes out facing OPPOSITE ground[0] - the live normals
     have Y +0.89 and -0.98 - and that is not a slip: the height
     solve divides by the normal's Y, so the sign cancels.

   o A DECOMPILE COULD NOT HAVE GIVEN THIS. Three readings of the
     disassembly produced three different wrong answers (3.1); four
     memory reads and a dot product settled it in one pass. WHERE THE
     GAME IS RUNNING AND THE DATA IS DERIVED, READ THE DATA.

3.5.  Each corner clips against its OWN segment's wall

   +--------------------------+--------------------+--------------------+
   | Corner                   | seg300 wall0/1     | seg301 wall0/1     |
   +==========================+====================+====================+
   | seg300 LEFT              | -0.95 / 2016.70    | -61.07 / 2052.00   |
   +--------------------------+--------------------+--------------------+
   | seg300 RIGHT             | 1978.59 / -2.38    | 1778.05 / 138.91   |
   +--------------------------+--------------------+--------------------+
   | seg301 LEFT              | -0.86 / 2044.92    | 0.05 / 2018.88     |
   +--------------------------+--------------------+--------------------+
   | seg301 RIGHT             | 2043.02 / -2.39    | 1979.59 / -0.03    |
   +--------------------------+--------------------+--------------------+

   o LEFT CORNERS CLIP AGAINST wall0, RIGHT AGAINST wall1, and the
     next segment's corners against the NEXT segment's walls. The
     near-misses are the tell: seg301's left corner sits 0.86 from
     segment 300's wall0 but 0.05 from its own.

   o +-2048 IS NOMINAL. On this track the walls put the real corners
     at +-1024, so the road is 2048 across rather than 4096. An
     earlier draft used 4096 to reason about the world scale; that
     inference stays withdrawn.

3.6.  Transcribed, and VERIFIED against the original

   src/trk.c has plane_from_normal_point, plane_intersect_segment,
   plane_distance, trk_ground_height_at and trk_build_segment_planes,
   and trk_load calls the builder before returning.

   THE OUTPUT WAS DIFFED AGAINST THE RUNNING GAME. Both were given
   ile01.trk - 788 segments - and segment 300 compared plane for
   plane [verified]:

   +--------------+------------------------------------------+--------------+
   | Plane        | Original, read out of live memory        | Ours         |
   +==============+==========================================+==============+
   | entry        | (-0.96593, 0.00000,-0.25882)             | MATCH        |
   |              | d=-37340.320                             |              |
   +--------------+------------------------------------------+--------------+
   | wall0        | (-0.21088, 0.00000, 0.97751) d=          | MATCH        |
   |              | 12654.575                                |              |
   +--------------+------------------------------------------+--------------+
   | wall1        | ( 0.20492,-0.00000,-0.97878)             | MATCH        |
   |              | d=-10577.889                             |              |
   +--------------+------------------------------------------+--------------+
   | groundA      | (-0.00663, 0.99998,-0.00178) d=          | MATCH        |
   |              | -2122.894                                |              |
   +--------------+------------------------------------------+--------------+
   | groundB      | ( 0.00847,-0.99996, 0.00180) d=          | MATCH        |
   |              | 2185.618                                 |              |
   +--------------+------------------------------------------+--------------+
   | selector     | ( 0.99992, 0.00000, 0.01293) d=          | MATCH        |
   |              | 34090.684                                |              |

   o EVERY DIGIT WE LOGGED. The segment position matches exactly too,
     so it is the same track and the same indexing.

   o SO THE WHOLE CHAIN IS CONFIRMED, not just the arithmetic: the
     nominal +-2048 corners, clipping each against ITS OWN segment's
     wall on the correct side, the B->C diagonal, both cross-product
     windings and every plane's anchor point. 3.3 and 3.4 proved the
     ALGORITHM on the game's data; this proves OUR LOADER reproducing
     the game's output.

   o THE DUMP THAT PROVED IT HAS BEEN REMOVED, having done its
     job. If it is ever wanted again: log each segment's seven
     planes from trk_build_segment_planes, and LEAD WITH THE
     SEGMENT COUNT rather than seg0's position - seg0 sits at
     (0, 0, 512) on more than one track, so only the count says
     which one you are looking at.

3.7.  plane1 is populated after all

   The same read settled the other half of section 4's first open
   question. plane1, at +0x50 on segment 300 of ile01:

       (-0.00759, 0.99997, -0.00161)  d = -2151.808

   o IT IS A NEAR-FLAT GROUND PLANE, close to groundA
     (-0.00663, 0.99998, -0.00178, d = -2122.894) but NOT equal to
     it. So it comes from the file and is a nominal or averaged road
     plane rather than either triangle.

   o STILL NO KNOWN READER. Nothing found so far consumes it, and
     maybe_Trk_BuildSegmentPlanes does not write it. But it is no
     longer a blank slot, and "an averaged road plane with no reader"
     is a much narrower question than "unknown". [open]

4.  Open questions

   o plane1 at +0x50 has no reader and no builder, but it IS
     populated - a near-flat ground plane from the file, close to
     groundA and not equal to it. See 3.7. plane0 is overwritten
     by the builder, so whatever the file put there is discarded.

   x Where the three ground planes come from. ANSWERED - they
     are BUILT by maybe_Trk_BuildSegmentPlanes at load time,
     - plane0, the selector and both ground planes. The
     WALLS are not built; they come from the file and are
     read to clip with. See section 3.1.

   o +0x3c, the dword after the matrix. Present in the file record,
     never read.

   o The gaps: +0xb0, +0xb6..+0xd3, +0xda..+0xe3, +0xec..+0xef and
     +0xf8..+0xfb. 62 bytes unaccounted for, which for a record this
     heavily used is a lot.

   o decorCount at +0xd4 is typed int on the strength of decor.txt
     calling it a count; nothing has read its width directly.
